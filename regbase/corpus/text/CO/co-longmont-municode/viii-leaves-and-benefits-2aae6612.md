This chapter shall constitute and be referred to as the official "personnel rules"
of the city. The provisions thereof shall apply to all employees of the city.
(Code 1981, § 1-23-1; Code 1993, § 3.04.010; Code 2009, § 3.04.010; Ord. No. O-79-13;
Ord. No. O-85-14, § 1)
As used in this title, unless the context otherwise requires, the following definitions
apply:
Acting appointment means temporary assignment of an employee to a different classification in accordance
with the personnel rules.
Administrative leave means an absence, with full pay and benefits, from the employee's normal worksite
pursuant to section 3.04.465 . Administrative leave shall not constitute discipline.
Appeal means a written, formal complaint by an employee based on an alleged impropriety
in the administration of discipline other than reprimand.
Appointment (selection) means formal authorization of the hiring of an individual to fill a budgeted vacant
position. Appointments can be regular, part-time, temporary, interim or acting.
Callback means overtime required of an employee that requires the employee to return to the
worksite after leaving the worksite at the end of the regular working day.
Career series means a set of classifications within a job family designated by the human resources
division which are closely related in terms of work performed and distinguished from
each other primarily by level of responsibility and scope of duties assumed. A career
series may include classifications from the entry through various supervisory levels.
Cause means motive or reason for discipline of regular employees, determined by the standards
of job performance and maintenance of the public interest.
Class specification means a written statement of the characteristic duties, responsibilities and qualification
requirements that distinguish a given position or job title from another.
Classification (noun) means a position or job title.
Classification (process) means analysis based on comparative duties, responsibilities and qualifications,
by which appropriate classes are determined.
Classification plan means a schematic list of positions or job titles.
Compensatory time off means compensation for overtime in the form of time off in lieu of cash payment,
which is calculated at the rate of 1½ times the overtime hours worked.
Conflict of interest means an employee obligation outside of the workplace that has the potential to interfere
with the employee's duties as a city representative or create the appearance of impropriety.
Conflicts are identified as, but not limited to, purchasing code conflicts, advisory
board conflicts, and conflicts with council rules and procedure.
Deadly weapon has the same meaning given that term in title 10 .
Demotion means the change of an employee from a position of one class to a position of another
class with a lower maximum pay rate.
De novo hearing means a new hearing.
Discipline means action taken with regard to an employee's unsatisfactory work performance or
other employee conduct deemed prejudicial to the public interest, including reprimand,
suspension, demotion or dismissal.
Dismissal means the involuntary separation of an employee from employment.
Division head means administrative personnel reporting directly to an executive director or another
high-level management position.
Employee means an authorized and appointed incumbent of a position in the municipal service.
Executive director means administrative personnel reporting directly to the city manager as well as
directors of city departments as identified in the administrative regulations. Division
heads and other high-level supervisors or administrative personnel may, from time
to time and except as otherwise provided by these rules, be designated to act on behalf
of an executive director. Such designation shall be in writing and approved by the
city manager. The term "executive director" also includes the city attorney, who does
not report to the city manager.
Fixed-term employee means an employee who is hired to work for a specific project and a designated period
of time. Fixed-term employees may be either full-time or part-time. Fixed-term employees
working full-time or three-quarter time have the same rights and responsibilities
under this Code as regular employees.
Fringe benefits means compensation to employees, in-kind and in cash, except leave, in addition to
salary paid for services rendered; however, the term "fringe benefits" does not include
awards to employees under the employee recognition program or similar programs designated
by the city manager which recognize exceptional employee accomplishments. (Note: exceptional
pay is part of base salary and not included in the definition of the term "fringe
benefits.")
Grant funded positions means positions funded entirely by subsidies from entities other than the city.
Grievance means a written, formal complaint by an employee, based on a perception that the
personnel rules have not been followed by the city, not including disciplinary actions.
Hearing officer means a person appointed by the city manager to hear appeals by employees regarding
disciplinary action.
Incumbent means the current occupant of a position in the municipal service.
Interim employee means an outside applicant appointed, on an interim basis, to a temporarily vacant
regular position.
Job vacancy means a budgeted position for which funds are allocated, but which is not occupied.
Leave means an authorized absence from regularly scheduled work hours for reasons specified
in the personnel rules (holidays, vacation, sickness, injury, family and medical leave,
disability, jury duty, witness, bereavement, military).
Non-benefitted means a position which is less than 30 hours per week and designated to receive limited
benefits.
Overtime means time an employee is directed and authorized to work in excess of the standard
work week, or in the case of fire and police personnel, the standard work period.
Part-time means an employee who works on a schedule of less than 30 hours per week. Part-time
employees may be regular or non-benefitted.
Part-time, non-benefited means a part-time position designated for more than one year duration and not designated
in the annual budget to receive paid leave and fringe benefits unless required by
law. As applied to employees, part-time, non-benefitted means a temporary employee
with a continuous employment period of one year or more.
Pay or compensation plan means the aggregate of pay rates (either single rates or ranges) assigned to each
class of positions in the classified plan.
Pay range means those points between and including the minimum and maximum rates of pay established
for a class by the classification and pay plan.
Pay step (pay rate) means a single rate of pay within a pay range.
Performance review means written or oral evaluation of the job performance of an employee by a supervisor.
Position means the aggregate of duties and responsibilities performed by one person. A position
may be regular, part-time, fixed term, temporary or acting and may be occupied or
vacant.
Prevailing market rate means the level of pay for a given class within a given labor market area.
Probationary period means a period following appointment to a regular position for employees, during
which the employee is on trial and during which the employee is evaluated for job
suitability in accordance with the personnel rules.
Promotion means the change of an employee from a position of one class to a position of another
class with a higher maximum pay rate.
Provisional status means a trial employment period of one year following a promotion within a designated
career series in the fire and police departments. The period of provisional status
shall serve as an integral part of the selection process and shall be used to closely
observe the employee's work in order to achieve the most effective transition of the
employee to the higher level position and to avoid promoting any employee who does
not meet required standards. A promotion shall be considered provisional and shall
not be considered final until successful completion of the trial employment period,
but shall entitle the employee to the salary for the position during provisional status.
Reclassification means the process of analysis by which an established position is reviewed to determine
whether the duties and responsibilities of that position merit a change in class designation.
Regular employee means an employee appointed to a regular position according to the personnel rules.
A regular employee may be full-time or three-quarter time. Employees hired into a
part-time, benefitted position prior to January 1, 2018, are also considered regular
employees.
Regular position means a full-time or three-quarter time position of 30 or more hours per week designated
in the annual budget to receive all paid leave and fringe benefits in proportion to
hours worked. This includes full-time and three-quarter time fixed-term appointments,
and half-time positions hired as benefitted prior to January 1, 2018.
Reprimand means a written censure of an employee by a supervisor.
Resignation means the voluntary termination of an employee from the municipal service.
Retirement means the termination of an employee, due to the employee's eligibility for retirement
under the city's retirement plan.
Review period means a period following a promotion or transfer appointment to a regular position
for employees, during which the employee is evaluated for job suitability in accordance
with the personnel rules.
Seasonal employee means an employee appointed to a temporary position who is hired to work a designated
period of 26 weeks or less in order to complete assignments that only last for part
of the year. Seasonal positions are identified as such, and the seasonal employee
is notified at the time of hire.
Selection techniques means methods by which the city compares the knowledge, skills and abilities of job
applicants, to determine which applicants to appoint, if any.
Separation means the voluntary or involuntary end of employment of an individual employee.
Supervisor means an employee who has been given the authority in the interest of the employer
to hire, promote, reprimand, suspend, discharge, assign, recall, reward or discipline
other employees, or the responsibility to direct other employees, adjust their grievances,
or effectively to recommend such actions.
Suspension means an absence without pay imposed on an employee for disciplinary reasons by a
supervisor.
Temporary employee means an employee appointed to a temporary position of less than one year according
to the personnel rules. A temporary employee may be full-time or part-time.
Temporary position means a full-time or part-time, non-benefited position designated for less than one
year's duration.
Termination means the involuntary end of employment of an individual employee.
Transfer means the appointment or assignment of an employee to a new position either within
the same classification or within a new classification still within the municipal
service.
Worker's compensation means the city insurance fund established to provide benefits to employees injured
on the job.

A.

and establish a pay plan based on the prevailing market rate, as defined by both salary
and benefit levels and other factors the city manager deems appropriate for job titles
within the pay plan. The pay plan shall annually be recommended by the city manager
to the city council.
B.
Market rate is defined as the average salary or salary range for a position. Market
rates for all positions will be determined utilizing valid governmental or private
survey data. When applicable a mixture of governmental and private data will be used.
(1)
For government-specific positions that can be compared to a local labor market and
filled via local recruitment, market rate will be determined by valid local government
market surveys. The city will set the market rate at the 50th percentile of municipalities
of similar size.
(2)
For cross-industry positions with a local labor market and filled via local recruitment,
market rate will be determined by an appropriate mixture of governmental and private
data. The city will combine surveys and set the market rate at the 50th percentile
of the combined market.
(3)
For positions involving national recruitment, the market rate will be determined by
an appropriate mixture of governmental and private data. Any national survey data
utilized will include a geographical differential to account for local cost of labor
differential. Any national survey data utilized will incorporate a local market index.

nonexempt employee is actually called back to the workplace or a designated work site

administration of the on-call pay policy for those positions in the organization that
perform essential functions for the city that might require them to return to the

to arrange for the training during an employee's regularly scheduled work hours. Should
training time result in overtime, prior authorization by the executive director or
designee must be obtained. However, an executive director may change an employee's
normal work hours to accommodate the training schedule.
G.
Pyramiding. Compensation shall not be made more than once for the same hours under any provision
of the personnel rules or other applicable regulations.

A.
Selection techniques. The selection techniques used in the examination process shall be objective, of a
practical nature, and shall relate to those factors which, upon the review of the
chief human resources officer, can reasonably be expected to measure the relative
capabilities of the persons examined in their ability to execute the duties and responsibilities

not limited to, achievement and aptitude tests, other written tests, personal interviews,
oral boards, assessment centers, performance tests, physical agility tests, evaluation

or other tests.

one or more of the tests in the selection process for applicants whose qualifications

examinations may include any of the selection techniques mentioned in this section,
or any combination of them. Only employees who meet the requirements set forth in
the promotional announcements may compete in promotional examinations.

Regular appointment. An employee appointed to a regular position of 30 or more hours per week, as well
as full-time and three-quarter time fixed-term appointments shall receive all benefits
offered by the city, including, but not limited to, paid leave and fringe benefits
in proportion to hours worked, and has rights of grievance and appeal except during

B.
Part-time non-benefited appointment. An employee appointed to a position for more than one year, in which the employee
is working a part-time schedule of less than 30 hours. Part-time, non-benefited appointments
are generally not eligible for employee benefits except for bereavement leave and
as required by state law, and do not have grievance and appeal rights during their
employment.

Temporary appointment. An employee appointed to a temporary position shall not receive paid leave except
jury and witness leave, paid sick leave as required by state law. These positions
are generally not eligible for employee benefits except as required by state law and
do not have grievance and appeal rights. Temporary appointments may not exceed 12
months.

the city manager may appoint an employee to a different job classification. The city
shall pay the employee the salary for the appointed job during the acting appointment,
if the salary is at least equal to the employee's salary before the acting appointment.
The city manager may not make an acting appointment for more than 12 calendar months,
but may, upon a showing of good cause and with city council approval, extend such
acting appointment in increments of six months.
E.
Interim appointment. The city manager or designee may appoint an outside applicant, with or without examination,
on an interim basis for up to 12 months, to a temporarily vacant regular position.

extend such interim appointment in increments of six months. Persons so appointed
must meet the minimum training, experience and other employment qualifications for
the position. Interim employees serve at the pleasure of the city manager.

Anniversary date. If a temporary or interim employee becomes a regular employee, the employee's anniversary
date is the effective date of the regular appointment.

Policy. It is the policy of the city to provide prompt and equitable resolution of employee
grievances. Supervisors and employees are expected to make every effort to resolve
problems timely and informally. If such informal means fail to resolve such problems
satisfactorily, a formal grievance procedure is established. The purpose of this procedure
is to provide a just and equitable method for the resolution of grievances without
prejudice, coercion or reprisal.
B.
Grounds for grievance. With the exception of matters pertaining to harassment of any kind (which must be
reported immediately and directly to human resources), disciplinary action, performance
evaluation and/or failure to grant a step or salary increase, any regular employee
who has completed the probationary period may file a grievance in response to any
perceived inconsistency in the application of any personnel rules or procedures, administrative
regulations, approved departmental policies and rules, or any personnel-related provisions
of the Charter.

Any employee who is aggrieved may present the grievance according to the following
procedure:
A.
The eligible employee shall present the grievance in writing to the employee's immediate
supervisor within 14 calendar days of the action which gives rise to the grievance.
The grievance shall state the factual basis for the employee's complaint, the date
on which the matter complained of occurred, and the disposition sought by the aggrieved
employee. The supervisor and the employee shall attempt to resolve the matter. The
supervisor shall notify the employee in writing of the supervisor's decision regarding
the grievance within seven calendar days of receipt of the grievance.
B.
If the grievance is not settled to the satisfaction of the employee and the employee
wishes to pursue the grievance, the employee shall present the grievance to the next
level of supervision within seven calendar days of the notice of the decision of the
immediate supervisor. The supervisor at this level and the employee shall attempt
to resolve the matter. The supervisor shall notify the employee in writing of the
supervisor's decision within seven calendar days of receipt of the grievance.
C.
If the grievance is not settled at this level to the satisfaction of the employee,
and the employee wishes to pursue the grievance, the employee shall continue consistent
with the procedure outlined in subsection B of this section to present the grievance
to successive levels of supervision, up to and including the city manager. The decision
of the city manager shall be final in the grievance procedure.
D.
In the event that an employee's grievance does not involve the employee's respective
supervisors, the written grievance, upon concurrence of the chief human resources
officer, shall be submitted directly to the executive director of the employee's department,
who shall notify the employee of the decision within seven calendar days of receipt
of the grievance. However, such grievance, in order to be considered by the executive
director, shall be submitted to the executive director within 14 calendar days of
the occurrence of the aggrieved action. The decision of the executive director may
be appealed directly to the city manager, whose decision shall be final.
E.
The chief human resources officer may be requested to be involved at any stage of
the grievance procedure by either the employee or the executive director. The aggrieved
employee may also obtain the assistance of legal counsel in preparing or presenting
the grievance to any level of appeal.
