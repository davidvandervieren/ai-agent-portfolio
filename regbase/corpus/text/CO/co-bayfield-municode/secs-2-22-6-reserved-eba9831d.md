(a)
Board of trustees; authority, qualifications.
(1)
Authority. The board of trustees shall constitute the legislative body of the town, shall have
the power and authority, except as otherwise provided by statute, to exercise all
power conferred upon or possessed by the town, and shall have the power and authority
to adopt such laws, ordinances and resolutions as it shall deem proper in the exercise
thereof.
(2)
Qualifications. Each trustee shall be a resident of the town and a registered elector who has resided
within the town limits for a period of at least twelve (12) consecutive months immediately
preceding the date of the election. However, in case of annexation of property, any
person who has resided within the annexed territory for the time prescribed in this
subsection shall be deemed to have met the residence requirements for the town.
(b)
Terms of office.
(1)
Trustees. Unless a trustee position or positions was vacated three trustees shall be elected
April 2, 2024. The three candidates for trustee receiving the highest numbers of votes
shall be elected for four-year terms, and if a vacancy exits candidates for trustee
receiving the next highest numbers of votes shall be elected for two-year terms. At
the next subsequent regular election and at each regular election thereafter, three
trustees shall be elected to serve four-year terms.
(2)
Mayor. At the April 2, 2024 election, and at the regular election every four years thereafter,
a mayor shall be elected to serve a four-year term.
(c)
Time and place of meetings.
(1)
Regular meetings. The board of trustees shall hold its regular meetings on the first and third Tuesday
of every month. A regular meeting may be canceled, rescheduled or postponed by the
mayor, in the mayor's absence by the mayor pro tem or acting mayor. Meeting time and
notice or cancelation of meetings shall comply with 24-6-402 (1) (III) (d) (I) C.R.S.
(2)
Special meetings and work sessions. Special meetings or work sessions of the board of trustees may be called at any time
by using the following methods:
a.
By a signed request of any three members of the board of trustees filed with the town
clerk.
b.
By the mayor by notification either written or oral to the clerk.
c.
By the town manager in the event three members and the mayor are available the date
and time scheduled and notification either written or oral to the clerk. Notice shall
be given to the other members including the mayor either orally or in writing at least
24 hours before the meeting. Meeting notice or cancelation of meetings shall comply
with 24-6-402 (1) (III) (d) (I) C.R.S.
(d)
Remote meetings.
(1)
Authorization for a remote meeting; declaration of state of emergency. When an in-person meeting is not reasonable or prudent to protect the public health,
safety or welfare, the mayor may, by providing written notice to the town manager
and each trustee declare a state of emergency and direct that a board of trustee meeting
be conducted remotely. A state of emergency may arise from, among other reasons, an
epidemic or pandemic, extreme weather events, natural disasters or damage to roads
or other transportation facilities. The mayor's written notice of a declaration of
emergency shall be provided by hand delivery or electronic mail, be posted on the
front entryway of town hall and, as soon as practicable, on the town's website and
be included in the minutes of the remote meeting. A remote meeting shall be considered
a regular meeting or special meeting, as applicable, and, except as set forth herein
or otherwise impractical, all meeting requirements shall apply to the remote meeting.
(2)
Type of remote meeting; notice. In the discretion of the town manager, remote meetings may be conducted by telephone,
video conferencing or other electronic means. The town manager shall endeavor to ensure
that the board of trustees, town staff and the public are able to hear or read the
discussion and testimony presented during the remote meeting. The town manager shall
provide direct notice regarding the manner in which to participate in the remote meeting
to the board of trustees and town staff and shall post notice for the benefit of the
public on the front entryway of town hall and, as soon as practicable, on the town's
website.
(3)
Public participation. The town manager shall take reasonable and practical measures to ensure that each
remote meeting is open to the public and provides the public with an opportunity to
be heard, unless public participation is not practicable or feasible due to the emergency
situation. If public participation is not available, any action taken at the remote
meeting shall be effective only until the next meeting that allows for public participation.
At that next meeting, the board of trustees may ratify any action taken at the remote
meeting that did not allow for public participation. If the board of trustees fails
to ratify the action, such action shall be deemed rescinded.
(4)
Voting. All votes during a remote meeting shall be conducted by roll call.
(5)
Minutes. Minutes of the remote meeting shall be taken and retained in the records of the town
and shall be open to public inspection.
(6)
Public meeting with remote public participation. To protect the public health, safety or welfare, in lieu of a remote meeting, the
mayor may request, but not require, that members of the public participate in a public
meeting remotely. In such case, the mayor shall direct the town manager to provide
notice to the public that attendance at a public meeting is discouraged by posting
such message on the front entryway of town hall and on the town's website. The town
manager's notice shall include the manner in which the public may attend and be heard
at the meeting remotely.
(7)
Conditions to withdraw a remote option or restrict remote meeting participation. When conditions for a meeting can reasonably be mitigated to avoid and protect against
harms identified in section 2-1 (a) (1) a. declared emergencies, the town manager has the discretion to prepare the
agenda and withdraw the remote option. In the event a remote option is available,
participation will be restricted to board of trustee members, presenting applicants,
consultants or members of the public that have indicated, a minimum of thirty-six
(36) hours in advance of the meeting start date and time, the agenda item and their
desire to participate via a remote link. Participation restrictions in place for remote
meetings shall not restrict any person to observe via remote attendance if available.
(e)
Attendance, removal and vacancies.
(1)
Attendance. It shall be the duty of the members of the board of trustees to attend all regular,
special and work session meetings. Failure to notify the town clerk of a planned absence
24-hours in advance or emergency absence is considered a willful absence. Three or
more consecutive meetings, the board of trustees may, in its discretion declare the
office of such vacant.
(2)
Removal. Any member of the board of trustees may be expelled or removed from office for good
cause in the board of trustees' discretion they declare the office of such vacant.
(3)
Vacancy. A vacancy on the board of trustees can occur under a variety of circumstances, including:
resignation; inability to fulfill the duties of office; hired as a Town of Bayfield
employee, failure or refusal to take the oath of office; failure to meet residency
requirements (including moving out of town); removal from office; a seat left unfilled
after an election, or an official passing away during the term of office.
a.
In the event a vacancy occurs, the board of trustees shall declare a vacancy.
b.
Within sixty days of the declaration the board of trustees shall fill the vacancy
by appointment or order an election.
(f)
Conduct of meetings; voting.
(1)
Meetings of the board of trustees shall be conducted by the mayor, according to the
custom and procedures established by the board of trustees.
(2)
A majority shall constitute a quorum to do business at all meetings of the board of
trustees. Each member, including the mayor, shall vote upon every question put by
the mayor unless allowed by the board of trustees to abstain or otherwise required
by Colorado law to abstain. The mayor shall preside at all meetings of the board of
trustees, and shall be counted as a member of the board of trustees for purposes of
establishing a quorum at any regular, special or work session meeting held by the
board of trustees.
(3)
At the hour appointed for meeting, the board of trustee members shall be called to
order by the mayor or, in the mayor's absence, by the mayor pro tem or acting mayor,
and the town clerk shall proceed to call the roll, note the absentees and announce
whether a quorum is present. If a quorum is present, the board of trustees shall proceed
with the business before it, in the manner and order as established by the board of
trustees.
(g)
Compensation.
(1)
All members of the board of trustees shall receive $150 per month as compensation
for their services and the mayor shall receive the sum of $300 per month as compensation
for service.
(2)
Any mayor or trustee who has resigned or vacated an office prior to the end of his
or her elective or appointed term shall not be eligible for election or reappointment
to the same office during such term if the rate of compensation has been increased.

(a)
Creation of position. There is hereby created the position of town manager for the town, for the purpose
of managing and overseeing the business of the town. The terms and conditions of employment
shall be as designated by the board of trustees, and the position may be either part-time
or full-time at the discretion of the board.
(b)
Duties. The town manager shall be responsible for managing the day-to-day business of the
town. In that capacity the town manager shall supervise employees, oversee expenditures
and advise the board of trustees of any necessary or suggested action with respect
to any matter involving the town. The board of trustees may, by agreement, specifically
limit or restrict the authority of the town manager as the board in its sole discretion
shall see fit. In all duties and activities the manager shall be directly responsible
to the board of trustees.
(Ord. No. 158, § I, 12-7-82)
The board of trustees shall appoint an eligible and qualified employee or consultant
as the finance director also known as treasurer. The finance director shall have charge
of the financial records of the town and shall collect, receive, and disburse all
monies as authorized by the board of trustees or delegated to the town manager and
shall have all other duties required to administer properly the financial affairs
of the town.
(Ord. No. 444, 7-7-20)
The board of trustees shall appoint a chief of police. The chief of police shall be
the head of the police department and shall see that the ordinances of the town and
statutes of the State of Colorado are duly enforced, and shall have such other powers
and duties as referenced to a marshal or to a chief of police provided by ordinance
or statute. The town manager within hiring procedures adopted by the board shall recruit,
hire/fire and delegate law enforcement duties to an eligible and qualified individual
as the police chief's immediate supervisor.
(Ord. No. 452, 2-2-21; Ord. No. 489, 6-6-23)
The board of trustees shall appoint a town clerk. The town clerk shall perform such
duties as required by law. The town manager within hiring procedures adopted by the
board shall recruit, hire/fire and delegate town clerk duties to an eligible and qualified
individual as the town clerk's immediate supervisor.
(Ord. No. 452, 2-2-21)
By a majority vote of all members of the board of trustees, the mayor, the clerk,
the treasurer, any member of the board, or any other officer of the town may be removed
from office. No such removal shall be made without a charge in writing and an opportunity
of hearing.

(a)
Appointment. The board of trustees shall appoint a town attorney, who shall hold office at the
pleasure of the board.
(b)
Suits and actions. The attorney shall prosecute or defend any and all suits or actions at law or equity
to which the town may be a party, or in which it may be interested or which may be
brought against it, or by any officer of the town on behalf of the town or in the
capacity of such person as an officer of the town.
(c)
Judgment. It shall be the duty of the attorney to see to the full enforcement of all judgments
or decrees rendered or entered in favor of the town, and of all similar interlocutory
orders.
(d)
Advice. The attorney shall be the legal advisor of the town, and shall render advice on all
legal questions affecting the town, whenever requested to do so by any town official.
Upon request by the mayor or by the board of trustees, he shall reduce any such opinion
to writing.
(e)
Special assessments, etc. It shall be the duty of the attorney to see to the completion of all special assessment
proceedings and condemnation proceedings.
(f)
Ordinances; documents. It shall be the duty of the attorney to draft or supervise the phraseology of any
contract, lease or other documents or instruments to which the town may be a party,
and upon request of the board of trustees to draft ordinances covering any subjects
within the power of the town.
(Ord. No. 123, § 4(4-1—4-6), 5-2-78)
