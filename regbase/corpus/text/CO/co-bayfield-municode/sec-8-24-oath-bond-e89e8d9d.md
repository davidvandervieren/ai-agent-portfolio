A municipal court in and for the town is hereby created and established.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-1), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Authority to create municipal court, § 13-10-104, C.R.S. 1973.
The municipal court of the town is hereby designated as a "qualified municipal court
of record" as provided in C.R.S. 1973, section 13-10-102(3).
(Ord. No. 153, § 1, 7-7-82)
(a)
The municipal court shall have original, exclusive jurisdiction of all cases arising
under the ordinances of the town with full power to carry the same into effect and
to punish violations thereof by the imposition of such fines and penalties as in said
ordinances provided. The court shall have the power to compel the attendance of witnesses
and to punish for contempt of said court by a fine not to exceed $1,000.00 or a jail
sentence not to exceed one year and shall have all powers incident to a court of record
in relation to same.
(b)
The police department of the town shall have the authority to issue citations or summons
to appear in Bayfield Municipal Court to any person under the age of 18 years for
the violation of any ordinance of the town. The municipal court of the town shall
have the authority to act on any such action commenced in municipal court, provided
however, that no person who is under the age of 18 [years] shall be sentenced to serve
any jail sentence for the violation of any ordinance of the town.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-7), 5-2-78; Ord. No. 231, § 1, 7-5-94)
State Law reference— Jurisdiction § 13-10-104, C.R.S. 1973; powers and procedures, § 13-10-112; fines and
penalties, § 13-10-113.
(a)
The board of trustees may, whenever it deems the volume of business transacted by
the municipal court justified, allow the judge to appoint a clerk and such assistants
as may be necessary to carry out efficiently the business of the court. Said clerk
and assistants shall receive such salary as the board of trustees may decide, and
said clerk and assistants shall work under the direct supervision of the municipal
judge.
(b)
The clerk and assistants shall have the power to administer oaths and affirmations
in all court matters and may issue writs and notices including subpoenas, summons
and executions of all cases coming within the jurisdiction of the court. Said clerk
and assistants shall execute oaths and bonds in the same manner and amount as provided
for municipal judges.
(c)
The clerk and assistants or the presiding judge if there is no clerk shall keep a
register of action in said court including all fees and money collected and pay over
to the town treasurer all fees, fines and penalties received.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-19), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Clerk of the municipal court, § 13-10-108, C.R.S. 1973; oaths administered by whom,
§ 24-12-103, C.R.S. 1973; performance bond, § 13-10-109, C.R.S. 1973; oath of civil
officers, Art. XII, § 8, Code Const.; fines and costs, § 13-10-115, C.R.S. 1973.
There shall be regular sessions of court for trial of cases, and the municipal judge
shall hear and determine complaints for a violation of any ordinance of the town where
there is probable cause to believe that an offense has been committed. It shall be
lawful for the municipal judge to hold a special session of court at any time if in
the discretion of the municipal judge a special session is deemed advisable. The court
shall be in session during such hours as are set by the municipal judge with the consent
and approval of the board of trustees.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-15), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Local rules of procedure, § 13-10-112, C.R.S. 1973.
All cases in municipal court shall be open to the public; provided, however, that
where the type of offense charged and the nature of the case are such that it would
be in the best interest of the witness and/or defendants to exclude all persons not
directly connected with the case, the municipal judge may order that the court be
cleared of all persons not so directly connected with the case except qualified representatives
of news agencies. The decision shall be solely in the discretion of the municipal
judge.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-16), 5-2-78)
All proceedings and evidence at trials before the court shall be recorded verbatim
through the use of an electronic recording device or, if so desired by the defendant
or the town, a certified court stenographer shall make a verbatim record of the proceedings.
In the event either the defendant or the town desires a stenographic record, the cost
of making such a record shall be assessed as a court cost by the municipal judge against
the party making the request.
(Ord. No. 153, § 2(a), 7-7-82)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, § 13-10-102, C.R.S. 1973)
The municipal judge is empowered in his discretion to assess court costs which shall
not exceed $40.00 for any defendant who pleads guilty or nolo contendere or who enters
into a plea agreement of any type prior to trial and which shall not exceed $60.00
for any defendant who, after trial, is found guilty of the violation of any provision
of this Code or of the ordinances of the town.
(Ord. No. 234, § 2, 8-1-95; Ord. No. 493, 6-20-23)
The court shall be presided over by a presiding judge, appointed by the board of trustees
for a term of two years, or until a successor is appointed and qualified. The board
of trustees may appoint additional judges from time to time as may be needed to transact
the business of the court or to preside in the absence of the presiding judge. Any
municipal judge may be removed by the board of trustees for cause as provided in section
13-10-105(2), C.R.S. 1973.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-2), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Appointment and removal of judge, § 13-10-105, C.R.S. 1973.
The board of trustees may, by ordinance or resolution duly considered and adopted,
establish from time to time such qualifications for the office of municipal judge
as it may deem fit and proper and consistent with the procurement of persons of judicial
temperament and ability. Municipal judges need not be residents of the town.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-3), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Town may require that judge be elector of town or county, § 13-10-106, C.R.S. 1973.
The judge of the municipal court of the town shall be a qualified attorney who has
currently been admitted to and licensed in the practice of law in the state.
(Ord. No. 153, § 2, 7-7-82)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, § 13-10-102, C.R.S. 1973.
Before entering upon the duties of his office, the municipal judge shall take and
subscribe, before a judge of a court of record or other officer authorized to administer
oaths, and file with the board of trustees, an oath or affirmation that he will support
the Constitution of the United States and the Constitution of the state and the ordinances
of the town and will faithfully perform the duties of his office. Said municipal judge
shall give bond to the town, which bond shall be in such amount as the board of trustees
may establish and shall be conditioned for the faithful performance of the duties
of the office of the municipal judge, and for the faithful accounting for the payment
of all moneys coming into his hands by virtue of said office.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-4), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Oath of civil officers, Art. XII, § 8, Colo. Const.; performance bond of judge when
serving as clerk § 13-10-109, C.R.S. 1973.
The annual salary of the municipal judge shall be set by the board of trustees. The
board of trustees shall from time to time budget and appropriate such moneys as it
deems fit for the operation of the municipal court.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-5), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Salary of judge, § 13-10-107, C.R.S. 1973; court facilities and supplies, § 13-10-110.
The presiding judge of the municipal court shall make rules and regulations for conducting
the business of said court, and said rules and regulations shall be reduced to writing
and must be approved by the board of trustees before becoming effective.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-6), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Power of judge to issue local rules of procedure consistent with state supreme court
rules, § 13-10-112, C.R.S. 1973.
An action for the violation of any ordinance of the town shall be brought in the names
of the town as plaintiff against the person who is alleged to have violated the ordinance
as defendant, and shall be commenced by the filing of a complaint or by the issuance
of a summons or by both summons and complaint. Each act or series of acts committed
by the same person constituting a violation of any of the provisions of the ordinances
of the town may be consolidated for the purpose of filing a complaint, issuing and
serving summons and subpoenas, trial and appeal, but the judge shall impose a separate
fine or imprisonment for each offense of which the defendant is convicted.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-8), 5-2-78)
(a)
In all cases arising under the provisions of this Code a complaint setting forth the
violation shall be filed by any person in the municipal court, which complaint shall
be in the name of the town by and on behalf of the people of the state.
(b)
In all of the said cases herein the defendant at his own request, or at the request
of his counsel, shall be furnished with a copy of the complaint and a list of the
witnesses.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-10), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Filing of action or summons, § 13-10-111, C.R.S. 1973.
(a)
Every complaint or summons under this chapter shall state the name of the defendant,
the number and section of the ordinance of the town alleged to have been violated,
the type of offense to which each of said sections relates, the date and place of
each alleged violation, that the defendant is known or believed to have committed
such offense and that the defendant is required to appear to answer the charges on
a date and at a time and place designated in the complaint or summons. The complaint
or summons, except as provided herein, shall be signed by the person alleging the
violation and the complaint shall be verified by the complainant unless he is a police
officer or a member of the personnel of the court acting in his official capacity.
Each warrant shall state the name of the defendant, the number and section of the
ordinance of the town violated, the date and place of the alleged violation and that
the defendant is alleged to have committed the offense. Each subpoena shall state
the name of the witness to whom the subpoena is directed and that the witness is required
to appear to give testimony on the date and place stated in the subpoena.
(b)
Upon the filing of a verified complaint by a person not a police officer, or a member
of the personnel of the municipal court, the judge, court clerk or authorized agent
of the court may issue a summons, or the judge may issue a warrant against the defendant
named in the complaint.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-9), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Issuance of arrest warrant without information or complaint, § 16-3-108, C.R.S. 1973;
subpoena in municipal court, § 13-10-111, C.R.S. 1973.
Any summons, subpoena or other process issued in accordance with this article shall
be served by delivering a copy thereof to the person named in such process and may
be served by any police officer of the town or by the county sheriff's office or in
any particular case by any person specifically designated by the court for that purpose.
(Ord. No. 123, §§ 5(5-10), 6(6-7), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Service of process, § 13-10-111, C.R.S. 1973.
No objections to the form of any summons or complaint shall be considered by the municipal
court because of any defect unless such objection was made by the defendant prior
to trial of the case on its merits, but trial of the case on its merits shall not
waive any objections theretofore made.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-11), 5-2-78)
Every person arrested for a violation of the provisions of any ordinance of the town
shall have the right to be admitted to bail on his executing a cash surety or recognizance
bond, conditional that he will appear on a day and hour therein mentioned before the
municipal court and not depart the court. Said bond shall be in an amount adjudged
sufficient by the municipal judge or arresting officer, which bond shall be in an
amount adjudged sufficient by said judge or officer to insure the appearance of the
defendant, but in no case to exceed $500.00.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-12), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Right to bail, Art. II, § 19, Colo. Const.; release on bail, § 16-4-101 et seq., C.R.S.
1973; excessive bail, Art. II, § 20, Colo. Const.
It is unlawful for any person to fail to obey a written promise to appear, given to
an officer upon arrest, issuance of a summons or obtaining bail.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-10), 5-2-78; Ord. No. 143, § I, 5-6-80)
In the event any defendant in any case before the municipal court shall fail to appear
according to the terms, requirements and conditions of his bond for appearance or
appearing shall depart the court without leave, the bond shall automatically be forfeited.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-13), 5-2-78)
(a)
Where a surety bond is forfeited, the surety on said bond shall pay the amount of
the bond into the municipal court upon the date of forfeiture. Failure of the surety
to satisfy a bond forfeiture shall result in proceedings being instituted in a court
of competent jurisdiction in the name and on behalf of the town for recovery of the
penalty in such bond named. The surety shall have the right to apply to the municipal
court within 30 days from the date of forfeiture for a return of the whole or a part
of the bond amount paid to the municipal court. Said application shall be in writing
and shall be supported by affidavit setting forth the grounds for the demand. If good
cause is shown by the surety, the municipal judge shall order the return of the bond
amount determined by the municipal judge to be due the surety. The surety shall have
the right to institute a claim against the town in a court of competent jurisdiction
for recovery of any bond payment believed to be wrongfully held.
(b)
When any defendant, duly summoned or admitted to bail, fails to appear at the time
his case is made returnable or set for trial, the municipal judge may issue a warrant
for the arrest of said defendant.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-14), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Disposition of security deposits upon forfeiture or termination of bond, § 16-4-109,
C.R.S. 1973.
A person who is duly summoned, who cannot be tried for any good and sufficient cause,
may request a continuance of his case. The municipal judge may continue the matter
upon terms set by him. Nothing herein shall affect the right of the town or the town
attorney to request a continuance.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-17), 5-2-78)
Every person against whom any fine or penalty shall be assessed under the ordinances
of the town, who shall refuse or neglect to pay the same when demanded, may be committed
in default thereof to the town or county jail or such other enclosure as may from
time to time be designated by the board of trustees as a lawful place for the detention
of town prisoners, under the direction of proper officers, until said fine is fully
paid and satisfied, said satisfaction to be allowed at the rate of $2.00 per day of
24 hours or as is in accordance with the state law. Any such person may be required
to do any reasonable work; provided, however, that no single term of imprisonment
shall exceed 90 days. In the discretion of the municipal judge a stay of execution
may be granted to enable the defendant to pay the fine or penalty at a later date
or in installments. In case the defendant shall not have met the terms of the stay
of execution, then the execution shall issue and the defendant shall be placed in
jail until said judgment is satisfied.
(Ord. No. 123, § 5(5-18), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Imprisonment to satisfy fine for violation of state law, § 16-11-502, C.R.S. 1973.
(a)
When a summons, complaint or other municipal court order of any type is issued to
any person under the age of 18 years, then notice of the issuance of the summons,
complaint or other order shall be provided to the parent or guardian of the minor
by personal service or by mailing a notice by certified mail, return receipt requested
at least ten days prior to the scheduled court appearance. The notice shall include
the alleged violation, date of violation and the date, time and place of the scheduled
court appearance, and shall provide notice that the parent or guardian is required
to appear in municipal court on the specified date and time with the minor. The parent
or guardian of any minor who is required to appear in municipal court shall have the
duty to assure that the minor appears as ordered, and it shall be unlawful for any
parent or guardian to fail to appear at each and every court appearance with the minor,
and the municipal judge, in addition to any other penalty, may issue a warrant for
the arrest of any parent, guardian or minor who fails to appear as provided in this
section.
(b)
When any person under the age of 18 years, as a result of the violation of any section
of this Code, is sentenced or otherwise ordered by the municipal judge to pay a fine,
court costs, restitution or any other fee or is sentenced or otherwise ordered to
perform community service, attend counseling, attend classes or perform any other
action, then the parent or guardian of the minor shall become jointly responsible
for payment of any amounts ordered to be paid and shall have a duty to assure that
the minor performs any ordered community service or other action, and the failure
of any parent or guardian to assure that the minor complies with any municipal court
order shall be a separate offense for which a summons may be issued, or which may
be punishable as contempt of court.
(c)
The provisions of this section 8-51 shall not be applicable to any parent or guardian who demonstrates to the municipal
judge that the minor is emancipated, which shall mean any minor who is over 16 and
under 18 years old and who does not reside with the parent or guardian and who is
solely responsible for his or her own support, or who is married, or who is in the
military.
(Ord. No. 236, 3-5-96)
The judge shall advise defendants of their rights under this division, as required
by law.
State Law reference— Duties of judge, §§ 13-10-114(5), 16-10-109(3), C.R.S. 1973.
In all trials before the municipal court in cases arising under the provisions of
the ordinances of the town wherein a defendant shall have the right to trial by jury,
such defendant may, in writing, within ten days after arraignment or entry of a plea,
demand a jury, which shall consist of three jurors, unless a greater number, not to
exceed six, is requested by the defendant in the jury demand. The jury, when impaneled,
shall be sworn by the judge to try the cause according to the evidence and the law
as provided for criminal trials.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-1), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Trial by jury for petty offenses, § 16-10-109, C.R.S. 1973; trial by jury in municipal
court, § 13-10-114, C.R.S. 1973.

(Ord. No. 123, § 6(602), 5-2-78)
In all cases where a person shall be summoned as a juror to try any cause before the
municipal court or a judge thereof, and shall fail to attend at the time and place
appointed in such summons, the said court or judge thereof shall have the power to
issue an attachment, directed to any person authorized herein to serve warrants of
arrest or other processes in cases in said municipal court, commanding him forthwith
to bring before said court or judge the body of such juror so failing to attend, to
show cause why he should not be punished for contempt; and on the appearance of such
juror or such attachment, it shall be unlawful for such court or judge to punish him
for contempt by a fine not less than $1.00 nor more than $100.00, or wholly discharge
him if satisfactory excuse is made.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-3), 5-2-78)
(a)
If any juror summoned shall be interested in the event of the case or kin to a defendant,
or shall have expressed his opinion on the matter about to be tried, or shall, for
any cause, to be judged by the judge of the municipal court, be considered as a partial
or improper juror, the judge shall discharge such juror; and when, by such discharge,
or in the failure of any juror to attend, the jury shall not be complete, the judge
shall direct any person authorized in this chapter to serve writs or other process
issued in cases in said municipal court to summon as many persons as shall be required
to complete such jury, instantly, from among the bystanders or other persons in the
county who are residents of the town, which summons shall be verbal; and the person
so summoned shall be bound to serve on such jury, and on refusal or failure to do
so may be attached and punished for contempt.
(b)
In all cases arising under the provisions of this chapter wherein a jury trial is
had, each party shall be entitled to three peremptory challenges.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-4), 5-2-78)
Upon the jury returning its verdict of guilty, the judge shall record the same in
the docket and shall proceed to fix or determine the punishment, penalty or sentence,
and render judgement upon such verdict for the punishment, penalty or sentence so
determined by him and for costs; but if the jury returns a verdict of not guilty,
the judge shall record the same and discharge the defendant without costs.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-5), 5-2-78)
If any person accused of violating any of the provisions of an ordinance of the town
has a right to trial by jury, and after having been so advised of his right does not
demand same, thereby waiving his right thereto, and thereafter shall confess himself
guilty or have his case tried to the court, a judge of the municipal court, without
a jury, shall hear the evidence and fix and determine the sentence, penalty or punishment
and such judge shall enter judgment and issue execution thereon.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-6), 5-2-78)
A jury fee of $25.00 shall be tendered with the jury demand and taxed as part of the
costs in each case arising under the provisions of the ordinances of the town tried
by a jury, unless the fee is waived by the judge because of the indigence of the defendant.
Such fee collected shall be paid into the general fund as other costs collected. If
the action is dismissed, or the defendant is acquitted of the charge, or if the defendant
having paid the jury fee files with the court at least ten days before the scheduled
trial date a written waiver of jury trial, the jury fee shall be refunded.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-8), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Jury fee, §§ 13-10-14, 16-10-109, C.R.S. 1773.
Each of the jurors summoned as provided in this chapter shall be entitled to a fee
of $3.00 per day, or fraction thereof, except that those selected to try the case
shall be entitled to a fee of $6.00 per day. The judge of the municipal court shall
furnish to each juror who shall attend at any case so tried a certificate showing
the number of days that such juror shall have attended at such case, and upon presentation
thereof to the town clerk, the clerk shall issue to such juror a warrant upon the
general fund for the amount of his entitled fee.
(Ord. No. 123, § 6(6-9), 5-2-78)
State Law reference— Compensation of jurors, § 13-10-114, C.R.S. 1973.
