This chapter shall be known and cited as the town traffic ordinance.
(Ord. No. 116, § 8, 12-6-77)
(1)
Adoption. Pursuant to Parts 1 and 2 of Article 16 of Title 31 and Part 4 of Article 15 Title
30, C.R.S., there is hereby adopted by reference Articles I and II, inclusive, of
the 2024 edition of the "Model Traffic Code" promulgated and published as such by
the Colorado Department of Transportation, Safety and Traffic Engineering Branch,
4201 East Arkansas Avenue, EP 700., Denver, CO 80222. The subject matter of the model
traffic code relates primarily to comprehensive traffic control regulations for the
town. The purpose of this ordinance and the code adopted herein is to provide a system
of traffic regulations consistent with state law and generally conforming to similar
regulations throughout the state and the nation. Copies of the model traffic code
adopted herein are now filed in the office of the clerk of the Town of Bayfield, Colorado,
and may be inspected during regular business hours.
(2)
Deletions. The 2024 edition of the Model Traffic Code is adopted as if set out at length save
and except the following articles and/or sections which are declared to be inapplicable
to this municipality and are therefore expressly deleted: None.
(3)
Penalties. The following penalties, herewith set forth in full, shall apply to this section:
(a)
It is unlawful for any person to violate any of the provisions adopted in this section.
(b)
Every person convicted of a violation of any provision adopted in this ordinance shall
be punished in accordance with general penalty provision of the Town of Bayfield Municipal
Code.
(4)
Application. This section shall apply to every street, alley, sidewalk area, driveway, park, and
to every other public way or public place or public parking area, either within or
outside the corporate limits of the Town of Bayfield, the use of which the town has
jurisdiction and authority to regulate. The provisions of sections 1401, 1402, 1413,
and part 16 of the adopted Model Traffic Code, respectively concerning reckless driving,
careless driving, eluding a police officer, and accidents and accident reports shall
apply not only to public places and ways but also throughout the Town of Bayfield.
(5)
Validity. If any part or parts of this section are for any reason held to be invalid such decision
shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this section. The town
board of trustees hereby declares that it would have passed this section and each
part or parts thereof, irrespective of the fact that any one part or parts be declared
invalid.
(6)
Repeal. Existing or parts of ordinances covering the same matters as embraced in this section
are hereby repealed and all ordinances or parts of ordinances inconsistent with the
provisions of this section are hereby repealed, except that this repeal shall not
affect or prevent the prosecution or punishment of any person for any act done or
committed in violation of any ordinance hereby repealed prior to the taking effect
of this section.
(7)
Interpretation. This section shall be so interpreted and construed as to effectuate its general purpose
to conform with the state's uniform system for the regulation of vehicles and traffic.
Article and section headings of the section and adopted Model Traffic Code shall not
be deemed to govern, limit, modify or in any manner affect the scope, meaning or extent
of the provisions of any article or section thereof.
(8)
Certification. The town shall certify to the passage of this section and make not less than three
copies of the adopted code available for inspection by the public during regular business
hours.

(a)
Definitions.
Horns is a device equipped to every vehicle operated upon a highway that is capable of
emitting sound audible under normal conditions from a distance of not less than 200
feet.
Jake brake is a transmission brake or any other engine retarding brake system that alters the
normal compression of the engine and subsequently releases that compression. Also
known as engine retarding brake, dynamic brake, jacobs brake, c-brake or paccar brake.
Muffler is a device consisting of a series of chamber or baffle plates or other mechanical
design for the purpose of receiving exhaust gas from an internal combustion engine
and effective in reducing noise.
Warning alarm. Vehicles operated for fire, ambulance, law enforcement are reasonably equipped with
a siren, whistle or bell.
(b)
Every motor vehicle subject to registration and operated on a street shall at all
times be equipped with an adequate muffler in constant operation and properly maintained
to prevent any excessive or unusual noise, and no such muffler or exhaust system shall
be equipped with a cutoff, bypass or similar device. No person shall modify the exhaust
system of a motor vehicle in a manner which will amplify or increase the noise emitted
by the motor of such vehicle above that emitted by the muffler originally installed
on the vehicle, and such original muffler shall comply with all of the requirements
of this section.
(c)
It shall be unlawful for the driver of any vehicle to use or operate or cause to be
used or operated within the Town of Bayfield a jake brake to aid in the braking or
deceleration of any vehicle that results in excessive, loud, unusual or explosive
noise from such vehicle, unless such use is necessary to avoid imminent danger.
No horn or warning alarm shall emit an unreasonably loud or harsh sound, except as
provided in C.R.S. Section 42-4-213 or C.R.S. Section 42-4-222.
(Ord. No. 117, § 2(1-18), 12-6-77; Ord. No. 454, 2-2-20)

(a)
The use of neighborhood electric vehicles within the town is authorized and allowed,
subject to the restrictions and limits contained herein.
(b)
For purposes of this section, a neighborhood electric vehicle is defined as a vehicle:
(1)
Which is propelled by an electric motor;
(2)
Which meets the equipment standards set forth in article 1, part 2, of the model traffic
code or in any other code accepted by the State of Colorado;
(3)
Is designed to carry four or fewer persons;
(4)
Is designed to be operated at speeds of 25 miles per hour, or less;
(5)
Has at least four wheels in contact with the ground; and
(6)
Has an unladen weight of less than 1,800 pounds.
(c)
Neighborhood vehicles operating within the town shall be subject to the following
regulations:
(1)
No person shall operate a neighborhood electric vehicle on a limited access highway.
(2)
No person shall operate a neighborhood electric vehicle on any street or highway with
a posted speed limit of more than 30 miles per hour; provided however, that neighborhood
electric vehicles shall be allowed to cross, at intersections, streets that have a
posted speed limit in excess of 30 miles per hour.
(3)
Neighborhood electric vehicles shall not be operated at a speed in excess of 25 miles
per hour.
(4)
Neighborhood electric vehicles shall not carry a greater number of persons or carry
a greater load than that specified by the manufacturer. The driver and all passengers
must be seated in seats substantially similar to those placed in the vehicle by the
manufacturer.
(5)
No person shall operate a neighborhood electric vehicle on a city owned or maintained
trail.
(6)
Neighborhood electric vehicles shall only be operated by persons holding a valid drivers
license.
(7)
Operators of neighborhood electric vehicles shall obey all traffic laws of the State
of Colorado, including but not limited to, laws concerning financial responsibility,
and all traffic laws and regulations of the United States of America.
(8)
All persons operating a neighborhood electric vehicle within the town shall comply
with any provisions of state law related to the registration of such vehicles and
the license plate issued by the state shall display on that vehicle at all times.
