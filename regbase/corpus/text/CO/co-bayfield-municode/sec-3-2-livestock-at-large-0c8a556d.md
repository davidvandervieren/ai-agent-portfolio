It shall be unlawful for any person owning or have control over any horse, mule, donkey,
cow, bull, steer, hog, sheep, goat or any other livestock, including fowl, to permit
the same to be at large or to be unattended on any public property or on the property
of another without consent of such person.
(Ord. No. 170, § I, 5-1-84)
It shall be unlawful and constitute a nuisance for any person to own, maintain or
otherwise keep any animal or livestock or to maintain the place in which the animal
or livestock is kept in a manner that creates an offensive condition in the vicinity
thereof. An offensive condition is created when noises, odors, aromas, dust, waste,
insects or vermin are created, emitted or given off so as to be offensive, disturbing,
repulsive or obnoxious to the senses any person in the vicinity of the animal or livestock.
In addition to any other penalty provided for in this Code, the municipal court shall
have the jurisdiction and the authority to abate any such nuisance by requiring that
the conditions in which animals are kept be changed or amended so as to eliminate
or mitigate the offensive condition, including but not limited to a requirement for
the reduction of the number of animals or livestock that may be kept on any property.
(Ord. No. 308, 8-2-05)

Animal: For the purposes of this article, animal shall mean dog or cat.
Cat: Both male and female members of the species of domestic cat, Felis catus.
Dog: Both male and female members of the species of domestic dog, Canis familiaris.
Kennel: Includes any building, facility or fenced location that is used for breeding, boarding,
rescue or any similar facility that contains four or more cats, four or more dogs,
or more than four of any combination of cats and dogs.
Owner: Any person owning or having the custody of or who keeps or harbors a dog or cat or
knowingly permits a dog or cat to remain on or about any premises occupied by that
person for a period of two weeks or more. An owner includes any person who feeds,
shelters or attracts an otherwise stray animal.
Running at large: A dog off or away from the premises of the owner or keeper thereof, and not under
the control of such owner or a member of his immediate family, either by leash, cord
or chain not more than eight feet long. The head of any household having a dog in
its possession shall be presumed to be the owner of such dog.
Vicious or dangerous dogs: A dog that has committed an unprovoked bite or attack on a person or animal either
on public or private property, or that approaches a person in an apparent attitude
of attack when unprovoked. A dog which lunges aggressively at its enclosure, chain
or tether when a person walks past, or chases children or adults passing by shall
be considered vicious or dangerous.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-1), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 190, 8-11-87; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89; Ord.
No. 303, § 1, 12-21-05; Ord. No. 497, 2-6-24)

It shall be unlawful for any owner or person who keeps a dog to permit the same to
run at large. Dogs being or running at large shall be impounded, reclaimed and disposed
of upon such terms, conditions and provisions as may be prescribed by this article.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-2), (1-3), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89; Ord. No. 303, § 2, 12-21-05)
All dogs or cats kept, harbored or maintained by their owners, which have not been
vaccinated for rabies shall be vaccinated with anti-rabies vaccine by a licensed veterinarian.
All dogs or cats kept, harbored or maintained by their owners, which have been vaccinated
for rabies shall be again vaccinated with anti-rabies vaccine not later than the end
of the duration of every vaccination. The provisions of this section shall not be
intended to apply to dogs or cats whose owners are temporary residents.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-3), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
A dog license is required for all dogs over the age of four months that live within
the town limits. Licensing will help reunite lost pets with their families and must
be renewed every year prior to February 1 st and requires proof of vaccination. Said licenses shall be effective for the calendar
year of issuance. Failure to license any dog within a calendar year does not negate
the licensing charges due for that calendar year and will result in the requirement
that the delinquent charges be paid at such time as the animal is next licensed. Dogs
born during any calendar year shall be licensed within 30 days after the dog reaches
the age of four months. Dogs purchased, obtained, or otherwise acquired, and dogs
of new residents of the town shall be licensed within 30 days after such acquisition
or establishing residence. All dogs are required to wear their metal license tag on
their collar or harness at all times. The metal tag received with the initial license
registration is permanently assigned to that dog unless the owner request to pay for
a replacement tag. The town will not issue a new tag every year. No license shall
be issued to any person under the age of 18 years.
The annual license fee for persons keeping, maintaining, harboring or possessing dogs
within the town shall be set by the board of trustees by Resolution. The town clerk
shall make said fee schedule available to any person upon request. Discounts will
be given to spayed/neutered dogs with proof of procedure, and to seniors over the
age of 65 with spayed/neutered dogs.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-4), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89; Ord. No. 303, § 3, 12-21-05;
Ord. No. 497, 2-6-24)
(a)
Keeping of vicious or dangerous dog: It shall be unlawful for any person to own, keep or maintain a vicious or dangerous
dog except in a secure pen, kennel or other enclosure which is at least six feet in
height, with a secure top attached to the sides, and with the entrance thereto secured
by a chain and padlock.
Any person who owns, keeps or maintains a vicious or dangerous dog must maintain a
$100,000.00 public liability insurance policy for the premises upon which the vicious
or dangerous dog is kept which provides coverage for injury to or death of any person
or for damage to any property which may result from the ownership, keeping or maintenance
of such dog. Such owner or keeper shall provide proof of such insurance to the town
marshal or his deputies upon his request. The owner or keeper of a vicious or dangerous
dog must also display a sign with at least three-inch letters declaring "Vicious Dog
on Premises" in a visible and conspicuous location near the entrance to the property
where the dog is kept.
(b)
Determination of vicious or dangerous dog: The determination of whether a dog is vicious or dangerous shall be made by the town
marshal or his deputies. Upon making such a determination, the town marshal or his
deputies shall give written notice of such determination to the owner or keeper of
the dog by personal service or by posting such a notice in a conspicuous place on
the premises. Unless the dog can be adequately restrained on the owner or keeper's
premises, or removed from the town limits by the owner or keeper, the vicious or dangerous
dog shall be impounded and be subject to all provisions of impoundment provided for
in this article; provided however, that if such a vicious or dangerous dog cannot
be safely impounded, such dog may be destroyed by the marshal, his deputies, or any
other peace officer authorized hereunder to enforce the provisions of this article.
(c)
Appeal of determination: The owner or keeper of any dog which is determined to be vicious or dangerous may
appeal such determination to the municipal judge by making a request for such a hearing
in writing within three days after the determination is made. The notice provided
for in subsection (b) hereof shall inform the owner or keeper of the dog of this right
to an appeal. Nothing contained herein shall be construed to deny any person the constitutional
right to a hearing or trial as to any violation charged under this article.
(d)
Destruction of dog: Upon a showing of necessity in the interest of public safety, the municipal judge
may order the destruction of any vicious or dangerous dog.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-5), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 145, § I, 9-2-80; Ord. No. 190, 8-11-87;
Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
It shall be the duty of every police officer, sheriff or other person so designated
by the town to apprehend any animal found running at large contrary to the provision
of this article, and to impound such animal in a suitable place designated by the
town.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-6), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
Within 24 hours after the impounding of any animal, a written notice shall be given
through posting for three days at the town hall, describing the animal and the time
of impounding. The owner of any animal so impounded may reclaim such animal upon payment
of the license fee, if unpaid, and all of the costs incurred by the town of impounding
and maintenance of said dog. Charges for impounding any animal and for keeping any
animal on a per diem basis shall be determined by the board of trustees.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-7), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
All animals impounded under this article shall be kept for a period of three days
as provided in this section, unless sooner reclaimed, as provided in the preceding
section. If, at the expiration of three days from the date of the posting of the notice
as provided in the preceding section, such animal shall not have been reclaimed, it
shall be disposed of in an appropriate manner. If an animal is suspected of being
affected by rabies, the health officer or health department shall be notified of the
location of the animal. Any animal which appears to be suffering from rabies or affected
with hydrophobia, mange or other infectious or dangerous disease may forthwith be
destroyed.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-8), 8-7-73; Res. No. 72, 12-9-80; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
It shall be unlawful for any person to keep, maintain, harbor or possess upon the
premises of any one household more than three cats, three dogs or a combination of
four of these animals, unless the owner or person in charge thereof shall have obtained
a kennel license. Application for a kennel license shall be made to the town's land
use administrator after determination has been made of the appropriate land use process
for the use. Puppies or kittens shall not be counted for purposes of this section
until they reach the age of three months.
(Ord. No. 88, § 2(1-9), 8-7-73; Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89; Ord. No. 497, 2-6-24)

It shall be unlawful for any dog or cat to constitute a nuisance or for any owner
to allow a dog or cat to constitute a nuisance. A dog or cat shall be considered a
nuisance if it: Damages, soils, defiles, or defecates on property other than the owner's
or on public walks and recreation areas unless such waste is immediately removed and
properly disposed of by the owner; causes unsanitary, unhealthy, dangerous, or offensive
conditions; causes a disturbance by excessive barking or other noisemaking; or chases
vehicles, or molests, attacks, or interferes or conflicts with persons or other domestic
animals on public property.
(Ord. No. 207, 7-11-89)
