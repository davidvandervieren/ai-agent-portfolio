(a)
There is imposed an enhancement assessment fee on all undeveloped real property now
in the City or to be annexed into the City, in addition to other existing City fees,
except that this enhancement assessment includes the former park fee. Said enhancement
assessment is to be payable to the City at the time a building permit is obtained
from the City. Under no circumstances shall the City issue a building permit or certificate
of occupancy to an applicant until and unless the enhancement assessment fee has been
paid in full.
(b)
The fee schedule for the enhancement assessment fee may be amended by resolution of
the City Council from time to time and shall be reviewed by the Council as often as
needed. The categorical minimums establishing a base line of assessments may be increased
by the City Council by resolution where the need for assessments higher than the base
line can be demonstrated by reason of increased impact of the proposed development,
by reason of the size or scope of the individual unit or project.
(c)
Substitution of equivalents may be negotiated in lieu of fees for an individual unit
or an entire project, subject to Council approval.
(d)
A separate interest-bearing fund shall be created by the Finance Director to maintain
enhancement fees paid hereunder for creation, improvement, acquisition, maintenance
and capital replacement reserves for recreational, historical, cultural and parks
purposes as generally outlined in the ordinance codified herein.
(e)
Payment or agreement to pay the enhancement fee hereunder is an admission of the City's
authority and the reasonable exercise of that authority to collect this fee and an
acknowledgement that the City will rely on the payment of this fee as an estoppel
upon the payor from challenging the amount or necessity of the fee.

(a)
Definition. Small wind energy system means a wind energy conversion system consisting of up to three (3) wind turbines,
towers and associated control or conversion electronics, which have a rated capacity
of not more than one hundred (100) kilowatts and which are intended to primarily reduce
on-site consumption of utility power.
(b)
Permitted use. Small wind energy systems shall be permitted uses or special uses as
provided by this Code, subject to certain qualifications and criteria as set forth
below:
(1)
Height. Total height, including blade length, shall not exceed sixty (60) feet.
(2)
Setback, minimum lot size and access restriction. No part of the wind system structure,
including guy wire anchors, may extend closer than ten (10) feet to the property boundaries
of the installation site. Placement must be such that if the system collapses, no
occupied structure on adjacent property is within its collapse zone. It is understood
that this restriction may prevent most residential zone placement. If physical access
to the system by business invitees or even trespassers, like children, would pose
a danger by climbing on or otherwise touching the system, adequate access restriction
must be constructed. Minimum lot size for each unit is four thousand (4,000) square
feet.
(3)
Noise. Small wind energy systems shall not exceed sixty (60) dBA, as measured at the
closest corner of the closest neighboring residential structure, or eighty (80) dBA,
as measured from the closest corner of the closest neighboring business structure.
The level, however, may be exceeded during severe wind storms, as the combination
of high wind, high rotation and wind turbulence around the tower or guy wires may
temporarily exceed these noise standards.
(4)
Wind turbines. Small wind turbines must have been approved under the Emerging Technologies
program of the California Energy Commission or any other small wind certification
program recognized by the American Wind Energy Association and must demonstrate by
those approvals or otherwise that the system meets accepted standards of safety under
tornadic conditions.
(5)
Compliance with International Building Code. Building permit applications for small
wind energy systems shall be accompanied by standard drawings of the wind turbine
structure, including the tower, base and footings. An engineering analysis of the
tower showing compliance with the International Building Code and certified by a licensed
professional engineer shall also be submitted. This analysis is frequently supplied
by the manufacturer. Wet stamps shall not be required.
(6)
Compliance with FAA regulations. Small wind energy systems must comply with applicable
FAA regulations, including any necessary approvals for installations close to airports.
(7)
Compliance with National Electric Code. Building permit applications for small wind
energy systems shall be accompanied by an engineer's stamped one-line drawing of the
electrical components in sufficient detail to allow for a determination that the manner
of installation conforms to the National Electrical Code. This information is frequently
supplied by the manufacturer.
(8)
Utility notification. No small wind energy system shall be installed until evidence
has been given that the utility company is informed and has approved the customer's
intent to install an interconnected customer-owned generator. Off-grid systems shall
be exempt from this requirement.
(9)
Color. The tower cannot be painted another color other than the manufacturer's color
without consent of the Planning Department. The Planning Department shall use its
discretion consistent with the concept colors being compatible with the area in question.
(10)
Advertising. The tower shall not be used for advertising purposes. No signs, lettering,
balloons, banners or other attention-getting media shall be placed on the tower other
than high voltage signs and other signs that exhibit manufacturer warnings and statistical
information about the system.
(11)
Overrides. All towers shall be equipped with a manual override system to shut down
the unit in case of an emergency.
(12)
Miscellaneous. If a tower is abandoned for more than a one-year period, the building
permit is automatically revoked and the tower shall be removed at the owner's expense.
As part of the building permit process, the applicant must provide the City with written
permission to enter and remove the system after abandonment, should the owner fail
to do so, and permission to lien the property for the cost of such removal, if necessary,
and that such recorded lien shall run with the land and inure to successors and assigns
of the owner at the time of installation.
(13)
If all of these qualifications and criteria are not met, then an applicant may apply
for a special use pursuant to Section 16-7 of this Code. The above qualifications
and criteria shall be a guide for the Planning Commission and the City Council in
determining whether a special use should be granted.
(Ord. 2009-905 Part 3; Ord. 2014-980 §3; Ord. No. 2022-1144 , § 3, 7-5-2022)
