Private alarm systems may constitute a valuable resource, which promotes the interests
of public safety by providing immediate notice of situations that require an immediate
response. The purpose of this Article is to reduce the frequency of false alarms and
to defray the costs incurred by the City in responding to false alarms by establishing
standards and controls for various types of alarm devices.
(Ord. 2002-794)
As used in this Article:
Alarm system means any device designed to detect the unauthorized intrusion onto protected premises
or the existence of an emergency on such premises, and which results in or is intended
to result in a response by the police department, and which emits a sound or transmits
an alarm signal or message when activated. The following devices shall not constitute
an alarm system within the meaning of this definition:
a.
Alarm devices affixed to motor vehicles.
b.
Single-unit smoke detectors or personal protection alerting devices which alert only
the user and which do not require special wiring.
Alarm user refers to any person, firm, business, partnership, association or corporation or
employee thereof which maintains control of, has responsibility for and/or is associated
with the operation of an alarm system or who buys and/or leases or otherwise obtains
or uses any alarm system.
False alarm means the activation of an alarm system which results in or is intended to result
in a response by the police department of the City, where there exists neither an
emergency nor evidence of crime committed or attempted. The following circumstances
shall not be included as false alarms in reference to this Chapter:
a.
Unusual weather conditions when such an alarm could not have been prevented by reasonable
precautions.
b.
Intentional activation with the reasonable but mistaken belief that an emergency or
criminal activity was occurring or about to occur.
c.
Maintenance or construction activity not under the control of the alarm user, in which
case the person doing the maintenance may be held responsible for causing the false
alarm.
d.
Testing of the alarm system, with prior notice to and approval of the police department
of the City.
e.
Circuit troubles other than those the alarm vendor or user could have reasonably foreseen.
f.
Alarms caused by area power outages.
g.
Activations in conjunction with the police department for the purposes of training
or observing performance.
Permittee means any person, firm, business, partnership, association or corporation or employee
thereof that has obtained a permit under this Chapter.
(Ord. 2002-794)
(a)
Audible alarm devices/restrictions. It shall be unlawful for an alarm user to permit
any audible exterior alarm signal for more than fifteen (15) minutes after being activated.
Police responding to such an alarm may use reasonable and necessary means to deactivate
the audible signal. Neither the City, its officers, agents, employees nor representatives
shall be responsible or liable for damages resulting in such deactivation.
(b)
An alarm system that utilizes an automatic dialing system that goes directly to the
police department is prohibited.
(c)
Any alarm agent doing business in the City must present proper identification to a
police officer, building code official of the City, or to an alarm user upon demand.
(d)
Any alarm business selling, leasing, replacing, altering, servicing, modifying or
installing any alarm system within the City is required to provide the alarm user
with complete and understandable written instructions.
(Ord. 2002-794)
(a)
No alarm user shall install, have installed, maintain or use any alarm system located
within the City without first obtaining a permit from the Chief of Police or his or
her designate.
(b)
An applicant for a permit under this Section shall file an application with the police
department on forms supplied by the police department. The applicant will supply all
the requested information including, without limitation, the name, address and telephone
number of the applicant and the name and address of the owner of the property. The
applicant shall also supply the name, address and phone number of three (3) responsible
persons having access to the property who may be notified and assist the police in
the event an alarm is activated.
(Ord. 2002-794)
(a)
No fee shall be charged for a permit issued under this Chapter.
(b)
An alarm system permit continues in effect for a period of one (1) calendar year unless
terminated at the request of the permittee or revoked by the Chief of Police.
(Ord. 2002-794)
Each permittee shall:
(1)
Inspect, maintain and repair a police alarm device to ensure its proper operation.
(2)
Educate and train all employees and other persons who may in the course of their activities
be in a position to accidentally activate an alarm system.
(3)
Assure that a responsible person responds to every activation of an alarm system within
thirty (30) minutes of being notified by the dispatcher.
(4)
Report promptly any change of information on the application to the Chief of Police.
(Ord. 2002-794)
The Chief of Police may revoke or suspend the permit issued under this Chapter for
accumulating three (3) false alarms in a month or six (6) false alarms in a year.
The permit may also be revoked or suspended for failure to pay an assessed alarm charge.
Within ten (10) days after suspension or revocation of the permit, the permittee shall
disconnect the alarm system and shall not reconnect until the suspension or revocation
has been lifted. No police response will be made to a location where the permit has
been suspended or revoked.
(Ord. 2002-794)
A false alarm charge shall be imposed upon the permittee for every false alarm commencing
with the fourth false alarm in a calendar year to which the police department responds.
The schedule and amount of charge shall be set by resolution of the City Council.
Charges will be assessed by the City, but may be appealed to the City Administrator.
The City shall grant a sixty-day grace period, beginning on the date of application
for the permit, after the installation of a new alarm system. It shall be unlawful
to fail to pay a false alarm charge.
(Ord. 2002-794)
Information concerning the location and type of alarm systems and customers of an
alarm business furnished and secured pursuant to this Chapter shall be confidential
and not subject to public inspection. It is declared that the public interest served
by not making such information public clearly outweighs the public interest served
by disclosure. Nothing in this Chapter, however, shall be construed to prohibit disclosure
of a schedule of revenues derived pursuant to this Chapter or enforcement proceedings
to secure compliance with the requirements hereof.
(Ord. 2002-794)
The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to all alarm systems, including those which
were installed, connected, operated or maintained on or prior to and continue in use
after the effective date of the ordinance codified in this Chapter. Existing alarms
must be in compliance with all parts of the ordinance codified herein within sixty
(60) days of its adoption.
(Ord. 2002-794)
It is unlawful for an alarm user to fail to pay a charge assessed pursuant to this
Chapter. The municipal court may assess unpaid charges as fines and court costs as
part of the punishment for conviction under this Chapter.
(Ord. 2002-794)
