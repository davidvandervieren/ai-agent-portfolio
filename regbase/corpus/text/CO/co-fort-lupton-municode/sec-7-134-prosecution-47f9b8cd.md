Unless otherwise stated, the provisions in this Chapter are subject to all applicable
definitions, defenses, and presumptions provided by the Colorado Revised Statutes.
As used in this Chapter, the following words and phrases shall have the following
meanings, unless otherwise clearly indicated:
Abandon means the leaving of an animal without adequate provisions for animal's proper care
by its owner, the person responsible for the animal's care or custody, or any other
person having possession of such animal.
Animal means any living dumb creature.
Animal which creates a danger means any animal, except a dog assisting a law enforcement officer engaged in law
enforcement duties, as may be demonstrated by any of the following behaviors:
(1)
Without provocation, causes any injury, less than serious bodily injury, to any person
or domesticated animal at any place within the City;
(2)
Attacks any person or domesticated animal that is lawfully on the owner's property;
(3)
Without provocation, menaces, chases, displays threatening or aggressive behavior,
or otherwise threatens or endangers the safety of any person or domesticated animal;
(4)
Without provocation, approaches any person in a menacing or terrorizing manner, or
in an apparent attitude of attack, whether such person is in motion or standing still,
and whether such person is on foot or on or in a vehicle or device which allows such
person to be in motion; or
(5)
Acts in a highly aggressive manner within a fenced yard or enclosure and appears to
a reasonable person to be able to jump over or escape such fenced yard or enclosure
or, in a vicious or frightening manner, approaches any person in an apparent attitude
of attack, whether or not the attack is transpired or capable of transpiring.
Backyard chicken means any chicken kept on a property for the purpose of providing fresh eggs that
are not used for profit purposes.
Cat means any domesticated animal of the species felis catus.
Chicken means any member of the species gallus domesticus. Chicken means a domestic foul
kept for its eggs or meat, excluding ducks, geese, pigeons, turkeys, pea fowl and
guinea hens.
Chicken coop means an enclosed and secured structure for housing backyard chickens.
Chicken facility or facilities means any other structure constructed to maintain backyard chickens.
Chicken run means an enclosed and secured area connected to a chicken coop where backyard chickens
are able to move around freely.
City means the City of Fort Lupton as a municipality or the territory contained therein,
as the context dictates.
Code Compliance Officer means any person who is an employee or designee of Police Department or the Planning
and Development Department and who is authorized and responsible for enforcement of
this Code.
Control means that the animal is on a leash, cord or chain not more than ten (10) feet in
length and held by a person of sufficient age, size and physical ability to restrain
the animal. Electric leashes are specifically prohibited.
Cruelty means any intentional act or omission whereby unjustifiable pain, suffering or death
is caused or permitted, including failure to provide appropriate food, water, air,
space, shelter or protection from the elements, and necessary veterinary care of any
such care sufficient to maintain life.
Disposal or disposition means adoption of an animal, return of an animal to the owner, release of an animal
to a pet facility licensed pursuant Pet Animal Care Facilities Act ("PACFA"), C.R.S.
35-80-101 through 35-50-117, release to a rehabilitator licensed by the Colorado Division
of Wildlife or the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, or euthanasia.
Dog means domesticated animal of the species canus lupus familaris.
Domesticated animal or pet animal means any domesticated animal commonly kept in a household, excluding livestock,
such as dogs, cats, chinchillas, domestic rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, domestic
mice, domestic rats, gerbils, domestic ferret, hedgehogs, sugar gliders, canaries,
finches, parakeets, doves, domesticated parrots, aquarium fish, nonpoisonous amphibians,
nonvenomous snakes, nonvenomous invertebrates, and turtles of a species and size which
are permissible under state law.
Emotional support animal means a companion animal that provides therapeutic benefit to an individual with
a mental or psychiatric disability. The person seeking the emotional support animal
must have a verifiable disability. The animal is viewed as a "reasonable accommodation"
under the Fair Housing Amendments Act of 1988 (FHA or FHAct). Comfort animals or therapy
dogs are not considered service animals as defined in this Chapter. Other species
of animals, whether wild or domestic, trained or untrained, are not considered service
animals.
Exotic animal means those species, subspecies, and hybrids of wildlife not originating naturally,
either presently or historically, in Colorado, except those which have been introduced
into the wild in Colorado by the Division of Wildlife; and those which have been classified
as wildlife by the Division of Wildlife Commission.
Feral cat means a cat that lives outside, fends for itself, does not have an owner, is unsocialized
to people and typically avoids contact with humans.
Guard dog means any dog trained or used to protect persons or property by attacking or threatening
to attack any person found within the area patrolled by the dog.
Humane trap means a box-type trap that does not cause bodily harm to an animal intended to be
captured or to any other animal or person coming in contact with such trap.
Keeper means any person who is in possession of, or is keeping or caring for any animal.
Keep(ing) means to care for, to have custody of, to provide premises to which the animal regularly
returns for food and shelter, or to exercise physical control over, or to have any
right of property in an animal, or to own, or allow an animal to remain about any
premises with in the City.
Livestock means any animal commonly kept, as a source of food, hides, income through agricultural
sale, as a pack animal or draft animals or for use as transportation. Livestock includes,
but is not limited to, horses, mules, sheep, goats, cattle, swine, ducks, geese, pigeons,
turkeys, pea fowl, and guinea hens. In the event of uncertainty concerning whether
a particular animal is a species of livestock, the presumption is that such animal
is a species of livestock until the owner of such animal proves by a preponderance
of the evidence to the satisfaction of the Municipal Court that the animal is not
a species of livestock.
Miniature horse means a horse generally ranging in height from twenty-four (24) inches to thirty-four
(34) inches measured to the shoulders and generally weighing between seventy (70)
and one-hundred (100) pounds.
Neutered means any male animal that has undergone an operation where both testes have been
surgically removed.
Owner means any person who has right of property in an animal; or keeps an animal or allows
an animal to remain about his or her premises for a period of seventy-two (72) hours
or longer; claims responsibility for an animal; or is declared by court decree to
be the responsible party for an animal or the authorized agent of any such person.
The parent or guardian of any minor claiming ownership of an animal is deemed to be
the owner of the animal for purposes of this Chapter.
Pet animal facility means any place or premise used in whole or part, for the keeping of pet animals
for the purpose of adoption, breeding, boarding, day care, grooming, handling, selling,
sheltering, trading, or otherwise transferring such animals, as set forth in the Pet
Animal Care Facilities Act ("PACFA"), C.R.S. 35-80-101 through 35-50-117. Pet animal
facility also includes keeping any individual animals for breeding stock. Pet animal
facility shall not mean a common carrier engaged in intrastate or interstate commerce.
For purposes of this Chapter, two (2) or more animal facilities that have the same
or a similar purpose and operate from one place or premises is considered a single
pet animal facility.
Predator means any animal likely to harm backyard chickens.
Rabies vaccination means the inoculation of a domestic animal with an anti-rabies vaccine approved by
the Colorado Department of Health and Environment or similar agency and administered
by a licensed veterinarian. The vaccination is valid for a period of time specified
in writing by the veterinarian for the specific vaccine used.
Serious bodily injury means bodily injury which, either at the time of the actual injury or at a later
time, involves substantial risk of death, a substantial risk of serious permanent
disfigurement, a substantial risk of protracted loss or impairment of the function
of any party or organ of the body, or breaks, fractures, or burns of the second or
third degree as defined in the Colorado Revised Statutes.
Service animal means a dog or miniature horse that has been individually trained to do work or perform
tasks for the benefit of an individual with a disability.
Spayed means any female animal on which an ovariotomy or ovariohysterectomy or other neutering
procedure has been performed.
Stray means any animal for which there is no identifiable owner.
Tether means to tie up or chain to a fixed or heavy inanimate object so as to restrict the
free movement of any animal to a distance no greater than the length of its leash
or chain.
Vicious animal means any animal, except a dog assisting a law enforcement officer engaged in law
enforcement duties, that:
(1)
Causes serious bodily injury to any person or death to a domesticated animal or behaves
in a manner that would have resulted in such serious bodily injury except for the
fact that there was intervention by a person to stop the behavior.
(2)
Has been previously adjudged to be an animal which creates a danger and the animal
owner has failed to comply with the Court's orders for maintaining the animal within
the City or the animal has engaged in subsequent behavior that poses a threat to public
safety.
(3)
Engages in or has been trained for animal fighting as described and prohibited in
C.R.S. §18-9-204.
Wild animal means all wildlife, including but not limited to, bears, coyotes, foxes, mountain
lions, opossums, raccoons, skunks, raptors, all game animals, and all other species
of animals, which exist in their natural, unconfined state and are usually not domesticated.

The enforcement of the Chapter is the responsibility of, and under the authority of,
the Police Department and the Planning and Development Departments.
(a)
The Chief of Police, all sworn Police Officers, Animal Control Officers, Code Enforcement
Officers or the Community Service Officers (herein referred to as Code Compliance
Officers) is responsible for enforcing the provisions of this Chapter and shall possess
the necessary authority to ensure compliance with the provisions of this Chapter to
include:
(1)
Written and/or verbal warning or notices requesting compliance by animal owners;
(2)
Issuance of municipal citation to persons in violation of this Chapter or any of its
provisions;
(3)
Impoundment of animals as authorized by the Chapter;
(4)
Inspection of premises for the purpose of public health and safety, as well as the
condition and health of an animal;
(5)
It is lawful for any Code Compliance Officer to go upon private property to capture
any animal to be impounded or, to investigate any report of a violation of this Chapter,
if:
a.
The officer has obtained consent of the person in possession of the property;
b.
The officer has obtained a search warrant pursuant to Rule 241 of the Colorado Municipal
Court Rules;
c.
The officer is in pursuit of an animal which is, or has been, running at large;
d.
The officer is in pursuit of an animal which the officer has probable cause to believe
has bitten a person or other domestic animal;
e.
The officer is attempting to abate a continuing violation when the owner of the property
is not available.
f.
Nothing in Subsections a. through e. is deemed to authorize entry into any enclosed
building on private property.
(6)
Emergency destruction of animal when conditions exist that require such destruction.
Such conditions include, but are not limited to, extremely harmful, dangerous or life-threatening
situations to citizens, including enforcement personnel, or when the physical condition
of the animal is such that the immediate destruction of such animal is the most humane
and proper action to take. Emergency destruction of animals is completed in the safest,
most humane manner available to enforcement personnel.
(7)
If an affidavit has been submitted to the Municipal Court Judge which satisfies the
Judge that an animal which is the subject of a violation of this Chapter is located
within the City and that the animal either poses a clear and present danger to the
health, general welfare or safety of other persons or animals or is suffering extreme
neglect or cruelty, the Municipal Court Judge may order the animal seized by a Code
Compliance Officer of the City pursuant to Rule 241 of the Colorado Municipal Court
Rules. The owner of the animal may request a hearing concerning any such order for
seizure entered pursuant to this Section by filing an appropriate motion pursuant
to Rules 241(e) of the Colorado Municipal Court Rules. Whenever an animal is seized
by order of the Municipal Court or other court, the animal shelter is prohibited from
adopting, donating or euthanizing the animal unless such action is permitted by subsequent
order of the same court which ordered the initial seizure.
(8)
Notwithstanding the paragraphs of this Chapter, if a Code Compliance Officer has reasonable
cause to believe that the keeping of any animal is so hazardous, unsafe or dangerous
as to require immediate inspection to safeguard the animal or the public health or
safety, the Code Compliance Officer shall have the right to immediately enter and
inspect the property or vehicle in or upon which the animal is kept, and may use any
reasonable means required to effect such entry and make such inspection, whether,
the property or vehicle is occupied or unoccupied and whether permission to inspect
has been obtained or not. If the property or vehicle is occupied, the Code Compliance
Officer shall first present proper credentials to the owner or occupant of the property
or vehicle and demand entry, explaining his or reasons therefor and the purpose of
inspection. Such entry is solely for the purpose of abating the violation and no evidence
obtained during or as a result of such entry is admissible for purposes of prosecution.
(b)
Neither the City nor any of its officers are civilly or criminally liable for going
upon private property for the enforcement of this Chapter or for the emergency destruction
of an animal.

It is unlawful for any person to interfere with, molest, hinder or prevent any Code
Compliance Officer in the discharge of their respective duties as herein prescribed
or to violate any of the provisions of this Chapter.
(b)
It is no defense to a prosecution under this Section that the Code Compliance Officer
was acting in an illegal manner, if he or she was acting in the regular course of
their assigned duties, and in good faith based upon the surrounding facts and circumstances.
(c)
It is unlawful for any person to refuse to reveal their name, address and date of
birth when requested to so by a Code Compliance Officer in his or her official duties
as prescribed herein.

For the purpose of prosecuting any violations of this Chapter, it shall not be necessary
in order to obtain a conviction to prove knowledge or notice on the part of the owner
of the animal in question that said animal was violating any of the Sections of this
Chapter at the time and place charged. The purpose of this Section is to impose strict
liability upon the owner of any animal for the actions, conduct and condition of such
animal, unless the violation specifically provides otherwise.

Every person within the City who owns or keeps any dog and/or cat shall obtain an
animal license for and register every dog and/or cat he or she owns or keeps within
the City.
(1)
It is unlawful for any owner of any dog or cat being kept in the City over the age
of six (6) months, or within thirty (30) days of acquisition of said animal, or within
thirty (30) days of moving into the City limits, whichever occurs last, to fail to
obtain a license of such animal as required by this Chapter. Any animal present in
the City that has been kept in the City for thirty (30) days or more shall have a
current license.
(2)
It is unlawful for an owner to fail to renew the license within thirty (30) days of
expiration.
(3)
Any animal that has not been licensed and registered within the prescribed time limits
is considered to be an unlicensed animal, and the owner may be cited into Municipal
Court for such violation.
(4)
It is unlawful for any owner of an animal to transfer the license between animals.
In the event there is a transfer of ownership of any animal, the new owner must obtain
an animal license within the prescribed time limits.
(5)
Animal licenses are issued at the direction of the City. The license is valid from
date obtained to the expiration date of rabies vaccine.
(6)
An owner may request an exemption to the license provisions if, for medical reasons,
the animal cannot be vaccinated. In this event, the animal owner must submit to the
City an affidavit from a licensed veterinarian stating the reason(s) why the animal
is unable to be vaccinated. The City or its designee, may approve or deny this exemption
and shall issue its findings to the owner, in writing.
(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
To insure the health, safety and welfare of the community, there are no fees for the
issuance or renewal of an animal license under this Chapter.
(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
The City or its designee is responsible for issuing licenses and tags consistent with
the provisions of this Chapter.

(a)
An applicant for a dog or cat license may apply at the Police Department and/or at
such other locations as may be designated by the City. Such application shall contain
the following information:
(1)
The name and address of the owner or keeper;
(2)
The breed, age, color and sex of the animal;
(3)
Proof of current rabies vaccination or proof of exemption;
(4)
Documentation that the animal has been neutered or spayed, if applicable; and
(5)
Any other information the City deems necessary in the administration and regulation
of these licenses.
(b)
It is unlawful and a violation of this Section for the applicant to knowingly make
any material or false misrepresentations in the license application.
(c)
The tags are made of durable material suitable to be attached to the collar or harness

(a)
Every owner or keeper of a dog shall attach the tag evidencing the licensing and inoculation
with rabies vaccine or inoculation exemption to the collar or harness of the inoculated
and licensed or exempt dog that the tag is registered to. The collar or harness is
to be worn by the dog at all times. The license is retained by the owner or keeper
of the inoculated and licensed dog for inspection by any Code Compliance Officer at

(b)
Due to the unique activity of cats and due to the potential safety factors concerning
cats wearing collars and harnesses, the owner or keeper of a cat is not required to

(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
(a)
Vaccination required. Every owner or keeper of any dog or cat that is at least four
(4) months of age or older shall have such animal vaccinated against rabies and revaccinated
thereafter at the expiration of the validity of the vaccine used, as shown on the
written document prepared by the a licensed veterinarian. Each person moving within
the City limits shall comply with this Section within thirty (30) days after having
moved into the City.
(b)
Proof of vaccination. It is unlawful for any person who owns or keeps a vaccinated
animal to fail or refuse to exhibit a copy of the certificate of vaccination upon
demand to any person charged with enforcement of this Chapter.
(c)
Keeping unvaccinated dogs or cat. It is unlawful for any person to keep any dog or
cat that has not been vaccinated against rabies, as provided herein, or that cannot
be identified as having a current vaccination certificate.
(d)
Non-transferability. Rabies vaccination certificates and tags are not transferable
and cannot be used for any animal other than the animal that received the vaccination
and for which the certificate was originally issued.
(e)
Exceptions. The owner of a dog or cat may request an exemption to the vaccination
provisions if, for medical reasons, the dog or cat cannot be vaccinated. In this event,
a dog or cat owner must submit an affidavit from a licensed veterinarian stating the
reason(s) why the dog or cat is not able to be vaccinated.
(f)
Reporting animal bites. It is unlawful for any person having knowledge that an animal
has bitten a human or another animal to fail to immediately report the incident to
the Police Department, a Code Compliance Officer or to the Colorado Department of
Health. Every physician or other medical practitioner who treats a person for such
bites shall, within twenty-four (24) hours, report such treatment as required by Colorado
Revised Statutes.
(g)
Suspected rabies. It is unlawful for any veterinarian who clinically diagnoses or
suspects rabies infection in an animal, or any person who suspects rabies infection
in an animal, to fail to immediately report the incident to the Police Department,
County Health Department or Colorado Department of Health and Environment, stating
precisely where such animal may be found. If a known or suspected rabid animal bits
or attacks another animal, it shall also be reported as required above.
(h)
Confinement of animals. Any dog or cat that has bitten a person is immediately quarantined
to be observed for symptoms of rabies. A dog or cat must remain quarantined for ten
(10) days from the date of the bite, based upon the guidelines and requirements of
the County Health Department and the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment.
The procedure and place of observation or analysis is designated by the investigating
officer or responsible agency. If the animal is not quarantined on the owner's premises,
quarantine is by impoundment in the local animal shelter, or any veterinary hospital
of the owner's choice within the City. Such quarantine is at the sole expense of the
animal owner. Stray animals whose owners cannot be located are quarantined as designated
by the City. Circumstances may arise that result in the death of the animal prior
to the completion of the quarantined period. The deceased animal will be required
to have post mortem rabies testing, regardless of vaccination status. The owner will
be responsible for all expenses incurred for post mortem testing. Results of the test
must be provided to the City.
(i)
Failure to produce. The owner or keeper of any animal that has been reported to have
inflicted a bit of any person shall, on demand, produce said animal for impoundment
if deemed necessary by the investigating officer as prescribed in this Section. Refusal
to produce said animal constitutes a violation of this Section and each day of such
refusal shall constitute a separate and continuing violation.
(j)
Removal of animals from confinement. It is unlawful for any person to kill, remove
or release any animal that has been quarantined, impounded, or is in the custody of
the City or its agents as authorized under this Chapter, without the consent of the
City and/or approval of the Court.
(k)
The owner of any dog or cat shall inform the veterinarian before any rabies inoculation
is given whether the subject dog or cat is under bite confinement or has inflicted
a bite or scratch on any person causing an abrasion of the skin or on any other animal
within the last ten (10) days.
(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
(a)
Any time the Chief of Police or any Code Compliance Officer has reasonable suspicion
to believe an animal has engaged in any form of prohibited behavior under this Chapter
or in violation of any law of the state, such animal may, at the discretion of the
Officer, be taken into custody and impounded by a Code Compliance Officer and impounded
in a humane manner.
(b)
If, after making every reasonable effort to seize and capture any animal that has
attacked without provocation or caused injury to any person or domesticated animal,
and the Code Compliance Officer determines that the animal cannot be captured without
exposing the Officers or other persons to danger of personal injury from the animal
and the animal presents a present danger to any person or other animal, it is lawful
for the Officer to humanely destroy the animal without notice to the owner.
(c)
When a veterinarian or animal shelter or Code Compliance Officer has determined that
an animal is critically ill or injured, is suffering extreme pain, or has a poor prognosis
for recovery, nothing in this Chapter is construed to prevent the immediate humane
destruction of such animal.
(d)
Unless other time frames are specifically provided for in this Chapter (including
bite confinement periods), any animal impounded which is not claimed within a five
(5) day period may be disposed of by the animal shelter, only upon written Order of
the Municipal Court. Disposal may be by adoption, donation or humane destruction at
the sole discretion of the Code Compliance Officer or animal shelter. During the period
of impoundment, the Officer shall make reasonable efforts to ascertain and notify
the owner of impoundment.
(e)
Any animal held as evidence at the animal shelter at the request of the Code Compliance
Officer shall remain impounded pending a hearing. Any animal so impounded which is
not claimed, or for which a financial bond is not paid as set forth in this Chapter,
may be disposed of as set forth below. In no event shall said animal be released to
the owner prior to the service of a summons and complaint upon the owner.
(f)
The owner of any impounded animal is responsible for the payment of all charges and
fees, including those for impoundment, boarding, euthanasia, disposal, veterinary
and all other serviced required during this period. Fees and charges are set by the
City. No impounded animal is released until the owner has paid or arranged to pay
all charges and fees associated therewith.
(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
(a)
Whenever an animal is seized or impounded pursuant to this Chapter and a summons has
been issued, the animal owner may be summoned before the Municipal Court on the next
available court date following the seizure or impoundment to address only the issue
of disposition of the seized or impounded animal. The City, through its Code Compliance
Officer, shall make reasonable efforts to notify the owner, in writing, by personal
service or by posting notice on the front door of the owner's residence at the address
shown on the animal license records. Unless the animal owner waives the time frame
for advance service of such notice in order to expedite a hearing, this notice is
served at least five (5) days prior to the hearing and shall state the time, date
and location and purpose of the hearing. Such hearings resulting in animal seizures
or impoundments may be given priority on the Municipal Court docket in order to minimize
the expense to the animal owners for impoundment of seized animals.
(b)
If an animal is seized and impounded on an evidence hold and the owner cannot be ascertained
or served with a summons, disposition of the animal may proceed in accordance with
the time frames required by this Chapter.
(c)
If, on the date of the hearing, notice to the owner was provided as required under
this Section, the Municipal Court may proceed with the hearing as to the disposition
of the animal, whether or not the owner appears.
(d)
Formal rules of evidence shall not apply at such animal disposition hearings and any
statements made at such hearings shall not be used as evidence at any subsequent hearing
in the prosecution of the underlying charges. If the City established, by a preponderance
of the evidence that there is a reasonable likelihood of future injury to persons,
property or animals, the Municipal Court shall order the animal to remain impounded
at the owner's expenses until final disposition of the pending municipal charges.
If the Municipal Court determines that it is inappropriate to order the animal impounded,
the Municipal Court may order the animal returned to the owner and kept under such
circumstances as will ensure the safety of persons, property or other animals, as
may be the case.
(e)
The owner of an animal that has been ordered impounded after a hearing may be required
to post a bond with the Municipal Court in an amount sufficient to provide for the
care and keeping of the animal through the resolution of the Municipal Court case.
If a bond is ordered by the Court, the owner shall have five (5) days to post such
bond.
(f)
If the owner does not appear at the disposition hearing, the City shall, if ascertainable,
send notice of the bond amount to the owner allowing the owner five (5) days to post
such bond.
(g)
If the owner fails to post the bond or cannot be ascertained by the City following
reasonable efforts, the animal is deemed abandoned, and the animal shelter, after
issuance of a Court order, may dispose of the animal at any time after five (5) days
from the date the bond was due to be posted.
(h)
If a summons has been filed in the Municipal Court against the owner of an animal
for a violation of this Code, the Municipal Court may, upon making a finding that
the animal poses a clear and present danger to the citizens or to other animals in
the community, order the animal to be destroyed in a humane manner.
(i)
If the Municipal Court determines that an impounded animal should be released, the
Court shall issue a release order which is given to the owner of the animal. The Municipal
Court shall also send the release order to the Code Compliance Officer. Any animal
held pursuant to a court order may be disposed of by the animal shelter if unclaimed
by the owner within five (5) days following the issuance of a court order authorizing
release of the animal.
(j)
Surrender of an animal to be destroyed in a humane manner does not relieve or render
the owner immune from the decision of the Municipal Court nor does it relieve the
owner from responsibility to pay fines, fees, costs or other penalties which may have
been ordered by the Court for violations of this Chapter.
(k)
Trials involving charges resulting in animal seizures or impoundments may be given
priority on the Municipal Court docket to minimize the expense to animal owners for
impoundment of seized animals.
(Ord. No. 2019-1039 , § 2, 3-4-2019)
(a)
It is unlawful to any owner to fail to pay charges and fees associated with the lawful
impoundment of an animal pursuant to this Chapter. Failure to pay these fees pursuant
to this Chapter shall result in the issuance of a summons.
(b)
Fees and costs related to impoundment, confinement, boarding or other official actions
required under this Chapter are set by the City.
(c)
In a Municipal Court case, the Court shall assess uncollected fees and costs against
a convicted defendant as part of the sentence.

It is unlawful for any person to set or cause to be set any steel-jaw leg hold trap,
poison, snare, or any trap other than a humane trap for the purpose of capturing an
animal, whether wild or domestic, except a licensed or recognized business that has

It is unlawful to place or maintain backyard chickens in any area of the City unless
the following conditions and requirements are met:
(1)
Backyard chickens, chicken coops and chicken runs are allowed as an accessory use
only in conjunction with an established single-family residence. No backyard chickens,
chicken coops or chicken runs is located in common areas of a multi-unit, multi-use
or multi-family property, or any property owned or leased by the City.
(2)
All backyard chicken uses and facilities shall require a permit to be obtained from
the City prior to maintaining backyard chickens on any property. The permit application
process is described in Subsection (17) below.
(3)
No more than six (6) backyard chickens are permitted per established single-family
residence.
(4)
Roosters are prohibited.
(5)
Other poultry or fowl are prohibited. No person may own or keep peacocks, pigeons,
turkeys, ducks or other like fowl.
(6)
Water is provided onsite and accessible to chickens at all times.
(7)
Chicken feed is stored in a resealable, airtight, predator-proof container. Spillage
and leftover feed must be removed daily.
(8)
Chicken waste shall only be stored in a resealable, airtight, predator-proof container.
(9)
On-site slaughtering of backyard chickens is prohibited.
(10)
Any animal that attacks, injures or kills a backyard chicken not on the permitted
property shall not be deemed an "animal which creates a danger," and the owner of
such an animal shall not be charged with violating any provisions of Section 7-148 of this Chapter.
(11)
During daylight hours, backyard chickens shall have access to a chicken run that is
adequately fenced and protected from predators and shall also have access to the chicken
coop.
(12)
From dusk until dawn, backyard chickens are protected from predators by being enclosed
within a chicken coop.
(13)
At no time are backyard chickens permitted to move around freely on a property outside
of a chicken coop, chicken run or other facility.
(14)
No backyard chickens, chicken coops, chicken runs or other facilities is allowed in
the front yard of a property at any time.
(15)
It is required that a chicken coop and chicken run be constructed on any property
approved for a backyard chicken permit. The chicken coop and chicken run must meet
the following conditions and requirements:
a.
The chicken coop and chicken run is located in the rear or backyard of the principal
structure. Neither the chicken coop nor chicken run is located between the rear of
the principal structure and the front yard line.
b.
The chicken coop shall have a minimum five-foot (5) setback from any side or rear
property line.
c.
The maximum chicken coop size permitted is one hundred twenty (120) square feet.
d.
A minimum of four (4) square feet of space per chicken is provided in the chicken
coop and chicken run.
e.
The maximum height of a chicken coop is no more than seven (7) feet at the highest
point of the roof.
f.
Chicken coops is predator-resistant with a solid covered roof.
g.
Chicken runs is fenced and provide adequate protection from predators.
h.
Chicken coops and chicken runs is maintained and is regularly cleaned to control dust,
odor and waste and not constitute a nuisance, safety hazard or health problem to surrounding
properties.
i.
The location of the chicken coop and chicken run in relation to the principal structure,
and any other structures on the property, is illustrated on the application during
the permit application process described in Subsection (17) below.
(16)
Many homeowner association bylaws do not allow backyard chickens. It is the responsibility
of the applicant to confirm that any homeowner association bylaws applicable to the
property do not disallow backyard chickens prior to submitting an application to keep
backyard chickens.
(17)
Backyard chicken permit application process.
a.
Applicant must submit a completed backyard chicken permit application provided by
the City;
b.
Applicant must pay a thirty-dollar ($30.00) application fee that is nonrefundable
and nontransferable;
c.
Applicant must provide proof of ownership of the property prior to being issued a
permit;
d.
Applicant must provide a site plan showing the location of any proposed chicken coop,
chicken run and existing structures on the property at the time of submitting the
backyard chicken application;
e.
After the backyard chicken permit application and application fee are received by
the City, the applicant must schedule an inspection of the chicken coop and chicken
run by a designated employee of the City to ensure that it complies with the requirements
set forth in Subsection (15) above; and
f.
Once the backyard chicken permit application and fee are submitted and the chicken
coop and chicken run are found to comply with Subsection (15) above, after inspection
by a designated employee of the City, the applicant will be issued a nontransferable
backyard chicken permit.
