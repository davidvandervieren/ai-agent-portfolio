For purposes of this Article, the following words shall have the meanings ascribed
hereafter:
Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) shall mean the quantity of oxygen utilized in the biochemical oxidation of organic
matter under standard laboratory procedure in five (5) days at twenty degrees (20°)
Centigrade, expressed in milligrams per liter.
Building drain shall mean that part of the lowest horizontal piping of a draining system which receives
the discharge from soil, waste and other drainage pipes inside the walls of the building
and conveys it to the building sewer, beginning five (5) feet (1.5 meters) outside
the inner face of the building wall.
Building sewer shall mean the extension from the building drain to the public sewer or other place
of disposal, also called house connection.
Combined sewer shall mean a sewer intended to receive both wastewater and storm or surface water
(not permitted).
Easement shall mean an acquired legal right for the specific use of land owned by others.
Floatable oil is oil, fat or grease in a physical state such that it will separate by gravity from
wastewater by treatment in an approved pretreatment facility. Wastewater shall be
considered free of floatable fat if it is properly pretreated and the wastewater does
not interfere with the collection system.
Garbage shall mean the animal and vegetable wastes resulting from the handling, preparation,
cooking and serving of foods.
Industrial wastes shall mean the wastewater from industrial processes, trade or business as distinct
from domestic or sanitary wastes.

Natural outlet shall mean any outlet, including storm sewers and combined sewer overflows, into
a watercourse, pond, ditch, lake or other body of surface or groundwater.
pH shall mean the logarithm of the reciprocal of the hydrogen-ion concentration. The
concentration is the weight of hydrogen ions, in grams, per liter of solution. Neutral
water, for example, has a pH value of 7 and a hydrogen-ion concentration of the log
(1/H +).
Properly shredded garbage shall mean the wastes from the preparation, cooking and dispensing of food that have
been shredded to such a degree that all particles will be carried freely under the
flow conditions normally prevailing in public sewers, with no particle greater than
one-half (½) inch (1.27 centimeters) in any dimension.
Public sewer shall mean a common sewer controlled by a governmental agency or public utility.
Sanitary sewer shall mean a sewer that carries liquid and water-carried wastes from residences,
commercial buildings, industrial plants and institutions together with minor quantities
of ground, storm and surface waters that are not admitted intentionally.
Sewage is the spent water of a community. The preferred term is wastewater, defined in below.
Sewer shall mean a pipe or conduit that carries wastewater or drainage water.
Sewer plant investment fee shall mean a fee charged for the privilege of connecting into the City's sewer system.
Sewer tap means an opening or connection in the mains through which wastewater is discharged.
Shall is mandatory - see may , above.
Slug shall mean any discharge of water or wastewater which, in concentration of any given
constituent or in quantity of flow, exceeds for any period of duration longer than
fifteen (15) minutes more than five (5) times the average twenty-four-hour concentration,
or flows during normal operation, and shall adversely affect the collection system
and/or performance of the wastewater treatment works.
Storm drain (sometimes termed storm sewer) shall mean a drain or sewer for conveying water, groundwater, subsurface water or
unpolluted water from any source.
Suspended solids shall mean total suspended matter that either floats on the surface of or is in suspension
in water, wastewater or other liquids and that is removable by laboratory filtering
as prescribed in "Standard Methods for the Examination of Water and Wastewater" and
referred to as nonfilterable residue.
Unpolluted water is water of quality equal to or better than the effluent criteria in effect or water
that would not cause violation of receiving water quality standards and would not
be benefited by discharge to the sanitary sewers and wastewater treatment facilities
provided.
Wastewater shall mean the spent water of a community. From the stand-point of source, it may
be a combination of the liquid and water-carried wastes from residences, commercial
buildings, industrial plants and institutions, together with any groundwater, surface
water and stormwater that may be present.
Wastewater facilities shall mean the structure, equipment and processes required to collect, carry away
and treat domestic and industrial wastes and dispose of the effluent.
Wastewater treatment works shall mean an arrangement of devices and structures for treating wastewater, industrial
wastes and sludge. Sometimes used as synonymous with waste treatment plant or sewer
treatment plant or wastewater treatment plant or water pollution control plant.
Water requirements means Colorado Big Thompson (CBT) or Windy Gap water shares, or cash-in-lieu of such
water shares, required to be conveyed or paid to the City at the time of the development
of land. (See Sec. 13-122 .)
Watercourse shall mean a natural or artificial channel for the passage of water either continuously
or intermittently.

(a)
No person shall discharge or cause to be discharged any nonsanitary waters such as
stormwater, surface water, groundwater, roof runoff or subsurface drainage to any
sanitary sewer, except by written permission of the Operations Director.
(b)
Stormwater other than that exempted under Subsection (a) above and all other unpolluted
drainage shall be discharged to such sewers as are specifically designated as storm
sewers, or to a natural outlet approved by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater
Plant Manager and other regulatory agencies. Unpolluted industrial cooling water or
process water or air conditioning wastewater may be discharged, on approval of the
Operations Director and/or the Wastewater Plant Manager, to a sanitary sewer as permitted.
(c)
No person shall discharge or cause to be discharged to any public sewer any of the
following described domestic, commercial or industrial water or wastes not meeting
the following limitations:
(1)
Must have an instantaneous pH value in the range of five and one-half (5.5) to nine
(9.0) standard units. Must not contain pollutants which will cause corrosive effects,
damage or hazards to structures, equipment or personnel of the treatment works or
Public Works Department.
(2)
Solid or viscous substances in quantities or of such size capable of causing obstruction
to the flow in sewers or other interference with the proper operation of the collecting
system or wastewater facilities such as, but not limited to, ashes, cinders, sand,
mud, straw, shavings, metal, glass, rags, feathers, tar, plastics, wood, unground
garbage, whole blood, paunch manure, hair and fleshings, entrails, paper dishes, cups,
milk containers, etc., either whole or ground by garbage grinders.
(3)
Must not contain improperly shredded garbage that has not been ground or comminuted
to such a degree that all particles will be carried freely in suspension under flow
conditions normally prevailing in the wastewater system to which the user is connected.
At all times, no particle shall be greater than one-half (½) inch in any direction.
(4)
Must not contain explosive mixtures consisting of liquids, solids or gases which by
reason of their nature or quantity are, or may be, sufficient either alone or by interaction
with other substances to cause fire or explosion or be injurious in any other way
to the operation of sewers or the treatment works. At no time shall two (2) successive
readings on an explosion hazard meter at the point of discharge into the wastewater
system be more than five percent (5%), nor may any single reading be over ten percent
(10%) of the lower explosive limit (L.E.L.) of the meter. Prohibited materials include
but are not limited to: arsenic, cadmium, chromium, chromium, copper, lead, mercury,
molybdenum, nickel, selenium, silver, zinc, benzene, BTEX, gasoline, kerosene, fuel
oil, naphtha, benzene, toluene, xylene, ethers, alcohols, ketones, aldehydes, peroxides,
chlorates, perchlorates, bromates, carbides, hydrides and sulfides.
(5)
Must not contain pollutants which create a fire or explosion hazard in the treatment
works, including but not limited to waste streams with a closed cup flashpoint of
less than eighty-six degrees (86°) Centigrade (187° Fahrenheit) using the test methods
specified in 40 C.F.R § 261.21. Pollutants must not contain any flammable substance
with a flashpoint lower than eighty-six degrees (86°) Centigrade (187° Fahrenheit).
(6)
Must not contain heat in amounts which will inhibit biological activity in the treatment
works resulting in interference, but in no case heat in such quantities that the temperature
at the treatment works exceeds forty degrees (40°) Centigrade (104° Fahrenheit). At
no time shall the temperature of the water or wastes at the point of discharge into
the public sewer be less than thirty-two degrees (32°) Fahrenheit or more than one
hundred fifty degrees (150°) Fahrenheit.
(7)
Must not contain grease or oil or other substances that will solidify or become viscous
between thirty-two degrees (32°) degrees Fahrenheit and one hundred fifty degrees
(150°) Fahrenheit.
(8)
Must not contain insoluble substances in excess of ten thousand (10,000) parts per
million.
(9)
Must not contain solids in excess of twenty thousand (20,000) parts per million.
(10)
Must not contain insoluble substances having a specific gravity greater than 2.65.
(11)
Must not contain gases or vapors either free or occluded in concentrations toxic or
dangerous to humans or animals.
(12)
Must not contain toxic, carcinogenic, poisonous, irritating, noxious or malodorous
solids, liquids or gases in sufficient quantity, either singly or by interaction with
other wastes, which could contaminate the sludge of any municipal system, injure or
interfere with any sewage treatment process, constitute a hazard to humans or animals,
create a public nuisance, create a hazard to treatment plant employees, Public Works
employees or create any hazard in or have an adverse effect on the waters receiving
any discharge from the treatment works.
(13)
Must not have a chlorine demand greater than fifteen (15) parts per million.
(14)
Must not contain any pollutant, including oxygen demanding pollutants (BOD, etc.)
released at a rate and/or concentration which has a reasonable potential, in the opinion
of the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager to adversely affect the
treatment plant (inhibition, pass-through of pollutants, sludge contamination, or
endangerment of treatment plant workers and/or Public Works employees).
(15)
Must not have a BOD in excess of three hundred twenty-five (325) milligrams per liter.
(16)
Must not contain in excess of fifty (50) parts per million of fat, grease or any other
fat or grease products from animal or vegetable origin, or both. Oil, floatable oil,
nonbiodegradable cutting oil or any substance from mineral or petroleum origin, or
both, including but not limited to: Cooling or quenching oils, lubrication oils, cutting
oils and nonsaponifiable oils must not exceed ten (10) parts per million.
(17)
Must not contain organic toxic pollutants, introduced by the intentional or accidental
dumping of solvents, used in operations involving degreasing, surface preparation,
tank washing, paint thinning, paint equipment cleaning or any other process.
(18)
Shall not be any trucked or hauled pollutant, except as approved by the Operations
Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager and discharged at a point designated by the
Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager.
(19)
Must not contain storm water, surface water, groundwater, roof runoff or subsurface
drainage without first obtaining a permit from the control authority for such a discharge.
(20)
Must not contain the following pollutants in excess of the following limits as measured
at the point of discharge into the wastewater system:

(21) Must not contain any radioactive wastes or isotopes.
(d)
The following described substances, materials, waters or wastes shall be limited in
discharges to municipal systems, to concentrations or quantities which will not harm
either the sewers, wastewater treatment processes or equipment, will not have an adverse
effect on the receiving stream, or will not otherwise endanger lives, limb or public
property or constitute a nuisance. The limitations or restrictions on materials or
characteristics of wastes or wastewaters discharged to the sanitary sewer which shall
not be violated without approval of the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant
Manager are as follows:
(1)
Any garbage that has not been properly shredded (See Subsection (c)(3) above). Garbage
grinders may be connected to sanitary sewers from homes, hotels, institutions, restaurants,
hospitals, catering establishments or similar places where garbage originates from
the preparation of food in kitchens for the purpose of consumption on the premises
or when served by caterers.
(2)
Any waters or wastes containing iron, chromium, copper, lead, zinc and similar objectionable
or toxic substances (See Subsection (c)(20) above) to such a degree that any such
material received in the composite wastewater at the wastewater treatment plant exceeds
the limits established by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager
for such materials.
(3)
Any waters or wastes containing grease, oil, nonbiodegradable cutting oil or any oily
substance from petroleum or mineral origin, or both (See Subsection (c)(16) above),
to such a degree that any such material received in the composite wastewater at the
wastewater treatment plant exceeds the limits established by the Operations Director
and/or Wastewater Plant Manager for such materials.
(4)
Any waters or wastes containing floatable oil, fat, grease or any oily substance of
vegetable or animal origin, or both (See Subsection (c)(16) above) to such a degree
that any such material received in the composite wastewater at the wastewater treatment
plant exceeds the limits established by the Operations Director and/Wastewater Plant
Manager for such materials.
(5)
Any waters or wastes containing suspended solids of such character and quantity that
unusual attention or expense is required to handle such materials at the treatment
plant.
(6)
Any waters or wastes containing noxious or odor-producing substances exceeding limits
which may be established by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager.
(7)
Quantities of flow, concentrations or both which constitute a slug as defined in Subsection 13-1 (20).
(8)
Waters or wastewaters which are not amenable to treatment or reduction by the wastewater
treatment processes employed, or are amenable to treatment only to such a degree that
the wastewater treatment plant effluent cannot meet the requirements of other agencies
having jurisdiction over discharge to the receiving waters.
(9)
Any waters or wastes which, by interaction with other waters or wastes in the public
sewer system, release obnoxious gases, form suspended solids which interfere with
the collection system or create a condition deleterious to structures and treatment
processes.
(e)
If any waters or wastes are discharged, or are proposed to be discharged, to the public
sewers that contain the substances or possess the characteristics enumerated in Subsection
(d) above, and which in the judgment of the Operations Director and/or Wastewater
Plant Manager may have a deleterious effect upon the wastewater facilities, processes,
equipment or receiving waters, or which otherwise create a hazard to life or safety
or constitute a public nuisance, the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager
may:
(1)
Reject the wastes;
(2)
Require pretreatment to an acceptable condition for discharge to the public sewers;
(3)
Require control over the quantities and rates of discharge; and/or
(4)
Require payment to cover the added cost of handling and treating the wastes not covered
by existing taxes or sewer charges under the provisions of Subsection (j) below.
When considering the above alternative, the Operations Director and/or Wastewater
Plant Manager shall give consideration to the economic impact of each alternative
on the discharger. If the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager permits
the pretreatment or equalization of waste flows, the design and installation of the
plants and equipment shall be subject to the review and approval of the Operations
Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager.
(f)
Grease, oil and sand interceptors or traps should be cleaned quarterly, or at the
discretion of the Operations Director. The Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant
Manager may take samples as needed and apply surcharges accordingly. All testing charges
are to be paid by owner. Grease, oil and sand interceptors or traps or best available
technology shall be provided when, in the opinion of the Operations Director and/or
Wastewater Plant Manager they are necessary for the proper handling of liquid wastes
containing floatable grease in excessive amounts as specified in Subsection (c)(16)
above, or any flammable wastes, sands or other harmful ingredients; except that such
interceptors shall not be required for private living quarters or dwelling units.
All interceptors or traps shall be of a type and capacity approved by the Operations
Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager and shall be located as to be readily and
easily accessible for cleaning and inspections. In the maintaining of these interceptors,
the owner shall be responsible for the proper removal and disposal by appropriate
means of the captured material and shall maintain records of the dates and means of
disposal which are subject to review by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater
Plant Manager. Any removal and hauling of the collected materials not performed by
the owner's personnel must be performed by currently licensed waste disposal companies,
firms or persons.
(g)
Where pretreatment of any flow-equalizing facilities are provided or required for
any waters or wastewaters, they shall be maintained continuously in satisfactory and
effective operation by the owner and at the owner's expense.
(h)
When required by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager the owner
of any property serviced by a building sewer service carrying industrial wastes shall
install a suitable structure together with such necessary meters and other appurtenances
in the building sewer to facilitate observation, sampling and measurement of the wastes.
Such structures, when required, shall be accessibly and safely located, and shall
be constructed in accordance with plans approved by the Operations Director and/or
Wastewater Plant Manager. The structure shall be installed by the owner at the owner's
expense and shall be maintained by the owner so as to be safe and accessible at all
times.
(i)
The Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager may require a user of sewer
services to provide information needed to determine compliance with this Article.
The requirements may include:
(1)
Peak wastewater discharge rates and volumes over a specified time period;
(2)
Chemical analyses of wastewaters;
(3)
Information on materials being discharged that affect wastewater characteristics;
(4)
Quantity and disposition of specific liquid, sludge, oil, solvent or other materials
important to sewer use control;
(5)
A plot plan of sewers of the user's property showing sewer and pretreatment facility
location;
(6)
Details of wastewater pretreatment facilities;
(7)
Details of systems to prevent and control the losses of materials through spills to
the municipal sewer; and
(8)
Additional information that affects the volume or characteristics of discharges and,
as such, may be required by the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager
to determine compliance with this Article.
(j)
All measurements, tests and analyses of the characteristics of waters and wastes to
which reference is made in this Article shall be determined in accordance with the
latest edition of "Standard Methods of the Examination of Water and Wastewater," published
by the American Public Health Association. Sampling methods, location, times, durations
and frequencies are to be determined on an individual basis subject to approval by
the Operations Director and/or Wastewater Plant Manager.
(k)
No statement contained in this Section shall be construed as preventing any special
agreement or arrangement between the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board and any
industrial concern whereby an industrial waste of unusual strength or characteristic
may be accepted by the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board for treatment.
(l)
All regulations contained in this Section shall be considered a part of the contract
of every person receiving sewer service from the sewer system and wastewater treatment
facilities, and no person may be served from the sewer utility unless the person agrees
to and abides by all rules and regulations of the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board
pertaining to the use of said sewer system.
(m)
Any directives, approvals, disapprovals or other administrative actions issued by

(Prior code 12-530-5; Ord. 645 §1, 1993; Ord. 96-698 §1; Ord. 2001-778)
There shall be the following types of permits to make connections with the sewer system
and wastewater treatment facilities:
(1)
Sewer tap permit to connect to the sewer system for the purpose of discharging into
the sewer system for normal municipal purposes; that is, domestic, commercial or industrial.
At the time this permit is granted, the sewer plant investment fee in effect at the
time of application, together with all other costs associated with the granting of
permission to connect with the sewer system of the City, shall be paid.
(2)
Sewer tap permit to enlarge or replace an existing sewer tap to the sewer system for
the purpose of discharging into the sewer system for normal municipal purposes; that
is, domestic, commercial or industrial. At the time the permit is granted, the additional
sewer plant investment fee, if any, shall be paid.
(3)
Permit to discharge into the sewer system to serve property situated outside the corporate
limits of the City. This permit shall be issued only under such terms and conditions
as the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board may provide by ordinance or resolution.
At the time the sewer tap permit is granted, the sewer plant investment fee shall
be paid for each individual sewer service.
(4)
40 CFR Section 03.3(q) requires industries designated as a "significant users" to
acquire a discharge permit through the state of Colorado. The City of Fort Lupton
does not currently issue these permits. It is a requirement that any industry contact
the Colorado Department of Public Health and environment treatment division to determine
if a discharge permit is required.

(Prior code 12-530-15; Ord. No. 2024-1163 , § 2, 3-19-2024)
(a)
Each user of the sewer system and wastewater treatment facilities shall be charged
a monthly sewer user charge. Such charge shall be established, and amended from time
to time, by written resolution of the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board, and such
amendments after April 16 2022, shall be after publication and a minimum of one (1)
public hearing, which amends the process described in Ordinance 95-675, Section 12-9 (2). Notice shall be published once at least thirty (30) days prior to the date set
for the public hearing on and adoption of the new schedule.
(b)
For all metered residential, commercial and industrial users who irrigate lawn or
other landscaping during the months of March through October, the sewer use charge
on bills may be adjusted to reflect the average monthly water use reflected in the
preceding January, February and March sewer billings (represents the usage of December,
January and February); however, wastewater facility users obtaining the rate benefits
of this Section may be required to submit to a review of their water use at such times
as may be designated by the Operations Director. Customers with less than one hundred
(100) gallons of water usage or new customers with no usage on the preceding January,
February, and March utility bills (representing December, January, and February usage)
will be charged a minimum usage based on the average usage of customers, with usage,
in their customer class in the December, January, and February usage period. Customers
with no water history may be moved to actual usage upon customer request. Customers
moved to actual may not change back that calendar year.
(c)
The Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board shall review the wastewater facilities' total
annual costs of operation, maintenance, replacement and debt service at least once
every year. Rates may be revised if necessary to generate revenue, combined with all
other revenues of the utility, sufficient to pay all said costs. Such rates shall
be established to proportionately recover all operation, maintenance and replacement
costs from users of the wastewater facilities, including those costs associated with
mitigating inefficiency, infiltration and inflow.
(d)
From and after July 1, 1999, the Fort Lupton Utility Enterprise Board may, notwithstanding
any other provision of this Section, approve a negotiated rate or rates for sewer
utility service for a large and/or unique user, taking into consideration the requirement
of Subsection (c) above that such negotiated rates recover the operation and maintenance
and replacement costs occasioned as a consequence of such user. Notwithstanding the
requirements of Subsection (a) above, any such negotiated rates shall be effective
upon approval by the Utility Enterprise Board by majority vote.
