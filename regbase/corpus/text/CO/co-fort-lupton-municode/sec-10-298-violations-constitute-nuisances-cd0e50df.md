(a)
No person shall knowingly smell or inhale the fumes of toxic vapors for the purpose
of causing a condition of euphoria, excitement, exhilaration, stupefaction or dulled
senses of the nervous system. No person shall knowingly possess, buy or use any such
substance for the purposes described in this Subsection. This Subsection shall not
apply to the inhalation of anesthesia or other substances for medial or dental purposes.
(b)
For the purposes of this Article, the term "toxic vapors" includes the following substances
or products containing such substances or emit from such substances:
(1)
Alcohols, including methyl, isopropyl, propyl or butyl;
(2)
Aliphatic acetates, including ethyl, methyl, propyl or methyl cellosolve acetate;
(3)
Acetone;
(4)
Benzene;
(5)
Carbon tetrachloride;
(6)
Cyclohexane;
(7)
Freons, including Freon 11 and Freon 12;
(8)
Hexane;
(9)
Methyl ethyl ketone;
(10)
Methyl isobutyl ketone;
(11)
Naphtha;
(12)
Perchlorethylene;
(13)
Toluene;
(14)
Trichloroethane;
(15)
Xylene;
(16)
Gasoline;
(17)
Glue;
(18)
Kerosene;
(19)
Paint; or
(20)
Varnish.
(c)
In a prosecution for a violation of this Article, evidence that a container lists
one (1) or more of the substances described in Subsection (b) above as one (1) of
its ingredients shall be prima facie evidence that the substance in such container
contains toxic vapors and emits the fumes thereof.
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994; Ord. 2015-994 §90)
The City Council hereby finds and declares that the possession, sale, manufacture,
delivery or advertisement of drug paraphernalia results in the legitimization and
encouragement of the illegal use of controlled substances by making the drug culture
more visible and enticing and that the ready availability of drug paraphernalia tends
to promote, suggest or increase the public acceptability of the illegal use of controlled
substances. Therefore, the purposes of the provisions controlling drug paraphernalia
are:
(1)
To protect and promote the public peace, health, safety and welfare by prohibiting
the possession, sale, manufacture and delivery or advertisement of drug paraphernalia;
and
(2)
To deter the use of controlled substances by controlling the drug paraphernalia associated
with their use.
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994)
As used in this Article, unless the context otherwise requires: drug paraphernalia means all equipment, products and materials of any kind which are used, intended
for use or designed for use in planting, propagating, cultivating, growing, harvesting,
manufacturing, compounding, converting, producing, processing, preparing, testing,
analyzing, packaging, repackaging, storing, containing, concealing, injecting, ingesting,
inhaling or otherwise introducing into the human body a controlled substance (as defined
in Section 18-18-101, et seq., C.R.S., including marijuana, marijuana concentrate,
hashish, cocaine, crack cocaine, opium and opium derivatives) in violation of the
laws of this State. Drug paraphernalia includes, but is not limited to:
(1)
Testing equipment used, intended for use or designed for use in identifying or in
analyzing the strength, effectiveness or purity of controlled substances.
(2)
Scales and balances used, intended for use or designed for use in weighing or measuring
controlled substances;
(3)
Separation gins and sifters used, intended for use or designed for use in removing
twigs and seeds from refining marijuana;
(4)
Blenders, bowls, containers, spoons and mixing devices used, intended for use or designed
for use in compounding controlled substances;
(5)
Capsules, balloons, envelopes and other containers used, intended for use or designed
for use in packaging small quantities of controlled substances;
(6)
Containers and other objects used, intended for use or designed for use in storing
or concealing controlled substances; or
(7)
Objects used, intended for use or designed for use in ingesting, inhaling or otherwise
introducing marijuana, cocaine, hashish or hashish oil into the human body, such as:
a.
Metal, wooden, acrylic, glass, stone, plastic or ceramic pipes with or without screens,
permanent screens, hashish heads or punctured metal bowls;
b.
Water pipes;
c.
Carburetion tubes and devices;
d.
Smoking and carburetion masks;
e.
Roach clips, meaning objects used to hold burning material, such as a marijuana cigarette
that has become too small or too short to be held in the hand;
f.
Miniature cocaine spoons and cocaine vials;
g.
Chamber pipes;
h.
Carburetor pipes;
i.
Electric pipes;
j.
Air-driven pipes;
k.
Chillums;
l.
Bongs; or
m.
Ice pipes or chillers.
(8)
Drug paraphernalia does not include equipment possessed by a licensed pharmacist for business purposes.
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994)
In determining whether an object is drug paraphernalia, a court, in its discretion,
may consider, in addition to all other relevant factors, the following:
(1)
Statements by an owner or by anyone in control of the object concerning its use;
(2)
The proximity of the object to controlled substances (as defined in Section 18-18-101,
et seq., C.R.S., including marijuana, marijuana concentrate, hashish, cocaine, crack
cocaine, opium derivatives);
(3)
The existence of any residue of controlled substances on the object;
(4)
Direct or circumstantial evidence of the knowledge of an owner or of anyone in control
of the object, or evidence that such person reasonably should know that it will be
delivered to persons who he or she knows or reasonably should know could use the object
to facilitate a violation of this Article.
(5)
Instructions, oral or written, provided with the object concerning its use;
(6)
Descriptive materials accompanying the object which explain or depict its use;
(7)
National or local advertising concerning its use;
(8)
The manner in which the object is displayed for sale;
(9)
Whether the owner, or anyone in control of the object, is a supplier of like or related
items to the community for legal purposes, such as an authorized distributor or dealer
of tobacco products;
(10)
The existence and scope of legal uses for the object in the community; or
(11)
Expert testimony concerning its use.
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994; (Ord. 946 §72, 2012)
Possession of drug paraphernalia in the City is prohibited. Having possession of drug
paraphernalia includes having it within physical reach or in sight inside a vehicle.
Drug paraphernalia in the trunk, closed bed or other compartment outside the passenger
area of a vehicle is in the possession of the driver of that vehicle.
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994)
Any person who sells or delivers, possesses with intent to sell or deliver or manufactures
with intent to sell or deliver equipment, products or materials knowing, or under
circumstances where one reasonably should know, that such equipment, products or materials
could be used as drug paraphernalia commits a Code violation. Violation of this Section
carries a minimum mandatory penalty of one hundred dollars ($100.00).
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994; Ord. 2015-994 §91)
Any person who places an advertisement in any newspaper, magazine, handbill or other
publication and who intends thereby to promote the sale in the City of equipment,
products or materials designed and intended for use as drug paraphernalia commits
a Code violation in the City if the advertisement was placed in the City or published
in the City at the direction of the person placing the ad. Violation of this Section
carries a minimum mandatory penalty of one hundred dollars ($100.00).
(Ord. 656 §1, 1994)
(a)
It shall be unlawful for any person under the age of twenty-one (21) to possess, use,
display, purchase, transport, or transfer marijuana, marijuana products, marijuana
plants or marijuana accessories and/or drug paraphernalia.
(b)
It shall be unlawful for any person over the age of twenty-one (21) to possess, use,
display, purchase, transport or transfer more than one (1) ounces of marijuana.
(c)
It shall be unlawful for any person to openly, publicly or in any manner that endangers
others display, consume or use marijuana. Open and public display, consumption or
use of any amount of marijuana shall be deemed possession thereof.
(1)
The term "openly" means occurring or existing in a manner that is unconcealed, undisguised,
or obvious.
(2)
The term "publicly" means:
a.
Occurring or existing in a public place; or
b.
Occurring or existing in any outdoor location where the consumption of marijuana is
clearly observable or detectible from a public place.
(d)
Possession of marijuana for medical use in compliance with both Article XVIII, Section
14 of the Colorado Constitution and the Colorado Medical Marijuana Code shall be an
affirmative defense to prosecution under this Section.
(e)
Violation of this Section is designated a non-criminal violation, subject to the penalties
set forth in Subsection 1-72 (a) of this Code.

(Ord. 2011-930 §2)
It is the intent of Sections 10-295 through 10-298 to prohibit certain land uses related to marijuana for personal use within the City,
and in furtherance of that intent, the City Council makes the following findings:
(1)
Article XVIII, Section 16 of the Colorado Constitution specifically authorizes a municipality
"to prohibit the operation of marijuana cultivation facilities, marijuana product
manufacturing facilities, marijuana testing facilities or retail marijuana stores
through the enactment of an ordinance."
(2)
Based upon careful consideration of Article XVIII, Section 16 of the Colorado Constitution
and the potential secondary effects of the cultivation and dispensing of marijuana
for personal use and the retail sale, distribution and manufacturing of marijuana
for personal use, the City Council finds that such land uses have an adverse effect
on the health, safety and welfare of the citizens of the City.
(3)
The City's authority to adopt these sections and amend its Code is found in Article
XVIII, Section 16 of the Colorado Constitution, Colorado statutory law and this Code.
(Ord. 2014-976 §1)
(a)
It is unlawful for any person to operate, cause to be operated or permit to be operated
a marijuana cultivation facility, marijuana product manufacturing facility, marijuana
testing facility or retail marijuana store or related facility within the City.
(b)
It is unlawful to grow, cultivate, manufacture, prepare, process or package marijuana
for personal use anywhere in the City other than in an enclosed locked space within
an individual's primary residence which is not open or public. Enclosed shall mean
having a roof and all sides closed to the weather with walls or doors and locked to
prevent unauthorized entry. Primary residence shall mean the place that a person,
by custom and practice, makes as his or her principal domicile or address, or occupies
for slumber, meals, regular mail delivery, utility registration or voter registration.
No marijuana may be grown, cultivated, manufactured, prepared, processed or packaged
by anyone in the yard, lot or other area or structure located outside the enclosed
locked space within an individual's primary residence as defined above, including
but not limited to no marijuana may be grown, cultivated, manufactured, prepared,
processed or packaged in any outdoor yards, outdoor gardens, garages, ancillary or
accessory buildings, greenhouses, sheds or storage units.
(1)
No person other than the person growing for his or her personal use shall have access
to the locked enclosed space where the marijuana is grown.
(2)
Any person growing, cultivating, manufacturing, preparing, processing or packaging
marijuana for personal use shall comply with criminal and civil laws of the State
and all land use regulations of the State, the City, the International Building Code,
as amended and updated or any subsequent building code adopted by, utilized by or
enforced by the State, the City and all applicable provisions of this Code.
(3)
It is unlawful for any person who grows, cultivates, manufactures, prepares, processes
or packages marijuana for his or her own personal use, to make such marijuana available
for sale or distribution in any manner.
(4)
It is unlawful for any person who lawfully grows, cultivates, manufactures, prepares,
processes or packages marijuana for his or her own consumption to provide for any
such marijuana to be distributed in any manner to any third party.
(5)
It is unlawful for any person to openly and publicly consume marijuana in any form
in the City.
(6)
It is unlawful for any person to grow, cultivate, manufacture, prepare, process or
package any marijuana in any common area or garage area of a multi-family or single-family
residential property or domicile.
(7)
It is unlawful to grow, cultivate, manufacture, prepare, process or package marijuana
plants within a primary residence or domicile in such a manner as to be perceptible
from the exterior of the primary residence by means including, but not limited to,
common visual observation, odors, smells, fragrances or other olfactory stimulus generated
by the cultivation, production, possession or processing of marijuana plants, or light
pollution, glare, brightness of artificial illumination associated with the cultivation,
production, possession or processing of marijuana plants.
(8)
No marijuana may be grown, cultivated, manufactured, prepared, processed, packaged,
consumed, transferred, distributed or transported on property owned by the City or
leased by the City.
(9)
This Section is designated a noncriminal violation. Penalties set forth in Subsection 1-72 (a) of this Code may be imposed by the Municipal Court upon conviction. Each person
found to be violating any provision of this Chapter shall be guilty of a separate
offense for each and every day during any portion of which any violation of this Chapter
is found to have been committed, continued or permitted by such person.
(Ord. 2014-976 §1)
(a)
The City Council declares that a violation of this Chapter involves a serious threat
to public safety or order within the meaning of Rule 241 of the Colorado Municipal
Court Rules of Procedure.
(b)
If the owner or occupant of the premises upon which marijuana is being grown, cultivated,
manufactured, prepared, processed or packaged denies authorized officials of the City
permission to inspect the residential structure or property, authorized law enforcement
personnel may request the Municipal Court to issue a search warrant for the inspection
of the premises pursuant to procedure and standards as set forth in Rule 241 of the
Colorado Municipal Court Rules of Procedure. Any such issued search warrant shall
comply with the applicable provisions of Rule 241 of the Colorado Municipal Court
Rules of Procedure.
(Ord. 2014-976 §1)

exclusive, a violation of this Article is declared to be a public nuisance that may
be abated as provided for in Section 7-8 of this Code.
