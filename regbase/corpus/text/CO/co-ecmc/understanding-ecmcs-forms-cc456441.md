ECMC collects and verifies information from operators and members of the public to provide effective regulatory oversight and ensure that operators take all required steps to protect public health, safety, welfare, the environment and wildlife resources. To do this, an official communication mechanism is needed to establish a verifiable record for inspections.
On a basic level, "forms" serve as the official communication mechanism. ECMC uses over 50 different forms to collect information from various parties, but primarily operators. The information collected through forms is robust and aligns with ECMC's protective regulations and mission.
Not sure where to begin? Use the guide below to learn about some of the most common forms ECMC uses to complete its work.
Please note: this is an informational guide and does not currently include all the forms used by ECMC. To submit a form or to reference a form not found in the list below, please use the primary Forms page.

## A Plain Language Guide to Forms
Using the list below, view examples and read descriptions of commonly used forms.
ECMC’s Form 2A is one of the first steps in the permitting process. Along with Forms 2B and 2C, Series 2 serves as the core series of permitting forms, which together make up an Oil and Gas Development Plan, the application for a permit to begin production operations at a given location.

The Form 18 is ECMC’s Complaint Report and the 18A is the Complaint Resolution Form. This form documents a request for an investigation with compliance with ECMC rules. This is an online form that can be completed by anyone who would like to report an oil and gas issue to ECMC.

Form Description
The Form 19 is ECMC’s Spill/Release Report. The form is used by operators to provide notice of a spill or release to the ECMC, provide additional information about these incidents and certify compliance with ECMC’s rules for investigation, clean-up and remediation of the spill/release.
ECMC uses the information collected by the Form 19 to track the operator’s response, issue conditions of approval, coordinate with local authorities and other state agencies, ensure the compliance with rules and regulation and, if necessary, take enforcement action.
