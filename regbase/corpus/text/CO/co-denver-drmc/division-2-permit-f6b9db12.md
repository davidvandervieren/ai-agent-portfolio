It shall be unlawful to tow or move any vehicle or salvage vehicle, within the City
and County of Denver, by use of a towing vehicle without the following required equipment.
I.
Trucks:
1.
A full free floating rear axle.
2.
Tires, not less than 700 × 15 in size, with a tread depth of not less than 3/32 of
an inch or less than eight (8) ply rated for vehicle with single rear wheels and six
(6) ply rated for vehicles with dual rear wheels, on rims secured with not less than
eight (8) lug bolts for single rear wheels and not less than five (5) lug bolts for
dual rear wheels, all of which lug bolts are required to be present and in good condition
at all times.
3.
Fender coverings for front and rear wheels.
4.
A power driven winch, and crane or hydraulic lift with a capacity of not less than
eight thousand (8,000) pounds with comparable cable and hardware free of kinks, frays
or other defects that would impair its safe use.
5.
A cradle, with tow plate or sling equipped with safety chains of sufficiently heavy
construction and maintained in a manner to ensure the safe lifting of towed vehicles.
6.
Lights. Each towing vehicle shall be equipped with the following electric lights,
maintained in operating order:
a.
At least two (2) headlamps, an equal number on each side;
b.
Four (4) turn signals; two at the front, two at the rear, equipped with a four-way
system;
c.
Four (4) clearance lamps, two (2) at each side;
d.
On the rear at least two (2) tail lights and two (2) stop lights;
e.
One flashing amber warning light with a diameter of not less than five (5) inches
mounted either on the highest and most visible point or on top of the cab of each
towing vehicle, which will be actuated only at the scene of an accident or while engaged
in picking up a disabled vehicle;
f.
One spot light behind cab capable of lighting scene of disability and illuminating
vehicle being towed;
g.
One portable left and right combination stop, turn signal, and tail light capable
of being securely attached on the rear of the towed vehicle and operated in conjunction
with the stop and tail light on the towing vehicle.
7.
Brakes. Every towing vehicle shall be equipped with two (2) separate brake systems:
a.
A parking brake system that will hold in the applied position by other than fluid
pressure, air pressure or electric energy and is capable of holding the vehicle or
combination of vehicles on any grade on which it is operated under any conditions
of loading or lifting;
b.
A service brake system that is capable of stopping the vehicle and/or combination
of vehicles in a distance of forty (40) feet at a speed of twenty (20) miles per hour.
The service brake system must be maintained so that there are no fluid or air leaks
and so that all brake shoes have at least 3/32 inch of lining or ⅛ of an inch for
pads and apply fully and evenly to each brake drum on all wheels of the towing vehicle.
8.
Emergency equipment. Every towing vehicle under this part shall be equipped with the
following emergency equipment:
a.
One (1) hand ax, one (1) shovel, one (1) broom, one (1) wheel tie;
b.
One metal first aid kit (ten-unit type) completely equipped at all times and meeting
the requirements as prescribed in Federal Specifications 66-K-391(a), 1954;
c.
Three (3) red flares (reflector type), three (3) red flags, or three (3) triangular
reflectors, three (3) thirty-minute railroad-type fusees.
d.
At least one (1) fire extinguisher, two and one-half (2½) pound dry chemical (air
pressure CO 2 booster type) or five (5) pound CO 2 type (carbon tetrachloride not permissible).
e.
A safety chain or chains whose combined length shall be no less than twelve (12) feet
long, comprised of welded links of at least 5/16-inch steel.
f.
All public tow truck vehicles having cranes will be required to have dollies on board
at all times.
9.
Clear and permanent identification visible at all times on both sides of the towing
vehicles showing the name under which the public towing service or private towing
operator is licensed, the business address and telephone number of the licensee, such
identification to be in letters not less than two and one-half (2½) inches in height.
II.
Trailers:
1.
Brakes on at least one (1) axle, electric or hydraulic 3/32-inch lining or better.
2.
Brake, tail and turn lights, marker lights on all four corners.
3.
Weight limit on tongue: two thousand (2,000) pounds.
4.
At least a two and five-sixteenths-inch ball hitch, pindle hook or fifth-wheel hitch.
5.
Clear and permanent markings on both sides to include:
a.
Name.
b.
Address.
c.
Phone number.
d.
PUC number.
6.
Fenders to cover all wheels.
7.
Tandem axle or better.
8.
At least four (4) permanent tie downs for the load (one for each corner).
9.
A winch, electrical or hydraulic, of eight-thousand-pound capacity.

A full free floating rear axle;

Tires not less than 700 × 15 in size;

Flashing amber warning lights mounted on top of the cab or roof;

The following terms shall have the following definitions for purposes of this article:
(a)
Business: means any business or commercial operation or establishment, whether operated for
profit or not for profit, including but not limited to hotels, motels, restaurants,
taverns, retail stores, funeral homes, office buildings, schools, colleges, wedding
and reception halls, theatres, concert halls, and hospitals. The term does not include
private residences.
(b)
Manager: means the manager of licensing and consumer protection.
(c)
Entity: means a corporation, limited liability company, limited partnership or partnership.
(c.1)
Face block: means a zone lot or lots fronting on one (1) side of the block. The "same" face block
indicates only those lots fronting on the same side of the block as the subject property.
The "facing face block" includes those zone lots located on a different block than
the subject property, but located directly across the street from and fronting the
same street as the subject property, and bounded by the same intersecting streets
as the subject property's block.
(d)
Hire: means to employ, contract with, or utilize.
(e)
Person: means a natural person; not an entity.
(f)
Premises: means private real property owned, leased, or licensed to the valet client business.
(g)
Temporary valet parking sign: means a movable sign placed on the public right-of-way stating or advertising that
valet parking is available at the valet client business.
(h)
Valet attendant: means any person hired by a valet operator to perform valet services for the valet
operator, a valet client business, or the valet client business's valet customers.
(i)
Valet client business: means a business that hires a valet operator to perform valet services for valet
customers of the business.
(j)
Valet customer: means the customer, employee, visitor, or other person who owns, leases, or has the
lawful right to possess the motor vehicle that receives parking valet services while
visiting, working at, occupying, or patronizing the valet client business and all
drivers and passengers of such motor vehicle.
(k)
Valet drop-off location: means a place at or near the valet client business's location where valet customers
and their passengers embark and disembark from their vehicles and load or unload their
motor vehicles when utilizing valet services at the valet client business.
(l)
Valet operator: means any person or entity hired as an independent contractor by a valet client business
to perform valet services for the valet client business or the valet client business'
valet customers. The term includes the valet client business itself where the valet
client business elects not to hire a parking valet operator as an independent contractor,
but instead performs parking valet services for its valet customers through its own
employees.
(m)
Valet parking lot: means a parking lot or lots or other place or places where the valet operator or
valet attendants park one (1) or more valet customers' vehicles until the vehicle
is retrieved by a valet operator, valet attendant, or the valet customer. There shall
be no on-street valet parking.
(n)
Valet parking receipt: means a piece of paper or token with a unique number, letter, or symbol which identifies
and connects the valet customer with his or her motor vehicle, which is issued in
triplicate, one (1) copy of which is given to the valet customer, one (1) copy of
which is attached to the key to the valet customer's motor vehicle and kept at the
valet station, and one copy of which is left in the interior of the valet customer's
motor vehicle on the dash or at some other place where the receipt is visible from
the exterior of the vehicle when looking at or through the windshield of the motor
vehicle.
(o)
Valet parking services: means the act of driving a motor vehicle to park or retrieve the motor vehicle for
a valet customer while the valet customer visits, works at, or patronizes a valet
client business. The term does not include merely loading or unloading people, animals
or cargo from a motor vehicle, opening or closing doors of motor vehicles, greeting
customers and patrons in motor vehicles, giving directions for parking, or providing
parking spaces.
(p)
Valet parking station: means a table, podium, desk, or similar place at or near the valet client business
where one (1) copy of the valet parking receipts and keys to the valet customers'
vehicles are kept and where valet customers may go to drop off or retrieve their motor
vehicles or the keys to their motor vehicles.
