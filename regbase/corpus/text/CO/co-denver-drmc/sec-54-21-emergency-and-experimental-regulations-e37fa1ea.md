It shall be the duty of the police department to enforce the street traffic regulations
of this city, and all of the state motor vehicle laws applicable to street traffic
in this city; to make arrests for and assist in the prosecution of persons charged
with traffic violations; to investigate accidents and cooperate with the city traffic
engineer and other officers of the city in administration of the traffic ordinances
and in developing ways and means to improve traffic conditions; and to carry out those
duties especially imposed on the department by the traffic ordinances of this city.
(Code 1950, § 502.1)
(a)
The police department shall keep easily accessible records of all traffic violations
except parking violations of which each person has been convicted during the preceding
five (5) years.
(b)
The police department shall study the cases of all drivers charged with frequent or
serious violations of this chapter, or involved in frequent accidents, or any serious
accidents, and shall attempt to discover the reasons therefor, and take whatever steps
are lawful and reasonable to prevent further violations and accidents including, where
desirable, recommendations to the proper authorities for the suspension or revocations
of the drivers' licenses of such persons.
(Code 1950, §§ 502.2-1, 502.2-2; Ord. No. 669-06, § 1, 10-9-06)
The manager of safety, in collaboration with the city traffic engineer, where a function
of the city traffic engineer is involved, is hereby empowered to make regulations
necessary to make effective the provisions of this chapter, and to make and enforce
temporary and experimental regulations to cover emergencies and special conditions;
provided, such regulations are promulgated in writing, signed by the manager of safety
and maintained in the manager's office as public records. No such experimental or
temporary regulation shall remain in effect for more than ninety (90) days.
(Code 1950, §§ 502.3-3, 502.3-4)
The police department shall annually prepare a traffic report which shall be filed
with the mayor. Such report shall contain information on traffic matters in this city
as follows:
(1)
The number of traffic accidents, the number of persons killed, the number of persons
injured, and other pertinent traffic accident data;
(2)
The number of traffic accidents investigated and other pertinent data of the safety
activity of the police department;
(3)
The number and types of all traffic violations and totals of each type of violation.
(Code 1950, § 502.2-3)

The office of the city traffic engineer is hereby established in the department of
transportation and infrastructure and the city traffic engineer shall be appointed
by the manager of transportation and infrastructure.
(Code 1950, § 502.3; Ord. No. 39-20, § 204, 2-3-20)
(a)
The city traffic engineer shall maintain accurate records in the office of all speed
limits and parking traffic restrictions, limitations, regulations or prohibitions
over one hundred fifty (150) feet in length, except for records of parking meter charges,
time limitations, and days and hours of parking meter operation.
(b)
The records of the city traffic engineer shall be prima facie evidence of the existence
of such controls or posting of such signs.
(c)
The records of the manager of transportation and infrastructure or designee shall
be prima facie evidence of the existence of such parking meter charges, time limitations,
and days and hours of parking meter operation.
(Code 1950, §§ 502.3-6, 502.3-7; Ord. No. 32-86, § 1, 1-21-86; Ord. No. 771-00, §
3, 10-2-00; Ord. No. 39-20, § 205, 2-3-20)
The city traffic engineer shall exercise the powers granted in this chapter, consistent
with the provisions of the Charter relating to the manager of transportation and infrastructure.
(Code 1950, § 502.3-1; Ord. No. 39-20, § 206, 2-3-20)
The traffic engineer shall be responsible for that phase of engineering which deals
with planning and geometric design of streets, highways and abutting lands and with
traffic operation thereon.
(Code 1950, § 502.3-1)
(a)
The city traffic engineer shall have power and authority and is hereby empowered and
authorized, upon the basis of traffic engineering or other investigations and studies
or where necessitated by the peculiar character of the roadway, highway or streets,
or any other public way, to determine that any basic maximum speed limit is greater
or less than is reasonable and safe under the conditions found by the traffic engineer
to then exist, at any intersection, or other place, or upon any part or portion of
any street, roadway, highway or other public way, and in such event the traffic engineer
shall determine and declare a safe and reasonable speed limit thereat, which speed
limit shall be effective when appropriate signs giving notice thereof, are erected
and posted at reasonable intervals along such place, part, portion or section of the
street, roadway, highway or other public way. The traffic engineer is authorized to
accept, upon the basis of traffic engineering investigations and studies, recommendations
of the state department of highway traffic engineer for speed limits upon any state
highway or federal aid highway within the city limits; and such speed limits shall
be effective when appropriate signs giving notice thereof are placed along such highways,
regardless of whether the signs are placed by the traffic engineer or state department
of highways traffic engineer.
(b)

power and authority upon the basis of traffic engineering investigations and studies
to:

to intersections;

Establish construction zones for special parking;

other special parking zones;
(9)
Establish tow away zones;
(10)

(11)
Establish bus stops;
(12)
Permit the posting or erecting of signs by contractors or public utilities companies

(13)

(14)

(15)

(16)

(17)
Establish safety zones of such kind and character, at such places where necessary

(18)
Designate and maintain by appropriate devices, marks or lines upon the surface of

danger to pedestrians crossing the roadway;
(19)

of this city;
(20)
Established routes to be used by trucks, private, common and contract carriers and

under article IV of chapter 22 , in the regular course of business;
(21)
Designate those streets upon which trucks of a certain size or weight shall be prohibited
from traveling;
(22)
Designate maximum weight, length, width and height of vehicles and loads;
(23)
Authorize temporary street or alley closures;
(24)
Establish traffic safety provisions with regard to street enclosures and building
projects;
(25)
Designate intersections and place signs where turns on red signals shall be prohibited;
(26)
Designate bicycle, bus and carpool lanes by pavement marking or traffic signs;
(27)
Reserved.
(28)
Establish construction zones of such kind and character and at such places as deemed
necessary.
(c)
By way of example, but not by way of limitation, the manager of transportation and
infrastructure or designee shall have power and authority upon the basis of parking
investigations and studies to:
(1)
Establish parking meter time limits and charges;
(2)
Establish days and hours of parking meter operation.
(Code 1950, §§ 502.3-2, 502.3-5, 502.4; Ord. No. 363-83, § 1, 6-20-83; Ord. No. 771-00,
§ 4, 10-2-00; Ord. No. 1082-02, § 3, 12-23-02; Ord. No. 559-07, § 1, 10-8-07; Ord.
No. 39-20, § 207, 2-3-20)
(a)
The city traffic engineer shall install and maintain traffic-control devices to prohibit
the classes of vehicles excluded from an area designated as a pedestrian and transit
mall.
(b)
It shall be unlawful for any person operating a vehicle of such class or classes excluded
from an area designated as a pedestrian and transit mall to enter upon the area designated
as a pedestrian and transit mall without first obtaining a written permit from the
city traffic engineer.
(c)
It shall be unlawful for any person to operate a bicycle, skateboard, electrical assisted
bicycle or moped, roller skates or any unauthorized motor vehicle on the 16th Street
pedestrian and transit mall, as defined in ordinance and rules and regulations of
the city; provided, however, that:
(1)
Bicycles are permitted to be operated on the designated transitway only of the 16th
Street pedestrian and transit mall on Saturdays and Sundays; and
(2)
Bicycles may be operated on designated route at any time on that portion of the 16th
Street pedestrian and transit mall between the north-west curb line of Cleveland Place
and Broadway, provided all other provisions of the Denver Revised Municipal Code are
obeyed.
(d)
This section shall not apply to a uniformed city employee riding a bicycle or a police
officer riding a bicycle that is a marked or unmarked official police bicycle, while
engaged in the discharge of his or her official duties.
(Ord. No. 141-80, § 1, 3-31-80; Ord. No. 569-83, § 1, 10-24-83; Ord. No. 71-84, §

(a)
Transportation demand management ("TDM") means a collection of strategies that shift the how, when, and where of people's
travel to increase the efficiency of the transportation system by maximizing travel
choices.
(b)
Single occupancy vehicle ("SOV") means a motor vehicle designed to accommodate more than one (1) person but is used
to transport only one (1) occupant.
(c)
TDM plan means an approved plan of strategies, including TDM supportive infrastructure and
TDM programmatic strategies to maximize options for non-SOV travel modes for building
occupants/resident and visitors.
(d)
TDM strategies means physical improvements, incentives, and subsidies that encourage the reduction
of single-occupancy vehicle travel.
(Ord. No. 342-21, § 1, 4-19-21)

and the executive director of community planning and development (CPD) shall have

Annual monitoring will be required of every property subject to this division.
(Ord. No. 342-21, § 1, 4-19-21)
(a)
Notice of violation. If it is determined by the manager of DOTI or the manager of CPD, or either of them
or their designee(s) that a violation of this division or the rules and regulations
promulgated hereunder has occurred, the manager or his designee shall, in writing,
notify the owner of the property or responsible party through the issuance of a notice
of violation. The notice of violation shall require compliance within ten (10) days,
unless otherwise specified in the rules and regulations. The notice of violation may
be served by personal service; first class mail and posting the subject property or
by posting the subject property and publishing the notice in the official city newspaper.
(b)
Notice of violation; contents. The notice of violation shall identify the property in violation, generally describe
the violation, describe the proposed remedy to cure said violation, and give notice
that failure to cure the violation is prohibited by city law and may lead to legal
action. The notice of violation shall state that the owner or responsible party may
appeal the notice of violation by following the appeal process found in section 56-106 of this Code within the same time period allowed to cure in the notice of violation,
and by paying an appeal fee of one hundred dollars ($100.00).
(c)
Appeals. An owner who has been affected by a determination made pursuant to the provisions
of this division 3 may appeal that determination to the manager by following the procedures
set forth in section 56-106 of this Code. An appeal made pursuant to this section must be filed within the time
prescribed for cure in the notice of violation, and an appeal fee of one hundred dollars
($100.00) must be paid at the time the appeal is filed.
(d)
In addition to any other method of enforcement of this article, the executive director
of DOTI, or the executive director of CPD, or either of their respective designees
may, by rules and regulations, administer citations pursuant to article XII (Administrative
citations), chapter 2 , Denver Revised Municipal Code, to enforce this division.
(Ord. No. 342-21, § 1, 4-19-21)
