the property line of the building site, except as specified in this article.

of materials as required in the building code.
(Code 1950, § 331.1-5)

Oriel windows, balconies, unroofed porches, cornices and belt courses and appendages
such as water-tables, sills, capitals, bases and other decorative features may only
project over the right-of-way or other public property as provided in this section.
(1)
No projection into the right-of-way shall exceed a distance as determined by the clearance
of the lowest point of the projection above the grade immediately below, as follows:
(a)
Clearance above grade less than eight (8) feet: No projection is permitted except
for architectural details such as belt courses, sills, bases and other architectural
details which may project up to six (6) inches.
(b)
Clearance above grade over eight (8) feet: One (1) inch of projection is permitted
for each additional inch of clearance provided that no such projection shall exceed
a distance of four (4) feet.
(2)
Any building element that projects more than twelve inches into the right-of-way shall
have a drainage system that prevents water from draining directly onto the right-of-way.
(3)
Provided they comply with (1) and (2) above, building cornices may extend more than
four feet into the right-of-way if the manager of transportation and infrastructure
determines that they do not interfere with the right-of-way or utilities and that
they do not constitute a safety hazard.
(4)
Balconies, other than those over alleys, must comply with (2) above and:
(a)
No part of any balcony shall be allowed below a height of twelve (12) feet above grade.
(b)
Nothing shall be placed on, over or beyond the railing or wall of a balcony unless
permanently attached to the building and properly permitted.
(c)
Balconies may extend up to eighteen (18) inches into the right-of-way provided they
meet the conditions of paragraphs (a) and (b) above.
(d)
Balconies may extend up to four (4) feet into the right-of-way provided they meet
the conditions of paragraphs (a) and (b) above and the following conditions:
1.
The distance between the back of curb and the furthest most projection of any balcony
is not less than ten (10) feet; and
2.
The width of unobstructed paved walking surface beyond the furthest most projection
of any balcony is not less than five (5) feet.
(5)
Balconies over alleys must comply with (2) and (4)(b) above and:
(a)
No part of any balcony shall be below twenty-four feet above grade and must meet the
minimum clearance requirements for utilities.
(b)
No part of any balcony may extend more than four feet into the right-of-way.

a.
Definitions.
(1)
Control shall mean any remedial action intended to stabilize, inhibit future exposure to
or misuse of, or reduce emissions or effluents from radium contaminated materials,
including leaving radium contaminated material in place pursuant to the application
of supplemental standards as specified in Subpart C of 40 CFR Part 192.
(2)
Disposal or disposes shall mean dumping, burial or placing of radium contaminated material into or on
any land, release through a sanitary sewerage system, incineration, or long-term storage
with no intention of or provision for subsequent removal.
(3)
Radioactive waste shall mean all radioactive materials which have no useful purpose and are to be or
have been discarded and for which the United States Environmental Protection Agency
or the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment has determined remedial
action is required to control the harmful effects to public health or the environment
of radioactive emissions from the materials.
(4)
Radium contaminated material shall mean any material in which the concentration of radium-226 in land averaged
over any area of one hundred (100) square meters exceeds the background level by more
than: a) five (5) picocuries per gram, averaged over the first fifteen (15) centimeters
of soil below the surface, and b) fifteen (15) picocuries per gram, averaged over
fifteen-centimeter thick layers of soil more than fifteen (15) centimeters below the
surface.
b.
Any person who disposes of or implements a remedial action to control or to attempt
to control radioactive waste or radium contaminated material shall be charged a fixed
fee of twelve thousand dollars ($12,000.00) for each separate geographic location
where radioactive waste or radium contaminated material is disposed of or controlled
plus a variable fee of thirty dollars and six cents ($30.06) per cubic foot of radium
contaminated material that remains beneath public rights-of-way or other property
owned by the city.
c.
Any fees paid pursuant to this section shall be deducted from the fees assessed for
the same waste pursuant to section 48-112 of the Revised Municipal Code.
d.
Any revenues generated by this fee shall be credited to the radioactive waste management
fund established pursuant to section 48-113 .
e.
Permanent disposal and control of radioactive waste and radium contaminated material
are not permitted uses of property pursuant to chapter 59 of the Revised Municipal Code and nothing contained herein shall be deemed to authorize
permanent disposal or control of radioactive waste or radium contaminated material.
This fee shall apply if, notwithstanding the prohibition, a person disposes of or
controls radioactive waste or radium contaminated material on property within the
city.
f.
The fees imposed by this section shall not apply to persons who dispose of or control
radium contaminated material incidental to installation, maintenance, repair, improvement
or replacement of utilities, streets, sidewalks and alleys in public rights-of-way
regulated pursuant to chapter 49 of the Revised Municipal Code and pursuant to the manager's regulations titled "Management
Plan Denver Radium Site, Operable Unit 7 Denver Streets."

Notice under section 49-446 shall be directed to and served upon the owner or person in possession of the premises
where such obstruction occurs, or both, and shall be served in the manner and as provided
by the rules of civil procedure for courts of record in the state for personal service
of process within the state, or by mailing a copy thereof to such owner or person
in possession at their address or last known address by registered or certified mail,
return receipt requested, and service shall be deemed complete upon such mailing;
provided, however, that if such owner or person in possession cannot be found in the
city, or if the address and last known address are unknown, service of such notice
may be made on the owner or person in possession by posting a copy of such notice
in some conspicuous place on the premises, and in such case service thereof shall
be deemed complete upon such posting.
(Code 1950, § 331.18-2)
(a)
Whenever the owner of any building with an obstructing building appurtenance encroaching
upon any street, sidewalk or public place shall refuse or neglect to remove the same,
after notice as provided in section 49-447 , it shall be lawful for the manager of transportation and infrastructure to cause
the projection or obstruction to be removed or taken down.
(b)
The expense thereof shall be recoverable from the owner in a civil action.
(c)
Such obstructing or projecting appurtenance shall be deemed a nuisance.
