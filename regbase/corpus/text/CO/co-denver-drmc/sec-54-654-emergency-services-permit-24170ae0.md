The city traffic engineer shall have the authority to designate those streets and
highways upon which trucks or truck-tractors are restricted or prohibited from operating.
(Code 1950, § 523.1)
(a)
It shall be unlawful to operate a truck on any street, highway or public way not posted
or signed for truck usage except where:
(1)
It is necessary to drive the vehicle on the street, highway or public way in order
to make a delivery or provide a service; and
(2)
The driver follows a route that is the shortest distance between his or her destination
and the street, highway or public way on which trucks are not restricted or prohibited
that is nearest to the destination; and
(3)
In leaving the original destination, the driver follows a route that is the shortest
distance back to the nearest street, highway or public way on which trucks are not
restricted or prohibited; and
(4)
If multiple stops must be made within one (1) grid on the truck map, the driver follows
the shortest route from stop to stop, and after making the final stop within that
grid, the driver then follows the shortest route to the nearest street, highway or
public way on which trucks are not prohibited. In so doing, drivers must make reasonable
efforts to plan their trips so as to minimize the distance driven on nontruck routes.
For purposes of this section, grid shall mean the smallest area completely enclosed
or surrounded by designated truck routes.
(b)
It shall be unlawful to park a truck on any public way or public property that is
not posted with signs allowing truck parking unless the truck is in the act of providing
service to the adjoining property and the truck is not parked in excess of two (2)
hours as specified in section 54-464 .
(c)
In addition to trucks as defined in section 54-2 (72), any truck-tractor shall be considered a truck under this section.
(d)
The city engineer shall post with signs all streets, highways or public ways where
truck traffic or truck parking is appropriate.
(Code 1950, § 523.1-1; Ord. No. 224-81, § 2, 4-27-81; Ord. No. 222-85, § 3, 4-15-85;
Ord. No. 243-91, § 1, 4-1-91)
The city traffic engineer and the manager of safety shall have the authority to designate
the routes to be followed by trucks or truck-trailer combinations, by private, common
and contract carriers or by any other person transporting hazardous material, as defined
under article IV of chapter 22 , in the regular course of business and traveling through and making deliveries within
the city. Such officials of the city shall adopt a route map for transporting such
hazardous materials, indicating such designated routes and designated portions of
the city, and it shall be unlawful for any of the above described vehicles, carriers
or persons to deviate from such routes except as provided in section 54-648 .
(Code 1950, § 523.1-2)
(a)
In metered parking areas, trucks over twenty (20) feet in length shall park only at
truck loading zones or loading zones or in alleys and shall not be parked in truck
loading zones or loading zones or alleys except while actually engaged in loading
or unloading merchandise, and such loading or unloading shall only take place during
the times and under the restrictions specified for the use of zones as enumerated
in accordance with provisions of this chapter.
(b)
All truck loading and unloading shall be made by parallel parking of trucks at the
curb except in areas where no curbs or other parking controls exist, and then they
shall be parked in such a manner so as not to block the flow of traffic or unreasonably
impede the flow of traffic.
(c)
If two (2) or more trucks are in an alley for the purpose of loading or unloading
merchandise at the same dock, drivers shall at all times be near their trucks and
shall not be permitted to block any other delivery facilities within the alley.
(Code 1950, §§ 523.1-3—523.1-5)
Cross reference— Metered loading zones, § 54-509 (2).
Where it is necessary to establish construction truck zones, construction trucks shall
park parallel in construction truck zones unless otherwise authorized by a written
permit issued by the city traffic engineer, when it is necessary to use such vehicles
contrary to this provision while actually engaged in construction or repair service,
otherwise construction trucks shall comply with all other provisions of this chapter.
(Code 1950, § 523.1-6)
(a)
Any vehicle or equipment may occupy any street or alley when engaged in providing
required services in the street or alley, or to adjacent property, providing the vehicle
or equipment is properly authorized. Vehicles or equipment which occupy the traveled
lanes or in any way interfere with the movement of traffic on the traveled lanes of
any arterial or collector street must have a written street occupancy permit from
the city traffic engineer, except for those operating under the following conditions:
(1)
Those operating under an excavation permit issued by the manager;
(2)
Those operating under an emergency service truck permit issued by the city traffic
engineer;
(3)
Those owned or operated by the city or the Colorado Department of Highways;
(4)
Those operating under a contract to the city or to the state department of highways.
(b)
A street occupancy permit issued under this section is valid only according to the
conditions and limitations described on the permit, and any violations of such conditions
and limitations is a violation hereof.
(c)
The city traffic engineer is authorized to propose to the council, from time to time,
rules and regulations relating to the fees for street occupancy permits issued under
this section; provided, that no such rules and regulations shall be adopted by the
city traffic engineer or take effect, nor shall the fees proposed thereunder be established,
unless the same are approved by ordinance.
(Code 1950, § 523.1-7; Ord. No. 74-81, § 1, 3-9-81; Ord. No. 492-91, § 2, 7-1-91)
(a)
The central traffic district is an area bounded by and including Broadway between
20th Street and West Colfax Avenue; West Colfax Avenue between Broadway and 14th Street;
14th Street between West Colfax Avenue and Wazee Street; Wazee Street between 14th
Street and 19th Street; 19th Street between Wazee Street and Blake Street; Blake Street
between 19th Street and 20th Street; and 20th Street between Blake Street and Broadway.
(b)
No single vehicle exceeding thirty (30) feet in length or combination of vehicles
exceeding thirty-eight (38) feet in length shall be permitted to operate within the
central traffic district during the hours of 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., except Sundays
and holidays.
(c)
Service and maintenance trucks using and occupying alleys to provide service to adjacent
buildings must be unloaded and removed from said alleys unless they are authorized
emergency service trucks providing emergency maintenance service under the provisions
of this article.
(Code 1950, §§ 524.1—524.3; Ord. No. 243-81, §§ 1, 2, 5-11-81)
(a)
Emergency service trucks may use and occupy any street or alley while actually engaged
in providing emergency maintenance service. Emergency maintenance service is defined
as service of such a necessity that undue hardship or hazard could occur if immediate
service were not provided. An annual permit for emergency service truck authority
may be issued by the city traffic engineer if the possibility of undue hardship or
hazard can be demonstrated to the traffic engineer's satisfaction. Emergency service
trucks may not obstruct traffic or park in violation of parking regulations if the
service can be performed reasonably from on-street or off-street parking places. Emergency
service trucks may not park at any one (1) location in violation of parking regulations
longer than four (4) hours without obtaining a street occupancy permit from the city
traffic engineer. While using and occupying a street or alley as an emergency service
truck, the permit from the city traffic engineer must be displayed in the lower right-hand
corner of the windshield and one (1) or more flashing yellow warning lights must be
in operation. The flashing yellow warning lights shall be visible from all directions
and shall be of a type approved by the city traffic engineer. A permit for an emergency
service truck shall not authorize such truck to be an authorized emergency vehicle.
(b)
An emergency service truck permit issued under this section is valid only according
to the conditions and limitations described on the permit, and any violation of such
conditions and limitations is a violation hereof.
(c)
The city traffic engineer is authorized to propose to the council, from time to time,
rules and regulations relating to the fees for emergency service truck permits issued
under this section; provided, that no such rules and regulations shall be adopted
by the city traffic engineer or take effect, nor shall the fees proposed thereunder
be established unless the same are approved by ordinance.
(Code 1950, § 523.1-8; Ord. No. 74-81, § 1, 3-9-81)
The city traffic engineer may grant permission for exceptions to any section of this
article where the traffic engineer deems the activity to be unusual, extraordinary
or of an emergency nature. Such permits shall be in writing and prominently displayed
on applicable vehicles.
(Code 1950, §§ 523.1-9, 524.4)
