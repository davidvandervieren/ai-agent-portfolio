(a)
When one (1) vehicle is towing another, the drawbar or other connection shall be of
sufficient strength to pull all weight towed thereby and the drawbar or other connection
shall not exceed fifteen (15) feet from one (1) vehicle to the other, except the connection
between any two (2) vehicles transporting poles, pipe, machinery or other objects
of structural nature which cannot readily be dismembered.
(b)
When one (1) vehicle is towing another and the connection consists of a chain, rope

less than twelve (12) inches square.
(c)
When one (1) vehicle is towing another, in addition to the drawbar or other connection,
safety chains shall be used, arranged in such a way that it will be impossible for
the vehicle being towed to break loose from the vehicle towing if the drawbar or other
connection were to be broken, loosened or otherwise damaged. This shall apply to all
trailers and other towed vehicles, except semi-trailers connected by a proper fifth
wheel, and to any dolly used to convert a semi-trailer to a full trailer.

(a)
The gross weight upon any wheel of a vehicle shall not exceed the following:
(1)
When the wheel is equipped with a solid rubber or cushion tire, eight thousand (8,000)
pounds;
(2)
When the wheel is equipped with a pneumatic tire, nine thousand (9,000) pounds.
(b)
The gross weight upon any one (1) axle of a vehicle shall not exceed the following:
(1)
When the wheels attached to the axle are equipped with solid rubber or cushion tires,
sixteen thousand (16,000) pounds;
(2)
When the wheels attached to the axle are equipped with pneumatic tires, twenty thousand
(20,000) pounds.
(c)
For the purposes of this section, an axle shall be defined as the total load on all
wheels whose centers are included within two (2) parallel transverse vertical planes
not more than forty (40) inches apart.
(d)
The gross weight upon any one (1) wheel of a steel tired vehicle shall not exceed
five hundred (500) pounds per inch of cross-sectional width of tire.
(Code 1950, § 525.1-9; Ord. No. 15, § 2, 1-14-80; Ord. No. 477-00, § 1, 6-19-00)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, C.R.S. 1973, 42-4-406.
(a)
No vehicle or combination of vehicles shall be moved or operated on any street, highway,
bridge or viaduct when the gross weight thereof exceeds the limits specified below:
(1)
The gross weight upon any one (1) axle of a vehicle shall not exceed the limits prescribed
in section 54-699 .
(2)
Subject to the limitations prescribed in section 54-699 , the gross weight of any single vehicle having two (2) axles shall not exceed thirty-six
thousand (36,000) pounds.
(3)
Subject to the limitations prescribed in section 54-699 , the gross weight of any single vehicle having three (3) or more axles shall not
exceed fifty-four thousand (54,000) pounds.
(4)
Where the maximum load limit is posted on any bridge or viaduct, it shall be unlawful
for any vehicle whose gross weight is in excess of such posted maximum weight limit
to proceed to cross such bridge or viaduct; nothing in this article to the contrary.
(5)
Subject to the limitations prescribed in section 54-699 , the maximum gross weight of any vehicle or combination of vehicles shall not exceed
that determined by the formula W = 1000 × (L + 40), where W equals the gross weight
in pounds, L equals the length in feet between the centers of the first and last axles
of such vehicle or combination of vehicles; but in computation of this formula no
gross vehicle weight shall exceed eighty-five thousand (85,000) pounds.
(b)
For the purposes of this section where a combination of vehicles is used, no vehicle
shall carry a gross weight of less than ten (10) percent of the over-all gross weight
of the combination of vehicles; except that these limitations shall not apply to specialized
trailers of fixed public utilities whose axles may carry less than ten (10) percent
of the weight of the combination. The limitations provided in this section shall be
strictly construed and enforced.
(c)
The gross weight limits provided in this section are increased by one thousand pounds
for any vehicle or combination of vehicles if the vehicle or combination of vehicles
contains an alternative fuel system and operates on alternative fuel or both alternative
and conventional fuel. For the purpose of this section "alternative fuel" has the
same meaning provided in C.R.S. 25-7-106.8(1)(a).
(Code 1950, § 525.1-10; Ord. No. 15, §§ 3—5, 1-14-80; Ord. No. 477-00, § 1, 6-19-00)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, C.R.S. 1973, 42-4-407.
(a)
Any police officer having reason to believe that the weight of a vehicle and load
is unlawful is authorized to require the driver to submit to a weighing of the vehicle
on certified weight scales. A certified weight scale can be either portable or fixed.
(b)
Whenever an officer, upon weighing a vehicle and load, as above provided, determines
that the weight is unlawful, such officer may require the driver to stop the vehicle
in a suitable place and remain standing until such portion of the load is removed
as may be necessary to reduce the gross weight of such vehicle to such limit as permitted
under this article. All materials so unloaded shall be cared for by the owner or operator
of such vehicle at the risk of such owner or operator, who shall promptly remove such
materials, leaving the premises in a clean and orderly condition.
(c)
It shall be unlawful for any driver of a vehicle to fail or refuse to stop or to submit
the vehicle and load to a weighing or to fail or refuse to permit the unloading of
the vehicle to the gross weight of such vehicle as permitted in this article.
(Code 1950, § 525.1-11; Ord. No. 15, §§ 6, 7, 1-14-80; Ord. No. 477-00, § 1, 6-19-00)

(a)
The city traffic engineer, in cooperation with the city engineer, may, upon application
in writing and good cause being shown therefor, issue a single trip, special or an
annual permit in writing, authorizing the applicant to operate or move a vehicle of
a weight exceeding the maximum specified in this article or otherwise not in conformity
with the provisions of this article; provided, however, that no permit shall be issued
for vehicles to cross structures if such vehicles are of weights in excess of the
structural capacity of such structures as determined by the city engineer. These permits
are subject to the rules and regulations adopted by the city engineer/transportation
director.
(b)
The application for any such permit shall specifically describe the vehicle and load
to be operated or moved and the particular streets or other public ways for which
permit to operate is requested and whether such permit is for a single trip or for
continuous operation and the time of such movement.
(c)
In granting such permit, the city traffic engineer may, at the traffic engineer's
discretion, limit the number of trips or establish seasonal or other time limitations
within which the vehicles described may be operated on the public ways indicated or
otherwise to limit or prescribe conditions of operation of such vehicle, when necessary
to assure against undue damage to streets, viaducts and other public ways and may
require such undertaking or other security as may be deemed necessary to compensate
for any injury to any public way, viaduct, bridge or other road or structure.
(d)
Every such permit shall be carried in the vehicle or combination of vehicles to which
it refers and shall be open to inspection by any police officer or authorized agent
of any authority granting such permit and no person shall violate any of the terms
or conditions of such special permit.
(e)
No permit shall be necessary for the operation of authorized emergency vehicles or
public transportation vehicles operated in the city.

The city traffic engineer is authorized to issue single trip or annual oversize moving
permits for vehicles, loads or equipment which exceed the maximum height, width or
length specified in this article. However no permit shall be issued for a vehicle
exceeding sixteen (16) feet in height, seventeen (17) feet in width, or one hundred-thirty

(Code 1950, § 525.1-6(7); Ord. No. 477-00, § 1, 6-19-00)
The city traffic engineer in cooperation with the city engineer may issue and allow
a permit for the movement of vehicles, loads or vehicles and loads combined which
exceed seventeen (17) feet in width, sixteen (16) feet in height, one hundred-thirty
(130) in length and two hundred thousand (200,000) pounds as measured and assembled
on the street ready to be moved, only upon the conditions applicable to oversize moves
as described in section 54-724 and satisfying the additional requirements of the city traffic engineer which can
include the following conditions:
(1)
The permittee (the owner or an agent, which may be the moving contractor but must
be authorized in writing) obtain the service, in writing, of a licensed contractor
and or engineer approved by the city traffic engineer to accompany the move along
its entire route;
(2)
The approved contractor and/or engineer shall, prior to a permit being issued for
the move, survey the route of the oversize move and make a written report to the city
traffic engineer for approval, setting forth a move plan. Said plan shall address
the following to the appropriate level of investigation:
(a)
Requirements for protection of pavement, curb, gutters and sidewalks;
(b)
Requirements for protection of structures, including structure reinforcement and protection
for any other structural appurtenances, landscaping, and street signs;
(c)
Requirements for traffic signals, and other control devices, setting forth how the
devices and signals are to be handled or modified to allow the move;
(d)
Requirements for the protection of private property, utility infrastructure in the
right of way and for removal of parked cars which may be normally parked on the street
and interfere with the move;
(e)
Requirements for the prompt replacement and returning to normal service all items
modified or removed for the move;
(f)
Requirements for the temporary storage of vehicles, and loads off the city ROW should
the move contractor or the city traffic engineer estimate that the move may not be
completed in a permitted period.
(3)
The city traffic engineer may reject or request more information following the submission
of the move plan. Additionally, the city traffic engineer may approve the permit contingent
upon none, any or all of the following:
(a)
Implementation of the requirements described in the original moving plan.
(b)
Further conditions that the city traffic engineer cooperating with the city engineer
may require.
(c)
Submission of a bond and or insurance for the protection of the city, and city facilities
along the route in an amount required by the city traffic engineer.
(d)
Written approval of affected utility companies, railroads, the regional transportation
district and the state department of transportation.
(e)
Concurrence of other appropriate departments of the city as designated by the city
traffic engineer.
(f)
A public information program if required by the city traffic engineer.
(g)
Payment of a deposit to cover the cost of city staff support for the move.
(4)
The contractor and the permittee indemnify and save the city harmless against all
claims for damages and injury arising out of the oversize move;
(5)
Traffic signal components and signs may be removed only for a period of time long
enough to allow the vehicle and load to be moved through the location involved and
these traffic signals and signs be reinstalled immediately and placed back into original
operation and no traffic signals and signs be left in any condition other than what
they were prior to removal, including no additional splicing of any overhead or underground
electrical cables;
(6)
All traffic signals be back in normal operating condition and all signs properly installed
and oriented prior to 6:00 a.m. of the day of the move.

No person shall drive, operate, or move upon or over any highway, street, highway
or structure any vehicle, object or contrivance in such a manner so as to cause damage
to said highway, street or structure. When the damage sustained to said highway, street
or structure is the result of operating, driving or moving of such vehicle, object
or contrivance in excess of the maximum weight or size authorized in sections 54-692 , 54-694 , 54-695 , 54-696 , 54-697 , 54-699 , and 54-700 , it shall be no defense to any action, either civil or criminal, brought against
such person that the weight or size of the vehicle was authorized by permit issued
in accordance with sections 54-702 , 54-724 , and 54-725 .
Every person violating this section shall be liable for all damage that said street;
highway or structure may sustain as a result thereof. Whenever the driver of such
a vehicle, object, or contrivance is not the owner thereof but is operating, driving,
or moving such a vehicle, object, or contrivance with the express or implied consent
of the owner thereof, then said owner or driver shall be jointly and severally liable
for any such damage. The liability for damage sustained by any such highway, street
or structure may be enforced by a civil action by the authorities in control of such
highway, street or structure. No satisfaction of any civil liability, however, shall
be deemed to be a release or satisfaction of any criminal liability for violation
of the provisions of this section.
