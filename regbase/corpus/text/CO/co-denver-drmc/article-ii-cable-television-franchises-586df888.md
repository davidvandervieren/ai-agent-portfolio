As used in this article, the following words or terms shall have the meanings respectively
ascribed to them, unless the context otherwise requires:
(1)
Chief Information Officer (CIO) shall mean the person in charge of the city's technology services agency or the CIO's
designee.
(2)
Technology services agency shall mean the agency under the mayor that includes among its functions the administration
and oversight of city's public, educational and governmental access cable television
channels.
(3)
Franchise shall mean any franchise for a cable system that has been granted by the city to
a cable operator in order to provide cable service within the city.
(4)
Cable operator shall have the same meaning as is provided at 47 U.S.C. § 522(5), as amended.
(5)
Cable system shall have the same meaning as is provided at 47 U.S.C. § 522(7).
(6)
Cable service shall have the same meaning as is provided at 47 U.S.C. § 522(6), as amended.
(Ord. No. 387-11, § 1, 7-18-11)
On and after August 1, 2011, the powers and duties formerly performed by the office
of telecommunications in relation to the administration of any franchise granted to
any cable operator within the city shall be transferred to and assumed by the technology
services agency of the city. Any contract of the city, including any franchise heretofore
granted by the city, that assigns any function to the office of telecommunications
or to the director of the office of telecommunications shall, on and after August
1, 2011, be construed to refer to technology services and the chief information officer
or his designee respectively.
(Ord. No. 387-11, § 1, 7-18-11)
(a)
The technology services agency shall be responsible for the administration and regulation
of any franchise granted to a cable operator within the city.
(b)
The duties and responsibilities of the chief information officer or the CIO's designee
shall include, but not be limited, to the following:
(1)
Serves as liaison for the city with any cable operator, state and federal authorities
concerned with cable service and with any organization which may be established or
approved by the city to administer any public, educational, or governmental access
channels.
(2)
Receives and investigates complaints concerning the operation of any cable system.
(3)
Assures cable operators' compliance with all applicable ordinances and laws and upon
determination of any violation of same, takes all necessary and appropriate actions
to enforce the applicable ordinance or law.
(4)
In consultation with the city council, monitors the cable operators' performance and
adherence to the terms of the franchise, including, but not limited to, construction
schedule, installation policy, rates, operational standards, services provided pursuant
to the franchise, maintenance, technical standards, system design, use of public ways,
service continuity, prohibitions against discrimination in the delivery of cable services,
requirements for delivery of cable services to low-income neighborhoods, and the provision
of support and facilities for public, educational and governmental access programming.
(5)
Receives for review and evaluation all data and reports required of the cable operators
and where appropriate, forwards such data to interested or affected city departments.
(6)
Makes such recommendations and reports to the city council and the mayor as may be
deemed necessary for the regulation and development of any cable system.
(7)
Represents the city in communicating with the public with regard to cable systems
and cable services.
(8)
Monitors all developments in the regulation of cable systems and cable services at
the state and federal level.
(9)
Receives all rate change requests, performs studies and evaluations of such requests,
and makes recommendations and serves as advisor to the mayor and to the city council
in such matters.
(10)
Coordinates and oversees the development of the government access television channels
and any institutional network (I-net) constructed pursuant to the terms of a franchise.
(11)
Acts as the representative of the city in matters pertaining to the development and
programming of the government access television channels and any institutional network
(I-net) constructed pursuant to the terms of a franchise.
(12)
Negotiates any new franchise or the renewal of any existing franchise in consultation
with the mayor and the city council.
(Ord. No. 387-11, § 1, 7-18-11; Ord. No. 151-15, § 1, 3-23-15)
The chief information officer may, as authorized under article VI of chapter 2 , promulgate such rules and regulations necessary to carry out the duties and responsibilities
set forth in section 10.5-53 .
(Ord. No. 387-11, § 1, 7-18-11)
Editor's note— Ord. No. 207-03, § 1, adopted March 24, 2003, repealed article III, sections 10.5-61—10.5-66,
in its entirety. Former article III pertained to the cable television board and derived
from Ord. No. 660-90, § 2, adopted Nov. 5, 1990; Ord. No. 628-97, § 3, adopted Sept.
22, 1997. Subsequently, Ord. No. 673-04, § 1, adopted Sept. 20, 2004, added a new
article III, section 10.5-61, to read as set out herein.
(a)
Non city-owned telecommunications facilities, as defined in section 59-2 (284) of the Denver Revised Municipal Code (DRMC) may not be installed on any city
property, unless the applicant wishing to install such facilities first demonstrates
to the satisfaction of the manager of safety that such installation will cause no
adverse effect on the emergency communications of the city, including all fire, police
and emergency medical communications.
(b)
Notwithstanding prior approval and testing of a telecommunications facility, if, at
any time, the manager of safety, or the manager's designee, finds that said facility
has caused or is causing interference with fire, police or emergency medical communications,
the manager of safety, or the manager's designee, shall have absolute right to cause
such facility to immediately cease operations and transmissions of any type.
(c)
For purposes of testing only, non city-owned telecommunications facilities may be
temporarily permitted on city property for a period of thirty (30) days; however such
temporary locations shall be allowed only for the purpose of testing for compliance
with section 10.5-61 (a), above, shall be approved by the manager of safety, and the manager of safety,
or the manager's designee, shall have absolute right to cause said facility to immediately
cease all operations and transmissions in the event of any interference with fire,
police or emergency medical communications. If necessary in order to complete the
testing required in this section, the above thirty (30) day period may be extended
in thirty (30) day increments, up to a maximum of one hundred and twenty (120) days.
(d)
Nothing in this section 10.5-61 implies that the city will in fact allow any non-city-owned telecommunications facilities
on city property.
(Ord. No. 673-04, § 1, 9-20-04)
