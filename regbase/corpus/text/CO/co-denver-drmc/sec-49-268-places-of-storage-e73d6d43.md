(a)
It shall be unlawful for any person to place or store any material or equipment necessary
for construction or demolition work on any alley, public sidewalk or other public
property except in accordance with the provisions of this article and in pursuance
of a permit issued hereunder.
(b)
It shall be unlawful for any person to perform any work on any building or structure
in such manner as to endanger persons and property on the street or public property.
(c)
Protection shall be provided as specified in this article.
(Code 1950, § 337.1)
(a)
It shall be unlawful for any person to place or store any materials or equipment used
in or required for construction or demolition work, or to erect any walkway, barricade,
canopy, railing or fence upon any public property without having first obtained a
permit in writing from the manager of transportation and infrastructure.
(b)
Where such construction or demolition is required to be done under a building permit,
the application for the building permit shall, before the issuance of the building
permit, be submitted to the manager of transportation and infrastructure for endorsement
of approval as to the placement or storage of such materials or equipment, and such
endorsement of approval shall be and constitute the permit required hereunder.
(c)
No building permit shall be issued by the building inspection division until there
shall have been obtained such permit from the manager of transportation and infrastructure.
(d)
The building inspection division shall enforce the provisions hereof with relation
to the construction and safety of walkways, barricades, canopies, railings or fences,
and prior to the erection thereof under permit of the manager of transportation and
infrastructure, plans and specifications therefor shall be filed in the building inspection
division.
(Code 1950, § 337.3; Ord. No. 39-20, § 110, 2-3-20)
(a)
Places of storage. Material or equipment required for construction or demolition work shall be stored
on the premises whenever feasible, but if such storage is not feasible, may be placed
or stored on public property in the following locations:
(1)
In the alley. In the alley adjoining the building site, provided that a clear and unobstructed
roadway of not less than ten (10) feet in width is maintained through such alley along
the building site.
(2)
Public sidewalk in front of building site. If access is not feasible from the alley adjoining the building site, then on any
portion of the public sidewalk adjoining the building site, except on the walkway
required to be maintained.
(b)
Waiver required. A waiver of claim against the city for damages on account of such placement or storage
shall be filed in the office of the manager of transportation and infrastructure before
such materials or equipment are placed or stored upon public property.
(c)
Indemnity:
(1)
Every person to whom permission has been granted by the manager of transportation
and infrastructure under the terms of this article to utilize public property for
the placement and storage of materials or equipment utilized in construction or demolition
work shall at all times assume full responsibility for such placement and storage.
(2)
Such permission shall be further conditioned that any person shall, as a consideration
for the use of public property, at all times release, hold harmless and indemnify
the city, the manager of transportation and infrastructure, the director of building
inspection and all of their agents and employees from any and all responsibility,
liability, loss or damage resulting to any person or property or caused by or incidental
to the placement or storage as aforesaid.
(3)
The placement or storage of any materials or equipment by any person upon any public
property shall be itself an acknowledgment that the conditions herein set forth are
accepted.
(4)
Under conditions of unusual hazard, the manager of transportation and infrastructure
may require liability insurance for the protection of persons and property on the
public property.
(Code 1950, § 337.2; Ord. No. 39-20, § 111, 2-3-20)
(a)
Materials or equipment used in or required for construction or demolition work shall
not be placed or stored on public property so as to obstruct free and convenient approach
to any fire hydrant, fire or police alarm box, any utility box, or to any catch basin
or manhole, or so as to interfere with the free flow of water in any street, alley
or gutter.
(b)
Every street lamp, utility box, fire or police alarm box, fire hydrant and every catch
basin and manhole that may be damaged by any work being done or by the placement or
storage of any materials and equipment upon the public property shall be protected
adequately against such damage.
(c)
This protection shall be maintained as long as is necessary and shall be completely
removed as soon as the status of the work permits.

(a)
Walkway required. A walkway not less than four (4) feet wide, with a railing on the street side shall
be maintained on the sidewalk in front of the building site during construction or
demolition work.
(b)
Type of protection required:
(1)
Protection shall be provided for pedestrians and property on the public property in
accordance with the following minimum requirements:

(2)
Such protection shall be maintained in place and kept in good order for the entire
length of time pedestrians on the public property abutting the building site may be
endangered and shall be completely removed as soon as the status of the work permits.
(c)
Railings. Railings shall be substantially built to a height not less than three (3) feet.
(d)
Fences and barricades:
(1)
Solid fences or barricades shall be substantially built to a height not less than
eight (8) feet above grade, placed on the side of the walkway nearest to the construction
or demolition work.
(2)
Fences or barricades shall extend the entire length of the building site, and each
end shall be turned and extended to the building line.
(3)
Doorways may be cut in such fences or barricades to provide ingress and egress, provided,
however, that such doorways shall be protected by sturdy doors which are kept closed
when not in use.
(e)
Protective canopies:
(1)
The protective canopy or barricade roof shall have a height of not less than eight
(8) feet above the walkway.
(2)
Such roof shall be leakproof and shall have a fence built along its entire length
on the side next to the building site.
(3)
The fence shall be solid from the sidewalk or walkway to the canopy roof, and each
end shall be turned and extended solid to the building line.
(4)
The entire structure shall be designed to carry the live and dead loads to be imposed
on it; provided, that every such structure shall be designed to carry a minimum live
load of not less than thirty-five (35) pounds per square foot, uniformly loaded.
(5)
If materials are placed or stored or work is done upon the canopy or barricade roof,
the street sides and ends of the canopy or barricade roof shall be protected by a
tight curb board not less than one (1) foot high and a railing not less than three
(3) feet high.
(6)
The space under the canopy or barricade roof over the walkway and the approaches thereto
shall be kept well lighted with artificial lighting continuously between sunset and
sunrise.
(f)
Increased protection. The manager of transportation and infrastructure may, as a condition of the granting
of any permit for the erection of walkways, barricades, canopies, railings or fences,
require heights, widths, distances or specifications greater than those provided in
this section, if reasonably necessary for the protection of the public in the use
of public ways and places.
(Code 1950, § 337.6; Ord. No. 39-20, § 112, 2-3-20)
(a)
When the area or portion thereof occupied by a public sidewalk is to be excavated,
the holder of the building permit shall construct a substantial temporary walkway
of a width to be prescribed by the manager of transportation and infrastructure, but
not less than four (4) feet wide, for pedestrian travel over or around the areas to
be excavated.
(b)
The walkway over the excavated area shall be designed for a uniform live load of one
hundred fifty (150) pounds per square foot.
(c)
The walkway shall be provided with suitable ramps or stairs at each end with a handrail
not less than three (3) feet high along each side or with a railing on one (1) side
and a fence or barricade on the other, as the case may require.
(d)
The walkway around the excavated area shall be as close to the excavation on the side
street as possible and constructed with a railing not less than three (3) feet high
and a fence or barricade on the excavation side of the walkway.
(Code 1950, § 337.7; Ord. No. 39-20, § 113, 2-3-20)
Before any permit shall be issued for the construction of any building or accessory
structure or work incidental thereto involving excavation extending within one (1)
foot of the angle of repose or natural slope of the soil under any public sidewalk,
street, alley or other public property, the owner of the property whereon the proposed
construction is located shall submit to the manager of transportation and infrastructure
an indemnity bond, written by a licensed surety company, in an amount approved by
the manager and in a form approved by the city attorney, indemnifying the city against
any loss or damage to public property, such as paving, curbs, sewers, utilities, sidewalks,
etc., together with evidence of bodily injury and property damage, public liability
insurance naming the city as a coinsured in the limits and form approved by the manager
of transportation and infrastructure and the city attorney.
(Code 1950, § 337.9; Ord. No. 39-20, § 114, 2-3-20)
