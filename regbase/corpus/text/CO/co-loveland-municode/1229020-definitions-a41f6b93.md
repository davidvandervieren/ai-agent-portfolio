The City Council finds:
A.
The primary purpose of roadway medians is to enhance the safety and free flow of traffic
on roadways by separating opposing lanes of vehicular traffic, channeling traffic
for turning purposes, calming traffic, and, in some cases, providing a temporary refuse
for pedestrians crossing busy and/or wide roadways.
B.
Some persons are occupying medians that are not safe for pedestrian use and such use
can cause danger to pedestrians and motorists.
C.
Beginning in fall 2021, the City of Loveland's Traffic Operations Division began a
Citywide Roadway Safety Study "Roadmap to Safety (August 2022)," that among other
things, utilized a data-driven process to review, evaluate, and analyze roadway safety
information. The report provides guidance for identifying locations that are unsafe
for pedestrian use.
D.
City Council finds that prohibiting pedestrian access to the medians identified in
this ordinance as unsafe for pedestrian use is the least restrictive means possible
to further the significant public interest of protecting the public health, safety
and welfare.
(Ord. No. 6663 , § 1, 10-31-2023)
The following words, terms and phrases, when used in this chapter shall have the meanings
ascribed to them in this section, except where the context clearly indicates a different
meaning:
Center median island means the raised area between two opposite directions of a divided roadway, measured
from the face of the curb on each side of the raised median.
Stop bar means a 12 to 24-inch-wide solid white line extending across intersection approach
lanes that identifies the location where vehicles should stop at an intersection.
Within 200 feet of the intersection is measured from the stop bar of that approach.
(Ord. No. 6663 , § 1, 10-31-2023)

B.
Exceptions. The prohibition set forth above shall not apply to the following:
1.
Pedestrians utilizing a center median island as a refuge for one traffic signal cycle
because they were not able to safely cross the entire roadway during the traffic signal
pedestrian phase (or green light).
2.
On-duty public safety personnel engaged in the performance of their official duties.
3.
State or city staff or contractors, or utility company staff or contractors, authorized
by the state or city to access the center median island, performing construction or
maintenance activities.
C.
Administrative Review of Intersections. In addition to the intersections identified in subsection A above, the City of Loveland
Traffic Operations Division will reevaluate crash statistics on an annual basis to
determine if additional intersections should be prohibited for pedestrian access.
Once a year, the Traffic Operations Division may propose an updated list of intersections
for Council approval by ordinance. Should Council approve additional intersections
for subsection A, the City shall publish an updated list of intersection locations
available to the public in both written and electronic format, as well as clearly
mark the additional intersection(s) with signage indicating that pedestrian access
is prohibited.
( Ord. No. 6780 , § 1, 7-15-2025; Ord. No. 6663 , § 1, 10-31-2023)
Violations of this chapter shall be subject to penalties as set forth in Section 1.12.010 .
(Ord. No. 6663 , § 1, 10-31-2023)
