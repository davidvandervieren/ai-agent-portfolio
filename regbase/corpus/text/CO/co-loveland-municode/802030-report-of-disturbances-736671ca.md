Nothing in this Title 8 is intended to and shall not be interpreted or applied so as to create any impermissible
conflict with federal or state law or the rules and regulations promulgated therefrom,
and to the extent a conflict exists, the federal or state laws, rules, and regulations
shall govern. Notwithstanding the foregoing, if federal law, state law, and the rules
and regulations of the state licensing authority are silent on a matter addressed
in this Title 8 , then this Title 8 shall govern.

A.
When used in this Title 8 , the following words and phrases shall have the meanings ascribed to them in this
section:
1.
Administrative application means an application for a local alcohol, tobacco or marijuana license or permit
that may be granted or denied administratively by the City Clerk.
2.
Alcohol Beverage means any fermented malt beverage, hard cider, and malt, vinous, or spiritous liquors.
"Alcohol beverage" does not include confectionery containing alcohol within the limits
prescribed by C.R.S. § 25-5-410(1)(i)(II), as amended.
3.
Applicant means any person or entity who is applying for or has applied for a license or permit
to sell, dispense, or serve pursuant to this title but is not yet licensed by the
local licensing authority. If the applicant is an entity and not a natural person,
"applicant" shall include all persons who are the members, managers, officers, directors
and shareholders of such entity.
4.
Authority means the Local Licensing Authority created and established by section 8.04.010 of this Title which presides over all matters involving the licensing of the sale,
distribution, manufacture, and consumption of alcohol beverages, of tobacco products
and marijuana pursuant to the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado Beer and Wine Code,
the Colorado Special Event Liquor Permits Code, the Colorado Tobacco Code, and the
Colorado Marijuana Code.
5.
Cigar means any roll of tobacco other than a Cigarette wrapped entirely or in part in tobacco
or any other substance containing tobacco. For purposes of this title, cigar includes,
but is not limited to, tobacco products known or labeled as "cigar," "cigarillo,"
or "little cigar."
6.
Cigarette means any product that contains tobacco or nicotine, that is intended to be burned
or heated under ordinary conditions of use, and consists of or contains:
a.
Any roll of tobacco wrapped in paper or in any substance not containing tobacco; or
b.
Tobacco in any form that is functional in the product, which, because of its appearance,
the type of tobacco used in the filler, or its packaging or labeling, is likely to
be offered to, or purchased by consumers as a cigarette; or
c.
Any roll of tobacco wrapped in any substance containing tobacco that, because of its
appearance, the type of tobacco used in the filler, or its packaging and labeling,
is likely to be offered to, or purchased by, consumers as a cigarette described in
subparagraph a. above; and
d.
The term includes all "roll-your-own," i.e., any tobacco that, because of its appearance,
type, packaging, or labeling, is suitable for use and likely to be offered to, or
purchased by consumers as tobacco for making cigarettes.
7.
Colorado Beer and Wine Code means the laws set forth in Article 4 of Title 44, C.R.S., as amended and any rules
or regulations promulgated thereunder.
8.
Colorado Liquor Code means the laws set forth in Article 3 of Title 44, C.R.S., as amended and any rules
or regulations promulgated thereunder.
9.
Colorado Marijuana Code means the laws set forth in Article 10 of Title 44, C.R.S., as amended and any rules
or regulations promulgated thereunder.
10.
Colorado Special Event Liquor Permits Code means the laws set forth in Article 5 of Title 44, C.R.S., as amended and any rules
or regulations promulgated thereunder.
11.
Colorado Tobacco Code means the laws set forth in Article 7 of Title 44, C.R.S., as amended and any rules
or regulations promulgated thereunder.
12.
Cultivation or cultivate means the process by which a person grows a marijuana plant.
13.
Department means the City of Loveland Police Department or any agency or person designated by
the City Manager to enforce or administer the provisions of this title.
14.
Electronic Smoking Device means any device that may be used to deliver any aerosolized or vaporized substance
to the person inhaling from the device, including, but not limited to, an e-cigarette,
e-cigar, e-pipe, vape pen, or e-hookah. "Electronic smoking device" includes any component,
part, or accessory of the device, and also includes any substance that may be aerosolized
or vaporized by such device, whether or not the substance contains nicotine or marijuana.
Electronic smoking device does not include drugs, devices, or combination products
authorized for sale by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, as those terms are defined
in the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.
15.
Fermented malt beverage means malt liquors, when purchased by a fermented malt beverage retailer from a wholesaler
licensed pursuant to the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado Beer and Wine Code, the
Colorado Special Event Liquor Permits Code, and this Title 8 ; or when sold by a fermented malt beverage retailer to consumers or to persons licensed
under the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado Beer and Wine Code, the Colorado Special
Event Liquor Permits Code, and this Title 8 . "Fermented malt beverage" does not include confectionery containing alcohol within
the limits prescribed by C.R.S. § 25-5-410(1)(i)(II), as amended.
16.
Financial interest means any ownership interest defined in the Colorado Marijuana Code.
17.
Good cause, for the purpose of granting or denying an application for initial licensure, renewal,
or any other application pertaining to a license or permit issued pursuant to this Title 8 , includes, but is not limited to, the following meanings:
a.
The information presented in the application is incomplete, inaccurate, or false;
b.
The applicant seeks a license at a location that is not properly appropriately zoned
for the use or is otherwise prohibited by local ordinance, State statute, or any regulation
promulgated thereof;
c.
The applicant seeks a license while the applicant's license is suspended or revoked;
d.
The licensee or applicant has violated, does not meet, or has failed to comply with
any of the terms, conditions, or provisions of this Title 8 or any rules promulgated pursuant to this Title 8 ;
e.
The licensee or applicant has failed to comply with any special terms or conditions
that were placed on its license in prior disciplinary proceedings or arose in the
context of potential disciplinary proceedings;
f.
In the case of a new alcohol beverage license, the applicant has not established the
reasonable requirements of the neighborhood or the desires of its adult inhabitants
as provided in C.R.S. § 44-3-301(2), as amended;
g.
Evidence that the licensed premises have been operated in a manner that adversely
affects the public health, welfare, or safety of the immediate neighborhood in which
the establishment is located, which evidence must include a continuing pattern of
fights, violent activity, or disorderly conduct. For purposes of this subsection (14)(d),
"disorderly conduct" has the meaning as provided for in C.R.S. § 18-9-106, as amended,
and Chapter 9.32 of this Code;
h.
The applicant and/or retail location is not in compliance with all local, state, and
federal laws;
i.
The applicant is indebted to, or obligated in any manner, to the City for unpaid taxes,
liens, or other moneys as provided by the various City Departments (notwithstanding
section 3.16.230 of this Code, the Secretary and/or Authority shall receive sufficient information
from the City's Finance Department);
j.
The applicant or an officer, director, owner, agent, employee, or any person having
a direct or indirect financial interest in the business are not of good moral character;
or
k.
The full amount of any applicable fee associated with the application for license
or renewal of license sought does not accompany such application.
18.
Good moral character means an individual who has a personal history demonstrating honesty, fairness, and
respect for the rights of others and for conformance to the law which may include
considerations of whether an individual has:
a.
Ever had a professional license denied, suspended, or revoked;
b.
Ever had a business license denied, suspended, or revoked;
c.
Ever surrendered, been denied, or had any type of marijuana related business license
placed on an administrative hold, suspended or revoked;
d.
Ever been denied any type of alcohol beverage-, tobacco product-, or marijuana-related
business license;
e.
Ever had a business temporarily or permanently closed for failure to comply with any
tax, health, building, fire, zoning or safety law;
f.
Ever had an administrative, civil or criminal finding of delinquency for failure to
file or failure to pay sales or use taxes or any other taxes;
g.
Ever been convicted of a crime of moral turpitude; or
h.
Been convicted of a felony in the three years immediately preceding his or her application
date or who is currently subject to a sentence for a felony conviction.

Hard Cider means an alcohol beverage containing at least one-half of one percent and less than
seven percent alcohol by volume that is made by fermentation of the natural juice

cider" shall in all respects be treated as a vinous liquor except where expressly
provided otherwise.

marijuana concentrate. "Marijuana" includes items designated "marijuana products,"

include industrial hemp, nor does it include fiber produced from the stalks, oil,

Marijuana product means concentrated marijuana products and marijuana products that are comprised of
marijuana and other ingredients that are intended for use or consumption, whether

Any product containing, made of, or derived from tobacco or nicotine that is intended

Any electronic smoking device and any substances that may be aerosolized or vaporized

A.
Any applicant for an alcohol beverage, tobacco product, or marijuana business license
whose building is not yet in existence or has not yet received a certificate of occupancy
issued by the City for the proposed licensed premises at the time of the City's initial
approval of the license application shall have 12 months immediately following the
date of the City's initial approval to obtain the necessary local and state permits
and licenses, obtain a certificate of occupancy issued by the City, and to commence
business operations.
B.
Whenever it appears the applicant shall not be ready to commence business operations
within the prescribed 12-month period, the applicant may file an affidavit of due
diligence with the local licensing authority, within 30 days prior to the end of the
12-month period, requesting an extension of time to complete any necessary construction
on the premises, obtain any other necessary permits and licenses, obtain the certificate
of occupancy, and commence business operations. In the affidavit of due diligence,
the applicant must demonstrate to the Authority the progress being made toward the
completion of the building; the due diligence in obtaining all necessary licenses
and permits; complying with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations; and any other
reasons why the establishment has not opened for business. The affidavit of due diligence
must be accompanied by an application and an extension fee, on a form and in an amount
established by the Authority, which fee shall not be refundable.
1.
The Authority shall consider the applicant's affidavit of due diligence. If the Authority
is satisfied the applicant is diligently making progress toward the completion of
the building, obtaining the necessary permits and licenses, and obtaining a certificate
of occupancy and will be reasonably likely to commence business operations within
an additional 12-month time period, the Authority may extend the initial approval
of the application for an additional period not to exceed 12 months. No more than
one extension shall be granted under this subsection to a maximum of two years from
the initial approval date.
2.
If the Authority is not satisfied the applicant is diligently making progress toward
the completion of the building, obtaining the necessary permits and licenses, or obtaining
a certificate of occupancy or is not reasonably likely to allow the applicant to commence
business operations within 12 months, the Authority shall not grant any extension
of time to approve the application.

A.
The remedies provided by this Title 8 are cumulative and in addition to any other remedies available at law or in equity.
In addition to other remedies provided by this Chapter or by other law, any violation
of this Chapter may be remedied by a civil action brought by the Office of the City
Attorney, including, but not limited to, nuisance abatement proceedings and injunctive
relief.
B.
In addition to any other penalty authorized by law, if the Authority determines based
on a preponderance of the evidence that a licensee or any of the licensee's officers,
agents, or employees has violated any law or ordinance relating to the sale of alcohol
beverages, tobacco products, or marijuana to persons under the age of 21 or pleaded
guilty, "no contest," or its equivalent, or otherwise admitted to a violation of the
same, the Authority shall impose the following sanctions upon the licensee:
1.
One violation within 12 months: a civil penalty of a minimum of $1,000.00.
2.
Two violations within 12 months: a civil penalty of a minimum of $2,000.00.
3.
Three violations within 12 months: a minimum 30-day suspension of the license.
C.
In addition to the possible denial, suspension, revocation, or nonrenewal of a license
under the provisions of this title, any person, including, but not limited to, any
licensee, manager, or employee of a licensee, or any customer of such business, who
violates any of the provisions of this title, shall be guilty of a misdemeanor punishable
in accordance with Section 1.12.010 (A) of this Loveland Municipal Code.
D.
A licensee under this Title 8 shall be vicariously liable for the conduct of the licensee's employees, managers,
agents, or members underlying any violations of this Title 8, the Colorado Beer and
Wine Code, the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado Special Event Liquor Permit Code,
the Colorado Tobacco Code, and the Colorado Marijuana Code, and any rules and regulations
promulgated thereunder.

premises modifications, and any other appropriate application administered under this Title 8 .
1.
Notwithstanding the provisions of Section 16 of Article XVIII of the Colorado Constitution,
the Authority shall not act upon any application for local licensing of a retail marijuana
business in circumstances where the State failed to issue the applicant an annual
license within 90 days after its receipt of such application.
C.
The Authority shall have the power to require any applicant or licensee to furnish
any relevant information required by the Authority. The Authority shall, through the
Secretary, have the power to administer oaths and issue subpoenas to require the presence
of persons and the production of papers, books, and records necessary to the determination
of any matter or hearing which the Authority is authorized to conduct. Any such subpoena
shall be served according to Rule 217 of the Colorado Municipal Court Rules of Procedure.
It is unlawful for any person to fail to comply with any subpoena issued by the secretary
on behalf of the local licensing authority.
D.
All local fees permitted or required by the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado Beer
and Wine Code, the Colorado Special Event Liquor Permits Code, the Colorado Tobacco
Code, and the Colorado Marijuana Code shall be set by the Authority and published
by the Secretary.

The Authority shall have the power to issue optional premises licenses and optional
premises for hotel and restaurant licenses pursuant to C.R.S. § 44-3-310, as amended,
and the provisions of this Chapter 8.06 . The provisions of this chapter shall be considered in addition to all other standards
applicable to the issuance of licenses under C.R.S. § 44-3-310 for optional premises
license or for optional premises for a hotel and restaurant license. These two types
of licenses for optional premises shall collectively be referred to as "optional premises"
unless otherwise specified.

Eligible facilities:
1.
An optional premises may only be approved when that premises is located on or adjacent
to an outdoor sports and recreational facility as defined in C.R.S. § 44-3-103, as

2.
There shall be no minimum size requirement for the outdoor sports and recreational

facility in relationship to the number of optional premises requested for the facility.

( Ord. No. 6804 , § 10, 12-16-2025)
A.
The Authority is authorized to issue alcohol beverage tasting permits in accordance
with the requirements of this chapter and C.R.S. § 44-3-301(10), as amended.
B.
Tastings on the licensed premises of a retail liquor store licensee, a liquor-licensed
drugstore licensee, or a fermented malt beverage and wine retail license, hereinafter
referred to as "off-premise retailers", are authorized to be conducted within the
City in accordance with C.R.S. § 44-3-301(10), as amended, and subject to the provisions
of this title.
C.
Off-premises retailers desiring to conduct tastings shall submit a tasting permit
application to the secretary accompanied by the required application fee.
1.
The Authority shall establish the procedures for obtaining a tasting permit, which
procedures shall include, without limitation, conducting a noticed public hearing
before the Authority at which hearing the applicant must establish that the applicant
is able to conduct tastings without violating the provisions of this chapter. The
Authority may deny the application if it finds that the applicant has not established
the ability to conduct tastings in accordance with the provisions of this chapter
or if the Authority finds that the proposed tastings would create a public safety
risk to the neighborhood. The notice required for the public hearing shall be the
posted and published notices required by C.R.S. § 44-3-311, as amended.
2.
The forms for the tasting permit application, the renewal application, and the tasting
permit shall be those proscribed by the Authority. These forms shall include, without
limitation, a schedule of the proposed, and in the case of the permit the approved,
schedule of dates and times of the tastings to be conducted on the licensed premises.
The licensee may deviate from the approved schedule provided that: (1) the licensee
gives the secretary seven days prior written notice of such deviation; and (2) such
deviation does not violate any provision of this Chapter. An applicant for a tasting
permit must also include with the filing of the initial and any renewal application,
and keep current with the secretary at all times, written proof that the licensee
and each employee of the licensee who will be conducting the tastings has completed
a server training program for tastings that meets the standards required by state
law.
3.
Renewal of tasting permits shall be concurrent with the renewal of licenses for off-premises
retailers. A licensee's initial tasting permit shall expire on the same date as the
date that the licensee's off-premises retailer license expires. The initial tasting
permit application fee shall not be prorated if the permit expires in less than a
year. Tasting permit renewal forms shall be submitted to the secretary accompanied
by a renewal fee.
4.
Tasting permits shall be conspicuously and prominently posted by the licensee on the
licensed premises at all times during business hours.
5.
A tasting permit shall only be issued to an off-premises retailer licensee whose license
is valid, not subject to a current or pending enforcement action by the City or the
State, and in full force and effect.
D.
Tastings within the City shall be subject to the following limitations:
1.
Tastings shall be conducted only by a person who has completed a responsible alcohol
beverage vendor training program compliant with and approved pursuant to C.R.S. §
44-3-1001 et seq., as amended, and 1 CCR 203-2:47-605, as amended;
2.
An off-premises retailer may conduct a tasting of alcohol beverages from the off-premises
retail licensee's existing inventory, and the alcohol beverages used in tastings must
be purchased through a licensed wholesaler, licensed brew pub, licensed distillery
pub, or winery licensed pursuant to C.R.S. § 44-3-403, as amended, at a cost that
is not less than the laid-in cost of the alcohol beverage;
3.
The size of an individual alcohol beverage sample provided shall not exceed one ounce
of malt or vinous liquor or one-half of one ounce of spirituous liquor;
4.
Tastings shall not exceed a total of five hours in duration per day, which need not
be consecutive;
5.
The licensee may conduct tastings only during the operating hours in which the licensee
on whose premises the tastings occur is permitted to sell alcohol beverages, and in
no case earlier than 10:00 a.m. or later than 9:00 p.m.;
6.
The licensee shall prohibit patrons from leaving the licensed premises with an unconsumed
sample;
7.
The licensee shall promptly remove all open and unconsumed alcohol beverage samples
from the licensed premises, destroy the samples immediately following the completion
of the tastings, or store any open containers of unconsumed alcohol beverages in a
secure area outside the sales area of the licensed premises for use at a tasting conducted
at a later time or date;
8.
The licensee shall not serve a person who is under 21 years of age or who is visibly
intoxicated;
9.
The licensee shall not serve more than four individual samples to a patron during
a tasting. The total amount of alcohol beverages to be samples at a tasting shall
be limited to, regardless of the number of items being tasted not more than four ounces
of malt liquor, four ounces of vinous liquor and two ounces of spirituous liquor per
customer per day;
10.
Alcohol samples shall be in open containers and shall be provided to a patron free
of charge;
11.
The licensee may conduct tastings on no more than 156 days per year;
12.
The licensee shall maintain on the licensed premises a log of all tastings on forms
proscribed by the Authority to be submitted to the secretary each year with the tasting
permit renewal application and at all business hours the log shall be subject to inspection
by City and state officials authorized to enforce the Colorado Liquor Code, the Colorado
Beer and Wine Code, the Loveland Municipal Code and/or associated regulations; and
13.
No manufacturer of spirituous or vinous liquor shall induce a licensee, through free
goods or financial or in-kind assistance, to favor the manufacturer's products being
sampled at a tasting, and the licensee shall bear the financial cost and all other
responsibilities for a tasting.
E.
Violations:
1.
It shall be unlawful for any person to conduct tastings within the city without having
first received a tasting permit issued in accordance with this chapter.
2.
A violation of a limitation specified in section 8.06.030.D of this Chapter or in
C.R.S. § 44-3-301(10), as amended, by an off-premises retailer, whether by the licensee's
employees, agents, or otherwise, shall be the responsibility of the off-premises retailer
who is conducting the tasting.
3.
Off-premises retailers conducting a tasting shall be subject to the same revocation,
suspension, and enforcement provisions as otherwise apply to those licensees.
F.
Nothing in this Chapter shall affect the ability of a Colorado winery licensed pursuant
to C.R.S. § 44-3-402 or C.R.S. § 44-3-403, as amended, to conduct a tasting pursuant
to the authority of C.R.S. § 44-3-402(2) or C.R.S. § 44-3-403(2)(e), as amended.

A.
To the extent the State adopts in the future any additional or stricter law or regulation
governing the sale or distribution of retail or medical marijuana, the additional
or stricter regulation shall control the establishment or operation of any marijuana
business in the City. Compliance with any applicable State law or regulation shall
be deemed an additional requirement for issuance or denial of any license under this
Article, and noncompliance with any applicable state law or regulation shall be grounds
for revocation or suspension of any license issued hereunder.
B.
Any licensee may be required to demonstrate, upon demand by the City Manager, Authority,
or by law enforcement officers, that the source and quantity of any marijuana found
upon the licensed premises is in full compliance with all applicable state and local
laws, ordinances, and regulations.
C.
If the State prohibits the sale or other distribution of marijuana through retail
or medical marijuana businesses, any license issued hereunder shall be deemed immediately
revoked by operation of law.
D.
The issuance of any license pursuant to this Article shall not be deemed to create
an exception, defense or immunity for any person in regard to any potential criminal
liability the person may have under federal law for the cultivation, possession, sale,
distribution or use of marijuana.
