Editor's note— It is the intent of the City Council in adopting Ordinance 671, 2006, to transfer
the administrative functions of the Police Commissioner to the City Administrator.
Following adoption of Ordinance 671, 2006, any and all other references to the Police
Commissioner in this Code shall be interpreted such that the City Administrator shall
fulfill any duties of the Police Commissioner. (Ord. 671 §4, 2006)
(a)
The Police Department shall be operated and managed in accordance with such departmental
policies, rules and regulations as may from time to time be prepared by the Chief
of Police for consideration and adoption by the City Manager.
(b)
Quarterly, the Chief of Police shall provide a summary report to the City Manager,
for submission to the City Council, of any changes made to departmental policies,
rules and regulations.
(c)
The policies, rules and regulations shall be made available in written or electronic
form to all members of the Police Department, who shall be instructed in their meaning.
(Prior code 2.12.030; Ord. 256, §1(a), 1981; Ord. 770 §1, 2012; Ord. 911 §2, 2021)
It shall be the duty of the Chief of Police to do the following:
(1)
The Chief of Police shall see that the ordinances of the City are duly enforced and
the rules and regulations of the Police Department obeyed, and perform such duties
as may be required by the City Council;
(2)
The Chief of Police shall direct the operations of the Police Department, subject
to the rules and regulations thereof;
(3)
The Chief of Police shall apprehend any person violating any of the City ordinances
and take such violator before the Municipal Court for trial;
(4)
The Chief of Police shall take charge of the detention facility, all prisoners confined
therein and all those who are sentenced to labor on the streets or public works of
the City, and see that orders and sentences with reference to prisoners are fully
executed and complied with;
(5)
The Chief of Police shall render such accounts of the Police Department, his or her
duties and receipts as may required by the City Council, and keep the public records
of his or her office open to inspection by the City Council at any reasonable time;
(6)
Before entering upon the duties of office, the Chief of Police shall take and subscribe
to a verified oath that he or she will support the Constitution and laws of the State,
Constitution of the United States and ordinances of the City, and that he or she will
faithfully perform the duties of the office upon which he or she is about to enter;
(7)
The Chief of Police shall communicate all police matters to the City Manager, who
shall serve as the conduit for such matters to the City Council.
(8)
The Chief of Police shall prepare policies, rules and regulations to govern and guide
the activities of the Police Department and submit the same to the City Manager for
review and approval.
(Prior code 2.12.040; Ord. 374 §1, 1990; Ord. 671 §3, 2006; Ord. 770 §2, 2012; Ord. 911 §3, 2021)
(a)
The Police Advisory Board shall be responsible for:
(1)
Reviewing, referring for investigation and making recommendations regarding complaints
and questions concerning the Police Department, its operations and personnel.
(2)
Keeping in strict confidence all reports, testimony or other evidence received or
reviewed by the Police Advisory Board to protect the privacy and reputation of all
parties concerned with the investigations.
(3)
Instilling confidence in officers of the Police Department and the public that: all
investigations will be thorough, timely and fair; the rights of all accused officers
will be protected; and no false reports will be tolerated.
(b)
All complaints made by the public relative to the Police Department shall be handled
by the Police Advisory Board according to the following procedures:
(1)
The complainant shall prepare in writing a description of the complaint, signed and
dated, with reasonable detail of names, dates, times and places, and other facts relevant
to the complainant.
(2)
The complaint shall be mailed or delivered to the Police Advisory Board, c/o the City
Clerk.
(3)
The City Clerk shall, as soon as possible, number, record and transmit the complaint
to the Police Advisory Board. The Chief of Police shall be given a copy of the complaint
and shall provide the Police Advisory Board with such assistance as requested by the
Police Advisory Board.
(4)
The Police Advisory Board shall be called into session promptly to review the complaint
and refer the same for investigation either by the Police Department or by an outside
agency. The Police Advisory Board shall notify the complainant that it has received
his or her complaint and advise that the complaint will be investigated pursuant to
this Section. All charges believed by the Police Advisory Board to be of a criminal
nature shall be referred to the District Attorney for investigation.
(5)
If it is determined that the investigation should be handled by the Police Department,
the complaint shall be forwarded to the Chief of Police and investigated according
to the Department's policies and procedures. Upon completion of the investigation,
the Police Department shall transmit its findings to the Police Advisory Board for
review. If the Board is not satisfied with the manner in which the investigation was
conducted by the Police Department or if additional information is desired, the Police
Advisory Board may either return the complaint to the Chief of Police for further
investigation or refer the complaint to an outside agency for investigation.
(6)
If it is determined the investigation should be conducted by an outside agency, the
City Manager or the Manager's designee shall facilitate transmission of the complaint
to the outside agency and shall ensure that the outside agency has access to any City
employee the outside agency requests to interview. Upon completion of its investigation,
the outside agency shall forward its findings to the Police Advisory Board for review.
If the Police Advisory Board is not satisfied with the manner in which the investigation
was conducted or if additional information is deemed necessary, the Police Advisory
Board may return the complaint to the outside agency for further investigation.
(7)
In all cases where a complaint is filed concerning any police officer, including the
Chief of Police, the officer shall be given written notice of the charges against
him or her and an explanation of how the complaint will be investigated. The written
notice to the officer shall also contain an explanation of the possible penalties
that could accrue to the officer if the charges in the complaint are sustained.
(8)
Upon completion of the investigation to the satisfaction of the Police Advisory Board,
the Police Advisory Board shall be called into session to review the results and determine
whether each allegation shall be classified as "Sustained" or "Not Sustained," and
if "Sustained," the Police Advisory Board shall recommend one (1) of the following:
"Verbal Reprimand," "Written Reprimand," "Suspension Without Pay," "Demotion" or "Termination."
The Police Advisory Board's written findings and recommendation shall be provided
to the Chief of Police, who shall consider the same and take such action as he or
she deems appropriate within fifteen (15) days. If the Chief of Police was the subject
of the complaint, the Police Advisory Board's written findings and recommendation
shall be provided to the City Manager, who shall consider the same and within fifteen
(15) days recommend to the City Council such action as he or she deems appropriate.
Any employment action taken as a result of a complaint reviewed and investigated pursuant
to this Section shall conform with all requirements of the City's personnel policies,
and neither the Chief of Police nor the City Council, as the case may be, shall be
bound by any recommendation made by the Police Advisory Board.
(9)
After the Police Advisory Board has forwarded its recommendation to the Chief of Police
or City Manager, as the case may be, the Police Advisory Board shall notify the complainant
that an investigation of his or her complaint was conducted and reviewed by the Police
Advisory Board pursuant to this Section. The complainant shall not be advised of the
outcome of the investigation, any recommendations made by the Police Advisory Board
or any disciplinary action taken.
(c)
All findings and recommendations of the Police Advisory Board must be approved by
a majority of the full membership of the Police Advisory Board.
(d)
A quorum must be present to conduct any business of the Police Advisory Board. A quorum
shall be constituted by three (3) members of the Police Advisory Board.
(e)
Any member of the Police Advisory Board shall be removed from the Police Advisory
Board by the City Council for: failure to attend three (3) consecutive sessions of
the Police Advisory Board; or violation of the confidentiality provisions contained
in this Section.

(a)
All duly empowered members of the Police Department shall perform all duties required
under the law, including the laws of the State and the City ordinances, including
Section 31-15-401, C.R.S.
(b)
Police officers shall also obey the Police Department rules and regulations as promulgated
by the Chief of Police and the City Council.
(c)
In addition to the provision of Subsections (a) and (b) of this Section, police officers
shall be empowered to, and shall, do the following:
(1)
Perform all duties required of police officers;
(2)
Suppress all riots, disturbances and breaches of the peace, apprehend all disorderly
persons in the City, and pursue and arrest any person fleeing from justice in any
part of the State;
(3)
Be the enforcement officers of the City and see that the provisions of City ordinances
and the laws of the State are complied with. They shall arrest without process all
persons engaged in the violation in their presence of any provision of City ordinances
or the laws of the State. Upon such arrest, they shall forthwith convey the offenders
before the proper officer to be dealt with according to law; provided that they may
incarcerate any person whom they arrest at a late and unusual hour of the night until
the following morning; and provided further that, in the special cases relating to
traffic offenses, they may release an arrested person upon his or her written promise
to appear in court;
(4)
Execute and return all writs and process to them directed by the Municipal Judge in
any case arising under a City ordinance, and may serve the writs in any part of the
county in which the City is situated;
(5)
Observe the condition of the streets, sidewalks and alleys of the City, and of any
obstruction, nuisance or impediment therein, and take necessary measures to remove
or abate the same;
(6)
Before entering upon the duties of his office, shall take and subscribe a verified
oath that he or she will support the Constitution and laws of the State of Colorado,
the Constitution of the United States, and the ordinances of the City, and that he
or she will faithfully perform the duties of the office upon which he or she is about
to enter.

(a)
There is created the position of community resource officer.
(b)
Community resource officers shall have the authority and responsibility to enforce,
under the supervision and direction of the Chief of Police any violation of this Code,
except for the following:
(1)
Chapter 8 , Article 1 (Model Traffic Code).
(2)
Chapter 10 , Articles 2 - 8 (certain General Offenses).
For violations of ordinances set forth in Chapter 18 , Building Regulations, or any codes adopted by reference therein, community resource
officers shall have concurrent authority with the Building Official for those violations
occurring outside buildings and other structures.
(c)
In addition to the responsibilities set forth in Subsection (b) of this Section, community
resource officers shall perform such other duties as required by the Chief of Police.
In no event shall any such duties include any activities related to law enforcement
except as specifically authorized by this Section.
(d)
Nothing in this Section shall deprive any police officer of the power to enforce any
provision of this Code or the Building Official to enforce violations of Chapter 18 of this Code.
(e)
Community resource officers shall not have arrest power and shall not be authorized
to carry a firearm.
(f)
Solely for the purpose of the issuance of a summons and complaint or a penalty assessment
notice for offenses within the scope of a community resource officer's enforcement
responsibilities pursuant to Subsection (b) of this Section, and for no other purpose,
community resource officers shall be deemed "peace officers" within the meaning of
the Colorado Municipal Court Rules of Procedure.
(g)
It is unlawful for any person, by using or threatening to use violence, force, physical
interference, or obstacle, to obstruct, impair, or hinder a community resource officer
or peace officer in the discharge of his or her duties herein prescribed. It is no
defense to a prosecution for any violation of this Code that the community resource
officer or peace officer was acting in an illegal manner, if the officer was acting
in the regular course of his or her assigned duties and in good faith based upon surrounding
facts and circumstances.
(Ord. 491 §1, 1997; Ord. No. 820 , § 1, 9-26-2016)
