Editor's note— Ord. 992 §21, adopted Sept. 8, 2025, repealed the former Art. 7, §§ 6-180—6-195, and enacted
a new Art. 7 as set out herein. The former Art. 7 pertained to peddlers, solicitors
and transient merchants and derived from Ord. 405 §1, adopted 1990.
This Article is intended to balance the First Amendment rights of solicitors in the
City with the privacy, safety, health, and welfare, of the City residents by: (1)
requiring solicitors to obtain a license issued by the City; and (2) prohibiting solicitations
at premises where a no solicitation or similar sign has been posted.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
For purposes of this Article, the following definitions shall apply:
City Clerk means the City Clerk or the Clerk's designee.
Hearing Officer means the City Manager or a person appointed by the City Manager to conduct hearings
under this Article. The Hearing Officer may not have personally determined, in the
first instance, the factual issues in controversy and may have no personal or financial
interest in the outcome of the hearing.
Property crimes mean the crimes of theft, burglary, breaking and entering, larceny, and other similar
felonies by whatever name.
Solicitation means to enter or remain upon any private property in the City, not having been requested
or invited by the occupants thereof, for the purpose of contacting said occupants
to solicit the immediate or future purchase or sale of goods, services, or any other
thing of value.
Solicitor means any person, whether as an owner, agent, independent contractor, or employee,
who engages in solicitation.
Violent crimes mean the crimes of homicide, attempted homicide, rape, attempted rape, sexual assault,
assault, battery, and other similar felonies by whatever name.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
It shall be unlawful for any person engaged in solicitation in the City to call on
or attempt to contact any person, by knocking, ringing the doorbell, or any other
method, at a premises displaying a "No Solicitation" sign, a "No Trespassing" sign
or any other sign or notice which conveys said message with reasonable clarity. This
provision shall apply to all solicitations, including without limitation, those conducted
by organizations eligible for tax exemption under Section 501(c) of the Internal Revenue
Code.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
No person shall engage in solicitation in the City without first obtaining a solicitor
license as provided in this Article.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
(a)
Application. Each applicant for a solicitor license shall file with the City Clerk an application
including the following:
(1)
The full name, residence address, mailing address, and telephone number of the applicant;
(2)
A physical description of the applicant, including height, weight, eye color, and
hair color;
(3)
If employed, the name and address of the employer and credentials establishing the
employment relationship;
(4)
A brief explanation of the nature of the merchandise or services to be sold;
(5)
The number and state of issuance of any state-issued identification card issued to
applicant, including a state motor vehicle operator's license;
(6)
A recent photograph of the applicant, which photograph shall be approximately two
(2) inches by two (2) inches, showing the head and shoulders of the applicant in a
clear and distinguishable manner;
(7)
A statement as to whether the applicant has been convicted of any property crime or
violent crime within the preceding five (5) years or any crime while engaged in solicitation
in the City;
(8)
Authorization for the City Clerk to conduct a background check of the applicant;
(9)
An application fee established by resolution of the City Council; and
(10)
Any other information determined to be relevant by the City Clerk.
(b)
Grounds for denial. The City Clerk shall issue the solicitor license unless the Clerk finds grounds for
denial. The City Clerk may deny an application for a solicitor license for the following
reasons:
(1)
Failure to comply with any provision of this Code;
(2)
A material misrepresentation or false statement in the application for the license;
(3)
A felony conviction for a property crime or violent crime within the five (5) years
preceding the date of the application; or
(4)
Conviction of any crime committed while engaged in solicitation in the City.
(c)
Notice and appeal. If the City Clerk denies an application for a solicitor license, the City Clerk shall
notify the applicant in writing, stating the reasons for the denial, and shall mail
such notice by email or regular United States mail to the address provided on the
application. Within seven (7) days of the date of mailing such notice, the applicant
may file a written request for an appeal to The Hearing Officer. The Hearing Officer
shall conduct a hearing on the appeal and issue a decision within sixty (60) days
of receipt of the appeal request.
(d)
Permits nontransferable. Solicitor licenses may not be transferred from person to person.
(e)
Expiration. Each license shall expire on the date specified on the solicitor license.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
Each solicitor shall carry the solicitor license and personal identification at all
times while engaging in solicitation, and shall present such identification and license
upon request of any person.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
(a)
A solicitor license may be suspended or revoked by a Hearing Officer, after notice
and hearing, for any of the following causes:
(1)
Fraud, misrepresentation, or incorrect statement contained in the application for
license;
(2)
Fraud, misrepresentation, or incorrect statement made in the course of conducting
solicitation activities in the City;
(3)
Any violation of this Article;
(4)
A felony conviction for a property crime or violent crime; or
(5)
Conducting solicitation in an unlawful manner or in a manner that constitutes a significant
threat to the health, safety, and general welfare of the public.
(b)
Notice of the hearing for revocation or suspension of a license shall be given by
the City Clerk in writing, setting forth specifically the grounds of complaint and
the time and place of hearing. Such notice shall be emailed or mailed, postage prepaid,
to the licensee at their last known address at least five (5) days prior to the date
set for hearing, or shall be personally served on the licensee.
(c)
No licensee whose license has been revoked shall make further application until at
least six (6) months have elapsed since the last previous revocation.
(Ord. 992 §21, 2025)
