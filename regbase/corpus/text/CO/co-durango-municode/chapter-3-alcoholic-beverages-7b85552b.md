The city council hereby finds, determines and declares that considering the nature
of the business of selling at retail alcoholic beverages for beverage purposes, and
the relation of such business to the municipal welfare, as well as the relation thereof
to the expenditures required of the city and a proper just and equitable distribution
of tax burdens within the city and all other matters proper to be considered in relation
there to, that the classification of such business as a separate occupation is reasonable,
proper, uniform and nondiscriminatory and that the amount of tax imposed by this chapter
is reasonably proper, uniform and nondiscriminatory and necessary for a just and a
proper distribution of the tax burdens within the City.
(Ord. No. O-2019-21 , § 1, 11-19-19)
The business of selling at retail alcohol beverages for beverage purpose is hereby
defined and separately classified as such occupations for the purpose of this chapter
as follows:
(1)
Class A Licensees. All persons licensed to sell alcoholic beverages for consumption on the premises
such as a hotel and restaurant shall be class A Licensees.
(2)
Class B Licensees. All persons licensed to sell fermented malt beverage or vinous liquors only by the
drink for consumption on the premises shall be class B Licensees.
(3)
Class C Licensees. All persons licensed as retail liquor stores to sell in original containers alcoholic
beverages for consumption off the premises, shall be class C Licensees.
(4)
Class D Licensees. All persons licensed as liquor-licensed drugstores to alcoholic beverages in original
containers for consumption off premises, shall be class D Licensees.
(5)
Class E Licensees. All persons licensed to sell alcoholic beverages as clubs or arts shall be class
E Licensees.
(6)
Class F-1 Licensees. All persons licensed to sell only fermented malt beverage and who sell the same for
consumption on the premises shall be class F-1 Licensees.
(7)
Class F-2 Licensees. All persons licensed to sell only fermented malt beverages and
vinous liquor who sell the same solely in the original package or container for consumption
off the premises shall be class F-2 Licensees.
(8)
Class F-3 Licensees. All persons licensed to sell only fermented malt beverage and vinous liquor who sell
the same solely in the original package or container for consumption on and off the
premises shall be class F-3 Licensees.
(Ord. No. O-2019-21 , § 1, 11-19-19; Ord. No. O-2024-0029 , 12-3-24)
(a)
There should be and is hereby established an optional premises liquor license classification
within the City of Durango, as authorized by C.R.S. § 44-3-310. Such licenses shall
be issued and administered at the local level by the Durango Local Licensing Authority
(DLLA).
(b)
Any such license shall be limited to hotel and restaurant licenses, as defined under
the Colorado Liquor Code, which shall have related outdoor sports and recreational
facilities, or premises specified in an application for an optional premises license
which are located on an applicant's outdoor sports and recreation facility. In addition
to the foregoing requirements, any such license shall be subject to the following
limitations and standards:
(1)
The license shall be applied for and issued in the name of the applicant who proposes
to sell and/or serve alcoholic beverages pursuant to said license;
(2)
If the premises proposed for such a license are leased by the applicant, the written
consent of the owner of the property must accompany the license application;
(3)
Licenses for outdoor sports and recreational facilities which are not located on or
adjacent to premises with an existing hotel and restaurant, as described in C.R.S.
§ 44-3-103(33)(a)(I), shall be limited to governmental agencies which own and are
responsible for the operation of such outdoor sports and recreational facilities;
(4)
No more than one (1) optional premises license shall be issued for any outdoor sports
or recreational facility at any time.

The occupational tax levied in this chapter shall be due and payable to the city clerk

(a)
It shall be unlawful for any person in the city to carry or have any open containers
of alcoholic beverages on any street, sidewalk, alley, parking lot, or other public
place in the city, or in any motor vehicle in the city or on the grounds of any public
or private school, college or university in the city. It shall be unlawful for any
person to drink any alcoholic beverages in any of the above-enumerated places in the
city; provided, however, that open containers of fermented malt beverages may be carried
or had in the possession of and may be consumed by persons in any residence hall located
upon the campus of any college or university if the residence halls have been officially
designated as such by the proper officials of the college or university.
(b)
Open containers of fermented malt beverages or vinous liquors may be carried or had
in possession of and may be consumed by persons on the premises of Hillcrest Golf
Club and such property and facilities are excluded from the provisions of subsection
(a) above.
(c)
Open containers of alcoholic beverages may be carried or had in possession of and
may be consumed by persons at designated city facilities when such persons are utilizing
the facilities pursuant to a special events permit duly authorized and issued by the
city which allows for the consumption and possession of such alcoholic beverages.
These facilities of the city are excluded from the provisions of subsection (a) above
during such times as the facilities are being lawfully used by persons or groups pursuant
to permits issued by the city.
(d)
For purposes of this section, the term "open container" shall not be construed to
include an open container of partially consumed vinous liquor of seven hundred fifty
(750) milliliters or less, purchased by a customer from a licensee licensed under
the Colorado Liquor Code and resealed and removed from the licensed premises pursuant
to the authority of C.R.S., § 44-23-423, as amended.
(Ord. No. O-2019-21 , § 1, 11-19-19)
All Durango police officers or other city officers vested with the responsibility
of enforcing all laws and ordinances pertaining to the sale, dispensation or consumption
of alcoholic beverages within the city and in conjunction therewith shall be specifically
empowered to initiate and complete investigations of alleged violations of any terms
and provisions of this Code and, if warranted, to issue citations or summonses and
complaints for violations of municipal ordinances occurring within the corporate limits
of the city.

possess or consume any alcoholic beverage;
(2)

(a)
Definitions.
(1)
Terms used within this section which are defined in the Colorado Liquor Code (Article
3 of Title 44, C.R.S.) or in the Colorado Beer Code (Article 4 of Title 44, C.R.S.)
shall have the meaning provided in such statutes. Additionally, as used in this section,
the following words, terms and phrases shall have the meanings ascribed to them in
this section.
(2)
Tastings permit means a separate permit issued by the DLLA pursuant to this section authorizing the
tastings of alcoholic beverages to be conducted by the licensee.
(b)
Permit required.
(1)
The city hereby authorizes tastings, as said term is defined in C.R.S., § 44-3-301(10),
to be conducted by liquor-licensed drug stores or retail liquor store licensees in
accordance with this section and pursuant to said C.R.S., § 44-3-301(10).
(2)
It is unlawful for any person or licensee to conduct tastings within the city unless
a tastings permit has been obtained in accordance with this section. The DLLA is authorized
to issue tasting permits consistent with the requirements of this section.
(3)
A liquor-licensed drugstore or retail liquor store licensee who wishes to conduct
tastings shall submit an application for a tastings permit to the DLLA, which application
shall be accompanied by an application fee of one hundred dollars ($100.00).
(4)
The DLLA may deny the application if the applicant's license is not in good standing
at the time of the application, or if the applicant fails to establish that the licensee
is able to conduct tastings without violating the provisions of this section or without
creating a public safety risk.
(5)
The DLLA shall prescribe the application form and shall establish application procedures.
The application form shall include a schedule of the planned dates of tastings, a
list of the names of the persons conducting the tastings [including documentation
reflecting that the person or persons conducting the tastings have completed the required
training pursuant to C.R.S., § 44-3-301(10)(c)(I)], a written control plan, and such
other information as the local licensing authority may require. Any change to the
information submitted as part of the permit application must be submitted to the DLLA
no later than one (1) week prior to the implementation of the change. Failure to do
so shall constitute a violation.
(6)
Renewal of tastings permits shall be concurrent with the renewal of the liquor-licensed
drug store or retail liquor store liquor license. The initial tastings permit issued
by the DLLA shall expire on the date of expiration of the liquor-licensed drug store
or retail liquor store license. The initial permit application fee shall not be prorated.
(7)
Except as herein modified, tastings shall be subject to the limitations of and conducted
in compliance with C.R.S., § 44-3-301(10)(c), as amended from time to time. Compliance
with the limitations and requirements set forth in said C.R.S. § 44-3-301(10)(c) and
this section 3-14 shall be a condition of any tastings permit, whether or not expressly
set forth in said permit. In addition thereto, the following conditions and limitations
shall apply to all tastings permits issued within the corporate limits of the City
of Durango:
a.
No more than four (4) individual samples of up to one (1) ounce each of beer or wine
may be provided to a customer. No more than four (4) individual samples of up to one-half
(½) ounce of spirituous liquors may provided to a customer. Samples shall be provided
free of charge.
b.
Tastings may occur any day of a given week that the licensee may be open for business
and the total number of tastings shall not exceed one hundred and fifty-six (156)
days per year.
c.
Tastings shall be conducted during the licensee's operating hours, shall not exceed
five (5) hours per day (which need not be consecutive), shall commence no earlier
than 11:00 a.m., and shall not extend later than 9:00 p.m.
d.
Samples used for tastings shall be served only in single use disposable cups or containers
of a size that contains no more than one (1) ounce.
e.
The licensee shall prohibit patrons from leaving the licensed premises with an unconsumed
sample.
f.
Tastings shall be subject to additional limits or regulations imposed or adopted by
the State of Colorado.
(8)
Tastings permits may only be issued to liquor-licensed drug store or retail liquor
store licensees operating within the city whose license is valid, in good standing
and in full force and effect.
(c)
Violations.
(1)
A violation of the limitations of this section or C.R.S., § 44-3-301 by a liquor-licensed
drug store or retail liquor store licensee, whether by his or her employees, agents,
or otherwise, shall be the responsibility of the licensee in whose name the tasting
permit has been issued.
(2)
A liquor-licensed drug store or retail liquor store licensee conducting tastings shall
be subject to the same revocation, suspension and enforcement provisions as otherwise
apply to the licensee for a violation of any part of C.R.S. § 44-3-301.
(d)
Exemption from open container prohibition. Cups or containers utilized on licensed premises pursuant to a validly issued tastings
permit shall be exempt from the open container prohibition set forth in section 3-9 of this Code.

(a)
Definitions.
(1)
Terms used within this section, which are defined in the Colorado Liquor Code (C.R.S.
§ 44-3-101 et seq.) or in the Colorado Beer and Wine Code (C.R.S. § 44-4-101 et seq.),
shall have the meaning provided in such statutes or the regulations promulgated pursuant
thereto.
(2)
Takeout and delivery permit shall mean a permit issued by the Durango Local Licensing Authority (DLLA) authorizing
the licensee to:
a.
Sell and deliver alcoholic beverages (delivery); or
b.
Allow customers to remove alcoholic beverages from licensee's licensed premises (takeout);
or
c.
Both a. and b. above.
(3)
Or purposes of this chapter and section 3-9 , the term "open container" shall not be construed to include a sealed container that
complies with the rules of the state licensing authority.
(b)
Takeout and delivery permit required.
(1)
The city hereby creates a permit for takeout and delivery of alcoholic beverages,
as authorized by and in compliance with C.R.S. § 44-3-911.
(2)
The DLLA is authorized to issue takeout and delivery permits to licensees consistent
with C.R.S. § 44-3-911, as amended, and with the requirements of this section and
shall have all the powers necessary to administer such permits, including, but not
limited to, the power to suspend or revoke such permits for cause in the manner provided
by law or regulation.
(3)
It is unlawful for any person or licensee to engage in sales of alcoholic beverages
through takeout or delivery unless the licensee holds a takeout and delivery permit
from the DLLA, and a comparable permit issued by the state licensing authority.
(4)
A licensee who wishes to engage in sales of alcoholic beverages through takeout or
delivery shall submit an application for a takeout and delivery permit to the DLLA,
which application shall be accompanied by an application fee in the amount of eleven
dollars ($11.00) or the amount of the state fee for such permits, whichever is greater.
(5)
The DLLA may deny the application if the applicant's license is not in good standing
at the time of the application, or if the applicant fails to establish that the licensee
will be able to comply with this section, C.R.S. § 44-3-911, or other regulations
of the DLLA and state licensing authority applicable to takeout and delivery of alcoholic
beverages.
(6)
Renewal of takeout and delivery permits shall be concurrent with the renewal of the
licensee's liquor license. The initial takeout and delivery permit issued by the DLLA
shall expire on the date of expiration of the licensee's liquor license.
(7)
Except as herein modified, takeout and delivery permits shall be subject to the requirements
and limitations of and exercised in compliance with C.R.S. § 44-3-911 and 1 C.C.R.
§ 203-2:47-1101. Compliance with such limitations and requirements set forth in C.R.S.
§ 44-3-911, 1 C.C.R. § 203-2:47-1101, and this section 3-19 shall be a condition of
any takeout and delivery permit, whether or not expressly set forth in said permit.
In addition thereto, the following conditions and limitations shall apply to all takeout
and delivery permits issued within the corporate limits of the City of Durango:
a.
The submitted application must include an operating plan that describes the service
and procedures to ensure compliance with this subsection (6), and a list of employees
making deliveries and their liquor training certificates.
(8)
Takeout and delivery permits may only be issued to licensees operating within the
city whose liquor license is valid, in good standing and in full force and effect.
(c)
Violations.
(1)
A violation of the limitations of this section, C.R.S., § 44-3-911, or 1 C.C.R. §
203-2:47-1101 by a licensee engaged in sales of alcoholic beverages through takeout
or delivery, whether by his or her employees, agents, or otherwise, shall be the responsibility
of the licensee in whose name the permit has been issued.
(2)
A licensee engaged in sales of alcoholic beverages through takeout or delivery shall
be subject to the same revocation, suspension and enforcement provisions as otherwise
apply to the licensee's liquor license.
(d)
Other provisions.
(1)
Takeout and delivery permits may be issued by the DLLA to the following licensees
in accordance with C.R.S. § 44-3-911, as amended:
a.
The following license types may provide takeout services only:

Fermented malt beverage off-premises licensed under C.R.S., 44-4-104;
Sales room establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-402; and

The following license types may provide delivery services only:

c.
The following license types may provide both takeout and delivery services:
Beer and wine establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-411;
Hotel and restaurant establishments licensed under 44-3-413, C.R.S;
Tavern establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-414;
Brew pub establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-417;
Club establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-418;
Vintner's restaurant establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-422;
Distillery pub establishments licensed under C.R.S., 44-3-426; and
