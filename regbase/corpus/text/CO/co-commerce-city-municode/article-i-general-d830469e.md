This chapter shall be known as the City of Commerce City Emergency Operations Plan
and all reference in this chapter to the "plan" shall be construed to mean that document.
All actions taken pursuant to the plan including, but not limited to, prevention,
preparedness, training, planning, response and recovery, shall be deemed actions taken
or attempted under color of the Colorado Disaster Emergency Act of 1992, as set forth
in C.R.S., tit. 24, art. 32, pt 21.
Charter. The Home Rule Charter of the City of Commerce City as adopted pursuant to the Constitution
of the State of Colorado.
Chief executive. For the purposes of this plan, in the absence of a declaration of extreme emergency
by the mayor, the city manager is the chief executive. In accordance with City Charter,
once the mayor declares an extreme emergency, the mayor becomes the chief executive
for purposes of this plan.
Disaster. Per C.R.S. § 24-32-2103(1.5), a "disaster" means the occurrence or imminent threat
of widespread or severe damage, injury, or loss of property resulting from any natural
cause or cause of human origin, including but not limited to fire, flood, earthquake,
wind, storm, wave action, hazardous substance incident, oil spill or other water contamination
requiring emergency action to avert danger or damage, volcanic activity, epidemic,
air pollution, blight, drought, infestation, explosion, civil disturbance, or hostile
military or paramilitary action. A "disaster" also includes any other declared disaster
that requires the aid and assistance of local, state, or federal agencies. A disaster
also includes mass casualty.
Emergency. An "emergency" is defined in the Charter of the City of Commerce City as: "An existing
condition actually arising from unforeseen contingencies which immediately endanger
public property, health, peace or safety".
For the purposes of this chapter, an "emergency" also means an impending condition
which may immediately endanger public property, health, peace or safety; an event
with a likelihood of endangering public property, health, peace or safety; or an event
which, if it develops other than as intended, would create an immediate danger to
public property, health, peace or safety.
For the purposes of this chapter, an "emergency" can be any event that meets the definition
of a "disaster" as defined.
Extreme emergency. An undefined condition used in City Charter as a condition in which the mayor becomes
the chief executive of the city.
Incident commander. A single individual in overall command of a response, setting the incident priorities,
developing the incident action plan, and allocating resources. The incident commander
can be from any discipline or any department.
The incident commander is overseen by policymakers for the city, and operates freely
within the guidelines set by the policy group (such as resource or budgetary limitations).
The incident commander is usually not a department head or elected official. The incident
commander functions in a tactical or strategic role, implementing the policies set
by the policy group.
(a)
The purpose of the City of Commerce City emergency operations plan is to provide general
guidelines and principles for planning, managing and coordinating the overall response
and recovery activities of the city before, during and after major emergencies and
disaster events.
(b)
This emergency operations plan (EOP) has been developed in accordance with the requirements
for local emergency planning established under the State of Colorado Disaster Emergency
Act of 1992 and also meets the requirements of other state and federal guidelines
for local emergency management plans and programs.
(c)
The contents of this EOP are intended to provide a basis for the coordinated planning
and management of the types of emergencies and disaster events most likely to occur
in the city. All entities, offices, departments, agencies, and other organizations
referenced in this plan are responsible for developing, maintaining and training on
up-to-date standard operating procedures and/or checklists necessary for implementing
assigned duties and functions. Attachments to this EOP may be developed as needed
and consistent with this EOP. Such attachments shall be called immediate action plans
and shall be incorporated into this EOP by reference, without the need for amendment
of this EOP by resolution of city council. Such immediate action plans will further
outline the various response and recovery activities outlined in this EOP. Any changes,
additions, or deletions to the substantive content of this EOP must be done by an
amendment to the EOP and approved by resolution of the city council. In the event
of a conflict between such attachments and this EOP, this EOP shall govern. In the
event of a conflict between this EOP and applicable law, then the applicable law shall
govern.
(d)
The chief of police or designee is responsible for regularly scheduled EOP updates
and revisions and for developing a training and exercise program to familiarize City
of Commerce City officials and employees and other emergency officials and agencies
with the provisions of this plan, including volunteer organizations, news media, and
appropriate private sector organizations. The chief of police or designee is responsible
for developing necessary guidelines for emergency operations, including both response
and recovery phases, consistent with applicable law and this EOP.
(e)
Emergencies will be managed in the field under the plan assignments as set forth herein.
The emergency operations center (once designated) will be activated to help support
the assigned agencies and to carry out the additional functions that are needed (e.g.,
damage assessment, coordination of outside agencies and volunteers, intergovernmental
relations, public information support, etc.). Activation of the emergency operations
center is encouraged when needed resources are scarce, or when multiple requests for
similar resources are generated from the field. The emergency operations center will
function in accordance with this EOP. The primary location of the emergency operations
center will be at a location determined by the city. The emergency operations center
will be staffed to serve as the coordination point for supplemental resources and
intergovernmental assistance.
(f)
The National Incident Management System shall be utilized to manage all incidents
managed under this plan.
(g)
Disasters and large-scale emergencies are rarely confined to one (1) jurisdiction.
A multijurisdictional effort would be required to manage major incidents. Accordingly,
emergency plans and exercises must incorporate procedures for integrating the resources
of various local government entities, private and volunteer organizations and state
and federal governments.
(h)
Emergency response agencies in the city request resources through mutual aid arrangements,
usually discipline-specific, such as fire, law enforcement, emergency medical services
or public works. Each discipline is required to obtain and maintain needed agreements
to fulfill their specific task responsibilities as defined in the EOP. The emergency
manager should be made aware of these agreements.
(i)
Based on the assessment of emergency conditions by the designated incident commander,
the city council and/or city manager or his/her designee may be notified and advised
of the situation and the need to report to the emergency operations center.
(j)
The Colorado Office of Emergency Management (COEM) is available twenty-four (24) hours
a day to provide advice and technical assistance to the city and to provide state
resources or coordinate other supplemental assistance in support of local emergency
management actions. A formal declaration of a disaster by the chief executive may
be required as a precondition of some forms of state assistance or to expedite state
assistance. COEM is also the state agency responsible for processing requests for
state and federal disaster assistance.
(k)
Pursuant to the authority granted by the City Charter, the mayor, as the conservator
of the peace of the City of Commerce City, has the power to declare that a state of
extreme emergency exists. The initial declaration may be verbal, but shall be followed
by a declaration of emergency in writing as soon as practical, and shall describe
the nature of the emergency, the area threatened, the conditions which have brought
it about, and the conditions that would remedy it. The mayor shall be responsible
for publication and dissemination of information to the public and shall file the
declaration with the city clerk and cause a copy to be forwarded to the Colorado Office
of Emergency Management.
(l)
Pursuant to the authority granted in the Charter, the issuance of a declaration declaring
a state of extreme emergency shall empower the mayor to exercise any and all of the
emergency powers permitted by state and local law and shall activate all relevant
portions of the EOP. The city council shall convene to perform its legislative powers
as the situation demands and shall receive reports through the mayor and evaluate
and enact policy and other incident support as required.
(m)
Pursuant to the authority granted in the Charter and city ordinances, nothing in this
chapter shall abridge or curtail the powers of the city council. A state of emergency
shall remain in effect until the mayor declares that the threat of danger has passed
or that the emergency conditions no longer exist.
(n)
Any declaration establishing, continuing, or terminating a state of emergency or extreme
emergency shall be filed with the city clerk, and a copy shall be forwarded to Colorado
Office of Emergency Management. Subsequent to the declaration of emergency, as defined
in the Charter and this chapter, the mayor and city manager may exercise their respective
powers granted by applicable local or state law subsequent to issuance of the emergency
declaration.

(a)
Organization.
As determined by current City of Commerce City structure.
(b)
Responsibilities.
(1)
General. All department heads of the city will be responsible for:
a.
Preparing plans and organizing their departments to meet emergencies and disasters,
both natural and man-made, and assuring continuity of government operations during
emergency situations. These plans must be consistent with the city's adopted emergency
operations plan (EOP).
b.
Providing personnel as requested to be members of the multi-agency emergency planning
committee. Also, providing necessary time for committee members to complete tasks
assigned by the committee.
c.
Identifying functions to be performed during an emergency and assign responsibility
for performing those functions to personnel of their departments.
d.
Ensuring that the emergency manager is aware of all plans for emergency prevention,
response and recovery.
e.
Providing a staff member to the emergency operations center to coordinate their emergency
response functions with those of other agencies represented therein.
f.
Ensuring that the city government is kept informed of the situation during emergencies
by reporting events and activities to the emergency operations center promptly.
g.
Obtaining and maintaining needed agreements to fulfill their specific task responsibilities
as defined in the EOP. The emergency manager should be made aware of these agreements.
(2)
The city council is responsible for:
Determining the level of commitment of city resources and funds for disaster or emergency
purposes and other emergency duties and authorities as permitted by law. Nothing in
this EOP shall abridge or curtail the powers of the city council.
(3)
The city manager or designee is responsible for:
a.
Those duties and authorities specifically set forth in the Charter or city ordinances;
b.
Assigning public information releases to a single point of contact, ensuring releases
are coordinated and synchronized;
c.
Assigning members of the city manager's office administrative staff to the emergency
operations center, in order to:
1.
Provide administrative assistance to the emergency operations center manager,
2.
Track personnel and expenses associated with operating the emergency operations center.
(4)
The chief of police or designee is responsible for:
a.
Ensuring the writing and coordination of all city emergency plans;
b.
Ensuring annual review of the city's EOP;
c.
Coordinating the multi-agency emergency planning committee (MAEPC);
d.
Coordinating efforts between the city manager, mayor, city council, and other officials
and assuming the responsibility to keep them informed;
e.
Monitoring the implementation of the EOP;
f.
Coordinating the emergency response operations of all governmental and support agencies
before, during, and after an emergency or disaster:
1.
This authority may be delegated to the assigned incident commander and/or emergency
operations center director,
2.
This authority may be delegated to members of other city departments, or members of
other agencies, in accordance with section 23-6 of this chapter;
g.
Assuring that the adopted incident management system is being used;
h.
Establishing the emergency operations center and managing its operation;
i.
Ensuring that appropriate response agencies and department directors are aware of
the responsibilities assigned herein;
j.
Informing other jurisdictions about emergency and disaster situations;
k.
Apprising the city manager, mayor and city council of overall preparedness of the
city's agencies' plan to respond to all types of emergencies and disasters;
l.
Establishing liaison and coordinate disaster planning and operations with area industries,
public utilities, welfare agencies, and emergency management directors of adjacent
political subdivisions and the state to ensure compatible integrated emergency plans;
m.
Conducting studies and surveys of private and local governmental resources, industries,
and facilities to determine their emergency disaster preparedness posture, and negotiating
written agreements for their effective use during major emergencies and disasters;
n.
Conducting or coordinating training programs and exercises to maintain and improve
the general emergency or disaster preparedness posture of all elements of the city's
disaster response organizations;
o.
Representing the city, as directed, with other city, county, state, or national organizations
concerned with emergency management;
p.
Assuring appropriate citizen notification:
1.
Emergency alert system (EAS),
2.
Emergency preparedness notification (EPN);
q.
Keeping the Colorado Office of Emergency Management (COEM) informed of the emergency
or disaster situation;
r.
Assist the city manager, mayor, or city council in seeking support from county, state,
and federal emergency resources, when required to supplement city efforts;
s.
Obtaining assistance of radio amateur civil emergency services (RACES) personnel in
establishing a logistics and resources communications net;
t.
Establishing and maintaining law and order throughout the city;
u.
Ensuring notification of key city officials regarding the nature of the emergency
or disaster;
v.
Receiving and relaying to proper authorities all alerts and warnings concerning the
potential of disaster or disturbance;
w.
Communications:
1.
Ensuring (with ADCOM) communications for emergency response is provided at all times,
2.
Establishing and maintaining coordinated radio and wire communications with other
public emergency organizations and agencies,
3.
Citizen notification;
x.
Establishing a field command post when necessary;
y.
Maintaining and operating a mobile command post and providing continuous communications
between the disaster scene, the incident commander, and the emergency operations center;
z.
Controlling traffic and access to the scene of an emergency or disaster;
aa.
Evacuating persons who are or may be threatened by an imminent emergency or disaster,
in the manner determined by the incident commander;
bb.
Establishing mutual aid agreements;
cc.
Gathering intelligence;
dd.
Providing law enforcement assistance to shelters as requested;
ee.
Establishing procedures to secure buildings and areas affected by a disaster;
ff.
Establishing procedures for the handling of animals affected by a disaster:
1.
Procedure for proper handling of dead animals,
2.
Coordination with tri-county health,
3.
Procedures for handling of live animals.
(5)
Director of the Department of Public Works or designee is responsible for:
a.
Developing plans, policies, and procedures for maximum emergency use and maintenance
of all city vehicles, facilities, heavy equipment, fuels, supplies, and assigned city
personnel, and for the central control and repair of all transportation assets. Privately
owned resources and facilities will be included as authorized by the chief executive
(or designee), chief of police (or designee), or incident commander (or designee)
in accordance with the scope of the emergency or disaster;
b.
Advising the city council regarding the establishment of priorities for decontamination,
rehabilitation, and restoration of those public buildings, roads, utilities, and other
essential facilities necessary to the operation of the city government, and contributing
to the emergency welfare of the population;
c.
Initiating actions for refuse disposal to eliminate any unsanitary conditions determined
(by the appropriate health department) as detrimental to public health and welfare;
d.
Developing pre-incident agreements with local landfills for acceptance of debris from
a disaster;
e.
Developing procedures for the disposal of hazardous household waste created by an
emergency or disaster;
f.
Coordinating, with the relevant providers, the shutting down of utilities to prevent
damage to, and monitoring repair and restoration of, electrical, gas, and water distribution;
g.
Providing systems to provide emergency sources of electricity, gas, and potable water
for essential city activities when needed;
h.
Developing agreements and procedures for working with utility companies that provide
services to city residents and businesses;
i.
Providing immediate clearance of major thoroughfares and removal of debris to permit
emergency rescue operations and movement of emergency vehicles and supplies;
j.
Assisting in facilities protection and emergency repairs to city buildings, roads,
utilities, and other essential facilities under established priorities for restoration
and continued operation of city government functions;
k.
Assisting in providing damage assessment information to the emergency operations center
on roads and equipment belonging to the department;
l.
Providing traffic engineering including the establishment of emergency traffic routes
and coordination with the Colorado Department of Transportation for the marking of
such emergency routes;
m.
Arranging with pledged intergovernmental support groups to render disaster support
assistance within their capabilities;
n.
Providing emergency traffic control devices such as barricades, flares, and temporary
signs;
o.
Assisting the emergency response authority in containing hazardous substances as requested;
p.
Securing appropriate buildings.
(6)
Director of the Department of Community Planning and Development Services (CPDS) or
designee will be responsible for:
a.
Providing damage assessment reports for the emergency operations center and city council.
This is to be coordinated with the Finance Department;
b.
Receiving and plotting current data concerning extent and type of building damage
and recording of significant population concentrations within the city;
c.
Conducting mitigation studies during normal operations for the purpose of making recommendations
that will reduce disaster vulnerability during emergencies or disasters. This is to
be coordinated with the emergency management team;
d.
Developing a program to quickly provide a damage assessment. This should be coordinated
with the Department of Finance and emergency management team;
e.
Determining habitability and whether services can be restored;
f.
Monitoring rebuilding in recovery phase.
(7)
City attorney or designee will be responsible for:
a.
Functioning as principal legal advisor to the city manager, mayor, city council, emergency
operations center, and department heads;
b.
Providing interpretation of laws and regulations issued by the federal, state, and
local governments;
c.
Preparing recommended ordinances, proclamations, and other legal documents for city
council action to implement federal and state directives that provide effective authority
in the city for a timely emergency response.
(8)
Director of the Parks and Recreation Department or designee will be responsible for:
a.
Providing staff to designated shelters and opening any city recreation facilities
that may be used as a shelter if needed.
b.
Providing for the safety of any citizens who may be in the facilities that are managed
by the Parks and Recreation Department at the time of an emergency or disaster.
c.
Providing additional manpower and equipment to other departments to assist in emergency
response or recovery efforts.
(9)
Director of finance or designee will be responsible for:
a.
Maintaining payroll for city employees;
b.
Providing emergency procedures for purchasing supplies and/or equipment necessary
for recovery operations to restore services to the public;
c.
Maintaining detailed financial records of all costs accrued during an emergency or
disaster operation and preparing reports of the city's financial position;
d.
Providing overall coordination in the compilation and presentation of requests for
disaster relief assistance, when required;
e.
Assisting in developing and coordinating a damage assessment system that meets state
and federal procedures;
f.
Responding to risk management issues like recovery of monies from insurance providers,
state/federal disaster assistance programs or other funds or combination of funding
sources.
(10)
The city clerk or designee is responsible for:
a.
Controlling the safety of all vital records to maintain governmental continuity;
b.
In the event the emergency occurs during an election, coordination to maintain open
polling locations.

2.

3.

Coordinating issuance of precautionary health warnings with other city/county agencies,

7.

1.

c.

1.
Assisting with evacuation and sheltering of disaster victims;
2.
Assisting with provision of food and clothing to disaster victims;
3.
Assisting with providing services for persons with unique nutritional or medical needs

4.
Providing all services necessary to accept public assistance, emergency assistance,
or diversion applications;
5.
Collaborating with the Police Department and Red Cross to ensure the availability
of mental health crisis services;
6.
Providing resources for stress management of victims and relief workers;
7.
Ensuring availability of interpreters for hearing impaired and non-English speaking;
8.
Providing emergency workforce services (rehabilitation and resettlement) for displaced
personnel;
9.
Coordinating with Red Cross to designate and operate information centers with facilities
for the registration of disaster victims, location of missing persons and reunification
of families;
10.
Providing separate shelter for children separated from parents or elderly with special
needs and no immediate family in the Tri-County Health Department Family Assessment
Center until reunited with parents, picked up by relatives or special placement;
11.
Establishing food distribution points for families and other groups to replenish supplies,
as necessary;
12.
Advising city management/city council on all human services matters;
13.
Administration of the individual and family grant program, in presidentially-declared
disasters in the city;
14.
Providing assistance with re-entry into the community (safety or stored goods, cleaning
of smoke damage or water damage).

(Ord. No. 1855, § 2, 4-4-11)
(a)
The city shall retain responsibility for direction and control of its own operations,
personnel, resources, and facilities when an emergency or disaster occurs.
(1)
Nothing in this subsection will conflict with concepts of unity of command under the
National Incident Management and Incident Command System.
(2)
City resources and personnel may be assigned under the control of another jurisdiction,
in accordance with the National Incident Management System. However, they remain city
resources and personnel, and may be withdrawn at the city's sole discretion.
(b)
The chief executive shall exercise general direction and control of all city agencies
and departments involved in emergency or disaster operations. The chief executive
shall be responsible for the coordination of all activities of agencies, departments,
and organizations in the execution of this plan.
(c)
The director or head of each city agency and department involved in the execution
of this plan, subject to direction and control by the chief executive or his/her authorized
representative, shall be responsible for the emergency operations of his/her agency
or department. This section shall not affect the authority of the chief of police
(or designee) to coordinate and/or direct resources from other city agencies to respond
to an emergency.
(d)
If the effects of an emergency require the normally established government of the
city to seek outside assistance, the assistance provided shall supplement, not replace,
the operations of the city agencies involved.
(1)
The city shall be responsible for direction and control of all outside assistance
assigned to work under city supervision, unless an agency with overriding jurisdiction
takes control of operations.
a.
In this event, the city shall maintain control of all personnel, resources, and facilities,
unless specifically assigned under the control of another jurisdiction.
(2)
In keeping with the National Incident Management System, members of another jurisdiction
may, at the discretion of the chief of police (or designee), be temporarily placed
in a position supervisory to Commerce City personnel, in order to meet the requirements
of an emergency. Personnel shall follow instructions from such temporary supervisors
as if the temporary supervisor were a Commerce City employee.
Such placement shall not make such person a city employee.
(3)
In keeping with the National Incident Management System, members of another jurisdiction
may, at the discretion of the chief of police (or designee), be temporarily placed
in positions of authority.
Such members shall have the revocable powers granted by the chief of police (or designee),
including, but not limited to, the authority to encumber city funds, issue directives
to city agencies and personnel, order evacuations and/or road closures, call in additional
resources from other jurisdictions, and coordinate response with other jurisdictions.
Such placement shall not make such person a city employee.
