The purpose and intent of this chapter is to regulate business activities conducted
in the city, to promote the health, safety and general welfare of the citizens of
the city, to promote and monitor the city's economic development and to implement
a comprehensive licensing program. The provisions of this chapter are not intended
to impose an unreasonable limitation or restriction on the ability of any person to
lawfully conduct business in the city. It is not the intent or purpose of this chapter
to limit or restrict intrastate or interstate commerce or any other rights conferred
by the Colorado or United States Constitutions.
All business conducted within the city shall comply with the requirements of this
chapter. However, any business lawfully operating before the adoption of this Code
shall be allowed to continue operating without compliance herewith for a period six
(6) months, or such longer period as may be granted by the city, in writing, conditioned
upon a showing that more time is reasonably required by the applicant to enable said
business to come into compliance; provided, however, that an application for a license
is submitted to the licensing agent within six (6) months of the effective date of
this Code.
The provisions of this chapter shall not apply to the city.
As used in this chapter, the following terms shall have the following meanings, except
where the context clearly indicates that a different meaning is intended and except
where an ordinance concerning a particular type of business contains a specific provision
to the contrary:
Applicant means the person(s) applying to the city for a business license.
General business license means the license that any person conducting business in the city is required to
obtain as part of the city's master licensing program.
Inspector means any employee or officer of the federal, state or local government assigned
to make investigations or inspections; any independent contractor retained by the
federal, state or local government to make investigations or inspections; and any
employee of any independent contractor retained by the federal, state, or local government
to make investigations or inspections.
Licensee means any person(s) licensed pursuant to this chapter and includes agents, attorneys,
officers, managers, employees and any other persons who are acting by or on behalf
of the licensee.
Licensing agent means that employee or employees of the city who is or are charged with processing
business license applications.
(a)
Forms and filing. Applications shall be filed with the licensing agent on forms approved by the city
and shall be accompanied by all required fees and any materials deemed necessary by
the city to carry out the purpose and provisions of this chapter or any applicable
law, including without limitation the submission of bonds. No application will be
considered complete until all fees have been paid and all requested information has
been provided to the city.
(b)
Fees. License and application fees shall be established by resolution of the city council.
All fees shall be nonrefundable, except that one-half (½) of the license fee shall
be refunded if the license is denied. In the event of a suspension or revocation of
a license, no portion of the license fee shall be refunded.
(c)
Incomplete applications. No application shall be considered which is not complete in every detail. Incomplete
applications may be returned to the applicant for completion or correction without
any further action. The city shall not be responsible for the failure of a license
to be issued due to incomplete or defective applications.
(d)
Duty to supplement. Applicants shall have a continuing duty to promptly supplement application information
required by the city in the event that said information changes in any way from what
is stated on the application.
(a)
Referrals. When an application is filed with the city, the application may be referred to members
of city staff and, where appropriate, representatives from other agencies for review
and comment. The purpose of these referrals is to define any conflict which the referral
party may have with the proposed license, to allow for the resolution of such conflicts
where possible, and to provide the city with pertinent information that will help
it render a decision on an application.
(b)
Decisions. A decision either approving or denying an application normally will be made within
fourteen (14) days of the date the complete application is received by the city. A
license approved by the city may include any reasonable condition deemed necessary
to ensure that the license is compatible with the public interest and to protect the
public health, safety and welfare.
(a)
General license. General licenses shall have an indefinite term. They shall be valid from the date
the license is issued until revoked or suspended by the city or otherwise terminated
by the city or the licensee. During the term of the license, licensees shall have
a continuing duty to promptly notify the city in the event that any information that
was required in the application changes in any way.
(b)
Specialty license term. Unless otherwise specified, all specialty licenses granted pursuant to this chapter
shall be for a term of one (1) year. The license year shall commence on the date the
license is issued and shall expire at 12:00 midnight exactly twelve (12) months from
the date of issuance. Nothing in this chapter, including failure to timely renew a
license, shall relieve the licensee from its continuing duties and obligations to
pay sales and use tax.
(c)
Specialty license renewal. No licensee may continue to operate its business after thirty (30) days from the
date the license year expires unless an application for renewal has been made on or
before the thirtieth day from the date the license year expires. Any applicant who
files an application for a renewal license after this deadline shall be required to
pay the new license fee instead of the renewal license fee.
(Ord. No. 1848, § 1, 1-10-2011)
Each licensee shall be responsible for monitoring and ensuring that any person acting
on the licensee's behalf complies with the provisions of this chapter and all other
applicable ordinances, laws and regulations.
No license issued under the provisions of this chapter shall be used or exhibited
at any time by any person or business other than the person or business to which it
was issued, and no transfer shall be permitted.
(a)
Grounds. The city may deny an application for, and the administrative hearing officer may
suspend or revoke, any business license issued pursuant to this chapter when it is
determined that the operation of such business by the applicant or licensee is not
in compliance with state, federal, or local law or has the potential for negatively
impacting the health, safety or welfare of its citizens. Without limiting the foregoing,
the following shall each constitute sufficient grounds for denial, suspension, or
revocation:
(1)
The property on which the business is or will be conducted is not zoned for such business;
(2)
Fraud, misrepresentation, false statement or material omission made in connection
with the application for a license or renewal;
(3)
Delinquent or unpaid taxes, assessments or other financial claims of any local, state
or federal government;
(4)
Revocation of a licensee's sales and use tax license;
(5)
Failure to comply with the duty to supplement application information within thirty
(30) days from the date of the change;
(6)
A violation of any condition placed on the license; and
(7)
Conduct of the business in any unlawful or dishonest manner, included but not limited
to, any violation of the provisions of this chapter, any other section of the Code
or any other federal, state or local law.
The failure of the city to discover the existence of a ground on which an application
for initial licensing or renewal of a current license shall not preclude the city
from taking action to revoke or suspend a license when such condition becomes known
to the city.
(b)
Process.
(1)
Denial. The city may deny the approval of any application, including an application
for renewal, without a hearing based upon the grounds set forth in paragraph (a) of
this section by issuing a written notice of such denial to the applicant. The written
notice of denial shall set forth the grounds for the denial and shall be mailed via
certified mail, return receipt requested, to the post office address given in the
application.
(2)
Appeal. An applicant may appeal the city's denial of an application, or approval of
an application with conditions, pursuant to article II of chapter 3 of this Code. Such appeal must be submitted to the administrative hearing officer
in writing, and shall be governed by the processes set forth in article II of chapter 3 of this Code.
(3)
Suspensions and revocations. The administrative hearing officer may suspend or revoke
any license issued pursuant to this chapter, after conducting a hearing in accordance
with article II of chapter 3 of this Code. The hearing officer may initiate such proceeding on its own motion,
or upon ex parte motion from the city, by issuing an order directing the licensee
to show cause why the license should not be suspended or revoked and notifying the
licensee of the time, date, and location of the hearing. The licensee shall be served
with notice of the hearing and the alleged causes(s) for revocation or suspension
not less than three (3) days prior to the date of the hearing. Service shall be deemed
complete either upon personal delivery of the written notice or five (5) days after
the date that such notice is sent by regular and certified mail, return receipt requested,
to the post office address given in the application for the license.
(4)
The failure of any person to receive any notice required under this chapter shall
not affect the validity of any proceedings taken under this chapter.

laws and regulations. A general business license shall also serve as a sales and use

As used in this division, the following terms shall have the following meanings:
Auctioneer means any person who shall sell or offer to sell any goods, wares or merchandise
or any personal property of any kind or any real estate or interest therein at any
store, stand or other place or location in the city by public outcry to induce bidding
for his own gain or profit or for the account of any other person or who shall advertise
or in any other way hold himself out as an auctioneer for public patronage or shall
receive fees or commission for his services as auctioneer.
Auction house means any place of business where goods, wares, merchandise or chattels of any kind
are regularly sold at auction.
Owner means, if the applicant is an entity and not a natural person, any partner(s), joint
venturer(s), officer(s), director(s), member(s) or shareholder(s) who holds at least
a ten (10) percent ownership interest in the applicable business entity.

however, that each licensed auctioneer may have one (1) assistant who shall not be
required to be licensed under this division to help catch the bidding or to assist
in the calling for bids only during the time the licensed auctioneer is in attendance.
The licensed auctioneer shall be responsible for any and all acts of his or her assistant
the same as if he himself or she herself had committed the acts.
The provisions of this division shall not apply to any of the following:

A person acting as a trustee under the provisions of a deed of trust or in the carrying

Sheriffs, tax collectors, coroners, marshals, executors, guardians;

A personal representative of an estate;
(4)
Auction sales conducted by nonprofit organizations qualified as a charitable organization
under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, as amended;
(5)
Assignees of insolvent debtors, bankrupts or debtors under the Federal Bankruptcy
Code of 1978 (Title 11 of the United States Code);
(6)
Any other persons required by law to sell real or personal property at auction;
(7)
Any governmental entity.
The city may conduct a background investigation on the applicant (or if the applicant
is an entity and not a natural person, every owner). Each applicant (or if the applicant
is an entity and not a natural person, each owner) shall pay a nonrefundable investigation
and/or fingerprint and photograph fee at the time the application is filed in an amount
not to exceed that charged by the Colorado Bureau of Investigation. Further, the applicant
(or if the applicant is an entity and not a natural person, each owner) shall furnish
a birth certificate, alien registration card or other reasonable identification card
to prove the applicant's name, date of birth and residency.

(1)
The denial or revocation of a business or professional license;
(2)
Any convictions of a felony, misdemeanor or ordinance violation, other than a minor

(3)
Any pending or judgments or convictions for fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation entered

(4)
The results of any investigation or inspection.

Business hours. The time period for sales outside an enclosed building of any auction licensee shall
be limited to the hours of 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. of each day. The time period for
sales inside an enclosed building of an auction licensee shall be limited to the hours
of 7:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. of each day.
(b)
Auction sales upon public ways and places. It shall be unlawful to conduct any auction sale upon any public street or alley
in the city.
(c)
Sales and storage to be within enclosed area. All auction licensees are required to conduct auction business within an enclosed
area concealed from the view of adjacent property and all products, goods, appliances,
equipment and property of whatever type and however defined used in connection with
or to be sold at public auction or stored on premises of the licensee before, during
or after auction sales shall at all times be kept within an enclosed area concealed
from the view of adjacent property. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the requirements
to conduct all auction business within an enclosed area shall not apply to any auction
conducted for the sale of motor vehicles or heavy construction equipment.
(d)
Unlawful transactions.
(1)
It shall be unlawful for any licensee under this division to accept any article for
sale at auction from any of the following:
a.
Any person under eighteen (18) years of age;
b.
Any person known to the licensee to be a thief or to have been convicted of a felony
without first notifying the finance director or a member of the police department.
(2)
It shall be unlawful for any auctioneer or person maintaining an auction house to
knowingly accept for sale any item or article with an altered serial number or with
no serial number at all if the item or article was originally manufactured with a
serial number.
(3)
No licensee shall substitute any other article for the articles displayed by the auctioneer
during the bidding and purchase by the bidder.
For the purpose of discovering violations of this division, the licensee shall provide
the city manager or any law enforcement officer of the city free and unfettered access
to the premises, books, accounts, papers, records, and files of the business and any
person employed by the licensee during regular business hours.

regulations governing pawnbrokers as allowed by state law. It is the intent of the
city that the provisions of this division shall be at least as restrictive as state
law but no more restrictive than state law with respect to fixed time and fixed price.
Except for the words defined in this section or within a specific ordinance, the words
and phrases used in this division shall have the meanings as set forth in the state's
statutes regulating pawnbrokers, as that statute is amended, and the case law interpreting
the same unless the context clearly indicates otherwise.

of the business on the basis of sound fiscal management.

b.
Any convictions of a felony, misdemeanor or ordinance violation, other than a minor
traffic infraction, in the past ten (10) years; and
c.
Any pending or judgments or convictions for fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation entered
in the past ten (10) years.
(2)
Failure of an applicant to demonstrate or a licensee to maintain financial responsibility.
The purpose and intent of this division is to protect the health, safety and welfare
of the public by licensing outdoor vendors selling food, goods or services in the
city, to avoid over-crowding or other dangerous situations in public places and public
rights-of-way and to allow pedestrian and vehicular movement in public areas and public
rights-of-way.
It is further the purpose and intent of this division to avoid the suppression of
speech activities protected by the First Amendment of the United States Constitution
and the Colorado Constitution.
As used in this division, the following terms shall have the following meanings:
Food means all articles used for human consumption as food, drink, confectionery or condiments,
whether simple, mixed or compound, and all substances or ingredients used in preparation
thereof.
Licensed business means any business licensed to operate as an outdoor vendor pursuant to this division.
Location means a specific geographic area, as determined by the city, within which a licensee
may operate under a single license.
Mobile vendor means an outdoor vender that makes stops at multiple sites within the city for the
purpose of serving customers located at such sites.
Outdoor means located in, conducted in or suited to the open air. This term shall not include
structures or buildings that are completely enclosed on all sides and have a fully
enclosed roof, but shall include any partially enclosed structure or building, including
but not limited to outdoor stadiums and vending pods.
Outdoor vendor means any person who sells or attempts to sell services, goods, wares or merchandise,
including food, from any outdoor location and who in furtherance of such purpose:
(1)
Sells or attempts to sell directly to passersby within any public right-of-way; or
(2)
Hires, leases, uses or occupies any readily movable structure or display device.
The following shall be excluded from the definition of outdoor vendor and shall be
exempt from the provisions of this division:
(1)
Any person distributing or disseminating information in the exercise of their First
Amendment rights under the U.S. Constitution and the Colorado Constitution;
(2)
Any federal, state, or local government;
(3)
Any person selling from outdoor locations upon private premises who also sells the
same or similar goods or services on a regular basis from indoor locations on such
premises;
(4)
Any person selling from a public sidewalk immediately adjacent to private premises,
who also sells on a regular basis from indoor locations on such premises;
(5)
Persons selling directly and exclusively to manufacturers, wholesalers or retailers
for the purposes of resale;
(6)
Yard sales or garage sales;
(7)
Persons conducting an auction and subject to licensure under the provisions of the
code; and
(8)
Persons selling within the confines of a flea market licensed pursuant to this article.
Stop means a specific site on which a mobile vendor conducts business.
Structure or display device shall mean and include, but shall not be limited to, any vehicle, kiosk, table, box,
container or other such structure or display device.
(d)
If the location or one (1) or more stops for which the application is being made is
located on private property, the outdoor vendor shall provide the city with the written
consent of such property owner, lessee or other person in legal control of such property.

(a)
License required. No person shall engage in the activities of an outdoor vendor without first obtaining
a license in accordance with the provisions of this section.
(1)
A separate license is required for each location where an outdoor vendor operates.
(2)
A single license may authorize up to ten (10) stops within a single location, provided

No license issued under this chapter shall supersede the zoning for any property.
(4)
Each structure or display device to be used by an outdoor vendor for on-site storage,

(b)
Display of license. The outdoor vendor license shall be firmly attached to the structure or display device
and visible to the public at all times during operation.
(Ord. No. 1848, § 1, 1-10-2011; Ord. No. 2119 , § 1, 1-9-17)
(a)
Distance requirements. Outdoor vendors shall not operate:
(1)
Within one hundred (100) feet of the property line of a residential dwelling any time;
(2)
Within three hundred (300) feet of the property line of a residential dwelling between
9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.;
(3)
If the outdoor vendor sells or attempts to sell food, within one hundred seventy-five
(175) feet of the property line of property occupied by any business, other than an
outdoor vendor, offering prepared food for sale to the public while such business
is open to the public; or
(4)
If the outdoor vendor sells or attempts to sell food, within three hundred (300) feet
of a school during school hours.
(b)
Use of public spaces. Outdoor vendors shall not obstruct, impede or otherwise interfere with the public's
use of public streets, rights-of-way, sidewalks, parking lots, metered parking spaces,
parks or any other public property or place, unless otherwise expressly authorized
by law. Except as otherwise expressly authorized by law, no structure or display device
shall be located:
(1)
Within any portion of a street, alley, roadway or highway designed or ordinarily used
for vehicular traffic, except for the purpose of transporting the licensee's goods,
wares or merchandise to, from or within the designated location;
(2)
Upon a public sidewalk within thirty (30) feet of the extended boundaries of a crosswalk.
(3)
Within ten (10) feet of the extension of any building entranceway, doorway or driveway;
(4)
In any location in a manner that impedes, interferes with or visually obstructs the
safe movement of vehicular or pedestrian traffic.
No outdoor vendor shall have any exclusive right to any location in the public streets,
alleys or rights-of-way except as authorized by the city.
(c)
Health and safety.
(1)
Outdoor vendors shall maintain each structure or display device in a safe condition
so as not to create an unreasonable risk of harm to the person or property of others.
(2)
Outdoor vendors shall not leave unattended any structure or display device shall be
left unattended on a public right-of-way or at the designated location while it is
in operation;
(3)
Outdoor vendors shall maintain the area within twenty-five (25) feet of the structure
or display device in a clean condition free of waste and litter and shall remove any
waste or litter deposited by any person within such area.
(4)
Outdoor vendors shall supply a waste receptacle of sufficient size and construction
to accommodate waste and litter generated by the outdoor vendor's operations. Outdoor
vendors operating in a common location may share waste receptacles provided that such
receptacles are of sufficient size and construction to accommodate all waste and litter
that may be generated by the outdoor vendors' operations. At the end of each day of
operation, all waste receptacles shall be removed and all waste and litter disposed
of therein shall be taken from the location and properly disposed of. Outdoor vendors
are encouraged but not required to provide for the recycling of waste.
(d)
Receipts required. No outdoor vendor selling any goods, food, merchandise or services to be delivered
or provided at a future date, shall refuse or fail to give to a purchaser at the time
of sale a written receipt that accurately sets forth: the name, mailing address and
telephone number of the outdoor vendor; a description of the goods, merchandise or
services to be delivered or provided; the anticipated date and manner of delivery
or provision; the amount paid by the purchaser; the balance due on the purchase; and
the terms of payment.
(j)
Fixed locations prohibited. No person shall allow any structure or display device used by an outdoor vendor to
become permanently fixed to any property. No person shall allow any structure or display
device used by an outdoor vendor to remain overnight at any stop or location without
the permission of the property owner and in no event shall any such structure or display
device remain overnight for more than three (3) nights.

The purpose and intent of this division is to regulate entertainment establishments
in the city, to promote the health, safety and general welfare of the citizens of
the city, and to establish reasonable and uniform business licensing procedures and
regulations for entertainment establishments in the city.
It is further the purpose and intent of this division to avoid the suppression of
speech activities and the freedom of assembly protected by the First and Fourteenth
Amendments of the United States Constitution and the Colorado Constitution while seeking
to minimize disorderly conduct on and off the premises of entertainment establishments,
including, but not limited to, increased noise, traffic and parking problems, as well
as, an increase in crimes and municipal ordinance violations and to minimize the effect
of such activities on neighboring residential and commercial areas causing a deterioration
in the quality of life, overburdening of city services and a decrease in property
values thereby contributing to environmental blight and the erosion of the financial
health and well-being of the city.
As used in this division, the following terms shall have the following meanings:
Athletic exhibition means any athletic contest or race between persons, animals or vehicles where an
admission fee is charged.
Class 1 entertainment establishment means any entertainment establishment located within two thousand (2,000) feet of
a residential zone district or any legally occupied residence, which provides any
form of amplified entertainment except:
(1)
The playing of background music, including live performance, which is incidental,
and neither primary nor significant, to the business operation; or
(2)
Entertainment provided only by a music device.
Class 2 entertainment establishment means an entertainment establishment not meeting the definition of a class 1 entertainment
establishment.
Entertainment means any show, play, skit, musical review, cabaret, theater, children's theater,
dance hall, reception hall, dance club, dance production or performance, musical concert,
live or recorded musical performance, musical device, athletic exhibition, or the
production, presentation or provision of sights or sounds or visual or auditory sensations
that are designed to or may divert, entertain or otherwise appeal to members of the
public, which is produced by any means, including radio, phonograph, tape recorder,
compact disc player, digital recording, microphone, amplifier, musical instrument,
film, slide or movie projector, spotlights or interruptible or flashing light devices
and decoration.
Entertainment establishment means any business or place where any form of amplified entertainment occurs, whether
indoor or outdoor, which is offered or given to the public in excess of two (2) calendar
days in a calendar year, whether or not a fee is charged for admission thereto.
Music device means any machine or mechanism to procure or reproduce music or singing, such as
a juke box or a karaoke machine.

Flea market means a business operation or bazaar that consists of more than one (1) independent
vendor who is allocated space and does business on the premises and is not in charge
of the premises and where goods and/or services are offered for sale or exchange at
retail to the general public, either indoors or outside, or both. The term does not
include yard sales, auctions, pawnbrokers, retail business establishments, secondhand
dealers or any other business or activity where sales are made to the general public

the flea market operation.

Disturbances on premises. Licensees shall immediately report to the city police department any unlawful or
disorderly act that is committed on the premises on which the flea market is conducted.
(b)
Business hours. The times of operation shall be limited to the hours of 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. during
Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, unless specifically provided otherwise in the flea
market license.
(c)
Litter control. Litter shall not be allowed to leave the flea market property or collect on adjoining

The purpose and intent of this division is to regulate sexually oriented businesses,

(a)
License inspections . No application for a new license shall be approved until the applicant has completed
a self-inspection checklist on a city approved self-inspection form to ensure the
safety and habitability of the proposed-short-term rental unit. This self-inspection
must be completed for the initial license application and all renewal applications.
(b)
Additional inspections required . Prior to the approval of any short-term rental license application, the city manager
or their designee may deem a self-inspection of a short-term rental insufficient and
require the property to successfully pass an inspection performed by an inspector
employed by the city or approved by the city manager or their designee if the property,
in the twelve (12) months prior to the submission of the initial or renewal license
application, has been cited with more than one (1) code violation, has been the subject
of more than three (3) complaints to the city, or if the city manager or their designee
has other good cause to believe a self-inspection of the property may be insufficient
to ensure the safety, security, and habitability of the property is sufficient for
the property to serve as a short-term rental. The city will provide to the applicant,
in writing, the reason for the additional inspection requirement when such requirement
is imposed.
(c)
Other inspections. Nothing in this division shall be deemed to prohibit the city from conducting an
inspection of a short-term rental unit at any time for violations of state law, regulations,
or this Code or for other good cause shown.

(a)
License displayed . A copy of the licensee's short-term rental license shall be displayed prominently
and conspicuously within each short-term rental unit.
(b)
Advertisement . The license number shall be included in any advertisement of the short-term rental
unit.
(c)
Noise . Each short-term rental unit shall have the city's noise ordinance, section 6-2011 (unreasonable noise), posted in a visible location inside the short-term rental unit.
(d)
Signage . A short-term rental unit may not have any exterior signage. All short-term rental
units must have a sign that displays the current contact information of the owner
and/or the responsible agent, the Commerce City Police Department's non-emergency
number, and any information pertinent to the neighborhood where the short-term rental
is located including, but not limited to, parking restrictions, restrictions on noise
and amplified sound, trash collection schedules, relevant water restrictions, fire
evacuation routes, and any other information required by the city manager or applicable
to the surrounding neighborhood. The sign shall also comply with the following requirements:
(1)
The sign shall be posted on the interior of the front door of the short-term rental
unit.
(2)
The contents of the sign must be translated in both English and Spanish.
(3)
The sign must be legible at all times.
(e)
Safety requirements . A short-term rental unit shall be equipped with functioning smoke detectors, carbon
monoxide detectors, and a fire extinguisher. All such equipment shall be accessible
to guests of the short-term rental unit.
(f)
HOA rules and covenants . Nothing in this division shall be interpreted as preventing a functional homeowners
association from promulgating additional HOA rules and covenants to govern the operation
of short-term rental units.
(g)
Good neighbor guide required . All short-term rental units shall have a good neighbor guide available which shall,
at a minimum, inform the short-term rental occupants of the following:
(1)
Trash and recycling pick-up times and days as well as requirements for the storage
of trash collection bins;
(2)
Quiet hours and any other "quality of life" considerations for the neighborhood where
the short-term rental is located;
(3)
Designated parking areas and rules;
(4)
Emergency and non-emergency contact information for the police and fire departments
that service the neighborhood where the short-term rental is located;
(5)
The contact information for the licensee and the licensee's responsible agent;
(6)
Rules, hours, and regulations concerning the use of any of the neighborhood common
or public spaces; and
(7)
Any other rules or restrictions imposed on the short-term rental property by any applicable
HOA or other similar community association.
(h)
Conformance with land development code. In addition to any requirements of this section, all short-term rentals shall be
in conformance with the applicable provisions of the Commerce City Land Development
Code.
