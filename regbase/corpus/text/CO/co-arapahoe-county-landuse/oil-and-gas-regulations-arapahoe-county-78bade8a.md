## Oil and Gas Regulations
The Arapahoe County Energy Program oversees oil and gas operations for unincorporated Arapahoe County through the County's Oil and Gas Regulations . Prior to April 16, 2019, oil and gas development in the County and across the state was regulated almost entirely by the Colorado Oil and Gas Conservation Commission, which is now known as the Energy and Carbon Managment Commission. A change in state law in 2019 allowed local jurisdictions to draft oil and gas regulations. After more than a two-year process, which included public workshops and two online surveys, the Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) adopted the County's first ever Oil and Gas regulations in November 2021. Since then, the regulations have been amended three times, creating some of the strictest regulations in the state. The most recent update to the County's Oil and Gas Regulations occurred in December 2024 after BOCC approval. The adopted amendments include:
Require financial assurance of $120,000 for each approved wells on a pad. The cost will be adjusted annually for inflation. This ensures there are sufficient funds to cover the costs of plugging, abandoning and meeting the State’s requirement for site reclamation, which protects the environment and public interest.
Require drilling and permanent production equipment be operated with electric equipment and power where available.
Require air quality monitoring that will identify areas with poor air quality and pollutants responsible for it. This proposal would use air quality sensors and other devices to monitor air quality to protect public health.
Require water monitoring.
Create a County inspection program that would conduct site inspections at every oil and gas operation within unincorporated Arapahoe County. The inspections will be in addition to inspections done by the state Department of Public Health and Environment and the Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC).
Increase setbacks from oil and gas facilities for new development. Setbacks increase from 250 feet to 2,000 feet. Setbacks from a plugged and abandoned oil and gas well that was abandoned in 2014 or later increase from 150 feet to 250 feet.
Prohibit commercial injection wells for the disposal of wastewater to alleviate the risk of potential leaks and compromising the water table. During oil and gas drilling, operators will inject used, contaminated water from the hydraulic fracturing process into underground wells placed in deep rock formations for disposal—this regulation would prevent this practice.
The Oil and Gas Regulations are part of the County's Land Development Code and can be found on pages 312-350. Learn more about the history of the regulations.

### Oil and Gas Inspection Program
As part of recently adopted Oil and Gas regulations, the County has launched a new oil and gas inspection program. This program is geared to provide further protection of public health, safety, welfare and the environment and avoid and mitigate adverse impacts of energy development.
The Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) adopted the fees associated with the inspection program on July 22, 2025. Fees will be paid by oil and gas operators. Learn more about the program .
