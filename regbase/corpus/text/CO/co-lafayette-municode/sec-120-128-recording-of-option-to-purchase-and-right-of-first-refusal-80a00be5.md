This article shall be known and may be cited as the "City of Lafayette Water Rights
Dedication Ordinance."
(Ord. No. 2003-7, § 1, 4-1-03)
It is the intent and purpose of this article to further the goals of sound water management
by requiring the dedication of reliable water rights prior to any commitment by the
city to extend or supply treated water service to new customers, and thereby to assure
an adequate and stable supply of water for the City of Lafayette service area; to
prevent the abandonment of water rights to the detriment of the city; to ensure the
financial stability of the city water utility; and to promote the general welfare
of the public.
This article, in part, provides a supplemental requirement for annexation pursuant
to the Municipal Annexation Act of 1965, C.R.S. 1973, Section 31-12-101 et seq., and
is not to be construed as altering, modifying, eliminating or replacing any requirements
set forth in that act or any requirements set forth in other city ordinances or the
City Charter.
It is not the intent of this article to extend water service outside the boundaries
of the city when such extension would be inconsistent with the Lafayette Comprehensive
Plan or the City Charter, or when the extension of such service would render the city
incapable of meeting any of its present or future obligations within the city boundaries.
(Ord. No. 2003-7, § 1, 4-1-03)
As used in this article, unless the context otherwise requires:
Annexation means the act of attaching, adding, joining or uniting a parcel of land to the legal
entity known as the City of Lafayette.
Applicant means any person who submits an application to the city requesting annexation, replatting,
subdivision or water service extension approval.
Appurtenant means belonging to, accessory or incident to, adjunct, appended, or annexed to.
Average net annual stream depletion yield means the net stream depletion yield that has occurred historically, over a representative
time period, divided by the number of years within that time period.
CBT water means an allotment contract entitling the holder to a share of the water supply from
the Colorado Big Thompson Project administered by the NCWCD.
City staff means any employee of the city, as well as any person or entity that the city council
or employees of the city elect to consult.
Conveyance of legal title to water rights means the legal process by which legal title to the water rights to be dedicated
is transferred to the city by appropriate deed and assignment of any applicable corporate
stock, or, in the case of CBT water, approval of a transfer of one (1) or more units
of CBT water by the Board of Directors of the NCWCD.
Dedication means the total divestiture of property, without consideration, for use by the public,
and acceptance for such use by the city council.
Lease means any grant for permissive use that results in the creation of a landlord-tenant
relationship on a contractual basis.
NCWCD means the Northern Colorado Water Conservancy District.
Net annual stream depletion yield means that quantity of water, associated with a decreed right other than CBT water
offered for dedication, that is totally lost to the stream system in a given year,
as a result of the application of the water involved to a beneficial use. The net
annual stream depletion yield for a water right that has been decreed as being from
a nontributary source, shall be one hundred (100) percent of the water available for
diversion and beneficial use.
Nonresidential development means those areas of the city that are not or will not be zoned for residential occupancy,
and shall include, but not necessarily be limited to, developments containing commercial,
industrial, office and similar uses.
Plat is an accurately surveyed map or chart of a piece of land subdivided into one (1)
or more lots with streets, alleys, roads and other such avenues of transportation
delineated thereon and drawn to scale.
Relative standard deviation (also referred to as the coefficient of variation) means the standard deviation of yield divided by the average (mean) yield of a water
right.
Replat means to make a change in the original plat, or, where there is no original plat,
the act of creating a plat for previously unplatted property.
Residential multifamily development means those areas of the city where people reside or are intended to reside on a
permanent basis in a building used by two (2) or more families living independently
of each other in separate dwelling units. This definition does not include motels
or hotels but does include townhouses, condominiums, apartments and mobile home courts.
Each mobile home space shall be considered as one (1) dwelling unit.
Residential single-family development means those areas of the city where people reside or are intended to reside on a
permanent basis in a detached principal building other than a mobile home and which
principal building is designed for or used as a dwelling exclusively by one (1) family.
Safe net annual stream depletion yield means the minimum net annual stream depletion that is expected to occur.

each year's yield from the average yield, divided by the number of years of the study
period used in the determination less one (1). In equation form, the definition appears

Subdivide or subdivision shall have the same meaning as defined in Chapter 26 of this code.
Sufficient legal priority means that the water right(s) proposed for dedication shall have a sufficient probability
of being physically and legally available for diversion from the stream system, in
order to provide a dependable supply of water to the city. CBT water is hereby deemed
to have sufficient legal priority. A direct flow water right shall qualify only if
its priority is such that some water will be physically and legally available for
diversion from the stream system into the city's municipal water system each and every
year. Further, the water right shall have a relative standard deviation of net annual
stream depletion yield of no more than forty (40) percent; or have a ratio of safe
net annual stream depletion yield to average net annual stream depletion yield of
at least fifty-hundredths (0.50). A storage right shall qualify only if there is a
seventy (70) percent probability of the reservoir right yielding, each year, the amount
of water for which credit is given. In making these determinations, factors to be
considered shall include, but not necessarily be limited to, the adjudication date
and appropriation date of the water right, the decreed use(s), the historic usage
of the water proposed for dedication, the physical flow available in the stream, and
the administrative practices of the Office of the State Engineer.
Transferable net stream depletion yield means that amount of average net annual stream depletion yield that is capable of
being transferred to the city's municipal water system in an appropriate water court
proceeding.
Unit of CBT water means 1/310,000 of the water supply from the Colorado Big Thompson Project declared
available, from year to year, to the NCWCD by its Board of Directors.
Water right means a decreed right to use, in accordance with its priority, a certain portion
of the waters of the state by reason of the appropriation and beneficial use of the
same, or one (1) or more units of CBT water.

The city recognizes that certain nonresidential uses within residential developments
and nonresidential developments may, when built, require less than two-acre feet of
water per acre each year. Therefore, should an applicant seeking subdivision, replatting
or water service extension approval for a nonresidential subdivision believe that
the two-acre feet of water per acre requirement exceeds the amount of water needed
to serve each acre of the proposed development when completed, the applicant shall
have the option of presenting data to the director of public works documenting the
development's projected annual water needs.
The director of public works shall review the data so provided and may review any
other pertinent data including, but not limited to, the actual water usage of other
developments comparable to the proposed development. After reviewing the data, the
director of public works shall determine whether the two-acre feet of water per acre
requirement exceeds the amount of water that will be needed to serve each acre of
the proposed development when completed. If the director of public works determines
that the amount of water required to serve each acre of the nonresidential uses within
residential developments, or the nonresidential development, is less than two-acre
feet per year, an appropriate downward adjustment in the dedication requirement shall
be made by the director of public works.
All charges incurred by the city in conjunction with the review process shall be assessed
to the applicant. Payment of said charges shall be required as a condition precedent
to application approval. A decision of whether the data provided by an applicant is
sufficient for purposes of conducting a meaningful review shall be determined solely
by the city. Adequate assurance that the lands described in the application will not
develop in a manner such that the ultimate water usage exceeds the projected estimates
(in the form of platting restrictions, covenants running with the land or similar
legal mechanisms that will bind not only the applicant, but subsequent owners and
lessees of both the land and all structures contained thereon), must be provided by
the applicant. If, in the future, the annual water needs of the developed area exceed
the projected level of usage, the then current owner shall be responsible for providing
the city with the additional water rights to make up for any shortfall. Said water
rights shall meet the criteria of the water rights dedication ordinance then in effect.
Failure to comply with this requirement shall result in the automatic imposition of
a moratorium on the issuance of new water taps for the development and/or the discontinuation
of water service to the affected area.

shall be required to escrow the amount so determined. As the city incurs charges attributable
to the water transfer process, monies in an equivalent amount shall be withdrawn from
the escrow account for the benefit of the city. Any unused funds shall be returned
to the applicant. However, in the event that the charges incurred by the city exceed
the amount placed in escrow, then the applicant shall be responsible for making up
the difference. Failure to comply fully with this requirement shall result in the
institution of a moratorium on the extension of city utility services to the property
for which the water rights or cash (or other consideration) in lieu of water rights
were earmarked. At such time as the city is fully reimbursed for the expenses incurred
in the transfer process, the moratorium shall be lifted.

(a)
Time. Any person required to comply with the basic dedication requirement shall also grant
to the city the option to purchase any and all water rights that are appurtenant to
the land involved, but that are in excess of the basic dedication requirement or that
are deemed not to be satisfactory. Said option may be exercised by the city at any
time for a period of two (2) years following approval of the application for annexation,
subdivision, replatting or water service extension.
(b)
Price. The option price shall be that price agreed upon by the parties. If the parties do
not agree upon an option price within thirty (30) days after notice of the city's
intent to exercise its option is received by the owner, appraisal at the city's expense
will establish the price that reflects the fair market value of the water right(s).
(1)
The appraisal shall be conducted by one (1) appraiser appointed by the city, one (1)
appraiser appointed by the owner of the water rights, and a third appraiser to be
appointed by both parties' appraisers. The average of the three (3) appraisals shall
be the option price.
(2)
The terms and conditions of the payment and conveyance of said water rights shall
be those that are mutually agreeable to both parties. Nonetheless, it is expected
that payment and conveyance will be completed within one hundred eighty (180) days
of the establishment of the option price.
(Ord. No. 2003-7, § 1, 4-1-03)
(a)
Grant of right. In addition to the grant of said option to purchase by the applicant, there shall
be a grant to the city by the applicant of a right of first refusal regarding the
water rights subject to said option to purchase. If the city, for any reason, should
choose not to exercise its option to purchase, the city shall retain said right of
first refusal, in the event the water rights are sold independently of the land, for
a period of ten (10) years following final approval of annexation, subdivision, replatting,
or extension of water service.
(b)
Notice of period. If the owner of the water rights subject to said right of first refusal wishes to
sell said water rights to a third party, he shall give to the city at least ninety
(90) days' notice of his intention to effect a sale of said water rights by delivering
to the city a bona fide written offer to purchase made by a third party.
(c)
Exercise of right. During the ninety-day notice period provided for above, the city shall enjoy its
right of first refusal entitling it to purchase the water rights subject to its right
and proposed for sale. If within ninety (90) days following notice by the owner of
his intention to sell his water rights, the city chooses to exercise its right of
purchase, then the city shall pay to the owner the amount tendered to the owner in
the bona fide offer by the third party. In the event that the city determines not
to exercise its right to purchase the water rights offered for sale, the owner shall
be free to sell the water rights to the third party; provided, however, that any such
sale to a third party shall be pursuant to the terms and conditions that were the
same as those tendered to and refused by the city.
(Ord. No. 2003-7, § 1, 4-1-03)
