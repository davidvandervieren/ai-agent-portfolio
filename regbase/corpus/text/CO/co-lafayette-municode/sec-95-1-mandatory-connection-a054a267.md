(a)
Generally. This article is enacted to protect, preserve and promote the health, safety and welfare
of the citizens of the City of Lafayette, to provide with respect thereto for the
mandatory hookup of sources of effluent within the city to the city's water reclamation
system, to protect the capacity of the system by preventing the hookup of noneffluent
sources and by requiring disconnection of existing noneffluent sources thereof, to
ensure that new connections are properly designed by providing for a system of plan
approval and on-site inspection and to protect the water reclamation facility and
all related mains, laterals and equipment, the city's workers, the organisms used
in treatment of effluent and the waters of the state by providing for either pretreatment
and/or the nonintroduction of hazardous or deleterious substances into the city's
system.
(b)
Strict liability. The Lafayette City Council, recognizing the threats upon the frail ecology and expressing
great concern for its protection, declares that merely committing any act prohibited
by this Chapter 95 and/or orders of the city issued under the provisions of this chapter, or failing
to perform any act required by this chapter or said orders of the city constitutes
a violation of this chapter. The violator's intent is not a defense to any violation
of this chapter or said order of the city, but intent may be considered as a matter
in determining aggravation or mitigation.

Building drain shall mean that part of the lower horizontal piping of a drainage system that receives

procedures.
Combined conduit shall mean a water reclamation conduit intended to receive both effluent and storm
or surface water.
Deleterious wastes shall mean any wastes contained in effluent that would be harmful or damaging to

city.

institutions. It is equivalent to normal effluent.
Easement shall mean an acquired legal right for the specific use of land owned by others whether
required by written conveyance, prescription or otherwise.
Effluent shall mean the combination of liquid or water-carried waste conducted away from residences,
business buildings and institutions that is known as domestic effluent, together with
the liquid or water-carried waste resulting from manufacturing process employed in
industrial establishments, including the washing, cleaning or drain water form such
process, that is known as industrial waste.

free of floatable fat if it is properly pretreated, and the effluent does not interfere

Amalgam separator required.
1.
Definitions. For the purpose of this section the following words and phrases shall mean as follows:
"Amalgam" means any mixture or blending of mercury with another metal or with an alloy
used in dental applications.
"Amalgam separator" means a type of wastewater treatment equipment that is designed
to remove amalgam particles and dissolved mercury from the wastewater discharged by
dental practices.
"Amalgam waste" means any waste containing mercury or residues from the preparation
of amalgam. This includes any waste generated or collected by chair-side traps, screens,
filters, vacuum system filters, amalgam separators, elemental mercury, or amalgam
capsules.
(i)
"Noncontact amalgam" includes scrap or excess amalgam after a procedure is completed,
as well as the remaining amalgam capsule.
(ii)
"Contact amalgam" means scrap that has come into contact with a patient or body fluids.
Examples include: extracted teeth and scrap collected in chair-side traps and vacuum
pump filters.
"Best management practices" (BMPs) means a set of operational standards designed to
minimize the environmental impact and human exposure to mercury.
"Dental practice" means any dental facility that discharges wastewater from the placement
or removal of amalgam.
"Exempt dental practice" means any dental facility in which no dentist places or removes
amalgams and/or teeth containing amalgams.
2.
[Compliance; waiver.]
A.
Any dental practice that discharges wastewater generated from the placement or removal
of amalgam shall, within six (6) months from the effective date of this ordinance,
implement and certify to the superintendent compliance with the BMPs set forth in
this section. The dental practice shall also, within one (1) year from the effective
date of this ordinance, install, operate and maintain an amalgam separator in accordance
with the requirements set forth in this section.
B.
Waivers will be granted by the superintendent to exempt dental practices. To receive
a waiver a dental practice must submit a written waiver request to the superintendent
within six (6) months of the effective date of this ordinance.
3.
[Amalgam separator requirements.] Amalgam separators shall meet the following requirements:
A.
Amalgam separator design. An amalgam separator shall be International Organization for Standardization (ISO)
11143 certified and shall be designed and approved for a flow rate capable of handling
the maximum volume discharged from the dental practice it serves.
(i)
The amalgam separator must be tested according to the most current ISO 11143 standard.
Each dental practice shall select a separator with the highest efficiency level attainable
for the practice's working conditions.
(ii)
If ISO increases the acceptable standard, the dental practice must comply with the
new standard within five (5) years of ISO's adoption.
B.
Installation. The amalgam separator shall be installed so that all amalgam-contaminated wastewater
will pass through the unit before being discharged into any water reclamation conduit.
C.
Inspection and maintenance. Each dental practice shall be responsible for inspection and maintenance of the amalgam
separator.
D.
Disposal. Waste removed from the amalgam separator shall be collected and handled in accordance
with the manufacturer's instructions and as described in the BMPs for this section.
4.
Best management practices. Every dental practice shall comply with the following BMPs:
A.
Equipment. The dental practice shall ensure that all dental chairs are equipped with chair-side
traps and that all vacuum pumps are equipped with traps and filters. All equipment
shall be cleaned and maintained in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.
B.
Clean-up procedures. The dental practice shall use nonchlorine or nonoxidizing disinfectants and neutral
cleaners. When cleaning filters or collecting scrap amalgam, the dental practice shall
not rinse filters or traps over sinks or drains. Each dental practice shall maintain
a mercury spill kit and train all staff on clean-up procedures. Spilled amalgam must
be cleaned up immediately in accordance with the most current Environmental Protection
Agency (EPA) procedures as set forth in: http://www.epa.gov/epaoswer/hazwaste/mercury/faq/spills.htm
C.
Salvaging. All contact and noncontact scrap amalgam shall be salvaged and stored in structurally
sound, tightly closed and appropriately labeled containers.
D.
Disposal. The dental practice shall not dispose of any amalgam waste. All amalgam waste shall
be removed from the dental practice only by an authorized amalgam waste recycler.

Use of proper materials and construction techniques. The size, slope, alignment, materials of construction of all water reclamation conduits,
including building water reclamation conduits, and the methods to be used in excavating,

A.S.T.M. and W.P.C.F. Manual of Practice No. 9 shall apply. Whenever possible, the

below the basement floor. In all buildings in which any building drain is too low

water reclamation conduit unless such connection is approved by the superintendent
for purposes of disposal of polluted surface drainage. The connection of the building
water reclamation conduit into the public water reclamation conduit shall conform
to the requirements of the building and plumbing code or other applicable rules and
regulations of the city or the procedures set forth in appropriate specifications
of the A.S.T.M. and the W.P.C.F. Manual of Practice No. 9. All such connections shall
be made gastight and watertight and verified by proper testing. Any deviation from
the prescribed procedures and materials must be approved by the superintendent before
installation.

(a)
Notification of violation. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, the superintendent may
serve upon that user a written notice of violation. Within seven (7) days of the receipt
of this notice, an explanation of the violation and a plan for the satisfactory correction
and prevention thereof, to include specific required actions, shall be submitted by
the user to the superintendent. Submission of this plan in no way relieves the user
of liability for any violations occurring before or after receipt of the notice of
violation. Nothing in this section shall limit the authority of the superintendent
to take any action, including emergency actions or any other enforcement action, without
first issuing a notice of violation.
(b)
Consent orders. The superintendent may enter into consent orders, assurances of voluntary compliance,
or other similar documents establishing an agreement with any user responsible for
noncompliance. Such documents shall include specific action to be taken by the user
to correct the noncompliance within a time period specified by the document. Such
documents shall have the same force and effect as the administrative orders issued
pursuant to subsections (d) and (e) of this section, and shall be judicially enforceable.
(c)
Show cause hearing. The superintendent may order a user that has violated, or continues to violate, any
provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, to appear before the superintendent
and show cause why any proposed enforcement action should not be taken. Notice shall
be served on the user specifying the time and place for the hearing, the proposed
enforcement action, the reasons for such action, and a request that the user show
cause why the proposed enforcement action should not be taken. The notice of the hearing
shall be served personally or by registered or certified mail (return receipt requested)
at least seven (7) days prior to the hearing. Such notice may be served on any authorized
representative of the user. A show cause hearing shall not be a bar against, or prerequisite
for, taking any other action against the user.
(d)
Compliance orders. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, the superintendent may
issue an order to the user responsible for the discharge directing that the user come
into compliance within a specified time. If the user does not come into compliance
within the time provided, water reclamation service may be discontinued unless adequate
treatment facilities, devices, or other related appurtenances are installed and properly
operated. Compliance orders also may contain other requirements to address the noncompliance,
including additional self-monitoring and management practices designed to minimize
the amount of pollutants discharged into the water reclamation system. Issuance of
a compliance order shall not relieve the user of liability for any violation, including
any continuing violation. Issuance of a compliance order shall not be a bar against,
or a prerequisite for, taking any other action against the user.
(e)
Cease and desist orders. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, or that the user's past
violations are likely to recur, the superintendent may issue an order to the user
directing it to cease and desist all such violations and directing the user to:
(1)
Immediately comply with all requirements; and
(2)
Take such appropriate remedial or preventive action as may be needed properly to address
a continuing or threatened violation, including halting operations and/or terminating
the discharge.
Issuance of a cease and desist order shall not be a bar against, or a prerequisite
for, taking any other action against the user.
(f)
Emergency suspensions. The superintendent may immediately suspend a user's discharge, after informal notice
to the user, whenever such suspension is necessary to stop an actual or threatened
discharge that reasonably appears to present or cause an imminent or substantial endangerment
to the health or welfare of persons. The superintendent may also immediately suspend
a user's discharge, after notice and opportunity to respond, that threatens to interfere
with the operation of the POTW, or that presents, or may present, an endangerment
to the environment.
(1)
Any user notified of a suspension of its discharge shall immediately stop or eliminate
its contribution. In the event of a user's failure immediately to comply voluntarily
with the suspension order, the superintendent may take such steps as are deemed necessary,
including immediate severance of the water reclamation connection, to prevent or minimize
damage to the POTW, its receiving stream, or endangerment to any individuals. The
superintendent may allow the user to recommence its discharge when the user has demonstrated
to the satisfaction of the superintendent that the period of endangerment has passed,
unless the termination proceedings in subsection (g) of this section are initiated
against the user.
(2)
A user that is responsible, in whole or in part, for any discharge presenting imminent
endangerment shall submit a detailed written statement, describing the causes of the
harmful contribution and the measures taken to prevent any future occurrence, to the
superintendent prior to the date of any show cause or termination under subsections
(c) or (g) of this section.
Nothing in this section shall be interpreted as requiring a hearing prior to any emergency
suspension under this section.
(g)
Termination of discharge. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, the superintendent may
terminate such user's discharge. Such user will be notified of the proposed termination
of its discharge and be offered an opportunity to show cause, under subsection (c)
of this section, why the proposed action should not be taken. Exercise of this option
by the superintendent shall not be a bar to, or a prerequisite for, taking any other
action against the user.
(h)
Injunctive relief. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, the superintendent, through
the city attorney, may petition a court of proper jurisdiction for the issuance of
a temporary or permanent injunction, as appropriate, that restrains or compels the
specific performance of the effluent discharge order or other requirement imposed
by this chapter on activities of the user. The superintendent may also seek such other
action as is appropriate for legal and/or equitable relief, including a requirement
for the user to conduct environmental remediation. A petition for injunctive relief
shall not be a bar against, or a prerequisite for, taking any other action against
a user.
(i)
Recovery of costs of investigation. When the superintendent finds that a user has violated, or continues to violate,
any provision of this chapter or any order issued hereunder, the superintendent may
bill the user for the city's costs of investigation of such violation. Such costs
may include, but are not limited to, cost of time of city employees, laboratory costs
incurred, and cost of transport or shipping of samples.
(j)
Remedies nonexclusive. The remedies provided for in this chapter are not exclusive. The superintendent may
take any, all or any combination of these actions against a noncompliant user.
