(a)
The owner of any property in Lafayette who installs and dedicates street, sidewalk,
water main, sewer main, bicycle trail, bridge, storm drainage facility or other public
improvements, may apply for the establishment of a reimbursement assessment district
to obtain reimbursement of a portion of the cost from the owners of other property
specially benefitted by the improvements.
(b)
If the city constructs any such improvement which specially benefits the owners of
private property, it may establish a reimbursement assessment district for its own
benefit, subject, however, to all the terms and conditions of this article.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
(a)
Property is eligible for inclusion in a district if it will be or has been specially
benefitted by a street, sidewalk, bicycle trail, storm drainage facility, bridge,
water main, sewer main or other improvement constructed and dedicated to the city
or constructed and owned by the city.
(b)
Property located outside the city limits may be included in a reimbursement district
upon the application of the person installing the improvements. Such inclusion, however,
shall be contingent and no assessment may be collected with respect to such property
unless it has been annexed to the city prior to the last date to which the assessment
may be deferred pursuant to section 92-4 (a), or ten (10) years from the effective date of the ordinance establishing or renewing
the reimbursement district, whichever date is later.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
(a)
Properties within a reimbursement district shall be assessed in such a manner as equitably
to apportion the cost among all properties specially benefitted by the improvement,
but no property shall be assessed an amount greater than the special benefit received
by it. In the absence of unusual circumstances requiring a different method, improvements,
other than bridges, shall be subject to reimbursement on the basis of front footage
for streets, sidewalks and trails and on the basis of acres served in the case of
water main, sewer main and storm drainage improvements.
(b)
The cost to be apportioned within a reimbursement district shall be the reasonable
cost of installing the improvement, but not including the cost of any part or portion
which solely benefits the developer such as curb cuts or main connections to serve
his property. Engineering, surveying, legal and similar costs, not to exceed fifteen
(15) percent of construction costs, may be included for determining the cost. When
possible, reimbursable construction costs shall be based upon the lowest responsible
bid of three (3) bids obtained by the developer. If the city constructs any improvement
which specially benefits the owner of private property and, in connection therewith,
is granted an easement by the owner without consideration, then the owner shall receive
a credit against any reimbursement assessment obligation established hereunder in
an amount equal to the fair market value of such easement; provided however, that
nothing herein shall be construed to give a credit to an owner that would otherwise
be required to grant such an easement upon development, as the term is defined in section 26-8-1 of this Code, of the owner's property.
(c)
Except as hereinafter limited, the term "special benefit" shall mean any benefit conferred
upon a property which is greater or different in kind from that conferred upon properties
in the city as a whole by an improvement. Among the factors to be considered in determining
the existence of a special benefit are:
(1)
Increased market value.
(2)
Improvement in safety or convenience of access.
(3)
Improved drainage.
(4)
Alleviation of health or sanitation hazards.
(5)
Adaptability of the property to a superior or more profitable use.
(6)
Improved availability of public water or sewer service to the property.
(7)
In the case of undeveloped property, the installation of an improvement which would
otherwise be required upon development of the property.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
(a)
The assessment shall be due and payable upon the effective date of the ordinance imposing
the assessment; provided, however, if any property included within a reimbursement
district is unplatted, undeveloped or has not connected to the improvement at the
time the assessment is imposed, the owner of such property may defer payment of the
assessment until such time as the property is platted, a building permit is issued,
or the property connects to the improvement, whichever shall first occur after the
assessment is imposed; provided however, that upon good cause shown, the city council
may by resolution alter or vary the act or event that triggers the requirement of
payment of deferred assessments.
(b)
If the owner elects to defer payment of the assessment, the payment shall include
interest at the rate provided by C.R.S. 5-12-102(4)(b) for judgments, as amended from
time to time. Such interest shall commence on the effective date of the ordinance
imposing the assessment. Notwithstanding any of the foregoing, the city council may
by resolution, upon good cause shown, vary the rate of interest, suspend interest
for a certain time period or waive interest entirely for deferred assessments.
(c)
A reimbursement district shall terminate ten (10) years from the effective date of
the ordinance establishing it and any property which is platted or connected to the
improvement or for which a building permit is issued thereafter shall not be subject
to reimbursement assessment; provided however, that the city council may, prior to
such termination and by ordinance, renew for not more than ten (10) years the reimbursement
district and reimbursement assessment as to all properties for which the assessment
is not then paid in full upon a finding of special benefit as to each such property
in accordance with the standards contained in section 92-3 of this chapter. Said renewal ordinance shall be filed as a notice applicable to
each such property in the office of the county clerk and recorder
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
(a)
Application.
(1)
The owner of any property who has previously installed one or more public improvements
may file with the city administrator an application for a reimbursement district.
The application shall include the nature, location, and cost of the improvements,
a description of the proposed district and a legal description of each of the individual
properties within it, the names and addresses of the property owners within the district,
the proposed manner of assessment and the amount proposed to be assessed against each
property for each improvement. Nothing herein shall be construed to allow an original
owner to seek reimbursement from a current owner of property once owned by the original
owner.
(2)
The owner of any property who proposes to install one or more public improvements
may file with the city administrator an application for a reimbursement district.
The application shall include the nature, location and anticipated cost of the improvements,
a description of the proposed district and a legal description of each of the individual
properties within it, the names and addresses of the property owners within the district,
the proposed manner of assessment and the amount proposed to be assessed against each
property for each improvement. When possible, the application shall be accompanied
by three (3) construction bids. Nothing herein shall be construed to allow an owner
to seek reimbursement from a future owner of property, which property is owned at
the time of the application by the owner making application.
(b)
Notice. Within thirty (30) days of the city's receipt of a complete application, a notice
shall be mailed, by the applicant, by registered mail to the owners of each property
within the proposed district together with a copy of the application. The notice shall
state that any owner may file, within fifteen (15) days, a written request for an
administrative hearing to contest the proposal. The request for hearing shall state
in general terms the grounds of objection.
(c)
Hearing. Upon written request for an administrative hearing, the city council shall schedule
a hearing to be held within sixty (60) days and shall so notify the applicant and
all objecting parties. The hearing shall be quasi-judicial in nature.
(d)
Adoption of ordinance. After the hearing, or if no hearing is requested, the city council shall then consider
the adoption of an ordinance establishing a reimbursement district and imposing appropriate
assessments which may be, in the discretion of the city council, liens upon the property
assessed. If the city council determines that the assessments shall be liens upon
the property assessed, it shall so state in the ordinance and cause a notice of the
lien to be filed for record in the office of the county clerk and recorder. If the
city council determines that the assessments shall not be liens upon the property
assessed, then it shall cause a notice of reimbursement assessment obligation to be
filed for record in the office of the county clerk and recorder.
(e)
Judicial review. Any reimbursement assessment ordinance adopted hereunder shall be subject to judicial
review pursuant to Colorado Rule of Civil Procedure No. 106.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
After the adoption of a reimbursement ordinance, it shall be the responsibility of
the person entitled to reimbursement to keep the city apprised of his current address.
In the event of the recipient's death, it shall be his personal representative's responsibility
to notify the city of the name and address of the person entitled to receive future
reimbursements. In the case of a corporate recipient, a successor shall be designated
prior to dissolution. Failure to comply with the developer's responsibilities shall
constitute abandonment of all rights of reimbursement and shall be grounds for repeal
of the reimbursement ordinance and refund of any assessments received by the city
on behalf of the developer after the abandonment.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
Upon payment of assessment or expiration of the reimbursement term, whichever occurs
first, the city shall, upon request, issue a written release to the owner of assessed
property.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
(a)
In lieu of the procedure set forth herein, the developer and the owners of any property
that would be subject to assessment may contract for reimbursement in such manner
and amount as they deem appropriate and the city will, at the request of the parties,
collect the reimbursement at the time of connection, platting, or as otherwise agreed.
(b)
In the event that a developer contracts with the owner of a portion of the property
which would be subject to reimbursement assessment, such property shall be excluded
from the district, but no property within the district shall be assessed in an amount
proportionally greater than that provided by contract.
(c)
The city council hereby ratifies and affirms all previously approved reimbursement
agreements, development requirements or any conditions of approval heretofore entered
into by the city and expresses its intent that the adoption of this chapter 92 not affect the validity of such preexisting contracts, requirements or conditions.
(Ord. No. 1999-39, § 1, 10-5-99)
