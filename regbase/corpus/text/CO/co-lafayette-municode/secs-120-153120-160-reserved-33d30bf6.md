It is the intent and purpose of this article to obtain and maintain safe drinking
water supplies for the citizens of Lafayette.
(Ord. No. 1986-19, § 1, 5-6-86)
Pursuant to section 31-15-707(1)(b), Colorado Revised Statutes 1973, as amended, the
jurisdiction of the City of Lafayette is hereby extended beyond the municipal boundaries
to include the following stream segments:
South Boulder Creek from a point 6,800 feet south and 2,200 feet east of the southwest
corner of Section 15, Township 1 South, Range 71 West, 6th P.M., to the point where
South Boulder Creek intersects South Boulder Road in Section 3, Township 1 South,
Range 70 West, 6th P.M.
Dry Creek a/k/a the New Dry Creek Carrier, from a point 1,500 feet north and 700 feet
east of the southwest corner of Section 36, Township 1 North, Range 70 West, 6th P.M.
to its confluence with Boulder Creek at a point 2,600 feet north and 2,100 feet east
of the southwest corner of Section 17, Township 1 North, Range 69 West, 6th P.M.
Boulder Creek from a point 2,100 feet south and 1,000 feet east of the northwest corner
of Section 23, Township 1 North, Range 70 West, 6th P.M., to the headgate of the Lower
Boulder Ditch in the SW ¼ SW ¼, Section 16, Township 1 North, Range 69 West, 6th P.M.
(Ord. No. 1986-19, § 1, 5-6-86)
(a)
The city staff is directed to conduct a survey of all natural and man related activities
within the stream segments above defined which are now or may in the future adversely
affect the quality of the Lafayette municipal water supply.
(b)
A summary of the adverse effects of each activity noted during the survey shall be
prepared.
(c)
To the extent that a natural activity having an adverse effect on water quality can
be corrected without adverse effects to the property of others (such as the removal
of beavers to eliminate giardia), the city staff is directed to control, remove or
eliminate the natural activity.
(d)
All other activities shall be evaluated on a case by case basis, once the specific
effect on Lafayette's water supplies is noted. The city staff and city attorney are
directed to coordinate with other local and state agencies having concurrent jurisdiction
over the affected stream segments to promote the implementation of rules and regulations
which will protect the municipal water supplies to the City of Lafayette and to enforce
all rules and regulations once enacted. To the extent that any person or entity is
conducting an activity that results in a deterioration of the quality of water flowing
in the above defined stream segments below the minimum drinking water standards set
by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, the city attorney, at the direction of
the city council, shall pursue whatever administrative and judicial remedies are available
to the city to eliminate such activities.
(Ord. No. 1986-19, § 1, 5-6-86)
