Editor's note— Ord. No. 1988-25, § 1, adopted Dec. 6, 1988, and approved by the voters at a special
election held Jan. 17, 1989, amended Ch. X, §§ 10.1—10.4. to read as herein set out.
Indebtedness of the city shall be incurred and limited as provided in the Constitution
of the State of Colorado as the same may from time to time be amended.
Unless otherwise provided herein, the following obligations (I) do not constitute
indebtedness of the city, (II) shall be issued without a vote of the electors, and
(III) shall not be included when determining the power of the city to incur indebtedness:
(a)
The council shall have the power to issue special improvement district bonds. Where
said bonds are secured solely by property benefited by any special or local improvement
within any special improvement district, the voter approval required for the issuance
of said bonds shall be by the owners of a majority of the property within the boundaries
of said district unless the council shall provide otherwise by ordinance; except that
where the owners owning one hundred (100) percent of the property within the boundaries
of any special district agree in writing to waive their right to an election, the
council may issue said bonds without an election.
(b)
The council shall have the power to borrow money, issue bonds, or otherwise extend
its credit for the purpose of financing the construction, acquisition, improvement,
or extension of a municipally owned and operated water, electric, gas, or sewer system
or other utility or income producing project, if such bonds or other obligations shall
be payable solely out of the net revenue to be derived from the operation of such
systems, utilities, or projects.
(c)
The council shall have the power to borrow money, issue bonds, or otherwise extend
its credit for any municipal purpose and may make such bonds or other obligations
payable solely from the gross or net revenue received from any municipal occupation
tax, sales tax, use tax, cigarette tax, lodging tax, or any other excise tax. Such
net revenue of any municipal occupation tax, sales tax, use tax, cigarette tax, lodging
tax, or any other excise tax also may be pledged as additional security for the payment
of the system, utility, or project revenue bonds described in paragraph (b) hereof.
(Ord. No. 965, 1-4-83; Ord. No. 1988-25, § 1, 12-6-88; Ord. No. 1993-39, § 1, 8-17-93)
Editor's note— Ordinance No. 1993-39, adopted Aug. 17, 1993, amending § 10.1, was approved at referendum
on Nov. 2, 1993.
The terms and interest rate of all bonds issued by the city shall be fixed by the
authorizing ordinance and such bonds shall be sold by public or private sale to the
best advantage of the city. All bonds may contain provisions for calling the same
at designated periods prior to the final due date, with or without the payment of
a prior redemption premium.
(Ord. No. 742, § 1, 10-4-77; Ord. No. 1988-25, § 1, 12-6-88)
The City may enter into lease option contracts for the purchase of property or capital
equipment. Each of such contracts shall contain a provision that the financial obligations
of the city in subsequent fiscal years are contingent upon funds for the purpose under
said contract being appropriated, budgeted, and otherwise made available and any such
contract shall not be deemed to create any City multiple fiscal year direct or indirect
debt or other financial obligation whatsoever for purposes of Section 20(4)(b) of
Article X of the state constitution.
(Ord. No. 1988-25, § 1, 12-6-88; Ord. No. 1993-36, § 1, 8-17-93)
Editor's note— Ordinance No. 1993-36, adopted Aug. 17, 1993, amending § 10.3, was approved at referendum
on Nov. 2, 1993.
The Council shall have the power and it shall be its duty to refund all outstanding
bonds and other obligations whenever the same is advantageous to the city. Subject
to the limitations on indebtedness set forth in section 10.1 hereof, refunding obligations may be issued without a vote of the electors in any
principal amount necessary to accomplish the refunding.
(Ord. No. 1988-25, § 1, 12-6-88)
