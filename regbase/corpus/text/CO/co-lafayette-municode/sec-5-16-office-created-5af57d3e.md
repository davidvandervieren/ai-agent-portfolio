(a)
Declaration of vacancy . When a vacancy on city council occurs in accordance with sections 5.2 , 5.4 , or 5.5 of the Charter, the remaining members of the council will declare the vacancy by
resolution at the next regular or special council meeting after the vacancy occurs.
The date the council approves the resolution declaring the vacancy shall be the date
the vacancy "occurs" for purposes of the timelines in section 5.7 of the Charter.
(b)
Notice of vacancy and request for applications . If council is required to fill the vacancy by appointment in accordance with section 5.7 of the Charter, then within three (3) working days after the vacancy is declared
in accordance with (a) of this section, the city clerk will post notice of the vacancy
and a request for applications on the city's website. The notice must include the
following:
(1)
Announcement of the council vacancy;
(2)
Reference to section 5.1 of the Charter regarding eligibility for elective office;
(3)
A statement that applications to fill the vacant office may be obtained from the city
clerk and on the city's website;
(4)
The deadline, including date and time, by which applications must be received by the
city clerk, which deadline shall be fourteen (14) days after the date of the notice,
unless council sets a shorter or longer time in the resolution declaring the vacancy
under (a) of this section; and
(5)
A statement that submitted applications will be posted online and made available for
public viewing at city hall.
(c)
Applications available to public . Within two (2) working days after the application deadline, the city clerk will
post copies of all applications received by the application deadline on the city's
website, and will make all applications available to the public for viewing at city
hall. Prior to making the applications available for viewing by the public, the city
clerk shall redact each applicant's home address, phone number(s), and email address.
(d)
Interviews of applicants . The council will decide by motion at a regular or special meeting which of the applicants
for the vacant council office it desires to interview. When deciding which applicants
to interview, the council will consider only those applicants who submitted timely
applications. The council will interview the selected applicants at a regular or special
meeting, and the interviews will be open to the public.
(e)
Public input session . Following interviews, but before appointment, the council will hold at least one
(1) public input session to allow comments from the general public. The time, manner
and location of such public input session shall be determined by the council.
(f)
Appointment by city council . The council will, by motion and voice vote, appoint an applicant to fill the vacant
council office in accordance with subsection 5.7 (a) of the Charter. The council's discussion regarding applicants and the motion will
be open to the public. If the vacant council office is not filled by appointment within
the time permitted in section 5.7 of the Charter, the vacancy shall be filled in accordance with subsection 5.7 (b) of the Charter.

Attend all city council meetings and participate in discussions in an advisory capacity.
(9)

the council and of the city administration.

Department heads are accountable for their activities in their departments in the
technical and professional areas. The city administrator, as administrator for the
council, is the first contact upward for department heads in the city organization.
The city administrator is expected to keep department heads informed of all administrative,
legal and fiscal matters affecting their departments, and to work in harmony with
them in order that each department performs in the most efficient, economical, and
practical method to achieve goals and accomplish projects. Department heads shall
not initiate any conversation with any council member concerning any administrative
policy or city matter unless they have first discussed the situation with the city
administrator. The city administrator may request a full and detailed narrative from
a department head which includes opinions and positions with regard to any administrative
policy or city matter which a department head wishes to discuss with a council member.
A department head shall not forward to city council or a council member any form of
communication without the prior knowledge of the city administrator. The city administrator
may require a department head to provide the city administrator with any and all supporting
data or information relating to the subject matter of the communication to be forwarded
to the city council or a council member prior to the communication.
Department heads shall have the right to request that information which is deemed
by them to be sufficiently important and pressing be brought to the attention of the
city council. The city administrator shall bring such information to the city council's
attention in an expeditious manner.
The city council or members of the city council may contact department heads directly
for information or facts regarding city matters at their discretion. A department
head so contacted shall promptly inform the city administrator of the nature of the
contact and the information provided by the department head to the city council or
council member.

(a)
Boards and commissions are established by city council to advise the city council
on matters within the scope determined by council or as authorized by the city Charter.
Boards and commissions do not have authority to direct city employees or their work.
The city manager may designate a staff member to serve as a non-voting staff liaison
to a board or commission to assist with drafting agendas, ensuring compliance with
meeting notice requirements, and other similar tasks.
(b)
The city council:
(1)
May appoint a current council member or city staff as advisory, non-voting members;
and
(2)
May dissolve, combine, or suspend the operation of a board or commission not required
by the city Charter.
(c)
Each city board or commission shall:
(1)
Hold regular meetings no more than once a month unless approved by the city manager;
(2)
Keep minutes of its meetings and records of its actions, which are publicly available;
(3)
Elect a chair, vice chair, and a secretary unless a secretary is provided by the city
manager;
(4)
Adopt rules of meeting procedure. Until such rules are adopted, meetings shall be
conducted under the simplified Bob's Rules of Order, or otherwise conducted in an
orderly and efficient manner;
(5)
Hold regular meetings noticed in compliance with Colorado law regarding open meetings,
and provide an opportunity for public comment at the meeting as provided for in the
board's or commission's rules of procedure;
(6)
Be composed of voting members who are eligible for appointed office pursuant to Section 5.1 of the city Charter; and
(7)
Include in meetings a city employee appointed by the city manager who will serve as
a staff liaison and secretary.
(d)
Quorum . Unless otherwise provided by law or ordinance, a majority of the voting members
in office, or three (3) members, whichever is greater, shall constitute a quorum.
In the absence of a quorum, no business shall be conducted by the board or commission,
except rescheduling of the meeting and adjournment.
(e)
All members of city boards and commissions shall serve without pay and shall not hold
any other office in the city except for an appointment to other boards or commissions.
All members shall be appointed by city council and shall serve at the pleasure of
city council. Members may be eligible for reimbursement for expenses subject to approval
of the city manager.
(f)
Voting required . If a member of a city board or commission is present at a meeting and refuses to
vote, the member's vote shall be recorded in the affirmative.
(g)
Term lengths . A full term length for each board shall be four (4) years for the planning commission,
one (1) year for the youth advisory board, and three (3) years for all other boards
and commissions established under this article.
(h)
Term limits .
(1)
Members of boards and commissions established under this article or the city Charter
shall be subject to a limit of three (3) consecutive terms except for members of the
youth advisory board.
(2)
Council may grant exceptions to the limit of terms in the event that there are no
other qualified applicants for a board or commission.
(i)
Conduct of members . Board and commission members shall adhere to the city's code of ethics ( chapter 42 of the Code) and shall endeavor to work collaboratively with staff to advise the
city council and carry out their assigned duties and responsibilities.
( Ord. No. 2025-20 , § 1, 10-14-25)
(a)
So long as it is technologically feasible for the city, the boards and commissions
may adopt rules of procedure to determine whether to hold meetings in-person, remotely,
or meetings that permit simultaneous in-person and remote participation by board or
commission members, the public, or other participants, provided that a fully remote
meeting is available to the public by telephone or other remote means and the board
or commission provides a means for public participation when applicable.
(b)
Such rules may allow individual members of the board or commission to attend meetings
remotely through telephonic or other remote means, provided that such means allow
for the public to hear the board or commission member and for the board or commission
member to hear both members of the public recognized to speak and other members of
the board or commission, and provided that all votes are taken by roll call. In addition,
any such rules adopted by the planning commission must provide that members of the
planning commission attending remotely are able to hear all testimony, presentations,
and deliberations, and see or review all documentary evidence presented in any quasi-judicial
hearing.
(c)
All boards and commissions will consult with their staff liaison to determine whether
remote meetings or remote participation is technologically feasible.
(d)
Rules adopted by a board or commission in accordance with this section are subject
to review and approval by the city attorney.
(e)
Definitions . As used in this section, the following terms shall have the following meaning:
(1)
The term "attend" means active participation either in person or remotely if allowed
by the board or commission's rules of procedure.
(2)
The term "technologically feasible" shall mean there are adequate and available staffing,
equipment, and telecommunications connections available to the city to allow for the
public to hear the board or commission members and other participants in the meeting
and for the board or commission members and other participants in the meeting to hear
members of the public recognized to speak, as applicable.

(a)
Scope. The historic preservation commission shall be an advisory board, and shall advise
the city council on matters related to preserving the historic character of the city,
and shall have the duties and responsibilities set forth in this section and as provided
in chapter 47 of the Code.
(b)
Membership. The historic preservation commission shall consist of seven (7) regular members and
two (2) non-voting ex officio members consisting of one (1) member of the city council and one (1) member of the
planning commission designated by the council.
(c)
Duties and responsibilities. In addition to other tasks that the city council may assign to it, the historic preservation
commission shall:
(1)
Adopt criteria for review of historic resources and for review of proposals to alter,
demolish, or move designated resources, that are in addition to and consistent with
the criteria set forth in this chapter.
(2)
Review resources nominated for designation as either an historic landmark or historic
district and recommend that the city council designate by ordinance those resources
qualifying for such designation.
(3)
Review and determine the appropriateness of any application for alterations to a designated
historic landmark or any structure in an historic district.
(4)
Review and determine the appropriateness of any application for moving or demolishing
an historic landmark.
(5)
Provide referrals, resources, and assistance to owners of historic properties on physical
and financial aspects of preservation, renovation, rehabilitation, and reuse, including
nomination to the Colorado and/or National Register of Historic Places.
(6)
Develop and assist in public education programs including, but not limited to, walking
tours, brochures, a marker program for historic properties, lectures, and conferences.
(7)
Conduct surveys of historic areas for the purpose of defining those of historic significance,
and prioritizing the importance of identified historic areas.
(8)
Actively pursue financial assistance for preservation-related programs.
(9)
For the purpose of variance requests pursuant to section 26-24-4 of the Code, review and make findings as to whether conformance with the provisions
of chapter 26 of the Code would have significant adverse impacts upon the historical character
of an individual landmark or a contributing building of a historic district. Such
findings shall be forwarded to the board of adjustment.
(10)
The historic preservation commission is authorized to hold special meetings at any
time upon the call of a quorum and after at least twenty-four (24) hours' notice to
members and as much public notice as is practicable under the circumstances.

(a)
Scope. The planning commission as created by section 4.14 of the Charter is a quasi-judicial commission and shall have the duties and jurisdiction
as set forth in the Charter and the Code.
(b)
Membership. The planning commission shall consist of seven (7) regular members.
(c)
Term . Planning commission members shall serve terms of four (4) years.
(d)
Duties and responsibilities. The planning commission shall have the following powers and duties:
(1)
Prepare and recommend to the city council a comprehensive plan of public improvements,
looking to the present and future development and growth of the city. Such a plan
after its adoption by the city council will be known as the official plan. Such plan
shall include reasonable requirements in reference to streets, alleys and public grounds
within the corporate limits and in contiguous territory outside of and distant not
more than one and one-half (1½) miles from such limits, and not included in any municipality,
such requirements to be effective whenever such lands shall be subdivided after the
adoption of such plan.
(2)
Prepare and recommend to the city council from time to time such changes in the plan
or any part thereof as may be deemed necessary by the city council or by the planning
commission.
(3)
Prepare and recommend to the city council from time to time plans and/or recommendations
for specific improvements in pursuance of such official plan.
(4)
Give aid to the city officials, charged with the direction of projects for improvements,
embraced within the official plan, to further the making of such improvements, and
generally to promote the realization of the official plan.
(5)
Arrange and conduct any form of publicity relative to its activities for the general
purpose of public understanding.
(6)
Cooperate with municipal or regional planning commissions and other agencies or groups
to further the local planning program and to assure harmonious and integrated planning
for the area.
(7)
Exercise such other powers and duties granted by the Code and germane to the powers
granted by statute.
(d)
Meetings. The planning commission shall hold meetings and hearings on a date and hour designated
by the planning commission. All meetings and hearings shall be open to the public.
The planning commission shall keep written records of its proceedings, which shall
be open to public inspection. The planning commission is authorized to hold special
meetings at any time upon the call of a quorum and after at least twenty-four (24)
hours' notice to members and as much public notice as is practicable under the circumstances.

(a)
Scope. The older adults advisory board shall be an advisory board, and shall advise the
city council regarding the quality of life in the city for older adults and the provision
of services for older adults.
(b)
Membership. The older adults advisory board shall consist of seven (7) regular members. A city
employee appointed by the city manager shall serve as a non-voting staff liaison to
the board.
(c)
Duties and responsibilities . In addition to other tasks that the city council may assign to it, the older adults
advisory board shall:
(1)
Prepare and submit to city council a plan for the enhancement of the quality of life
of older adults living in the city, and as necessary amend the plan.
(2)
Review the city's annual senior services budget request.
(3)
Actively support programs and services for older adults consistent with plans and
priorities established by city council.
(4)
Adopt policies that provide for the provision of services to older adults. Such policies
shall be effective upon adoption or at the time specified in the policy, unless and
until city council disapproves of the policy. The older adults advisory board shall
promptly submit any adopted policy to city council for its review.
(5)
Recommend to city council local legislation and policies that will encourage the inclusion
of older adults in the community and support their needs.

(a)
Scope. The sustainability and resilience advisory board shall be an advisory board, and
shall advise the city council on matters concerning environmental sustainability and
environmental resilience practices.
(b)
Membership. The sustainability and resilience advisory board shall consist of nine (9) members.
A member of city council shall serve as a liaison to the board.
(c)
Duties and Responsibilities . In addition to other tasks that the city council may assign to it, the sustainability

resource conservation, urban development, buildings, greenhouse gas emissions, transportation,
energy, air quality, water, and waste reduction for the benefit of all residents of
Lafayette.
(2)
Recommend to city council updates to the sustainability plan on an ongoing basis,
as necessary.

Prepare and make timely recommendations to city council for specific actions relating
to goals and strategies in conjunction with the sustainability plan and the comprehensive
plan relating to environmental sustainability and resilience matters.
(4)
Interface with other governmental advisory committees, boards, and commissions on
environmental sustainability and resilience issues and opportunities.
(5)
Promote, educate, and encourage environmental sustainability and resilience activities
with other city boards and commissions and at city events.

(a)
Scope. The youth advisory board shall be an advisory board, and shall advise city council
regarding the engagement of Lafayette youth in outreach, advisory, educational, civic
involvement, and youth leadership opportunities. The youth advisory board may make
recommendations to the city council, based on research and community-based dialogue,
on youth-related issues and other topics relevant to the city's youth.
(b)
Membership . The youth advisory board shall consist of not more than thirteen (13), and not less
than seven (7), youths from eleven (11) through eighteen (18) years of age, residing
or attending school within the city. All members of the youth advisory board shall
be appointed by city council upon recommendation made after a competitive application
process administered by an application committee consisting of one (1) councilmember,
one (1) city staff liaison, and one (1) other adult assigned by the staff liaison.
The youth advisor board shall strive to obtain representation from students enrolled
at each middle school and high school within the city. A city employee appointed by
the city administrator shall serve as the nonvoting city staff liaison to the youth
advisory board.
(c)
Term . Members shall serve a one (1) year term from September 1—August 31. There shall
be no limit to the number of terms an eligible member may serve. Members seeking re-appointment
must submit an application for each subsequent term. Vacancies shall be filled for
the unexpired term by city council appointment following the procedure in subsection
(c) of this section for other appointments to the board.
(d)
Duties and responsibilities . In addition to other tasks that the city council may assign to it, the youth advisory
board shall:(1) Gather information and communicate with private and public entities
regarding youth-oriented issues and concerns;
(2)
Review and discuss local, state, and federal legislation that may impact youth;
(3)
Develop, with the cooperation of public and private entities, programs and activities
that promote the civic involvement of youth and the development of youth leadership;
(4)
Make recommendations to city council regarding local legislation and policies that
will enhance the involvement, status, and well-being of youth in the community; and
(5)
Recruit other Lafayette youths to volunteer and participate in supporting youth advisory
board projects and events, as needed at the discretion of the board.
