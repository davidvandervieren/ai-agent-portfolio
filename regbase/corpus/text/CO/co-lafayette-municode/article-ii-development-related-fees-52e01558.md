(a)
Delegation of authority to city manager to establish certain fees .
(1)
The city manager may establish administrative fees and surcharges to be paid by the
users of services and facilities. Such fees may include, without limitation, fees
for recreation and cultural services, fees for activity registration, application
fees, permit fees, hearing and appeal fees, cemetery fees, fees for use of city facilities,
and costs for goods.
(2)
The city manager is authorized to charge fees up to the amount established by provisions
of the uniform building codes currently adopted by the city.
(3)
The city manager is authorized to establish a surcharge for customer payments made
by credit card or charge card in lieu of payment by cash, check, or similar means,
in an amount not to exceed two (2) percent of the total cost of the transaction, or
in an amount not to exceed the merchant fee that the city incurs in processing the
transaction, whichever is less.
(4)
The city manager shall include in their recommended budget a summary of administrative
fees and an estimate of the amount of annual revenue anticipated to be generated by
such fees during the budget term.
(b)
Delegation of authority to city clerk to establish certain fees.
(1)
Alcohol beverage permitting and licensing fees . The city clerk is authorized to set alcohol beverage licensing application fees
in an amount as set forth in C.R.S., Articles 3, 4, and 5 of Title 44, or such other
amount as determined by the city clerk. Such fees shall be payable to and collected
by the city clerk on behalf of the city.
(2)
Research and retrieval fees for public records . The city clerk is authorized to charge fees imposed in accordance with the Colorado
Open Records Act, C.R.S. § 24-72-200.1—24-72-206.
( Ord. No. 2025-25 , § 1, 11-18-25)
The city manager shall establish fees according to the following criteria:
(a)
The amount of estimated revenue to be generated by each such fee shall be reasonably
related to the city's estimated cost of providing the service or facility for which
such fee is charged;
(b)
Any distinctions made among feepayers, in terms of the amounts to be paid by such
feepayers for the use of a particular service or facility, shall be reasonably related
to a legitimate municipal purpose. Such distinctions may include, without limitation,
reduced rates for senior citizens, youth, lower income residents, and disabled persons;
(c)
In determining the extent to which the total cost of a particular service or facility
should be recovered through the imposition of fees, the city manager may consider,
without limitation:
(1)
The nature of the facilities or services;
(2)
The nature and extent of the benefit to the feepayers;
(3)
The level of demand for a particular service or facility;
(4)
Ease of collection; and
(5)
The strategic outcomes adopted by city council.
( Ord. No. 2025-25 , § 1, 11-18-25)
