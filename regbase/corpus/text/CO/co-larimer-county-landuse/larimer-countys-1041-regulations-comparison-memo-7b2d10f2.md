Larimer County’s 1041 Regulations – 
Initial Comparative Analysis 
May 5, 2021 DRAFT 
Introduction/Overview 
As part of the Land Use Code update (Phase 2), Larimer County is reviewing and updating its “1041 Regulations,” 
also known as regulations that apply to Areas and Activities of State Interest. 
This initial analysis addresses mostly the “Designated Matters” (areas and activities that are regulated by the 
local jurisdiction per statutory allowances) and gives a brief overview of processes and criteria that Larimer 
County and other jurisdictions have in their 1041 Regulations. A later paper will add more detail to the process 
and criteria sections. 
Larimer County’s 1041 Regulations (Areas and Activities of State Interest) appear in Article 10 of the Larimer 
County Land Use Code. This paper compares the County’s current regulations with a handful of other 
jurisdictions in Colorado that also have 1041 regulations: Arapahoe, Boulder, Eagle, Routt, and Summit 
counties. These counties seem to be a representative cross sample of how counties exercise their local 
authority, and links to the regulations can be found on the project webpage. 
What are 1041 Regulations? 
1041 Regulations are delegated powers from the State of Colorado to local jurisdictions to regulate specific 
geographically defined areas or specific types of activities of state interest. These are areas or activities where 
the impacts and benefits may not be limited to a particular county and as a result are subject to state level 
oversight. In 1974, HB 74-1041 was introduced at the legislature to address issues surrounding land use 
planning in Colorado. Later that year it was enacted as the Areas and Activities of State Interest Act (AASIA), 
which defines a set of activities and areas of state interests and delegates power to local governments to 
regulate such areas or activities. These are known as “1041 Powers” and the regulations as “1041 Regulations.” 
(Dakan, “Colorado Local Governments’ Use of 1041 Regulations,” for DOLA, 2017). 
What Does the State Allow Local Jurisdictions to do? 
The 1041 Powers allow local jurisdictions to have some local authority and decision-making ability over 
developments that are of state interest or that may be on public lands and that may have local impacts. 
Where do Larimer County’s 1041 Regulations Appear? 
Larimer County’s adopted “Areas and Activities of State Interest” (1041) regulations that appear in Article 10 of 
the Land Use Code. 
When Did Larimer County Adopt its 1041 Regulations? 
In 2008, Larimer County adopted its “Areas and Activities of State Interest” (1041) regulations designating most 
of the activities that are current regulated except for water transmission lines and solar energy power plants. In 
December 2009, the County amended the regulations to add siting a development of new or extended domestic 
water or sewer transmission lines, and in 2010, the County added regulations for siting and development of 
solar energy power plants.

How do Larimer County’s Regulations Apply – What Types of Projects Need a 1041 Permit? 
Larimer County regulates the following twelve activities of state interest. (Note: these types are abbreviated. 
Check Article 10 Section 10.3 for the full description of each type.) 
• 10.3.1, electrical power plant (50 MW+) 
• 10.3.2, conversion of an electrical power plant to a new type of fuel 
• 10.3.3, nuclear power plant 
• 10.3.4, wind power plant (3+ towers , 80-feet+, or 10%+ expansion) 
• 10.3.5, electric transmission lines (69,000 volts+) 
• 10.3.6, transmission line upgrade expanding easement right-of-way or increasing height 10-feet+ 
• 10.3.7, electrical substation or transition site for switching (69,000 volts+) 
• 10.3.8, new pipeline designated for transmission of natural gas or other petroleum derivatives , 10-in+ 
• 10.3.9, new or expanded storage facilities for natural gas (50,000 cu ft+ natural gas or 35,000 barrels+ 
petroleum derivatives) 
• 10.3.10, new or extended domestic water or sewer transmission lines (easements 30-feet+) 
• 10.3.11, new or expansion of water storage reservoir (50-acres+) 
• 10.3.12, solar energy power plant 
What Does the State Allow Counties to Regulate? 
1041 Regulations are fairly tightly guided by state statutory authority and C.R.S. §24-65.1-101 et seq. which 
describe how a local government may invokes its 1041 Powers. For instance, a local government must hold a 
public meeting to designate areas, and the statutes set forth the time periods for holding meetings and 
publishing notice. They also list the specific Areas and Activities for Designation for which the regulations may 
apply. 
Local jurisdictions may designate Areas of State Interest (C.R.S. §24-65.1-201), and must designate them with a 
map, as follows: 
a) Mineral resource areas; 
b) Natural hazard areas; 
c) Areas containing, or having a significant impact upon, historical, natural, or archaeological resources of 
statewide importance; and 
d) Areas around key facilities in which development may have a material effect upon the key facility or the 
surrounding community. 
And they may designate Activities of State Interest (C.R.S. §24-65.1-203), as follows: 
a) Site selection and construction of major new domestic water and sewage treatment systems and major 
extension of existing domestic water and sewage treatment systems; 
b) Site selection and development of solid waste disposal sites; 
c) Site selection of airports; 
d) Site selection of rapid or mass transit terminals, stations, and fixed guideways; 
e) Site selection of arterial highways and interchanges and collector highways; 
f) Site selection and construction of major facilities of a public utility; 
g) Site selection and construction of new communities; 
h) Efficient utilization of municipal and industrial water projects; 
i) Conduct of nuclear detonations; and 
j) The use of geothermal resources for the commercial production of electricity.

Local governments may then include the criteria for review and approval for each area or activity, including 
general and specific criteria. 
What Might Larimer County Address in the 1041 Regulations? 
In 2021, through the process of updating 1041 Regulations, Larimer County could choose to change or add the 
list of designated areas and activities and could add additional generally applicable or specific criteria for review. 
Additionally, the County could adjust its procedures or submittal requirements a bit so long as those procedures 
(e.g., timelines) adhere to state requirements. 
What is the Schedule and Public Process for Updating the Larimer County 1041 Regulations? 
The County proposes a public process through November 2021 to review and update the regulations in the 
following phases: 
• Part 1 – Update areas and activities for designation (through early June) 
• Part 2 – Amend the general review criteria and supplemental criteria (through late July) 
• Part 3 – Refine the procedures and prepare draft regulations for public input (through late August) 
• Public Adoption hearings with Planning Commission and Board of County Commissioners (tentatively 
scheduled for late October) 
• Effective Date, tentative. November 15, 2021 
See the detailed schedule on the project webpage with upcoming opportunities to provide public input.
