State Law reference— Authority to abate, C.R.S. § 31-15-401(1)(d).
For the purposes of this chapter, the following definitions shall apply:
Chemical management means the use of herbicides or plant growth regulators to disrupt the growth of weeds.
Director means the director of planning of the city, his successor in office, or designee.
Mechanical control means those methodologies or management practices that physically disrupt plant growth,
including but not limited to tilling, mowing, burning, mulching, hand pulling, and
hoeing.
Owner, agent or occupant means and shall be construed to include tenants as well as landowners and shall include
and be applicable to the city and all subdivisions and agencies thereof.
Plant growth regulators means a substance used for controlling or modifying plant growth processes by retarding
the rate of growth or rate of maturation or otherwise altering the behavior of plant
growth; except that it shall not include substances to the extent that they are intended
as plant nutrients, nutritional chemicals, plant inoculant, and soil amendments.
Property means any real property located within the city limits.
Weeds means and includes, but is not limited to, Canada thistle, common ragweed, fireweed,
milkweed, perennial sowthistle, Russian knapweed, Russian thistle, sandburs, woollyleaf,
povertyweed, curly dock, Johnsongrass, leafy spurge, dalmatian toadflax, yellow toadflax
and any other type of noxious weeds specified by the U.S. Department of Agriculture;
but not to include flower gardens, vegetable gardens, plots of shrubbery, grain plots,
pastures used for feed, pastures used for wildlife, fodder, forage or crops grown
for consumption or commercial purposes.
(Code 1977, § 8.08.010; Ord. No. 857-1985, § 2; Ord. No. 1149-1994, § 1)
A.
It is unlawful for the owner of any property, except property zoned agricultural or
property developed for residential use, to permit weeds to grow to a height in excess
of 12 inches. It is unlawful for the owner of property developed for residential use
to permit weeds to grow to a height in excess of six inches. It shall be the duty
of the owner, agent, or occupant of any property to control the growth of weeds on
such property as required by this section, on alleys abutting the property to the
middle of the alley, and on sidewalk areas abutting the property, including all parking
areas.
B.
It is unlawful for the owner of any property zoned agricultural to permit weeds to
grow to a height in excess of 12 inches if such property is located within a distance
of 100 feet from:
1.
Any dedicated street;
2.
Any property which is not zoned agricultural; or
3.
Any property which is zoned agricultural but is not used solely for agricultural or
commercial crop purposes, solely for animal production purposes, or solely as a horse
stable. In such event, it shall be the duty of the owner, agent, and occupant of such
property to control the growth of the weeds as required under this section a distance
of 100 feet from the adjacent property line or dedicated street.
C.
Any wildlife preserve, wildlife area, or open space designated by the city shall be
exempt from the requirements of this section.
(Code 1977, § 8.08.020; Ord. No. 857-1985, § 2; Ord. No. 1149-1994, § 1; Ord. No.
1160-1994, § 1)
A.
In agricultural zones, on real property actively used for agricultural purposes, the
control and abatement of the growth of weeds may be accomplished by the use of chemical
management or mechanical control, except that control by burning on such property
shall only be permitted for irrigation ditches or irrigation ditch rights-of-way.
B.
The use of chemical management shall be accomplished in accordance with applicable
state and federal law.
C.
Written notice of irrigation ditch or ditch right-of-way burning permitted by subsection
A of this section shall be given to the city and to the fire protection district in
which the property is located at least seven days prior to the proposed burning. Such
notice shall include the date, time and place of the proposed burning.
D.
Nothing in this section shall be construed to authorize the maintenance of any nuisance
or of any violation of any state or federal law or regulation.
(Code 1977, § 8.08.025; Ord. No. 1149-1994, § 3)
If weeds exist on any property to heights greater than permitted by this chapter,
the director, no less than ten days after notice by mail to the last known address
of the owner, agent, or occupant of the property, may order the cutting and removal
of the weeds by the city. The city shall assess the costs therefor, plus an additional
amount to cover administrative costs, against the lots or tracts of land from which
the weeds are removed. The amount of the additional charge to cover administrative
costs shall be as established by resolution of the city council. In the event that
such owner, agent or occupant cannot be contacted under this section, his address
is unknown or a mailed notice is returned undelivered, the director may post such
property with a notice giving such property owner, agent or occupant five days to
cut the weeds.
(Code 1977, § 8.08.030; Ord. No. 857-1985, § 2; Ord. No. 1160-1994, § 2; Ord. No.
1594-2011, § 18, 6-7-2011)
In the event an owner, agent, or occupant, within 30 days after billing for weed cutting
by the city, as provided for in section 8.08.030 , fails to pay the costs and expenses of such weed cutting, a lien shall be assessed
by the city clerk against the property for such costs and expenses. The city clerk
shall certify such assessment to the treasurer of the county, who shall extend the
assessment upon the tax roll and collect the assessment in the same manner as other
taxes upon such property.
(Code 1962, § 28.11(d); Code 1977, § 8.08.040; Ord. No. 519-1977, § 4)
Violation of any provision of this chapter is civil for which imprisonment is not
a possible penalty and for which the sole penalty provided shall be a fine. Any person
who shall violate any of the provisions of this chapter against whom judgment is entered
shall be subject to a fine of not more than $500.00 and shall not be subject to imprisonment
on account of such judgment. For any violation of any provision of this chapter, the
court may enter a judgment of liability by default against the defendant for failure
to appear or to pay, and may assess such penalty, together with such court costs and
surcharges, as are established by law.
(Ord. No. 1236-1996, § 2, 11-19-1996)
