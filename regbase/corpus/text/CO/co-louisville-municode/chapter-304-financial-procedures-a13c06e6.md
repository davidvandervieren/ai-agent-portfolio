The fiscal year of the city shall commence on January 1 in each year.
(Code 1962, § 6.1; Code 1977, § 3.04.010)
State Law reference— Fiscal year, C.R.S. § 29-1-102(9).
The city council shall adopt an annual budget for each fiscal year in accordance with
the procedure set forth in the Local Government Budget Law of Colorado, C.R.S. § 29-1-101
et seq.
(Code 1962, § 6.2; Code 1977, § 3.04.020)
State Law reference— Budgets, C.R.S. § 29-1-101 et seq.
The city council shall by resolution fix the rate of tax to be levied upon all the
taxable property within the city for municipal purposes; and, through the city clerk,
shall officially certify such levy to the county commissioners of the county each
year as provided by C.R.S. § 29-1-301 et seq.
(Code 1962, § 6.3; Code 1977, § 3.04.030; Ord. No. 974-1988, § 1)
State Law reference— Tax levy, C.R.S. § 29-1-301 et seq.
The city council shall pass an ordinance within the last quarter of each fiscal year
for the next fiscal year, to be termed the annual appropriation bill. In such bill,
the city council may appropriate such sums of money as are deemed necessary to defray
all necessary expenses and liabilities.
(Code 1962, § 6.4(a); Code 1977, § 3.04.040; Ord. No. 516-1977, § 2)
State Law reference— Adoption of appropriation ordinances, C.R.S. §§ 29-1-108, 31-16-103.
In cases of emergency caused by an act of God, the public enemy, or some contingency
which could not have been reasonably foreseen at the time of adoption of the budget,
the city council may authorize the expenditure of funds in excess of the appropriation
by ordinance or resolution duly adopted by a majority vote of the city council.
(Code 1962, § 6.4(b); Code 1977, § 3.04.050; Ord. No. 516-1977, § 2)
State Law reference— Emergency expenditures, C.R.S. § 29-1-111.
Neither the city council nor any department or officer of the city shall add to the
corporation expenditures in any one year anything over and above the amount provided
for in the annual appropriation bill of that year, except as is otherwise specifically
provided in this section. No expenditure for an improvement to be paid for out of
the general fund of the city shall exceed in any one year the amount provided for
such improvement in the annual appropriation bill. Nothing contained in this section
shall prevent the city council from ordering, by a majority vote, any improvement
the necessity of which is caused by any casualty or accident happening after such
annual appropriation is made.
(Code 1962, § 6.5; Code 1977, § 3.04.060)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, C.R.S. § 29-1-110.
No contract shall be made by the city council, and no expense shall be incurred by
any officer or department of the city, whether the object of the expenditure has been
ordered by the city council or not, unless an appropriation has been made previously
concerning such expense, except as otherwise provided in section 3.04.060 .
(Code 1962, § 6.6; Code 1977, § 3.04.070)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, C.R.S. § 29-1-114.
Depositories for funds and moneys of the city shall be approved and designated by
the city council pursuant to resolution adopted by a majority vote of the city council
according to law. The treasurer and the director of finance, as the treasurer's designee,
shall deposit all city funds and moneys in the name of the treasurer in one or more
of such banks as may from time to time be directed by the city manager.
(Code 1977, § 3.04.080; Ord. No. 718-1981, § 2; Ord. No. 1348-2000, § 3, 11-21-00)
State Law reference— Investments, C.R.S. § 31-20-303.
All checks drawn upon the city must be signed by the mayor and countersigned by the
clerk; and the checks must state the particular fund or appropriation to which they
are chargeable and the person to whom payable.
(Code 1977, § 3.04.090; Ord. No. 718-1981, § 2)
State Law reference— Checks, C.R.S. § 31-20-401.
The city council shall appropriate money for corporate purposes only, but the city
council shall have the power to appropriate money for the purposes set out in subsections
A through C of this section, which shall be considered to be public purposes:
A.
Public entertainment. The city council shall have the power to appropriate money, in an amount not exceeding
six-tenths of one mill on the assessed valuation, for the purpose of giving public
concerts and entertainments by the city.
B.
Advertising. The city council shall have the power to appropriate moneys for the purpose of advertising
the business, social, and educational advantages, and the natural resources and scenic
attractions of the city.
C.
Aid to charity organizations. The city council shall have the power to aid and foster, by all lawful measures,
associated charity organizations by appropriations and granting the use of suitable
rooms in city buildings. No portion of any money so appropriated shall be given or
loaned to any society, corporation, association, or institution which may be wholly
or in part under sectarian or denominational control.
(Code 1962, § 6.9; Code 1977, § 3.04.100)
State Law reference— Similar provisions, C.R.S. § 31-15-901.
The city council shall appoint a certified public accountant to serve as the city
auditor, and he shall serve at the pleasure of the city council. The city auditor
shall audit the books and records of the city, and its financial affairs and transactions
at least once each year, in the form provided by C.R.S. § 29-1-601 et seq.; and the
city auditor shall make a written report to the city council, after each audit, of
the condition of the city's finances and the results of his examination. The city
auditor shall also make recommendations to the city council concerning the system
of keeping the books, records, and accounts of the city.
(Code 1962, § 6.11; Code 1977, § 3.04.120)
State Law reference— Audits, C.R.S. § 29-1-601 et seq.
A.
Any lease-purchase agreement entered into by the city shall be approved by the city
council by non-emergency ordinance. For the purposes of this section, "lease-purchase
agreement" means:
1.
Any installment purchase agreement for the purchase of real or personal property which
requires or permits payments during more than one fiscal year, regardless of any right
of the city to terminate the agreement; or
2.
Any agreement for the lease or rental of real or personal property which requires
or permits payments during more than one fiscal year, regardless of any right of the
city to terminate the agreement, and under which title to the property is transferred
at the end of the term for nominal or no additional consideration.
B.
Any long-term leasehold agreement entered into by the city shall be approved by the
city council by ordinance or resolution, as the city council shall determine, subject
to the further requirement that any lease of park or open space land shall require
the approval of the registered electors as provided in the charter. For the purposes
of this section, "long-term leasehold agreement" means any lease agreement other than
a lease-purchase agreement which extends beyond more than one fiscal year, whether
or not terminable annually or subject to annual appropriation and whether or not the
city is lessor or lessee.
C.
Nothing in this section shall apply to the acquisition of water rights by lease-purchase
agreement, long-term leasehold agreement or other means.
(Ord. No. 1081-1992, § 1, 9-1-92; Ord. No. 1564-2009, § 1, 12-15-2009)
Editor's note— Ord. No. 1564-2009, § 1, adopted Dec. 15, 2009, changed the title of § 3.04.130 from
lease-purchase and long-term lease financing prohibited to lease-purchase and long-term
leasehold agreements.
