The street development policies, standards and specifications for the town, which
policies, standards and specifications contain the following provisions, are incorporated
herein by reference. The street development policies, standards and specifications
are on file in the office of the town clerk for public inspection.
A.
Article 1 , the town street system: This article designates the authority, jurisdiction and purposes of these regulations
and provides for the classification of the streets in the town.
B.
Article 2 , town street development: This article establishes the general planning, design and construction criteria for
new streets within the town and outlines procedures for the inspection, testing and
acceptance of new streets for maintenance. In addition, this article provides for
encroachment and overload permits and sets forth bonding requirements prior to new
street construction.
C.
Article 3 , street standards: This article establishes the detailed design criteria to be used in the design of
new roads and streets within the town in regard to layout, sight distance, curvature,
vertical alignment, geometric cross sections and structural cross sections. Specific
requirements for drainage structures, curb and gutter, sidewalks, paving, bridges,
traffic control devices and guardrails are also detailed in this article.
D.
Article 4 , specifications: This article establishes the specifications for earthwork, subbase and base course
material and paving. In addition, the procedures for sampling, testing and approval
of materials are detailed in this article.
E.
Article 5 , definition of terms: The definitions of terms and the construction of the same as used in these regulations
are given in this article.
F.
Article 6 , drawings and graphs: This article provides graphic representation of typical sections and specific design
charts to be used in the design of new roads and streets within the town.
G.
Article 7 , administrative provisions: This article provides administrative provisions relative to variances from these
regulations, the repeal of previously applicable ordinances, and the statement of
severability of the provisions of these regulations.

A.
Permit requirements:
1.
Permit required: It shall be unlawful for any person or entity, other than those persons or entities
under contract with the town, to perform work within the right-of-way, street, sidewalk,
curb, gutter, curbwalk, alley or other public place without having first obtained
a right-of-way permit from the department of public works.
2.
Application for permit: Application for the required right-of-way permit shall be made on the permit application
form as provided by the authorized representative of the town. The completed application
shall recite specifically and shall illustrate by sketch or plan the exact location,
depth, extent, nature and purpose of the work desired to be made, the purpose for
which the work is requested, and the duration of the time required for the work.
3.
Permit fee: Each application shall be accompanied by the permit fee established by resolution
of the town council.
4.
Qualified applicants: Any person or entity properly licensed pursuant to the provisions of the town ordinances
is eligible to apply for a right-of-way permit.
5.
Issuance of permit: The right-of-way permit shall be issued once the applicant has met all of the application
requirements of the town and the applicable town ordinance requirements addressing
the proposed work. Any person or entity specifically authorized to perform the proposed
work pursuant to the terms of an ordinance, resolution or contract of the town council
shall be recognized as possessing a right-of-way permit based on the ordinance, resolution,
or contract to complete said work and will not be required to obtain a right-of-way
permit as required herein.
6.
Conditions of permit:
a.
The right-of-way permit shall only be valid for the specific project as set forth
in the application and defined and specified on the right-of-way permit.
b.
A copy of the required right-of-way permit will be kept at the work site and presented
to the authorized representative of the town as requested.
c.
All work completed pursuant to the right-of-way permit shall meet the requirements
of the town design and construction standards.
d.
As a requirement of the right-of-way permit, no trenches or excavations will be backfilled
until such trench or excavation is inspected and approved by the authorized representative
of the town. The person or entity holding the right-of-way permit shall contact the
town representative, notify the town of the starting date of the work to be done pursuant
to the right-of-way permit, and shall schedule for such inspections as are required
herein.
7.
Records kept: The authorized representative of the town shall keep a record of all applications
for right-of-way permits and right-of-way permits issued.
8.
Licensed permittees; bond and/or insurance: All persons or entities applying for a right-of-way permit must be licensed pursuant
to town ordinances and must provide proof of the insurance and/or bonding as may be
required by the town ordinances to the town clerk.
B.
Street obstructions: It shall be unlawful for any person or entity during the course of the proposed work
to obstruct more than the space of one town block and one traffic intersection at
the same time in any one street. The time availability requirements for such obstruction
shall be scheduled by the holder of the right-of-way permit in advance with the authorized
representative of the town.
C.
Restoration and repairs:
1.
All restoration and repairs shall be made in accordance with the standards and specifications
for design and construction of public improvements.
2.
In the event of settlement or subsidence of a particular excavation or part thereof,
the person or entity having performed the excavation shall be responsible for fully
repairing such settlement or subsidence at his sole expense.
D.
Liability for damage or injury: Any person who undertakes work pursuant to a right-of-way permit issued under the
provisions of this section, or performs work under contract with the town or by permission
obtained from the town council shall be fully liable and responsible for any and all
damage or injury to persons, animals, objects and property caused by such work.

A.
Definitions: For purposes of this section, the following terms shall have the following meanings:
Block means the length of a street between two parallel streets on either side.
Block party means a closure of up to one block of a residential street for a community gathering
of 100 people or less, but excluding: any gathering organized by non-profits, commercial
or business interests.
B.
Permit required: A permit shall be required for a block party in the town.
C.
Permit specifications: A block party permit shall allow for the consumption of fermented malt beverages,
as defined in section 6-9-1 of this Code, by persons 21 years of age or older, in compliance with the permitted
date, time, location, and conditions imposed in the approved application. If issued,
the permit shall specify the date and time of the event and the authorized possession
and consumption of fermented malt beverages, and the location of the event where such
possession and consumption is authorized.
D.
Application: An application for a block party permit shall be filed with the town clerk at least
ten days prior to the block party. The application shall be accompanied by a fee in
the amount set forth in the fee schedule adopted by the town council, and shall contain
the following information:
1.
The name, address and phone number of the applicant;
2.
The name and description of the portion of the street intended to be blocked;
3.
A map or diagram depicting the portion of the street intended to be blocked;
4.
A general description of the purpose or nature of the block party;
5.
The dates and hours of the block party; and
6.
The names, addresses and signatures of at least 50 percent of the residents who reside
on the block, as evidence of their consent to the block party.
E.
Criteria: The town clerk or designee shall grant the block party permit unless the town clerk
finds that one or more of the following conditions exist:
1.
The application is incomplete;
2.
Blocking the street or portion thereof on the dates and hours requested will unduly
interfere with the flow of vehicular traffic or access to property; or
3.
Blocking the street or portion thereof on the dates and hours requested will constitute
a threat to the public health, safety or welfare.
F.
Notice:
1.
No later than one week prior to the block party, the applicant shall leave written
notice at the address of any resident whose signature was not included in the application.
2.
The town clerk or designee shall notify the Mountain View Fire Department of the dates
and hours of the block party no later than 14 days prior to the event.
G.
Permit conditions; restrictions:
1.
A block party shall not commence before 9:00 a.m. or terminate after 11:00 p.m., and
the permit shall be valid only for the dates and hours specified therein.
2.
The applicant shall be available either in person or by the provided phone number
for the duration of the block party.
3.
Emergency access to the street shall be maintained with a minimum of 12 feet of clearance
on either side of the street at all times, the sufficiency of which shall be determined
by the Erie Police Department.
4.
Fire hydrants shall not be obstructed.
5.
There shall be no open flames or cooking on the sidewalk or in the street.
6.
No person possessing or consuming fermented malt beverage pursuant to the permit shall
behave in an unruly, disorderly manner.
7.
Only persons 21 years of age or older may possess or consume fermented malt beverages.
8.
There shall be no sales of fermented malt beverages made during the block party.
H.
Removal of litter and debris: The applicant shall remove all litter and debris resulting from the block party.
I.
Revocation of permit: Failure to comply with the provisions herein may result in the immediate revocation
of the permit by a police officer.

A.
Definition. For purposes of this section, a "new public right-of-way" means a paved street, alley,
trail or other public right-of-way that has been constructed or resurfaced within
the last three years.
B.
Permit required. Open trench excavation or potholing of utilities in the pavement of any new public
right-of-way is prohibited without a permit issued under this section, in addition
to any other required permits.
C.
Application. An application for a permit shall contain the following information:
1.
A detailed and dimensional engineering plan identifying and accurately representing
the property that will be impacted by the proposed work, and construction methods;
2.
The location, width, length and depth, including curb and gutter, over the total length
of each town block that will be impacted by the proposed work;
3.
The total area of existing pavement in each individual town block that will be impacted

4.
A written statement addressing the approval criteria in this section.

Approval criteria. No permit shall be approved unless the town finds that all of the following criteria
have been met:
1.
Boring or jacking without disturbing the pavement is impractical due to physical characteristics
of the new public right-of-way or other utility conflicts; and
2.
The proposed work cannot reasonably be delayed until after the three-year deferment
period has lapsed.

Emergency operations. Emergency maintenance operations shall be limited to circumstances involving the

to the Erie Police Department and the Mountain View Fire Protection District shall
be required. On the next work day following such work, detailed engineering plans,
construction methods and remediation plans shall be submitted to the town.

This chapter is known as the Town of Erie Public Improvement Permit Ordinance.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
This chapter is enacted to ensure that proper installation of any public improvement
is accomplished in accordance with town specifications and regulations.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
It shall be unlawful for any person, firm, or corporation to construct, enlarge, alter,
repair, move, improve, remove, excavate, convert, or demolish any public improvements
or common facilities regulated by the town "Construction Standards and Specifications"
and this chapter, without first obtaining a public improvement permit (PIP) from the
town.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
Applicants for public improvement permits shall complete and submit an application
in writing on a form furnished by the town for that purpose.
The applicant must also submit two sets of plans with the application, as per section 7-2-5 of this chapter. As a minimum each application shall:
A.
Identify and describe the work to be covered by the permit being requested.
B.
Provide the legal description and street address or similar description that will
readily identify the land where the work will be completed.
C.
Indicate the type of work or improvement intended.
D.
Be accompanied by a set of plans, diagrams, computations and specifications, and other
data required in this chapter and any other regulations concerning construction standards.
Construction drawings and specifications will be signed and sealed by a valid licensed
State of Colorado professional engineer.
E.
Submit completed PIP worksheet which includes quantities of the work to be performed.
F.
Be signed by the owner(s) or his/her legally authorized agent.
G.
Contain an estimated project start and completion date.
H.
Any other information as may be required by the town.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
A.
Submittal: Plans, engineering specifications, diagrams and other data shall be submitted in
at least two full sets at the time of application for a PIP. The plans, etc., submitted
shall have been prepared and designed by a registered professional engineer licensed
to practice in the state.
Plans and specifications shall be drawn to scale and shall have sufficient clarity
to indicate the location, nature, and extent of the work proposed and show in detail
that they conform to town "Construction Standards and Specifications", all relevant
laws, ordinances, rules, and regulations. All plans and specifications will be prepared
in accordance with standards of the industry. All construction plans will be checked
for conformance with town construction standards and specifications prior to acceptance
and the issuance of a PIP to the applicant. One set will be returned if changes are
required or recommended. Engineering design will remain the responsibility of the
professional design engineer used by the applicant.
B.
Acceptance: Upon final acceptance of the plans by the town, a minimum of three sets of plans
in a 24-inch by 36-inch form will be submitted to the town. The plans shall be signed
and sealed by the registered professional engineer responsible for the design. The
plans submitted shall have been prepared and designed by a registered professional
engineer licensed to practice in the state, and a signature block for the town shall
appear on the plans in accordance with the town standards and specifications. Upon
issuance of a PIP, a minimum of one of the plan sets shall be signed and returned
to the applicant, and two plan sets will remain on file with the town. The contractor
must keep at least one set of plans on the project site for the duration of the project.
Should circumstances warrant a change to the accepted plans, approval to make such
changes must be obtained in writing from the town. It will be the duty of the permit
holder to ensure the changes have been recorded and resubmitted to the town on as
built drawings, which are in compliance with town "Construction Standards and Specifications".
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
The fees for a PIP shall be as set forth in section 2-10-4 of this Code. These fees
shall be calculated on a cumulative basis and shall be paid in full at the time the
plans and specifications have been accepted by the town and a PIP is issued. A PIP
shall be required for all public improvement construction in a public right-of-way
or in a public easement. However, the fee for installation of certain public utilities,
i.e., underground electrical, gas, telephone, cable TV, etc., or construction of town
capital improvement projects, may be waived by the town or governed by agreement with
the town.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
Applications for which no PIP is issued within 180 days of the date of application
shall expire, and any plans or data submitted for review shall be destroyed or retained
by the town, at its discretion, or will be returned to the applicant upon request.
If the applicant makes a written request to extend the application review, the town
may extend the life of the application for a further 180 days. No application shall
be extended more than once. If no action is taken by the applicant to pursue PIP beyond
the submittal of the application after the expiration of the extension, a new application
shall be required of the applicant to renew action by the town. Furthermore, should
it be deemed necessary for the town to hire a private consulting firm for the plan
review, the applicant shall pay these additional costs.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
The town will review the application, plans, specifications, and any other data filed
by the applicant for a PIP. Various departments may verify compliance. If the town
finds that the work described in an application is complete and in accordance with
the town "Construction Standards and Specifications" and other pertinent laws, and
a permit fee has been paid, a permit will be issued to the applicant. These accepted
plans may not be modified or altered without authorization from the town, and all
work will be completed as per the accepted plans. The issuing of a permit based upon
plans, specifications or other data will not prevent the town from requiring the correction
of errors in those plans, which were not corrected at the time of application. In
such cases, the town may, at its discretion, stop construction until such corrections,
which are necessary, have been made.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
Every PIP issued by the town shall expire if the work authorized by the PIP is substantially
completed. "Substantially completed" shall mean that all public improvements are operable
as determined by an inspection by the town. If it is determined that the project is
delayed, abandoned, or work has stopped for no apparent reason, the project shall
cease, and a new permit will be required to complete the project. The new permit will
require a fee of 25 percent of the original permit price, provided no changes to the
accepted plans have been made. Should the accepted approved plans be changed, a new
PIP permit and fees are required. In no case shall an extension of a PIP cause a site
to be left in such a state as to create a hazard or nuisance.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
Any work performed, which is not covered by an issued PIP, shall cease upon written
notice of the town. An investigation fee shall be levied which is equal to the amount
of the PIP fee, plus any fees required for investigation time. These cumulative fees
shall be collected whether or not a proper PIP is issued after the investigation.
The payment of such investigation fees shall not exempt any person from compliance
with all other provisions of this chapter nor from any other penalty prescribed by
law.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
A.
Inspection required; notice: All construction work for which a PIP is required shall be subject to inspection
by the town, and special types of construction shall be required to have continuous
inspections by special inspectors. It is the responsibility of the applicant or designated
representatives of the applicant to notify the town that the work performed is ready
for inspection. Every request for inspection shall be filed at least three days before
inspection is desired, unless an early inspection is made necessary by prior agreement
with the town. An inspection request shall be made in writing or by telephone call
to the appropriate town employee, at the option of the town and on a case by case
basis. The person requesting inspection shall provide access to the site and work
to be inspected by the town.
B.
Defective work: The town shall have the authority to halt construction when the accepted plans and/or
town regulations governing construction have not been adhered to, or the work is otherwise
defective. The town shall give the applicant written notice of the deficiencies to
be corrected and the time frame in which the corrections are expected to be completed,
and may order further construction to cease until all deficiencies are corrected.
If the deficiencies are not corrected by the time frame specified, the town may evoke
enforcement options authorized by this chapter, and/or any performance guarantees
under which the work is being performed.
C.
Final inspection: The procedure for final inspection and acceptance will be by mutual agreement between
the town and the applicant, or as specified in any contract documents or as provided
by applicable town regulations.
D.
Additional inspections and reinspection: The town may make or require any additional inspections of any work as deemed necessary
to ascertain and ensure compliance with this chapter, any other applicable town ordinances,
and town regulations concerning construction. A reinspection fee may be assessed for
each inspection or reinspection when such portion of work for which inspection is
called is not complete or when corrections called for have not been made. Reinspection
fees may be assessed when the permit and the accepted plans are not at the work site
in the possession of the permit holder or the permit holder's agent and readily available
to the inspector, or for deviating from the plans accepted by the town. This section
is not to be interpreted as requiring reinspection fees the first time a job is rejected
for failure to comply with town regulations concerning construction, but rather as
controlling the practice of calling for inspections before a job is ready for such
inspection or reinspection.
(Ord. 802, 1-14-2003)
Failure to comply with the terms of this chapter shall constitute an offense, punishable
as provided in title 1 , chapter 4 of this Code. Each separate day or any portion thereof during which violation
of any provision appearing in this division occurs or continues shall be deemed or
constituted as a separate offense. A violation shall be reason for cease and desist
of the project.

fertilize, relocate, and protect all trees and other woody plants upon the public
right-of-way of any street, alley, sidewalk, or other public place, or to require

standards and specifications of arboricultural practices affecting the cutting, trimming,
spraying, removing, planting, pruning, fertilization, relocation, protection, and
other maintenance and treatment of trees and other woody plants upon the public right-of-way

D.
The town arborist shall have the authority to supervise and inspect all work done
under a forestry permit or arborist license issued in accordance with the terms of
this chapter.
E.
1.
Whenever any person or contractor who engages in the business of tree care within
the town has failed to obtain the proper license or permit, who violates any provision
of this chapter or any provision of the American National Standards Institute documents,

to immediately stop work on any property.
2.

provisions of this chapter or other provisions allegedly in violation. Such written
order shall be posted on the subject property and a copy provided to the property
owner or occupant, or the person or contractor who is allegedly in violation. After
a stop work order has been posted and delivered to the property owner or occupant,
or the person or contractor allegedly in violation, no work shall proceed on the subject
property, except to correct any violation(s) noted in the stop work order.
3.
It is a violation of this section to fail to comply with the stop work order issued
by the town arborist. Once the condition(s) that are the subject of the stop work
order have been corrected, as determined by the town arborist or designee, the town
arborist shall rescind the stop work order.

Application: A person or group may file an application for an exclusive use permit for exclusive

B.
Criteria for issuance of permit: The parks and recreation director or designee may approve an exclusive use permit
if the application for the exclusive use permit meets all of the following criteria:
1.
The time, location and duration of the event will not interfere with public services.
2.
The number and concentration of participants at the event will not result in crowds
exceeding limitations in town codes, or other significant inconvenience to the residents
of the surrounding neighborhoods.

are likely to secure and supervise the area and the participants during the event.

The applicant agrees to be personally responsible for cleaning, trash disposal or
repairs necessary as a result of the event.

The applicant agrees to indemnify and hold harmless the town, its employees and agents
for all liability claims arising out of the event.
6.
The applicant agrees to meet all recommendations of the chief of police or designee
regarding establishing a security plan for the event.
7.
The applicant meets all other requirements established by the town manager or designee,
and the chief of police or designee.

of alcohol beverages or fermented malt beverages by minors, its location within the
community, or the failure of the applicant to conduct a past event in compliance with
applicable laws and regulations.

The town manager or designee, or the parks and recreation director or designee, may
deny a permit application if another event has been previously scheduled for that
location on the same date and time, the event would have an adverse impact on the

and general use and enjoyment of the park or facility.
