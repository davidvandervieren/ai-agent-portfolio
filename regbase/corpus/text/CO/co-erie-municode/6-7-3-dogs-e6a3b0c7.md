The purpose of this chapter is to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the general
population of the town.
(Ord. 608, 9-24-1998; amd. 2004 Code)
Animal: Any live vertebrate creature, domestic or wild.
Authorized representative: Includes police or animal control officers employed by the town.
Cat: Any live or dead member of the species Felis catus, regardless of sex.
Dog: Any live or dead member of the species Canis familiaris, regardless of sex.
Domestic animal: Includes all species of animals commonly and universally accepted as being tame or
domesticated.
Keeper: A person who has custodial or supervisory authority or control over an animal.
Pet: Includes dogs, cats, rabbits, rodents, birds, reptiles and any other species of animal
which is sold or retained as a household pet but shall not include skunks, nonhuman
primates and any other species of wild, exotic or carnivorous animal.
Run at-large: Off the premises of the owner or keeper, not restrained by means of an adequate leash
and under the control of the owner or keeper, or other competent person. For purposes
of this definition, the premises of the owner shall not include common areas of condominiums,
townhouses, and apartment buildings, and any animal not restrained by means of an
adequate leash and in direct control of its owner or keeper upon the common area of
a condominium, townhouse or apartment building, or the grounds thereof, shall be deemed
to be running at-large.
A dog shall not be deemed to be running at-large when the dog is within the enclosed
confines of a town designated dog park.
Vicious animal: An animal that bites or attacks a human or another animal, or approaches a human
in an apparent attitude of attack, whether or not an attack actually occurs.
Wild animal: Includes all species of animals which commonly exist in a natural, unconfined state
and are usually not domesticated. This shall apply regardless of state or duration
of captivity.
(Ord. 608, 9-24-1998; amd. 2004 Code; Ord. 07-2016, 2-23-2016)
A.
Licensing requirements:
1.
License required: Any person harboring, owning, possessing or keeping any dog six months of age or
older within the town for any consecutive 14-day period, or longer, shall secure a
license for such dog from the town clerk and pay the license fee therefor.
2.
Tag and collar: A metal tag shall be furnished as license identification with each license, bearing
the number of the license. The license tag shall be attached to the collar worn by
the animal so licensed, unless a microchip containing identifying information of the
owner has been placed in the animal by a licensed veterinarian.
3.
Expiration of license: Each license shall be for the term of the rabies vaccination administered to each
individual dog, for a term of one year or three years.
4.
Nontransferability of license: Licenses and symbols of license identification under this subsection are not transferable.
5.
Importation period; time limit: Any dog brought into the town or adopted shall comply with this subsection within
30 days of importation or adoption.
6.
License fee: The annual license fee shall be as provided in section 2-10-8 of this Code. The fee
shall apply for any full year or part thereof and shall not be refunded for any reason.
B.
Rabies vaccination required: It is unlawful for any person to own, keep, harbor, or possess any dog over the age
of six months which has not been vaccinated against rabies. The owner of any dog shall
have the animal vaccinated by a licensed veterinarian by six months of age, and shall
keep the vaccinations current. The vaccines used will be ones licensed by the U.S.
Department of Agriculture and approved by the Colorado Department of Public Health
and Environment and will provide for at least a one year duration of immunity.
C.
Restraint and control: No owner or keeper shall allow his dog to "run at-large", as that term is defined
in this chapter. Every owner or keeper of a dog shall keep such dog exclusively upon
his own premises; provided that such dog may be off such premises if it is under the
control of a competent person who is able to control the dog, and restrained by a
leash not exceeding 15 feet in length and in a manner as to not have access to any
passerby. Any owner or keeper with a dog at a designated dog park shall be governed
by the provisions at section 6-7-9 , "Dog Parks", of this chapter.
D.
Prohibited acts and conditions:
1.
Noise: It shall be unlawful for any owner or keeper of any dog to fail to prevent the animal
from disturbing any person by barking, howling, yelping or making any other audible
sound. No summons shall be issued and no person shall be convicted at trial for violating
this subsection D.1. unless a minimum of two witnesses from separate households testify
about the nature of the barking, howling, yelping, or other audible sound. A police
officer or code enforcement officer may corroborate the elements of this subsection
D.1. and may issue the summons and testify as a second witness.
2.
Damaging property: It shall be unlawful for any owner, possessor, or person who keeps any dog to permit
such dog to destroy or damage the real or personal property of any person, or any
public property of the town.

A.
Animal shelter: Animals impounded under the provisions of this chapter shall be placed in a designated
animal shelter. The town may provide by contract with any public agency, private society
or association, or animal hospital, for the establishment, maintenance and operation
of such animal shelter.
B.
Impoundment:
1.
An Erie Police Officer or code enforcement officer is authorized to seize and impound
any animal under the following circumstances:
a.
Where the animal is running at-large;
b.
When the officer has probable cause to believe that an animal constitutes an immediate
danger to any person, other animal or the community;
c.
In any case where the owner or keeper responsible for the care of the animal is incapacitated
or otherwise not available to provide for the animal's welfare;
d.
Any animal suspected of having bitten any person or other domestic animal. In such
case the animal shall be impounded for a period of ten days or until released by the
chief of police or designee. At the discretion of the police officer or code enforcement
officer, the animal may be ordered quarantined at the owner or keeper's residence
if the animal can be securely contained and the animal will be available for observation
during and after the quarantine period. It is unlawful for any owner or keeper of
a quarantined animal to fail to keep such animal exclusively upon the property of
the owner or keeper during the quarantine period;
e.
When a police officer/code enforcement officer has probable cause to believe that
the animal has acted in an aggressive manner as that is defined in section 6-7-10 of this chapter, or has engaged in conduct that constitutes an animal attack, as
defined in section 6-7-11 of this chapter, and the officer believes that during the pending criminal case or
pending hearing regarding surrender of the animal, the animal constitutes an immediate
danger to any person(s), other animal(s) or the community. At the discretion of the
officer, the animal may be ordered quarantined at the owner or keeper's residence
if the animal can be securely contained and will not be removed from the property
until such time as released by the officer or order of the court. It is unlawful for
any owner or keeper of a quarantined animal to fail to keep such animal exclusively
upon the property of the owner or keeper during the quarantine period.
2.
As soon as practical after impoundment, a bona fide effort shall be made to give notice
of impoundment to the owner. If, after a bona fide effort to locate the owner, he
or she cannot be identified and the animal is not claimed within five days, the ownership
of the animal is deemed to transfer to the animal shelter then in possession of said
animal. If the owner is identified and the impounded animal is not claimed by its
owner within five days, the animal may be disposed of at the sole discretion of the
animal shelter.
C.
Claiming impounded animals: Any animal impounded pursuant to this section shall be released to the owner upon
payment of the impound fee, boarding fee, veterinary care charges, and any other costs
associated with the impound. If the owner of an unclaimed animal is known, such owner
shall be responsible for any costs incurred related to the impoundment, keeping or
disposal of the animal.
D.
Emergency medical treatment: Any police officer or animal control officer may, at such officer's discretion, take
an injured animal found unattended and off of the owner or keeper's premises to a
licensed veterinarian for emergency medical treatment. The owner shall be liable for
payment of all such emergency treatment. Payment of medical fees may be required before
the animal is released to the owner.
E.
Proof of ownership: A valid rabies tag worn by any animal shall be presumptive evidence that the owner
of the animal is the person registered as obtaining the rabies vaccination for such
animal.

A.
Prohibited: No person shall:
1.
Overdrive, overload, drive when overloaded or overwork any animal;
2.
Beat, mutilate or torture any animal;
3.
Needlessly shoot at, wound, capture or in any other manner needlessly molest, injure
or kill any animal;
4.
Carry, transport or keep in a cruel manner any animal.
B.
Defense: It is a specific defense to a charge of violating subsection A. of this section that
the action was necessary to avoid injury to a person or that the animal was not a
domesticated animal and the action was necessary to avoid injury to a person or property.
C.
Care: It is unlawful for any person to fail to provide an animal owned or in the custody
of such person with adequate food, water, protection from the elements, veterinary
services, humane care and treatment necessary to maintain the good health of the animal
and to prevent suffering of the animal, or other care generally considered to be normal,
usual and accepted for the animal's health and well being consistent with the species,
breed, and type of animal.
D.
Removal of animal: Where there is reasonable suspicion to believe that an animal(s) is being mistreated
or neglected, as described in subsection A. or C. of this section, an animal control
officer or police officer may enter the premises and remove and impound the animal(s).
"Premises", as used in this subsection, is defined to include the land and structures
thereon, not to include the residence.
E.
Warrant: Where an animal(s) is located in the residence and is not otherwise accessible, and
an animal control officer or police officer has probable cause to believe that the
animal(s) is being mistreated or neglected, as described in subsection A. or C. of
this section, to the extent the life of the animal(s) is endangered, an animal control
officer or police officer may enter the residence for the purpose of removing and
impounding the animal(s) upon the issuance of a warrant issued by an appropriate court
for such purpose.
F.
Owner responsible for charges: The owner or keeper of the impounded animal(s) shall be responsible for all charges
incurred in impounding the animal.
G.
Notification: Upon impounding the animal(s) the police department shall make all reasonable efforts
to notify the owner or keeper of the impoundment. If attempts to notify the owner
or keeper of the impoundment in person or by telephone are unsuccessful, notice may
be made by posting written notification of the impoundment at the premises from which
the animal(s) was removed.
H.
Disposition of impounded animal:
1.
When an animal has been seized and impounded pursuant to the provisions of this section,
upon motion of the town prosecutor, the municipal court may enter an order of forfeiture
of the animal if the court finds that there is a continuing threat to the health and
welfare of the animal in the event the animal is returned to the owner or keeper.
2.
A copy of the prosecutor's motion for forfeiture, along with notice of the hearing,
shall be personally served on the owner or keeper, or mailed first class mail to the
last known address of the owner or keeper at least five days prior to the date set
for the hearing.
3.
The town shall bear the burden of proof at the hearing, by a preponderance of the
evidence. If the owner or keeper, without good cause, fails to appear at the hearing,
the animal shall be deemed abandoned and, upon proper proof of the need for forfeiture,
may be ordered forfeited.
4.
The owner or keeper of the animal shall have the rights afforded defendants in judicial
proceedings including the right to be represented by counsel, the right to cross examine
the town's witnesses, and the right to compel the attendance of witnesses. The defendant
shall not have the right to a jury trial on the issue of forfeiture.
5.
The hearing shall be conducted as an administrative hearing and the rules of evidence
shall not be binding. Any and all documentary evidence deemed relevant by the municipal
judge may be received in evidence. The municipal judge shall have the power to determine
that the animal be further confined, to order that the owner or keeper take specific
steps to provide for the health and welfare of the animal, or to order that the owner
or keeper forfeit ownership of the animal, which shall then be surrendered to the
appropriate animal shelter.
6.
The owner or keeper of an animal that is the subject of a motion brought under this
section shall be liable for the cost of the care, keeping, transport, or disposal
of the animal. In no event shall the Town of Erie or its employees, agents or staff
be liable for such costs.
7.
The municipal court in its discretion may order a forfeiture authorized by this section
as an element of sentencing, as a condition of probation, or as a condition of a deferred
sentence.
8.
The owner, possessor or keeper of an animal that has been impounded because of alleged
neglect, abuse or cruelty shall post a bond in an amount to be determined by the police
department, to cover the estimated costs of the care and keeping of the animal from
the date the animal was taken into custody to the date of the hearing. Such bond shall
be filed with the court within five days of the date of written notification of such
bond to the owner, possessor, or keeper. In the event the owner, possessor, or keeper
fails to post bond as required herein, the animal shall be deemed abandoned, the hearing
on the issue of forfeiture vacated, and the custody of the animal shall be deemed
transferred to the appropriate animal shelter.
9.
If, in the opinion of a licensed veterinarian, the animal is experiencing extreme
pain or suffering or is severely injured past recovery, severely disabled past recovery,
or severely diseased past recovery, the animal may be euthanized without a court order.
10.
An animal that is not claimed by its owner, possessor or keeper within five days after
being eligible for release from impoundment pursuant to this section, shall be deemed
abandoned and may be disposed of at the discretion of the appropriate animal shelter.

Actions constituting littering: It shall be unlawful for any owner, possessor, or person, who keeps any domestic
animal, to permit any such domestic animal to deposit or leave any fecal matter on
any public or private property of another, or in any waters. It shall be an affirmative
defense to this subsection that in the event of the depositing of fecal matter by
a domesticated animal, the animal is in the immediate control of a person, and such
person forthwith removes such material from the said private or public place.
B.
Offensive premises: The accumulation of animal feces compromises public health and constitutes a threat
to public safety and welfare. It is unlawful for the owner or keeper of an animal
to allow animal feces to accumulate so as to be a health hazard or so that the odor
is noticeable on adjoining property. Such premises are declared to be a public nuisance.

Owners/keepers shall obey the hours and rules of the dog park as posted.
2.
Owners/keepers shall keep their dogs on a leash held by a person able to control the

3.
Dogs must be accompanied by a competent person able to control the dog(s) when inside

5.

6.

A.
Prohibited conduct: It is unlawful to possess any animal which engages in any of the following conduct
in the town:
1.
Inflicts a puncture wound, abrasion, or other wound upon a person or a domestic animal.
2.
Without provocation, and without effective and immediate control, repeatedly snaps
at or repeatedly lunges at, or approaches a person while growling and snarling at
such person in a threatening manner.
3.
Without provocation, and without effective and immediate control, chases or runs at
a person while snapping or biting, growling or snarling at such person in a threatening
manner.
B.
Exception: For purposes of this section, no animal shall be deemed an aggressive animal by reason
of having threatened or harassed a person when such person was engaged in any of the
following acts:
1.
The unlawful entry into or upon the animal owner's or keeper's property where such
animal is kept.
2.
The unlawful entry into or upon the animal owner's or keeper's motor vehicle or other
such vehicle wherein the animal is confined.
3.
The attacking or molesting of a person.
C.
Impoundment:
1.
Pursuant to subsection 6-7-4 B. of this chapter, a police officer or code enforcement officer may seize and impound
an animal that acts in an aggressive manner, as defined above.
2.
It is unlawful for any owner of an animal subject to an impoundment under this section
to fail to produce the animal, on demand of any Erie Police Officer/Animal Control
Officer, for examination and impoundment. If the owner or keeper of any such animal
willfully or knowingly secrets or refuses to produce such animal, such act shall constitute
a separate violation of this section.
3.
The owner or keeper shall be responsible for payment of all fees, including impound
fee, boarding fee, veterinary care charges, and any other costs associated with the
impoundment regardless of the results of any municipal charges.

A.
Prohibited: It is unlawful for any person to own or keep an animal that:
1.
Inflicts or causes bodily injury or serious bodily injury to a person, whether on
or off the premises of the owner or keeper.
2.
Inflicts or causes bodily injury or serious bodily injury upon a domestic animal or
livestock or causes the death of any domestic animal or livestock, whether on or off
the premises of the owner or keeper.
B.
Definitions: As used herein:
Bodily injury: Shall mean any physical injury that results in bruising, muscle tears, or skin lacerations
requiring professional medical treatment or any physical injury that requires corrective
or cosmetic surgery.
Livestock: Shall include, but not be limited to, chickens, ducks, cows, goats, horses.
Serious bodily injury: Shall means any bodily injury which, either at the time of the actual injury or at
a later time, involves a substantial risk of death, a substantial risk of serious
permanent disfigurement, a substantial risk of protracted loss or impairment of the
function of any part or organ of the body, or breaks, fractures, or burns of the second
or third degree.
C.
Defense: It shall be an affirmative defense to subsection A. of this section if:
1.
That, at the time of the attack, which caused serious bodily injury or bodily injury
to a person, the victim of the attack was committing or attempting to commit a criminal
offense against the animal's owner or keeper or the property of the owner or keeper.
2.
That, the person who was the victim of the attack by the animal tormented, provoked,
abused, or inflicted injury upon the animal.
3.
That, at the time of the attack by the animal, which caused bodily injury or serious
bodily injury to, or the death of a domestic animal, the domestic animal was attacking
the animal or its owner/keeper.
D.
Exception: The provisions of this section shall not apply to the following:
1.
To any dog that is used by a police officer while engaged in the performance of officer's
duties.
2.
To any dog that inflicts injury upon or causes the death of a domestic animal while
the dog was working as a hunting dog, herding dog, or predator control dog on the
property of or under the control of the dog's owner and the injury or death was to
a domestic animal naturally associated with the work of such dog.

A.
When an animal has been impounded pursuant to section 6-7-6 , "Dogs Habitually At-large", 6-7-10 , "Aggressive Animals", or 6-7-11 , "Animal Attacks", of this chapter, the owner or keeper shall be entitled to a dispositional
hearing.
B.
The court shall provide written notice of the date and time of the dispositional hearing
to the owner or keeper of the animal, if the identity of such person is known. Such
notice may be personally served on the owner or keeper, or by leaving a copy at the
last known residence of the owner or keeper, or by mailing first class mail to the
owner or keeper. Service must occur at least 72 hours before the hearing.
C.
The hearing shall be conducted as an administrative hearing, and the formal rules
of evidence shall not apply, though may be a guide regarding admission of evidence.
The court shall allow the parties to present evidence, witnesses and have the right
of cross examination. Any and all documentary evidence and other evidence deemed relevant
by the court shall be received in evidence. The owner or keeper shall have the rights
of a defendant in a judicial proceeding, including the right to be represented by
counsel and the right to compel the attendance of witnesses. No defendant is entitled
to court appointed counsel for representation at the dispositional hearing.
D.
If the owner or keeper fails to appear for the hearing, the court may proceed with
the hearing in his or her absence.
E.
At the hearing, if the court determines that the town has established, by a preponderance
of the evidence, that there is a reasonable likelihood of future injury to persons,
property, or animals, the court shall order the animal to remain impounded at the
owner/keeper's expense until the final disposition of any pending municipal charges.
In making this determination, the court shall consider the following:
1.
The conduct of the animal during the incident charged;
2.
Any evidence of aggressive or violent behavior by the animal, or threats thereof;
3.
Any prior animal related violations by the owner or keeper, including any municipal
ordinance or state law related to animals;
4.
Any prior violation by any other owner or keeper, involving the same animal, including
any violations of municipal ordinance or state law related to animals;
5.
Any conditions existing on the property where the animal has been or will be kept
which would affect the likelihood of any danger to persons, animals or property;
6.
Any evidence of any mitigating action taken by the owner or keeper of the animal which
would affect the likelihood of any danger to any persons, animals or property;
7.
Any other evidence relevant to the issues as determined by the court.

Municipal Court, if the owner or occupant of the property presents the search warrant
to the owner or occupant.
