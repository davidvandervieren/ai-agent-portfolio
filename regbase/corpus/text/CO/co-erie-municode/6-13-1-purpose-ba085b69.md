The purpose of this chapter is to protect the public health, safety and welfare, and
preserve neighborhood landscape and native environments, by regulating the type and
height of weeds including nonnative herbaceous and woody vegetation and noxious weeds
that are allowed in the town, and to specify limitations of these restrictions on
certain areas of the town, in private properties, parks, riparian areas along ditches
and streams, open space, and stream rights-of-way, that should be allowed to remain
in a natural appearing state.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001)
As used in this chapter, the following definitions shall apply:
Aggressive exotic species: Aggressive nonnative grasses and other herbaceous plants, deleterious to native plant
communities, and ornamental landscapes, not otherwise listed as weeds, including,
but not limited to, creeping bellflower (Campanula rapuncuoides), Japanese knotweed
(Reynoutria japonica), Grossheimia macrocephala, sweetclovers (Melilotus officinale
and M. albus), wild carrot (Daucus carota), and smooth brome (Bromus inermis).
Backyard wildlife habitat certification: A certification from the Colorado department of natural resources or the national
wildlife federation backyard habitat (.) program or schoolyard habitat (®) program
of having the necessary restored native vegetation and physical elements to function
as a wildlife habitat and excluding aggressive exotic species and other weed species
through a regular annual maintenance program.
Brush: Nonnative shrubs and trees which self-propagate and are not part of a planned and
maintained landscape of either an ornamental or native preserved or restored type
including, but not limited to, such exotic species as Russian olive (Elaeagnus angustifolia),
salt-cedar (Tamarix ramosissma), Siberian or Chinese elm (Ulmus pumila or siberica)
and tree of heaven (Ailanthus altissima). The term "self-propagate" shall mean the
ability to spread beyond the original plant through seed or underground roots in a
manner which permits invasion of adjacent areas not intended for this type of vegetation.
The term "brush" includes "brush pile", which shall mean an accumulation of dead branches
from shrubs, trees or weeds.
Converted pasture: An area which has been plowed and seeded to exotic agriculturally developed pasture
species including, but not limited to, crested wheatgrass (Agropyron cristatum), smooth
brome (Bromus inermis), meadow fescue (Festuca pratensis), timothy (Phleum pratense),
orchardgrass (Dactylis glomerata), or alfalfa (Medicago sativa), or an area which
is currently dominated by bare soil or exotic weeds due to heavy grazing pressure
and the loss of native vegetation.
Detention or retention ponds: Those flood control structures designed and installed in drainages and open space
areas for the purpose of flood flow reduction.
Ditch: A manmade or natural channel for water conveyance and its maintenance and access
easements on either side.
Hay meadow: An area which has been plowed and seeded to exotic agriculturally developed hay production
species including, but not limited to, smooth brome (Bromus inermis), meadow fescue
(Festuca pratensis), or alfalfa (Medicago sativa).
Lawn grasses: Species of grass developed for and commonly used as turf including Kentucky bluegrass
(Poa pratensis), perennial rye grass (Lolium perenne), chewing fescue (Festuca rubra),
fairway crested wheatgrass (Agropyron cristatum), buffalo grass (Buchloe dactyloides),
blue grama (Bouteloua or Chondrosum gracile), and salt grass (Distichlis stricta).
Native turf grasses: Native species of grass developed for and commonly used as turf including buffalo
grass, blue grama, and inland salt grass.
Natural area certification: An area certified by the Colorado Department of Natural Resources as an area of one-fourth
acre or greater having the necessary characteristics to be registered as a natural
area and excluding aggressive exotic species and other weed species through a regular
annual maintenance program.
Open space area: Real property zoned as open space, or designated transition, or designated on plats
as open space, or other such undeveloped real property in single, common or joint
ownership.
Ornamental grasses: Exotic and native grasses which are commercially available for use in maintained
home landscapes as listed in the "Guidelines Of Potential Native And Ornamental Grasses"
adopted by the Town of Erie, as revised, but not including exotic species typical
of improved hay meadows or pastures such as smooth brome (Bromus inermis), orchard
grass (Daytylis glomerata), timothy (Phleum pratense), or meadow fescue (Festuca pratensis).
Owner: The fee owner of record, an agent or representative of the fee owner, any person
entitled by tenancy or lease to possession of the premises, or the occupant.
Prairie: A parcel of historically unplowed native vegetation or a parcel certified by the
Town of Erie which is designed, restored, and vigorously maintained to be true to
approved native community design or certified as a preexisting native plant community
consisting of grasses and associated wildflowers native to the region and including,
but not limited to, short grass prairie up to 12 inches tall, midgrass prairie up
to 18 inches tall, and tall grass prairie over 18 inches tall, and excluding aggressive
exotic species and other weed species through a regular annual maintenance program.
"Historically unplowed native vegetation" shall mean an area of essentially undisturbed
soil, by either agriculture or other development, and retaining significant cover
by local native vegetation, which is under active maintenance for weed and aggressive
exotic species and brush control.
Property: The area of the owner's lot or tract of land, improved or vacant, and the area to
the center of the alley adjacent to the lot or tract of land, and the sidewalk, parking
area, curb, and gutter adjacent to said lot or tract or land.
State of Colorado Noxious Weed List: Those weeds as are identified by the commissioner of the state of Colorado department
of agriculture or his or her designee on the state noxious weed list prepared in accordance
with the requirements of the "Colorado Noxious Weed Act", C.R.S. § 35-5.5-101 et seq.
Town manager: The town manager or the designated representative of the town manager.
Weed or weeds: Aggressive nonnative grasses and other herbaceous plants, deleterious to native plant
communities, ornamental landscapes, and agricultural areas including, but not limited
to, redroot pigweed (Amaranthus retrofexus), common ragweed (Ambrosia artemisifolia),
giant ragweed (Ambrosia trifida), wild oats (Avena fatua), wild mustards (Brassica
hirta, B. kaber, B. niger, B. rapa), knapweeds (Centaurea maculosa, C. repens, C.
diffusa), prickly lettuce (Lactuca seriola), curly dock (Rumex crispus), buckhorn
plantain (Plantago lanceolata), buffalo burr (Solanum rostratum), shepherd's purse
(Thlaspi arvense), cocklebur (Xanthium strumaruim), and all weed species on the Colorado
noxious weed list as defined in this section, including all new or old synonyms, but
does not include other nonlisted plants in flower or vegetable gardens; small plots
of wheat, barley, oats, or rye; actively grazed pastures; and planned and maintained
shrubs and woody plants on private and public lands.
Xeriscape: A landscape certified by the Town of Erie which is designed, planted, and vigorously
maintained to be true to approved design and excluding aggressive exotic species and
other weed species through a regular annual maintenance program.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001; amd. Ord. 30-2008, 10-14-2008; Ord. No. 031-2023 , § 1, 11-28-2023)
A.
It shall be deemed a public nuisance and unlawful to permit or maintain on any property
any growth or accumulation of weeds to a height greater than 12 inches.
B.
It shall be deemed a public nuisance and unlawful to permit or maintain on any property
the growth of species on the Colorado Noxious Weed List on such property regardless
of height.
C.
It shall be deemed a public nuisance and unlawful to permit or maintain on any property
any growth or accumulation of brush.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001; amd. Ord. 30-2008, 10-14-2008)
A.
Exotic grasses: It shall be deemed a public nuisance and unlawful to permit or maintain the growth
of aggressive exotic species of grasses including, but not limited to, smooth brome
(Bromus inermis), meadow fescue (Festuca pratensis), timothy (Phleum pratense), orchard
grass (Dactylis glomerata), alfalfa (Medicago sativa), in excess of 12 inches in height
on any part of any property, unless said parcel is an actively grazed pasture or annually
mowed hay meadow.
B.
Lawn grasses: It shall be deemed a public nuisance and unlawful to permit or maintain the growth
of lawn grasses in excess of six inches in height on any residentially zoned or landscaped
property.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001; amd. Ord. 30-2008, 10-14-2008)
A.
Community open spaces, ditches, ponds and certified areas:
1.
Any owner of any property that is a community open space area, ditch, detention or
detention pond area or has been granted a certified backyard wildlife habitat, a certified
school yard habitat, a certified Xeriscape, or a certified restored native prairie
may maintain growth of approved native or ornamental grasses in excess of 12 inches
in height.
2.
Approved ornamental and native grasses may be allowed to grow to full height. The
town manager may request mowing such grasses to reduce fire hazard when declared by
a relevant competent authority.
3.
Weeds, noxious weeds, and brush must be controlled on all property including community
open space areas, ditches, detention or detention pond areas, certified backyard wildlife
habitats, schoolyard habitats, certified xeriscapes, certified restored native landscapes
or prairies, and certified historically unplowed vegetation as required by this chapter.
B.
Grasses in landscaped flower or shrub beds: It is lawful for any owner to maintain approved ornamental and native grasses in
a well maintained landscape. Said grasses may be allowed to grow to full height. All
weeds, including noxious weeds, aggressive exotic species, hay meadow and converted
pasture grasses, and brush must be controlled throughout as required by this chapter.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001; Ord. 30-2008, 10-14-2008; Ord. No. 031-2023 , § 1, 11-28-2023)
A.
Any Erie Police Officer, code enforcement officer, or the town manager, is authorized
and empowered to examine or cause to be examined every premises suspected to contain
unlawful height and/or accumulation of weeds and brush for purposes of enforcement
of this chapter and/or abatement. If violations are found to exist, the officer may
issue the owner a summons and complaint to appear in the Erie Municipal Court. Compliance
with the requirements of this chapter prior to the court appearance may be considered
in mitigation.
B.
If practicable, such officer may initially request the owner for permission to enter
private property not then open to the public for purposes of inspection. Otherwise,
unless an emergency is found to exist resulting in exigent circumstances, such officer
shall obtain a search warrant as provided for in rule 241, municipal court rules of
procedure, as amended.
(Ord. 754, 7-10-2001; Ord. No. 031-2023 , § 1, 11-28-2023)
A.
Whenever the town manager, or designee determines that a nuisance exists on or in
any property, under this chapter, the town manager, or designee, may provide written
notice in the manner provided in subsection C. of this section.
B.
Written notice of any nuisance, shall:
1.
Include the property address;
2.
Describe the violation(s);
3.
Include a correction order allowing ten days, or less time where warranted at the
discretion of the town manager, or designee, to make necessary repairs and improvements
to abate the nuisance;
4.
Inform the owner, occupant of the right to appeal pursuant to this chapter;
5.
Include a statement of the town's right to abate the nuisance and file a lien in accordance
with this section.
C.
Service of the written abatement notice shall be by first class mail, postage prepaid,
to the owner as reflected on the records of the county clerk's office. Besides service
by mail, the officer may, if practicable, provide a copy of the abatement notice by
hand delivery to the owner, or by posting such notice on the subject property, or
by other means reasonably calculated to provide notice.
D.
It shall be the duty of the owner or occupant of any property, in or upon which any
nuisance may be found, to abate the same within the time specified in the abatement
notice. Timely filing of an appeal from the abatement notice under this section shall
extend the time for abatement until a final ruling on the appeal is issued. In addition
to any other available sanction or relief, if such owner or occupant neglects or refuses
to comply with the requirements of such order within the time specified, he or she
commits a violation of this chapter.
E.
The town manager, or designee, shall be duly authorized to abate any nuisance specified
in this chapter, which shall include authority to engage the necessary assistance
and incur necessary expense therefor.
F.
The expense incurred by the town in abating any nuisance as provided herein may be
recovered by proper action from the owner or occupant of such property.
G.
The town manager, or designee, shall certify to the town clerk the costs incurred
in the abatement of the nuisance, including administrative costs and attorney fees
and five percent for inspection and other incidental costs in connection therewith.
H.
Notice of assessment; appeal of charges:
1.
Upon receipt of the statement of charges, the town clerk shall mail to the owner of
record of the subject property a notice, stating the amount and grounds for the charges;
the deadline for payment of the assessment, and that in the event such payment is
not made, the town will assess the charges against the property; that any objection
to the proposed assessment must be made in writing and filed with the town clerk's
office within 14 days of the date of the notice of assessment. Upon the expiration
of the 14-day period, if the town clerk receives no objections, and if payment in
full has not been received, the town clerk shall certify such charges to the clerk
to the county treasurer who shall collect the assessment together with a ten percent
penalty for cost of collection, in the same manner as other taxes are collected.
2.
If the property owner files timely appeal with the town clerk, the town clerk shall
refer the matter to a hearing officer appointed by the town manager.
3.
Upon conclusion of administrative review, the hearing officer shall determine, in
writing, whether the charges are proper. The appellant shall have the burden of proof,
by a preponderance of the evidence. The hearing officer shall affirm, cancel or reduce
the charges. The written ruling of the hearing officer shall be provided to the town
clerk and the property owner.
4.
The hearing officer may reduce or cancel a proposed assessment if it is determined
that any of the following did not conform to the provisions of this section:
a.
Any required notice to correct the subject violations; or
b.
The work performed in abating the nuisance; or
c.
The computation of charges.
5.
If the hearing officer affirms the assessment, the owner shall pay the amount in full
by the reasonable deadline established by the hearing officer. In the event timely
payment is not received, the town clerk may certify the charges to the clerk to the
county treasurer as provided herein.
I.
In addition to the penal remedy and abatement procedure stated in this chapter, any
violation of this chapter is a public nuisance, for which there is no adequate remedy
at law, and therefore any court of competent jurisdiction shall, upon the town attorney's
application, enjoin the nuisance, as defined in this chapter. Nothing in this chapter
shall prevent the town attorney from seeking other appropriate legal or equitable
relief from any court of competent jurisdiction. In addition, the owner of any property
constituting a nuisance shall be liable to the town, jointly and severally with every
person creating or maintaining the nuisance, for the town's reasonable expense incurred
in abating the nuisance and the town attorney may commence and prosecute to final
judgment an action at law in any court of competent jurisdiction to collect such expenses.
As used in this section, court of competent jurisdiction includes, without limitation,
the Erie Municipal Court, which insofar as practicable, shall consider and determine
applications for legal or equitable relief according to the procedures otherwise applicable
to proceedings in Colorado District Courts.
(Ord. 08-2012, 3-13-2012; Ord. No. 031-2023 , § 1, 11-28-2023)
