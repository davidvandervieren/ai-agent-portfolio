At the organizational meeting following a town council election, the council shall
appoint any positions required by the Charter to be appointed directly by the town
council; provided, however, that the municipal judge shall be appointed in the time
and manner required by the Charter. All other positions are subject to appointment
by the town administrator, who has the power to hire, suspend, transfer and remove
any town official or employee not appointed by the town council. Any provision of
this code and/or any ordinance of the town which states to the contrary is repealed
to the extent it is inconsistent with this provision.
Except as otherwise provided by the Charter, all appointments shall remain in effect
until the appointee resigns or is removed or replaced pursuant to this section and/or
the town Charter.
Any positions appointed by the town council prior to the adoption of the ordinance
codified in this section, but which do not require town council appointment under
the Charter, shall be subject to suspension, transfer or removal at the discretion
of the town administrator.
(Ord. 2001-4 § 2)
A.
Before entering upon the duties of their respective offices, each person elected or
appointed to any office, shall take and subscribe the oath of office, and execute
and file the bond prescribed or required by law or ordinance in the case of such offices
respectively, which oath and bond shall be filed with the town clerk, except that
of the town clerk, which shall be filed with the town treasurer. The town treasurer,
town clerk, and all other officers who may be appointed or elected by the town council
to any office, shall each give bonds to the town, with good and sufficient sureties,
to be approved by the town council, in penal sums to be set by the town council at
the annual budget meeting, conditioned that they will faithfully perform the duties
of their respective offices, and that they will, in the manner and at the time required
by law or ordinance, or when required by the town council, pay all moneys and deliver
up all books, papers and other properties in their custody belonging to the town.
The town council, by resolution, may waive any of the above bond requirements, where
it is in the best interests of the town.
B.
In the event that the official bond of any officer of the town shall, after the taking
and approval thereof, become insufficient by reason of the death or insolvency of
any of the sureties thereon, the town council may require such officer to procure
additional sureties, or to give a new bond, and may designate the time when such additional
sureties or new bond shall be furnished, which shall be not less than ten (10) days,
and in the event that the additional sureties or new bond are not furnished within
the time so designated, the office shall be declared vacant and the vacancy shall
be filled as provided for by ordinance.
(Ord. 2001-1 § 2; prior code § 2-02-020)
A.
The compensation of the mayor shall be one thousand dollars ($1,000.00) per month.
B.
The compensation of each other council member shall be five hundred dollars ($500.00)
per month.
C.
In accordance with Section 3.9 of the Home Rule Charter of the Town of New Castle,
Colorado, these rates of compensation shall not take effect with respect to any of
the persons currently serving as mayor and other council members until a subsequent
term of office of any such person. Until these rates of compensation take effect,
the compensation of the mayor shall be four hundred seventy dollars ($470.00) per
month, and the compensation of each other council member shall be three hundred seventy
dollars ($370.00) per month.
(Ord. No. 2010-1, § 2, 2-16-2010; Ord. No. TC 2021-9, § 2, 10-19-2021)

A municipal court in and for the town, is created and established pursuant to and
governed by the provisions of C.R.S. Art. 13-10, as now existing or as may be hereafter
amended.
(Ord. 2003-11 § 5: prior code § 6-02-010)
The municipal court shall have the original jurisdiction of all cases arising under
the Municipal Code and ordinances of the town, with full power to punish violators
thereof by the imposition of such fines and penalties as are prescribed by the code,
ordinance, or court rule. The procedures of the court shall be in accordance with
the Municipal Court Rules of Procedure as promulgated by the Colorado Supreme Court.
(Prior code § 6-02-020)
There shall be regular sessions of court for the trial of cases. The municipal judge
may hold a special session of court at any time. All sessions of court shall be open
to the public. Where the nature of the case is such that it would be in the best interest
of justice to exclude persons not directly connected with the proceeding, the municipal
judge may order the courtroom cleared.
(Prior code § 6-02-030)
A.
The court shall be presided over by a municipal judge appointed for a term of two
years, by resolution of the town council. The town council shall also have the discretion
to appoint one or more assistant municipal judges for a term not to exceed two years.
Additional substitute judges may be appointed as may be necessary to transact the
business of the court. No judge shall be removable except for cause as provided in
the Charter.
B.
If the judge of the municipal court shall find it necessary to disqualify himself
or herself in a given case, or should he or she be unable to serve for any reason,
including vacation or illness, the town council may, by resolution, appoint a judge
to hear cases for such time as the presiding judge is absent.
C.
Compensation of the municipal judge shall be payable as other salaries to municipal
employees. Payment of fees or compensation based directly on the number of individual
cases handled or heard by the municipal judge is prohibited. Before entering upon
the duties of his or her office, the municipal judge shall make an oath by affirmation
that he or she will support the Constitution of the United States, the Constitution
of the state of Colorado, and the Municipal Code and ordinances of the town, and that
he or she will faithfully perform the duties of his or her office.
D.
The municipal judge shall operate the municipal court as a qualified municipal court
of record and shall cause to be kept a verbatim record of the proceedings and evidence
at trial by either electronic devices or stenographic means.
E.
The municipal judge shall fully comply and abide with all conditions of employment
that may be imposed by the town council, and also with all applicable Colorado statutes,
including Sec. 13-10-101, et seq., C.R.S. 1973.
F.
In addition to other powers, the municipal judge shall have the full power and authority
to make and adopt rules and regulations for conducting the business of the municipal
court, consistent with the Municipal Rules of Procedure promulgated by the Colorado
Supreme Court.
(Ord. 2006-8 § 2; prior code § 6-02-040)
The municipal judge shall appoint the municipal court clerk. The municipal court clerk
shall file monthly reports with the town clerk, of all moneys collected, and on the
last day of each month, shall pay to the town treasurer all moneys then in the hands
of the court. The municipal court clerk shall have such other duties as are delegated
to him or her by law, court rules or the municipal judge.
(Prior code § 6-02-050)
The town council shall, on an annual basis, budget and appropriate funds to pay the
annual salary of the municipal judge, together with such other expenses as may be
necessary for the proper operation of the municipal court.
(Prior code § 6-02-060)
The municipal judge is empowered, in his or her discretion, to assess the following
costs against any defendant (or in the case of an unemancipated minor defendant, such
minor and his or her parents or legal guardians) who is found guilty of violating
any of the ordinances of the town, against a defendant who is granted a deferred prosecution
or deferred sentence, or against a defendant cited for operating a motor vehicle without
insurance or for failing to present evidence of insurance:
A.
Court Costs. In addition to the actual cost of witness fees and juror fees for a trial
and such other costs as may be required by the Colorado Municipal Court Rules of Procedure,
thirty dollars ($30.00) per action filed in municipal court for violation of the Model
Traffic Code; thirty five dollars ($35.00) per action filed in municipal court for
all other violations;
B.
Witness Fees. Three dollars ($3.00) per day or part of a day in which the witness
is actually in court plus mileage at the current IRS rate per mile for each mile actually
and necessarily traveled from the witness' residence to the court;
C.
Jury Fee. Six dollars ($6.00) per day for actual jury service or three dollars ($3.00)
per day of service on the jury panel alone, plus mileage at the current IRS rate per
mile for each mile actually and necessarily traveled from the juror's residence to
the court, but in any event not less than the twenty-five dollar ($25.00) fee to be
paid to the court to request a jury trial pursuant to the Municipal Court Rules of
Procedure;
D.
Bench Warrant Fee. Fifty dollars ($50.00) per warrant issued plus any costs incurred
for transporting the defendant from his or her place of arrest to the town;
E.
Probationary or Other Services. Actual costs incurred either by the town or the defendant
for probationary or treatment services for the defendant or useful public service
by the defendant as such may be ordered by the court;
F.
Letter Fee. Five dollars ($5.00) per letter for any letter sent to a defendant where
the defendant has failed to respond to a written notice of court or a summons. Such
letter fee shall not limit the court's ability to fine or sentence a defendant for
contempt for such failure to respond.
G.
Jail Costs. Actual per day cost charged to the town by any county jail or any county
sheriff for each day a defendant is sentenced to jail plus any fees charged to the
town for services of any county bond commissioner for such defendant;
H.
Deferred Judgment and Deferred Prosecution Fee. Fifty dollars ($50.00) per deferred
judgment or deferred prosecution issued;
I.
Certified/Restricted Delivery Mailing Fee. Actual costs for certified/restricted delivery
mailing of notices to a defendant where the defendant has failed to respond to a written
notice of the court or a summons or has failed to make payment as directed by the
court;
J.
Surcharge. In addition to any court costs or other costs imposed by this section,
there shall be assessed against each defendant, upon conviction, the following surcharges:
1.
Eighteen dollars ($18.00), which surcharge shall be remitted to the town and used
exclusively for the purpose of training police officers in the performance of their
duties, and
2.
Two dollars ($2.00), which surcharge shall be remitted to the town exclusively for
the Dennis J. Mahan Memorial Scholarship Fund;
K.
Suspended Sentence Fee. Fifty dollars ($50.00) for each action in which the municipal
judge suspends all or part of a sentence, to compensate the municipal court for monitoring
such action to ensure compliance with the conditions of the suspension;
L.
Motor Vehicle Insurance Fee. Ten dollars ($10.00) for each action in which a defendant
is cited for operating a motor vehicle without insurance or for failing to present
evidence of insurance, which defendant later presents proof of having insurance in
effect at the time of the alleged violation. The purpose of this fee is to compensate
the municipal court for costs associated with processing the citation through the
municipal court.
M.
Sealed records fee. Sixty five dollars ($65.00) for all actions sealed pursuant to
C.R.S. Sec. 24-72-702.5.
(Ord. 2007-4 § 3; Ord. 2004-3 § 1; prior code § 6-04-130)
(Ord. No. 2012-2, § 2, 6-19-2012; Ord. No. 2022-7, § 2, 6-21-2022)
A.
The municipal judge is authorized to find any person properly before the court in
contempt for failing to follow or abide by a lawful order or act of the court. Such
contempt may be direct or indirect.
B.
Any person found in contempt of court by failing to perform a lawful act required
by or otherwise made mandatory by this code or the municipal court shall be punished
by a fine not to exceed two thousand six hundred fifty dollars ($2,650.00) and imprisonment
not to exceed a term of ten (10) days.
C.
In cases of indirect contempt, the alleged contemnor shall have all the rights, privileges,
safeguards and protections of a defendant in a petty offense case, including, but
not limited to, a formal written complaint, arraignment and trial by jury.
(Ord. No. 2013-3, § 2, 6-4-2013; Ord. No. 2022-6, § 2, 6-21-2022)
It shall be a municipal offense for any person to fail to appear in court when directed
to do so by a summons, subpoena, or when ordered to do so by the court. Upon conviction
of a violation of this section, such person shall be punished in accordance with the
provisions of Section 1.20.010 .
(Ord. No. 2013-3, § 2, 6-4-2013)
The office of the town clerk is designated the building department and the town clerk
shall serve as the building official with jurisdiction over the building department.
(Prior code § 12-02-010)
The building department and the building official shall have such duties, responsibilities
and powers as are set forth in the Uniform Building Code, as adopted by reference
in Chapter 15.08 .
(Prior code § 12-02-020)
The building official shall have the right to contract or delegate the duties of making
physical inspections of buildings as required by the Uniform Building Code to the
building department of Garfield County, Colorado. The building official shall issue
such permits, orders and make other administrative decisions under the Building Code
relying upon the inspection and other factual determinations of the Garfield County

(Prior code § 12-02-030)
A.
The town is authorized to execute and deliver to the Department of Employment Security,
state of Colorado, a plan, or plans, and agreement, required under Section 5 of [Chapter
111-7, C.R.S. 1953] and the Social Security Act, to extend coverage to employees and
officers of the town and do all other necessary things to effectuate coverage of employees
and officers under the Old-Age and Survivors' Insurance System.
B.
The clerk is authorized to establish a system of payroll deduction to be matched by
payments by the town to be paid into the contribution fund of the state through the
Department of Employment Security and to make charges of this tax to the fund, or
funds, from which wage or salary payments are issued to employees of the town. Such
payments are to be made in accordance with the provisions of Section 1400 and 1410
of the Federal Insurance Contribution Act on all services that constitute employment
within the meaning of that act. Payments made to the Department of Employment Security,
state of Colorado, shall be due and payable on or before the eighteenth day of the
month immediately following the completed calendar quarter, and such payments that
are delinquent shall bear interest at the rate of one-half of one percent per month
until such time as payments are made.
C.
Appropriation is made from the proper funds of the town, in the necessary amount to
pay into the contribution fund as provided in Section 5(c)(1 ) of [Ch. 111-7 CRS 1953]
and in accordance with the plan, plans, and agreement.
D.
Authority is given to the mayor and the clerk of the town, to enter into an agreement
with the Department of Employment Security, state of Colorado, which agreement shall
be in accordance with House Bill No. 291 and with paragraph 218 of the Social Security
Act. Such plan and agreement shall provide that the participation of this town shall
be in effect as of October 1, 1955.

As used in this chapter, the following words, terms, or phrases shall have the following
meanings:
"Gift" means a thing of value given from a person without such person receiving in
return lawful compensation or consideration therefor of equal or greater value.
"Person" means any individual, corporation, business trust, estate, trust, limited
liability company, partnership, labor organization, association, political party,
committee, or other legal entity.
"Thing of value" means any tangible or intangible item having a market value, including,
without limitation, the following: money, real property, personal property, services,
loans at a rate of interest substantially lower than the commercial rate then currently
prevalent for similar loans, discounts, promises of future employment, event tickets,
travel, lodging, and the forbearance or forgiveness of debt.
"Town employee" or "employee" means a compensated person in the service of the town,
whether full- or part-time, who is designated as an employee in the town's personnel
rules or payroll, but shall not include any person providing services for the town
who is considered for federal income tax purposes to be an independent contractor.
"Town official" means any person duly elected or appointed to, and currently serving
on the town council, the planning and zoning commission, or the zoning board of adjustment
of the town of New Castle.
(Ord. 2006-17 § 2 (part))
A town official or employee shall not solicit or accept any gift from any person,
either directly or indirectly through such town official's or town employee's spouse
or dependent child, which gift the town official or employee knows, or which a reasonable
person in the town official's or employee's position should know under the circumstances,
is either:
A.
A gift that would tend to influence that town official or employee to depart from
the faithful and impartial discharge of his or her public duties; or
B.
A gift being offered or given for the primary purpose of rewarding or showing appreciation
to the town official or employee for an official action he or she has taken.
(Ord. 2006-17 § 2 (part))
The following gifts shall be deemed not to satisfy the criteria of a prohibited gift
under Section 2.20.020 of this chapter:
A.
A campaign contribution authorized by law;
B.
A nonmonetary award, publicly presented, in recognition of public service;
C.
Gifts similarly available to the general public;
D.
Educational scholarships and grants available to members of the general public similarly
situated;
E.
Grants and services provided for medical, respite or hospice care or other social
welfare needs available to members of the general public similarly situated;
F.
A component of the compensation paid or other incentive given to the recipient in
the ordinary course of his or her employment;
G.
Gifts given on a special occasion by an individual who is a relative or personal friend
of the recipient;
H.
Unsolicited informational material, publications, or subscriptions related to the
town official's or employee's performance of his or her duties;
I.
An unsolicited token or award of appreciation in the form of a plaque, trophy, memento,
or similar item;
J.
Payment of or reimbursement for actual and necessary expenditures for registration,
travel, lodging and meals for attendance at a convention, training seminar, or other
meeting at which the town official or employee is scheduled to participate as a representative
of the town or as part of his or her official duties;
K.
An occasional, unsolicited opportunity to participate in a business meeting or social
function where a meal is served and/or entertainment is provided if the town official's
or employee's attendance would not be considered extraordinary when viewed in light
of the position held by the town official or employee; and
L.
Admission to, and the cost of food or beverages consumed at a reception, meal, or
meeting by an organization before whom the recipient appears to speak, answer questions,
or receive an award as part of a scheduled program.
(Ord. 2006-17 § 2 (part))
If a town official or employee was offered or received, within one year of the public
meeting, any gift from a person appearing at a public meeting in which the town official
or employee participated or is participating, the town official or employee shall
disclose such information in the record of the public meeting.
(Ord. 2006-17 § 2 (part))
A.
It is unlawful for any town official or employee to violate any provision of this
chapter. Proof of a violation shall be established by a preponderance of the evidence
presented at trial.
B.
Penalty. Any town official or employee convicted of a violation of any provision of
this chapter shall be deemed to have committed a civil infraction and shall be punished
by a civil fine not to exceed one thousand dollars ($1,000.00). In addition to any
civil fine imposed, a judgment shall be entered against the offending town official
or employee to pay restitution to the town in the amount of twice the fair market
value of the prohibited gift received.
C.
Commission Members. A violation of any provision of this chapter by a member of the
planning and zoning commission may be deemed cause for removal for purposes of Section
7.2 of the town Charter.
(Ord. 2006-17 § 2 (part))
A.
The town council shall hold regular meetings on the first and third Tuesdays of each
month at Town Hall at 450 West Main Street in New Castle. The town council may by
motion cancel or reschedule any such meeting, so long as it holds at least one regular
meeting per month.
B.
Regular meetings of the town council shall begin at seven p.m. and end at ten p.m.
Town council may by motion alter the starting time or the ending time of a meeting.
C.
Special meetings of the town council shall be called in accordance with Section 3.7(B)
of the Home Rule Charter of the town of New Castle.
