This title embodies minimum standards for design and improvement of land subdivision
projects to ensure that each building site is capable of accommodating a structure
devoted to the intended use of the land; is free from natural hazards such as flooding,
falling rocks, landslides, and snowslides; is served by a street system providing
safe and convenient access and is provided with acceptable utility installations;
with all of the foregoing intended to meet the minimum requirements of and to promote
the public health, safety and welfare of the citizens of the town. This title shall
be known as and may be cited and referred to as the "New Castle Development Code,"
or the "New Castle Municipal Code, Title 16."
(Prior code § 14-02-010)
For the purpose of this title, certain terms are defined as provided in this section.
"Alley" means a right-of-way reserved or dedicated for public use that provides secondary
vehicular access to adjacent properties. It may also be used for utility access.
"Development application" means the process through which all land use reviews are
conducted by the town, beginning with a development application form listing the name
of the property owner, the name of the applicant if different, the legal description
of the real property, and the various land use review processes proposed by an applicant,
and all phases of the project and every subsequent stage of review required by the
land use regulations and ordinances of the town, including any land use review application
pending before the town as of August 9, 2000.
"Easement" means a conveyance or reservation of the use of land for a specialized
purpose in the public interest.
"Lot split" means a subdivision of a single lot, tract, or parcel of land into no
more than two lots or condominium units where no new street is created.
"Minor subdivision" means a subdivision of a single lot, tract, or parcel into no
more than five lots or condominium units.
"Planned unit development" or "PUD" means a project of a single owner or a group of
owners acting jointly, involving a related group of residences, or businesses, or
industries, and associated uses, planned as a single entity and therefore subject
to development and regulation as one land use unit rather than as an aggregation of
individual buildings located on separate lots. The planned unit development includes
usable, functional, open space for the mutual benefit of the entire tract and is designed
to provide variety and diversity through the variance of normal zoning and subdivision
standards so that maximum long-range benefits can be gained and the unique features
of the development or site are preserved and enhanced, while still being in harmony
with the surrounding neighborhood. It shall contain sufficient total area in relation
to the intended use or uses to permit effective application of planning principles
to protect the land form, reduce street and utility costs, and to provide a superior
human environment. Approval of a planned unit development may not eliminate the requirements
of subdividing in accordance with the provisions of this title. A preliminary plat
and a final plat may be submitted and processed through the subdivision procedure
as provided in these regulations.
"Planning commission" means the planning and zoning commission of the town.
"Street" means a right-of-way reserved or dedicated for public use that provides principal
vehicular and pedestrian access to adjacent properties. It may also be used for utility
access. Streets will be classified according to the following categories:
1.
Expressway streets: those whose primary purpose is to facilitate traffic flow through
the town rather than to or from the town. All state highways within the town limits
shall be defined as expressways.
2.
Arterial streets: those designed for high volume, moderate speed circulation of traffic
between separate areas of the town with a minimum of hindrance to traffic flow.
3.
Collector streets: those with low to medium traffic volume, whose purpose is to provide
access to residential areas and to channel traffic from residential areas to arterial
streets.
4.
Local residential streets: those with low volume, low speed traffic that are intended
to provide access to adjacent residential lots.
"Subdivider" means any person, firm, partnership, joint venture, association or corporation
who shall participate as the owner or an agent of the owner in the planning, platting,
development, promotion, sale or lease of a subdivision of land.
"Subdivision" means the division of a lot, tract or parcel into two or more parcels,
building sites, tracts or lots or other divisions of land, for the purpose, whether
immediate or future, of sale or of building development. It includes resubdivision
and, when appropriate to the context, relates to the process of subdividing or to
the land subdivided. "Subdivision," as used in this title, also applies to the division
of any structure or building into condominium units as defined by Article 33, Title
38, C.R.S., and includes annexation or the process of annexation when appropriate
to the context.
"Town administrator" means the town administrator of the town.
"Town council" or "council" means the town council of the Town of New Castle, Colorado.
(Prior code § 14-02-020)
Whenever appropriate within the context, words in the present tense include the future
tense, words used in the singular number include the plural and words used in the
plural number include the singular. "Shall" is mandatory. "May" is permissive.
(Prior code § 14-02-030)
A.
Designation. This title shall apply to all divisions of land into two or more parcels,
building sites, tracts or lots, to the creation of separate interests in a parcel,
trust, or lot such as condominium ownership, and also to the process of annexing land
to the town.
B.
Exclusions. Unless the method of division is adopted for the purpose of evading this
title, requirements of this title shall not apply to divisions created by order of
any court in this state or by operation of law, by a lien, mortgage, deed of trust,
or any other security instrument, by estate proceeding, by the creation of cemetery
lots or from a conveyance or other transfer of land to the town which is accepted
by the town. The commission may, pursuant to rules and regulations or resolution,
exempt from the requirements of this title any division of land that the commission
determines is not within the purposes of this title and shall take such action by
written resolution.
(Prior code § 14-02-040)
The jurisdiction of this title shall include all land located within the legal boundaries
of the town, all land contiguous to the town which is proposed for annexation, and
all unincorporated land located within three miles of the corporate limits of the
town for major street plan purposes when a major street plan has been approved in
accordance with the requirements of Part 2, Article 23, Title 31, C.R.S.

For any land use application, the applicant shall pay all costs incurred by the town
for the preparation of plats, plans, other required data and documents, recording
fees, publication costs, legal and engineering review and advice, planning review
and advice, inspections and all other out-of-pocket costs incurred by the town in
connection with the land use application. The town shall have the right to charge
interest at the rate of one and one-half percent per month (eighteen (18) percent
per year) on all amounts not paid within thirty (30) days after a statement. If any
amount remains unpaid for more than sixty (60) days after a statement to the applicant,
the town shall have the authority to assess all unpaid amounts, including interest,
to the Garfield County treasurer who shall proceed to collect the same, together with
a ten (10) percent penalty for cost of collection, in the same manner as other taxes
are collected. The laws of the state of Colorado for assessment and collection of
general taxes, including the laws for the sale and redemption of property for taxes,
shall apply to the collection of such assessments. In the case of withdrawal or denial
of a land use application, the applicant shall be responsible for all costs actually
incurred by the town in connection with such application regardless of the state of
the review process at which the application is withdrawn or denied.

by subsection A of this section, all costs incurred by the community planner in connection
with review of the application at an hourly rate as shall be determined by resolution
of the New Castle town council and as may be amended from time to time.

The purpose of this chapter is to provide simplified procedures for applications for
minor subdivisions and lot splits as defined above. When proposed by the applicant
for minor subdivision approval, the procedures for sketch plan may be waived by the
town, and the requirements and procedures for preliminary plan and final plat may
be combined into one application submittal and review procedure before the town planning
commission and the town council. For lot splits, the applicant shall follow the procedures
and requirements set forth below, and the procedures for the submittal of sketch,
preliminary, and final plans prior to approval shall not apply. Certain submittal
requirements, as set forth in these regulations, may be waived by the town when deemed
unnecessary or not applicable. Except as expressly modified by this chapter or as
expressly waived by the town, all existing ordinances and provisions of the town code
shall apply to minor subdivisions and lot splits. Approval criteria for minor subdivisions
and lot splits shall include all those matters described in Section 16.04.010 regarding the purpose of all subdivisions. However, requirements for public improvements
shall take into account the character of the minor subdivision or lot split, and the
town may modify various requirements such as, for example, permitting narrower rights-of-way
or other deviations from town standards in light of the minor impacts of the lot split
or minor subdivision.
The procedures for minor subdivisions and lot splits shall be available only once
to the same parcel, unit, lot, or portion thereof. If any lot or unit was created
through the minor subdivision or lot split process, any further subdivision of such
lot or unit shall be accomplished, if at all, only through the regular subdivision
procedures in effect at the time.
(Prior code § 14-02-130(A))
A.
Application. Prior to submitting a formal application to the town for minor subdivision
review, the applicant shall meet with the town administrator or designated town staff
representative to review the proposed subdivision for general compliance with all
applicable town ordinances, regulations, and plans, to review applicable procedures,
and to determine the appropriate requirements of preliminary plan and final plat to
be applied to the proposed minor subdivision. If the town administrator or designated
town staff representative recommends waiver or modification of any of the procedures
for sketch plan, preliminary plan, or final plat, then the applicant may submit an
application in accordance with this section. Otherwise, the applicant shall be required
to follow the regular subdivision procedures of the town, including the plat provisions
described in Chapter 16.16 .
Following approval of the town administrator or town staff representative to proceed
as a minor subdivision application, the application shall be submitted to the town
administrator not less than forty-five (45) days prior to the regularly scheduled
official meeting of the town planning commission at which the applicant wishes to
be considered. At least fifteen (15) paper copies of the minor subdivision plan and
information and materials must be submitted together with the application. Only applications
deemed complete and in substantial compliance with these regulations will be scheduled
for consideration before the town planning commission. The application shall be accompanied
by a statement from the town administrator as to the recommended requirements and
procedures to apply to the application. The planning commission and the town council
shall not be bound by the recommendations of the town administrator and shall have
the discretion to require compliance with any of the procedures applicable to regular
subdivisions.
B.
Review and Actions. Upon determination by the town administrator (or designee) that
the application is complete, the application shall be: (1) referred to appropriate
town staff and outside agencies for review and comment, (2) scheduled for public hearing
before the town planning commission, and (3) given public notice as required by the
town code and any applicable provisions of the Colorado Revised Statutes.
After consideration of the proposed minor subdivision plan and submitted materials,
any reports from town staff and outside agencies, and comments from the public, the
planning commission by quorum vote of the members present shall recommend that the
town council either: (1) approve the proposed minor subdivision with or without conditions,
(2) deny the proposed minor subdivision for certain reasons to be stated, or (3) the
commission may continue its review for a period of not more than forty-five (45) days
to obtain additional information or for other certain reasons to be stated.
Upon final action by the planning commission, the proposed minor subdivision application
with all comments and recommendations shall be submitted to the town council at its
next regular meeting scheduled at least ten (10) days after final action by the planning
commission. The council shall take such action as set forth in these regulations for
approval with or without conditions or denial of a final plat, after due consideration
of all information as presented to it.
The applicant may waive any of the deadlines and timetables set forth herein.
(Ord. 2004-3 § 15 (part); prior code § 14-02-130(B))

a complete sketch plan application is not submitted within six (6) months of the initial
pre-application meeting. The town administrator may waive the pre-application meeting

town administrator.

The purpose of this chapter is to provide procedures necessary to implement the provisions
of Article 68 of Title 24, C.R.S., as amended, which purports to establish a vested
property right to undertake and complete development and use of real property under
the terms and conditions of a site-specific development plan. By the passage of the
ordinance codified in this chapter, the council of the town intends only to provide
the procedures necessary to implement the provisions of that legislation. The council
finds and determines that land use and development is a matter of particularly local
concern, and the findings to the contrary contained in C.R.S. Section 24-68-101 are
in error and entitled to no weight or consideration. Nothing contained in this chapter
shall constitute an admission by the town that the Colorado Legislature acted within
the scope of its constitutional authority in the passage of the above-referenced legislation,
nor shall it be construed as an admission that this legislation applies to a home-rule
municipality.
(Prior code § 14-14-010)
As used in this chapter:
"Site-specific development plan" means a plan denominated as such that has been submitted
to the town council by a landowner or his or her representative describing with reasonable
certainty the type and intensity of use for a specific parcel or parcels of property
and which includes the submittal requirements described in this chapter. No plan or
other land use document submitted to or approved by the town shall be deemed a site-specific
development plan unless such plan meets all of the requirements of this chapter and
has been approved at a separate hearing conducted by the town council pursuant to
this chapter.
"Vested property right" means the right to undertake and complete the development
and use of property under the terms and conditions of site-specific development plan.

No site-specific development plan shall be approved until after a public hearing is
held before the town council, at a regular or special meeting. This hearing may be
combined with other hearings before the council relating to the project. Notice shall
be given by the applicant of the public hearing either separately, or combined with
other notices that are required by the provisions of Title 16 or 17 of the New Castle Municipal Code. The public notices shall be published at
least fifteen (15) days prior to such hearing. Additionally, owners of property within
two hundred fifty (250) feet of the subject property shall be notified of the public
hearing by certified mail, and the property shall be posted at least fifteen (15)
days prior to the hearing along the part of such property fronting on a street, subject
to the planner's approval. Any interested persons shall have an opportunity to be
heard at the public hearing.

Approval by the town council of a site-specific development plan pursuant to this
chapter shall operate to grant vested property rights for a period of three years
from the date of the town council's final approval action, unless a longer or shorter
period is requested by the applicant and is approved by the town council in its sole
discretion. In the event amendments to the site-specific development plan are proposed
and approved, the effective date of such amendments, for the purpose of the duration
of the vested property right, shall be the date of the approval of the original site-specific
development plan, unless the town council specifically finds to the contrary and incorporates
such findings in its approval of the amendment. If and only if vested property rights
are approved by the town council pursuant to this chapter, such approval shall automatically
extend the duration of the approval periods described in Sections 17.72.070 (E) and 16.16.040 for preliminary PUD development plans or preliminary plats, as applicable, so that
such approval periods shall expire at the same time as any vested rights granted pursuant
to this chapter. If the property owner or his or her successors or assigns fails to
submit a complete application for approval of a final plat or final PUD development
plan within the vested rights period, then any and all prior approvals for the property
shall automatically expire at the end of the vested rights period, and any development
or subdivision of the property shall require recommencement of the entire processes
described in Titles 16 and 17 of this code, as applicable.
(Prior code § 14-14-080)
Town council may extend the period of vesting found in either a site-specific development
plan or site specific development plan agreement upon written request by the applicant.
Town council may review such a request in either a public meeting or noticed public
hearing, as the council determines is necessary in its sole discretion. In order to
grant an extension, the applicant shall assert and the town council shall find that:
1.
Circumstances beyond the control of the applicant have changed such that the original
period of vesting set forth in the site specific development plan or agreement is
no longer sufficient to address the proposed phasing or development of the project
in its original approved form.
2.
The applicant is otherwise in substantial compliance with the terms and of the original
site specific development plan approval and agreement, including any provision of
payment fees, of the municipal code generally, and specifically the provisions of
this chapter.
Council may condition any approval of an extension upon such requirements as may be
necessary to address the changed circumstances surrounding the development of the
property or project, including an amendment to any provision of any existing site
specific development plan agreement. Any approval shall be in the form of a recordable
resolution. Any request to extend vested rights for a period of more than three years
in addition to the original vested rights period shall require that the applicant
and the town enter into a new or amended site specific development plan agreement
on terms acceptable to the town council; provided, the council may still require such
an agreement or amendment for shorter extensions in the council's discretion.

Notwithstanding any other provision of this title, a single lot line between two conforming
lots within the legal boundaries of the town may be relocated or eliminated through
the preparation, processing, and subsequent approval by the town administrator of
a lot line adjustment/vacation application conforming to the requirements of this
chapter.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C) (part))
An applicant seeking approval of a single lot line adjustment or vacation shall submit
to the town administrator a complete application package consisting of the following
items:
A.
A lot line adjustment/vacation application as provided by the town (hereinafter "town
application") completed and signed by the applicant;
B.
A legal description of all property involved in the application and any other property
that will be affected by the proposed adjustment/vacation;
C.
To the extent not already provided in the town application, proof of legal ownership
of all property involved, the names and addresses of all property owners involved,
and any lienholders of all property involved;
D.
A copy of the original final plat drawings associated with the property in question
indicating the configuration of the lots and existing easements and/or setbacks associated
with such lots;
E.
A copy of title commitment(s) related to all property involved in the application
indicating all easements and/or other interests associated with such property;
F.
In regard to any and all proposed adjustments/vacations of a lot line on property
where drainage easements are located, written confirmation by a certified engineer
that historic drainage patterns will not be altered or otherwise affected by such
proposed adjustment/vacation; and
G.
A copy of the proposed deed(s) intended to effectuate the adjustment/vacation should
the application be approved (see Section 16.40.050 ).
(Ord. 2004-12 § 5 (part); prior code § 14-02-090(C)(1))
The town administrator shall determine if the application package is complete. Incomplete
application packages will not be accepted for review. If the application package is
complete, the town administrator will refer the application package for review and
comment to all relevant town departments, as well as to all utility companies that
currently serve the property or which otherwise have an interest in existing utility
or other easements located upon or through such property. The town departments and
referral agencies or individuals shall have fifteen (15) days to review the application
package and provide any comments or recommendations to the town administrator.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(2))
Within thirty (30) days of the deadline for referral agency and town department comments,
the town administrator shall approve or deny the application. Approval shall be granted
provided:
A.
The lot line adjustment or vacation does not result in the creation of additional
lots;
B.
The lot line adjustment or vacation does not result in the creation of lots that do
not comply with town zoning requirements, including floor area ratio requirements,
setback requirements, and minimum lot size requirements;
C.
The lot line adjustment or vacation does not result in the creation of a lot or lots
that will have an infeasible building envelope pursuant to any town setback, foot
area ratio, or other building/zoning requirement;
D.
All utility companies and/or any other beneficiaries having an interest in existing
easements thereon have granted approval in regard to the disposition of existing easements
as a result of the adjustment or vacation;
E.
All easements associated with property involved in the application are properly addressed
and/or granted in the proposed deed(s) effectuating the adjustment or vacation;
F.
The lot line adjustment or vacation does not alter or affect the location or arrangement
of any other lot line within the subdivision;
G.
No lot line adjustment or vacation has been granted by the town with respect to or
in connection with the same property or any adjoining property under common ownership
or control of same person within the past one (1) year; and
H.
The lot line adjustment or vacation does not in any way adversely affect any surrounding
lot.
The applicant shall be notified in writing of the decision of the town within seven
days from the date of such decision. In the event of denial of the application, the
applicant will be advised of the reasons for the denial.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(3))
Following town approval of any single lot line adjustment or vacation application,
the survey and deed(s) effectuating the adjustment or vacation shall be recorded in
the office of the Garfield County clerk and recorder within thirty (30) days of the
town administrator's signature of the survey. All costs related to recordation of
documents with the county shall be borne by the applicant, which costs shall be paid
to the town prior to recording.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(4))

no later than thirty (30) days after the town administrator's signature. The town
administrator shall not sign the plat until all conditions of approval have been satisfied.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(5))
The town administrator shall only review requests for lot line adjustments or vacations
concerning the adjustment or vacation of a single lot line between two conforming
lots. The town administrator may consult with other town staff, as well as any independent
town consultants as the town administrator deems appropriate, and the town shall be
responsible for any and all consultation costs incurred therein. Any request seeking
an adjustment or vacation of more than a single lot line shall be submitted to the
town planning commission upon recommendation by town administrator/town staff. The
town administrator also retains the discretion to, for any reason, submit applications
for single lot line adjustment/vacation directly to the town planning commission for
initial review.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(6))
Any request for an adjustment or vacation of multiple lot lines shall be referred
directly to the planning commission and shall be treated as a request to amend the
plat by which any property involved in the request was originally created. The plat
amendment process does not create additional lots or interests in property but is
merely a mechanism that adjusts, relocates, or vacates multiple lot lines or combines
more than one lot for building purposes. The amended plat process is limited to amendments
that affect less than twenty (20) lots within a subdivision. Amendments affecting
more than twenty (20) lots, or amendments within a subdivision with an expired subdivision
improvements agreement or which affect existing subdivision improvements agreements,
shall be subject to the subdivision requirements set forth in Section 16.16.010 et seq. Amended plats shall be subject to review by the planning commission, planning
staff, the town engineer, and all appropriate review agencies and subject to final
approval by the town engineer.
(Prior code § 14-02-090(C)(7))
Failure of an applicant to record evidence of a lot line adjustment or vacation within
one hundred fifty (150) days following approval shall result in an automatic revocation
of such approval.
