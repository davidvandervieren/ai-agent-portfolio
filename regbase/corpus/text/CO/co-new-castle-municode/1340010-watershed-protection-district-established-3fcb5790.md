There is hereby established the Town of New Castle watershed protection district (the
"district"). The jurisdiction of the district shall extend over the territory occupied
by the town's waterworks and all reservoirs, streams, trenches, pipes, drains and
other waterworks used in and necessary for the construction, maintenance and operation
of the town's waterworks and over Elk Creek, the Colorado River, and all water sources
and drainage areas tributary thereto within a five-mile radius above any point at
which water is diverted for use by the town.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 2)
(Ord. No. 2010-10, § 2, 12-7-2010)
The district is created under the authority granted in Section 31-15-707(1)(b), Colorado
Revised Statutes, as amended, and Article XX of the Constitution of the State of Colorado.
The district and these regulations are intended to extend and enforce the town's legal
jurisdiction and authority to the maximum extent allowed by law for the purpose of
protecting the town's watershed, water resources, and waterworks from interference,
pollution and other degradation. All ordinances and regulations adopted under the
authority of this chapter shall be liberally construed and enforced in order to satisfy
and further the purposes and intent as set forth above. Nothing in this chapter is
intended to prohibit or require a permit for recreational uses of the district waters,
such as swimming, wading, non-motorized boating, and fishing.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 3)
(Ord. No. 2010-10, § 2, 12-7-2010)
As used in this chapter, the following words and phrases shall mean as follows unless
the context plainly requires otherwise:
"Best management practices" (BMPs) means the most effective means of preventing, reducing
or mitigating the harmful impacts of certain activities consistent with the standards
set forth in this chapter. BMPs may include schedule(s) of activities, prohibitions
of practices, maintenance practices, management procedures, treatment requirements,
technology requirements, operating procedures and other practices to control runoff,
erosion, drainage, sediment accumulation and similar events.
"District" means the New Castle watershed protection district.
"District permit" means a permit issued pursuant to this chapter.
"District waters" means Elk Creek, the Colorado River, and all water sources and drainage
areas tributary thereto within a five-mile radius above any and all points at which
water is diverted for use by the town, now and in the future. The district waters
shall be depicted on a map (the "district waters map"), which map shall be advisory
only. The district waters map may be updated administratively from time to time to
reflect the town's current water diversions and facilities. The district waters map
shall be kept in the office of the town clerk for public inspection during regular
business hours, and copies of the map may be ordered for purchase at such cost as
is deemed necessary and reasonable by the town clerk.
"Excavate" or "excavating" means the artificial movement of ten (10) cubic yards or
more of soil, rock or other surface material.
"Fill" or "filling" means the deposition of ten (10) cubic yards or more of material
brought from another location by artificial means.
"Foreseeable risk" means the reasonable anticipation that harm or injury may result
from an act or omission.
"Grade" or "grading" means the artificial movement of over ten (10) cubic yards of
soil or other surface material; or the alteration of any natural or preexisting drainage
pattern or channel through the alteration, movement or addition of surface materials;
or the installation of any road or other surface utilized for the movement of vehicles;
or the use of vehicles or keeping of any animals upon any land that would lead to
a movement of ten (10) cubic yards of material within one year of the commencement
of such use or which use, if continued, would result in the movement of any earth
or material affecting or creating a drainage channel.
"Individual sewage disposal system (ISDS)" means an absorption system of any size
or flow or a system or facility for treating, neutralizing, stabilizing or disposing
of sewage that is not part of or connected to a sewage treatment works. Examples include,
without limitation, conventional septic tanks and leach fields, absorption trenches
and pits, constructed wetland treatment systems, evapotranspiration systems and mound
systems.
"Person" means any individual, partnership, firm, corporation, association, governmental
entity or other legal entity, except the town of New Castle.
"Pollutant" means dredged or excavated spoil, solid waste, incinerator residue, sewage,
sewage sludge, garbage, trash, munitions, chemicals, chemical waste, biological nutrient,
biological material, radioactive material, heat, wrecked or discarded equipment, rock,
sand, dirt, slurry, or any industrial, domestic or agricultural waste.
"Pollute" or "pollution" means the alteration of the physical, chemical, biological
or radiological integrity of water through human or human-induced activities.
"Remove vegetation" means to artificially cut, chemically kill or otherwise remove
any tree greater than fifteen (15) feet in height, any shrubs or trees covering an
area of more than one hundred (100) square feet, or any grasses covering any area
of more than one thousand (1,000) square feet.
"Sewage disposal system" means any facility designed, constructed and used for the
purpose of receiving, treating or disposing of sewage.
"Surface" or "surfacing" means the compaction, hardening or covering of the natural
land surface with asphalt, concrete, gravel or similar materials in areas greater
than one hundred (100) square feet. Surfacing includes, but is not limited to, such
things as compacting the surface of the earth, placing gravel, concrete or like substances
on the surface of the earth, or placing structures upon the land.
"Tributary" means any watercourse, stream, creek, spring or drainage area that provides
water to a natural stream.
"Watershed" means the area encompassed by the New Castle watershed protection district.
"Waterworks" means any and all human-made or designed components of the town's water
collection, treatment, and/or distribution system, including but not limited to transmission,
storage, treatment and filtration facilities, and any and all wells, springs, reservoirs,
streams, trenches, pipes, drains and diversion structures used in and necessary for
the construction, operation, maintenance and repair of the town's water supply system.
"Wetland" means land that has a predominance of hydric soils and that is inundated
or saturated by surface or groundwater at a frequency and duration sufficient to support,
and that under normal conditions does support, a prevalence of hydroponic vegetation
typically adapted for life in saturated soil conditions.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 4)
(Ord. No. 2010-10, § 2, 12-7-2010)
A.
It is unlawful for any person to do any act or thing whatsoever that may pollute or
contaminate the district waters, including but not limited to the following:
1.
Cast, place, dump or deposit any pollutant or contaminant in or near the district
waters;
2.
Store or retain any pollutant on any premises in such a manner that the pollutant
may be carried by natural causes into the district waters; or
3.
Permit to flow into the district waters from any place or premises, any foul or contaminating
fluid.
B.
It is unlawful for any person to trespass upon, injure, damage, tamper, meddle or
interfere in any way with any of the town's waterworks.
C.
It is unlawful for any person to cast, place, dump or deposit in any part of the town's
waterworks any object, substance or material that may injure or obstruct the town's
waterworks or the flow of water through the town's waterworks.
D.
Any animal which shall be killed or die from any cause in the town or within one hundred
(100) feet of the banks of the streams within the watershed shall be at once removed
to a designated landfill by the owner or person having had such animal in his or her
possession, control or charge, and the carcass of such animal shall be buried or disposed
of in a sanitary manner. If ownership of the animal cannot be determined, it shall
be the responsibility of the animal control department to pick up and dispose of such
animal carcass as provided in this subsection.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 5)
A.
It is prohibited and unlawful for any person to engage in or cause any of the following
activities or conduct within the district unless such person has first obtained a
district permit from the town:
1.
The construction, installation, expansion or removal of any ISDS or sewage disposal
system, excepting a system connected to a municipal sewage treatment works;
2.
Excavating, grading, filling, blasting or surfacing, including road building;
3.
Timber harvesting, excluding the removal of dead or diseased trees for firewood or
for noncommercial domestic purposes;
4.
Removal of vegetation;
5.
Drilling operations, except for the drilling of a domestic well pursuant to a valid
well permit issued by the Colorado Department of Water Resources;
6.
Altering or obstructing natural or historic water drainage courses;
7.
Surface and subsurface mining operations, including but not limited to gravel mining
and oil, gas and coalbed methane extraction;
8.
The out-of-doors spraying or using of fertilizers, herbicides or pesticides, excepting
noncommercial applications for domestic household or gardening purposes;
9.
Using, handling, storing or transporting toxic or hazardous substances, including,
but not limited to, radioactive materials, except for noncommercial domestic household
purposes as permitted by law;
10.
Using, handling, storing or transporting flammable or explosive materials, except
for noncommercial domestic household purposes as permitted by law, or within vehicular
fuel storage tanks;
11.
Having, keeping or maintaining within one hundred (100) feet of the banks of the streams
within the watershed any grazing livestock or any buildings, privy, pen, yard or corral
for stock;
12.
Increasing or decreasing any rate of stream flow or natural or existing drainage pattern
or course, except as permitted pursuant to an adjudicated water right; increasing
sediment deposition in any stream; causing or increasing erosion on any slope or stream
bank; or disturbing any wetland within the watershed;
13.
Any activity reasonably giving rise to a foreseeable risk of injury or pollution to
the town's waterworks or district waters.
B.
In the event that any activity not specifically listed in subsection A of this section
is deemed by the town council to present a foreseeable danger of pollution to the
district waters or waterworks, the town will notify the person responsible of such
determination, and the person shall immediately cease and desist such activity until
a permit has been obtained under the provisions of this chapter. Notification shall
be made by personal delivery or by deposit of the notice in the U.S. Mail, postage
prepaid, addressed to the last known address of the person responsible. The notice
shall contain a description of the activity, the date(s) the activity has been observed,
and a specific demand to cease the activity by a date certain.
C.
A district permit shall not be required for any activity listed in subsection A of
this section within the town's municipal boundaries that is specifically addressed
and approved in other permits associated with a land use approval pursuant to Title 16 or 17 of the New Castle Municipal Code.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 6)
(Ord. No. 2010-10, § 2, 12-7-2010)
A.
The following activities are allowed within the district, provided written notification
of such activity is provided to the town ten (10) days prior to the commencement of
such activity:
1.
Stock grazing more than one hundred (100) feet from the bank of any stream within
the watershed;
2.
Road maintenance and improvement by governmental entities.
B.
The written notice required under this section shall include the name and address
of the person undertaking the activity, a legal and common description of the location
of the proposed activity, a description of the proposed activity, a discussion of
the potential impacts upon the town's waterworks or district waters, and such other
information as the town may require. The purpose of this requirement is to allow the
town an opportunity to protect the district waters by suggesting best management practices
for such activity prior to its commencement.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 7)
A.
An applicant for a district permit shall submit three copies of a written application
to the town clerk no later than ninety (90) days prior to commencement of a proposed
activity. No application shall be accepted, processed or approved unless and until
it is complete and all fees have been paid. The town engineer may waive certain application
information requirements if he or she deems them to be unnecessary or overly burdensome
with respect to a certain activity.
B.
The application shall include, at a minimum, the following information:
1.
The name, mailing address and telephone number of the applicant;
2.
The name, mailing address and telephone number of the owner(s) of the land upon which
the activity is to occur if different from the applicant and written authorization
from the landowner(s) for the submission of the application;
3.
A legal description of the lot, tract, parcel or other land upon which the activity
is to occur;
4.
A written narrative describing the activity for which the permit is being sought,
including a general identification of the environmental characteristics of the subject
land and surrounding area;
5.
A vicinity map showing the land on which the proposed development is to occur and
all lots, tracts, parcels or other lands adjacent thereto, and illustrating any wetlands,
lakes, ponds, springs, watercourses or other bodies of water and water wells;
6.
A boundary and improvements map or sketch of the land subject to the application containing
sufficient detail and drawn at a scale to accurately illustrate, review and assess
the location of all proposed activity and existing structures, and illustrating the
existing direction of slope (contours) and direction of surface runoff. A professionally
prepared boundary and improvements survey may be required if the town engineer deems
it necessary in order to adequately assess an application;
7.
A listing and copy of all federal, state or local permits or approvals required or
obtained for implementation of the activity;
8.
A detailed description of the impacts or potential impacts the activity may have on
any surface or subsurface watersources or courses, including wetlands;
9.
A detailed description of the impacts or potential impacts the activity may have on
existing vegetation, trees and groundcover;
10.
A detailed description of the impacts or potential impacts the activity may have on
soils, including a description of the nature and condition of existing soils and any
planned grading, excavating, filling or surfacing;
11.
A detailed description of the impacts or potential impacts the activity may have on
existing drainage patterns and land contours, including comparative runoff and absorption
calculations for the subject land and any impacted adjacent land, both pre- and post-development;
12.
A detailed description of any proposed wastewater or sewage disposal system to be
installed and a copy of the designed/engineered plans, including soils and percolation
test results;
13.
A detailed description of any proposed water supply/delivery system to be installed,
including the watersource and anticipated consumptive use, and a copy of the design/engineered
plans;
14.
A detailed description and copy of any and all mitigation plans or measures addressing
impacts resulting from the activity to surface and subsurface watersources, wetlands,
vegetation and trees, soils, drainage and slopes;
15.
The identification of any activity to be undertaken by the applicant as part of the
development that presents, or may present, a foreseeable risk of pollution or injury
to the town's waterworks or district waters, along with a specific description of
the best management practices designed to eliminate or minimize such risk(s) to the
maximum extent feasible;
16.
Such additional information as the applicant or town may deem necessary to fully evaluate
the proposed activity and/or demonstrate or explain why a watershed permit should
be issued.
C.
The application shall be accompanied by a district permit application fee, which shall
be determined by the town engineer pursuant to Section 13.40.110 of this chapter. In addition, the applicant shall pay all out-of-pocket costs incurred
by the town in reviewing the application, including but not limited to the costs for
engineering, legal, consulting, testing, inspection, publication and copying.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 8)
A.
The town engineer shall review and evaluate the application and all supporting material
to determine whether the application is complete and satisfies the requirements of
this chapter. Where appropriate and weather permitting, the town engineer may schedule
a site visit to inspect the land on which the proposed activity is to occur. Advance
notice of the date and time of such site visit shall be provided to the applicant.
B.
Within forty-five (45) days following receipt of a completed application and any necessary
inspections, the town engineer shall prepare a written analysis of the proposed activity,
including a discussion of any factor that may result in a foreseeable risk of injury
to the town's waterworks or district waters and a discussion of best management practices
that may obviate such risk(s). The analysis shall include a recommendation of whether
the permit should be denied, granted or granted with conditions. The town engineer
shall promptly forward the application, analysis and recommendation to the town council,
and the town council shall conduct a public hearing pursuant to Section 13.40.090 of this chapter.
C.
The town engineer may classify an application as "de minimis impact" if he or she
determines that the proposed activity presents a "de minimis" risk of pollution to
the district waters or disruption of the town's waterworks. If a "de minimis impact"
finding is made, the town engineer shall immediately issue a district permit for the
proposed activity and report the permit issuance to the town council at its next regular
or special meeting. The town engineer shall maintain a record of all "de minimis"
permits issued for the purpose of assessing the cumulative impact of "de minimis"
activities. If the town engineer does not make a "de minimis" finding, the applicant
may appeal the decision to the town council by filing a written notice of appeal with
the town administrator within fifteen (15) days from the date of the town engineer's
decision. The town council shall then consider the application at a public hearing
pursuant to Section 13.40.090 of this chapter. The council may, based upon the same factors and standards set forth
in subsection D of this section and Section 13.40.100 (A) of this chapter, grant a "de minimis" permit for the proposed activity.
D.
In analyzing the proposed activity, the town engineer shall consider the following
factors:
1.
Nature and extent of the proposed activity;
2.
Proximity of the proposed activity to the town's waterworks and district waters;
3.
Surface and subsurface water courses;
4.
Drainage patterns;
5.
Soil criteria and erosion potential;
6.
Slope steepness and stability;
7.
Geologic formations and hazards;
8.
Effects of denudation;
9.
Existing point and nonpoint source discharges into water;
10.
Fire hazard;
11.
Likelihood or threat of pollution to district waters or injury to the town's waterworks;
12.
Effectiveness of protective or mitigation measures proposed by the applicant and the
availability of alternative protective and/or mitigation measures;
13.
Overall anticipated impact of the proposed activity on the town's waterworks, district
waters and watershed.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 9)
For all applications other than those that are classified as "de minimis impact,"
the town council shall conduct a public hearing to review the application within forty-five
(45) days of the date of the town engineer's analysis. Written notice of the date,
time and place for the hearing shall be sent by regular mail or personally delivered
to the applicant not less than ten (10) days prior to the hearing. Decisions of the
town council shall be entered within ninety (90) days from the date the application
is submitted and shall be reduced to writing, a copy of which shall be mailed to the
applicant. The town council may require additional information from any applicant,
in which event the public hearing and decision may be delayed or continued until the
additional information is received. A public hearing on any application may not be
continued more than twice. If the required information is not received by the second
continued hearing, the town council may deny the district permit. The deadlines in
this section may be extended by mutual agreement of the council and the applicant.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 10)
A.
Permit Issuance Standards. A district permit shall be issued when the town council
finds that the applicant has sustained the burden of proof that the proposed activity,
including best management practices, if any, does not present or create a foreseeable
risk of injury to the town's waterworks or pollution to the district waters. A district
permit shall be denied when the town council finds that the applicant has not sustained
such burden of proof.
B.
Permit Conditions. The town council may condition any district permit upon the applicant's
compliance with such mitigation measures, water quality monitoring, financial security,
performance standards or time deadlines, or such other terms and conditions as may
be deemed necessary to protect the town's waterworks, the district waters and/or the
watershed from pollution, disruption or damage. A failure by an applicant to accept
or timely adhere to such terms and conditions shall constitute cause to deny or revoke
a permit.
C.
Duration of Permit. A permit shall expire and become void at the earliest of five
years from the date of permit issuance; twelve (12) months from the date of permit
issuance if the permitted activity is not commenced within that time; or twelve (12)
months from the date of completion or cessation of the permitted activity. The town
council may in its discretion condition any permit to modify the duration of the permit
or to require periodic review of the permit, not to exceed once per year.
D.
Joint Review Processes. Any permit required hereunder can be reviewed and issued pursuant
to a joint review process with any other government entity or agency charged with
the review and approval of the same activity or activities.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 11)
The town engineer may from time to time devise, adopt and enforce supplemental administrative,
procedural or technical/engineering rules and regulations as he or she may deem necessary
and advantageous to the successful implementation and enforcement of the provisions
of this chapter, including the preparation of standardized forms and fees associated
with the evaluation and issuance of permits. All rules and regulations must be consistent
with the terms of this chapter and be approved by the town administrator. The town
council may review, amend or vacate such rules and regulations upon written complaint
or appeal. The rights and duties of the town engineer and/or town administrator pursuant
to this chapter may be delegated to other town employees acting under the supervision
of the town engineer and/or town administrator as applicable.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 12)
A.
Right of Entry. Whenever necessary to make an inspection to enforce a provision of
this chapter or the terms and conditions of any district permit, or where reasonable
grounds exist to believe that a condition, activity or facility on any premises presents
a threat of pollution or injury to any of the town's waterworks or district waters,
an authorized representative of the town may go upon any land at any reasonable time
to inspect the premises or to perform any duty imposed by this chapter, provided that
proper identification is shown to the person(s) on the premises. If the premises are
unoccupied, reasonable efforts shall be made to locate and/or provide notice to the
applicant or other person(s) having control of such land to notify them of such entry.
B.
Stop Work and Abatement Orders. Whenever any work or activity is being done contrary
to the provisions of this chapter or the terms and conditions of any district permit,
or where it is determined that a permit was issued in error or as the result of incorrect,
inaccurate or misleading information, the town engineer may execute and issue a written
stop work order and/or abatement order, requiring that the subject activity immediately
cease and/or be corrected. A stop work and/or abatement order shall set forth in plain
language the nature of any violation and shall be served on the applicant or any person(s)
engaged in the prohibited activity by personal service or regular mail. Appeals of
a stop work or abatement order shall be heard by the town council upon written request
filed with the town clerk not more than five working days after service of the order
on the permittee. The failure to timely file an appeal, or to appear at the hearing
thereon, shall constitute a waiver of the right to contest the order. Hearings shall
be conducted by the town council within thirty (30) days from the date on which the
written notice of appeal was filed with the town clerk. Written notice of the hearing
shall be sent by regular mail or personally served on the appellant not less than
ten (10) days in advance of the hearing. The continuation of any activity subject
to a stop work or abatement order shall constitute a violation of this chapter.
C.
Abatement. If any person or any owner or occupant of any premises upon whom notice
is served as provided in subsection B of this section does not cease or correct the
condition described in the stop work and/or abatement order within the time period
provided in the order, the town may at its discretion and in lieu of obtaining a court
injunction, abate the condition causing the pollution or contamination of the water
supply and shall charge the person or owner or occupant of the premises with all of
the cost of the abatement or removal.
D.
Permit Revocation.
1.
All district permits are subject to revocation by the town for violations of this
chapter, the terms and conditions of any district permit or any stop work order. The
town engineer shall mail written notice of a proposed revocation to the permittee
at least fifteen (15) days prior to the effective date of the revocation and shall
set forth in plain language the grounds justifying the revocation. A hearing on the
revocation shall be conducted by the town council upon the written request of the
permittee filed with the town clerk prior to the effective date of the revocation.
All hearings shall be promptly scheduled before the town council and written notice
thereof mailed to the permittee at least five days in advance thereof. The effectiveness
of any revocation order shall be stayed pending the decision of the town council on
appeal, except where the town engineer certifies in writing that a delay in revoking
the permit will present a clear and immediate danger to public health, safety, welfare
or property. All decisions on appeal shall be reduced to writing and a copy provided
to the permittee.
2.
Upon the revocation of a permit, the town may require the permittee to restore any
land, facility or site to such condition as deemed necessary to prevent pollution
or injury to the town's waterworks, district waters or watershed. Upon the failure
of the permittee to timely perform such restoration, the town may, at its option,
perform or have performed the restoration and assess the costs thereof against the
permittee, including the imposition of a lien against the permittee's property on
which such restoration work took place.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 13)
A.
Offense. Any person who violates any provision of this chapter or any term or condition
of any district permit shall be fined an amount up to one thousand dollars ($1,000.00)
for each offense and/or imprisoned up to one year in jail. Each day a violation occurs
shall be deemed a separate offense.
B.
Actions. Any activity, facility or structure which is undertaken, continued, operated
or maintained in violation of this chapter or the terms and conditions of any district
permit, stop work order or abatement order shall be subject to injunction, restraining
order and/or other appropriate legal remedy as may be sought and obtained by the town
in a court of competent jurisdiction, in which event the town shall be entitled to
recover its reasonable costs and attorney fees from the offending party or parties.
C.
Remedies. All penalties and remedies for violations of this chapter are nonexclusive
and cumulative, and the town's pursuit and/or exercise of one remedy or penalty shall
not foreclose or prohibit the pursuit and exercise of alternative or other remedies
provided by law. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed as a waiver of any civil
remedies available to the town.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 14)
The lawful continuance of any activity in progress at the time of the enactment of
this chapter may be continued even though it does not conform to the requirements
of this chapter. For purposes of this section, an "activity in progress" is a facility,
building, construction activity, land use or other activity that has been finally
permitted by all other governmental agencies having jurisdiction, and that has been
physically commenced. Ordinary repairs and maintenance of any existing building or
land shall be allowed. Any change, expansion, alteration or enlargement of such existing
lawful use shall be subject to all requirements of this chapter.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 15)
If any provisions of this chapter are determined to be void by any court of competent
jurisdiction, then the remainder of this chapter shall not be invalidated thereby
but shall nevertheless be interpreted to as fully as possible give force and effect
to the intent of these regulations as evidenced by the original provisions, including
the invalidated provision.
(Ord. 2005-11 § 16)
