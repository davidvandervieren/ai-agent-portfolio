The Town may borrow money and to evidence such borrowing may, by ordinance, authorize
the issuance of the following securities in accordance with the provisions of this
Article X, subject to the requirements of Article X, Section 20 and Article XI, Section
6 of the Colorado Constitution:
(A)
General obligation bonds and other like securities;
(B)
Revenue bonds and other like securities;
(C)
Special assessment bonds and other like securities;
(D)
Certificates of participation and other like securities;
(E)
Short term notes;
(F)
Refunding securities;
(G)
Any other securities not in contravention of this Charter.
The Town may issue securities payable from the proceeds of ad valorem taxes and any
other legally available funds. However, except as otherwise provided in this Article
X, no securities payable in whole or in part from the proceeds of ad valorem taxes,
or to which the full faith and credit of the Town are pledged, shall be issued until
the question of their issuance shall, at a special or regular election, be submitted
to a vote of the electors of the Town of New Castle and approved by a majority of
those voting on the question.
(A)
The Town may issue securities payable solely from revenues derived from the operation
of a project, a capital improvement, or a utility system, or from the proceeds of
any sales tax, use tax, or other excise tax, or solely from any source or sources
or any combination thereof other than ad valorem taxes of the Town.
(B)
The Council may, by ordinance, establish any one or more of the Town's water, wastewater,
or other utilities as an enterprise of the Town. The Council may also, by ordinance,
authorize any such Town-owned enterprise governed by the Council, sitting as the board
of the enterprise, to issue its own revenue bonds or other securities (including refunding
securities), which revenue bonds or other securities shall be payable solely from
the net revenues derived from the operation of the enterprise. Such revenue bonds
or other securities shall be issued by ordinance of the board of the enterprise, adopted
in the same manner and subject to referendum to the same extent as ordinances of the
Council. Nothing in this Section shall invalidate any enterprise existing, or any
enterprise revenue bond issued or approved, as of the time of adoption of this Charter.
The Council may, by ordinance, create special or local improvement districts and assess
the cost relating to the construction or installation of special or local improvements
of every character against specially benefitted property within districts by:
(A)
Order of the Council upon its determination that such improvement is necessary for
the preservation of the public peace, safety, health, or welfare; or
(B)
On a petition by the owners of more than fifty percent (50%) of the area of the proposed
district, provided that such majority shall include not less than fifty percent (50%)
of the owners of the land within the proposed district.
In either event, a public hearing shall be held at which all interested parties may
appear and be heard. Right to protest and notice of hearing shall be given as provided
by ordinance. Such improvements shall confer special benefits to the real or personal
property within said district and general benefits to the Town at large. The Council
may, by ordinance, prescribe the method of making such improvements and of assessing
the cost thereof. The Town may issue securities payable solely from special assessments
levied and other funds described herein for the cost of constructing or installing
such improvements, including the costs incidental thereto.
Where all outstanding securities of a special or local improvement district have been
paid and any money remains to the credit of the district, it may be transferred to
a Special Surplus and Deficiency Fund. Whenever there is a deficiency in any special
or local improvement district to meet payment of outstanding securities, it may be
paid out of said fund.
Whenever a special or local improvement district has paid and canceled three-fourths
of its bonds issued, and for any reason the remaining special assessments are not
paid in time to pay the remaining securities of the district, and there is not sufficient
money in the Special Surplus and Deficiency Fund, then the Town may pay the securities
when due and reimburse itself by collecting the unpaid special assessments due the
district.
In consideration of general benefits conferred upon the Town at large from the construction
and installation of improvements in special or local improvement districts, the Council
may levy annual taxes on all taxable property within the Town at a rate to be determined
by the Council for the purpose of advancing moneys to maintain current payments of
interest and principal of any securities issued for any special or local improvement
district hereafter created. The proceeds of such taxes shall be placed in a special
fund and shall be disbursed only for the purposes specified herein, provided that
in lieu of such tax levies, the Council may annually transfer to such special fund
any available money of the Town.
The Council may authorize the execution and delivery of certificates of participation
in lease-purchase or installment purchase obligations of the Town, the payments on
which may be subject to annual appropriation by the Council.
The Town may borrow money in anticipation of the collection of taxes, revenues, or
grant receipts and issue short-term notes or other securities to evidence the amount
so borrowed. Such securities shall mature within the same fiscal year in which they
are issued.
The Town may issue refunding securities for the purpose of refunding or providing
for the payment of any outstanding securities of the Town as they mature, or in advance
of maturity by means of an escrow or otherwise.
Except as explicitly provided in this Article X, there shall be no limitations on
the authority of the Town to incur indebtedness or to issue bonds or similar securities.
