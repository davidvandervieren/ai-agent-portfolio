Unless the context otherwise requires, definitions of word or phrases used in this
section are:
"Graffiti" means any inscription, word, figure, painting, coloring or other defacement
that is written, marked, etched, scratched, sprayed, drawn, painted, or engraved on
or otherwise affixed to any surface of public or private property, to the extent that
the graffiti was not authorized in advance by the owner or occupant of the property,
or, despite authorization, is otherwise deemed a public nuisance by the town administrator.
"Public nuisance" means graffiti that is designed to or has the effect of disturbing
the peace, is offensive or vulgar to a reasonable person, or otherwise detrimentally
affects public health, safety, or welfare. It shall be prima facie evidence that graffiti
is a public nuisance if it can be shown to violate the conditions, covenants and restrictions
applicable to the property.
"Record property owner" means the owner of property as shown on the most recent assessment
roll of the Garfield County assessor.
(Ord. 2007-12 § B (part))
No person shall place graffiti upon any property.
(Ord. 2007-12 § B (part))
A.
Removal by the Perpetrator. Any person who has applied graffiti to public or private
property is required to remove the graffiti and restore the property to its pre-graffiti
condition, or is responsible to pay for the costs of removal and restoration of the
property to its previous condition. Where graffiti is applied by an unemancipated
minor, the parents or legal guardian shall also be responsible for such removal and
restoration or to pay for such removal and restoration.
B.
Property Owner Responsibility. If graffiti is not removed by the perpetrator according
to subsection A of this section, graffiti shall be removed by the record property
owner. The property owner shall cause such removal to occur within seven days after
notice by the town to the record property owner. The town shall deliver such notice
by U.S. first class mail, and such notice shall be effective upon deposit by the town
in the U.S. mail, postage prepaid, to the address of the record property owner. Such
notice shall include:
1.
A description of the graffiti;
2.
The date the graffiti was discovered by the town;
3.
A statement that the graffiti must be removed within seven days of the date of the
notice;
4.
A statement that if the graffiti is not removed in that time period, the town may
seek an administrative entry warrant to remove the graffiti;
5.
A statement that if the town removes the graffiti, the record property owner may be
responsible for reimbursing the town for the reasonable costs of the removal;
6.
Contact information for the town administrator or his or her designee.
C.
Removal by the Town and Right of Entry. If the graffiti is not removed after proper
notice to the record property owner, the town administrator or his or her designee
may seek an administrative entry warrant from the municipal court for purposes of
removing the graffiti. Such request shall set forth probable cause to believe that
graffiti exists on the property, shall aver that proper notice was mailed to the record
property owner, and shall aver that the graffiti has not been removed. Upon receipt
of such request, the municipal court may issue a warrant authorizing the town administrator
or his or her designee to enter the property to remove the graffiti. Nothing in this
section shall impose a duty upon the town or the town administrator to remove graffiti
on public or private property.
D.
Reimbursement to Town for Removal of Graffiti. If the town removes graffiti on private
property pursuant to subsection C of this section, the town may seek reimbursement
from the record property owner for the reasonable costs of such removal. In order
to seek such reimbursement, the town administrator shall deliver to the record property
owner via first class mail a demand for reimbursement. Such demand shall include:
the reason the removal was required; the date the removal work was accomplished; and
the cost of such work. The record property owner shall make full payment to the town
within forty-five (45) days of the date of the demand. During this forty-five (45)
day timeframe, the record property owner may request a hearing in front of the town
council to challenge the requirement of reimbursement, the cost thereof, or both.
Such matter shall be set for review on the next available council agenda, and shall
constitute and be conducted as a quasi-judicial hearing. The payment/appeal deadline
may be extended by the town administrator in writing to a date certain.
E.
Collection—Certification to County Treasurer. If the record property owner does not
either make full payment or request a hearing within the applicable deadline, the
town administrator may, in addition to all other available remedies, certify the amount
due to the county treasurer to be placed on the tax list for the current year and
to be collected in the same manner as other taxes are collected. The town may assess
a charge of ten (10) percent of the total delinquent amount, which amount shall be
added to the balance due to defray the costs of collection. All laws of this state
for the assessment and collection of general taxes, including the laws for the sale
of property for taxes and redemption of the same, shall apply.
(Ord. 2007-12 § B (part))
A violation of this section shall be, upon conviction, punishable by up to one thousand
dollars ($1,000.00) in fines, and/or up to one year in jail, plus the requirements
and/or cost of removal and restoration, discussed in Section 9.68.030 (A) of this chapter.
(Ord. 2007-12 § B (part))
