The owner, occupant, lessee or person in possession or control of any premises or
property shall maintain the sidewalks adjoining such premises or property in good
repair and in a safe, unobstructed condition, free of snow, weeds and debris.
(Ord. 481 §1, 2003)
The owner, occupant, lessee or person in possession or control of any premises or
property shall maintain the sidewalks adjoining such premises or property, and shall
remove and clear away, or cause to be removed or cleared away, snow and ice from sidewalks
in all business districts within the Town by four (4) business hours after the cessation
of any fall of snow, sleet or freezing rain or by the beginning hours of the next
business day following such fall, whichever period is shorter, and from all other
sidewalks in the Town within twenty-four (24) hours of the cessation of any fall of
snow, sleet or freezing rain.
(Ord. 481 §1, 2003)

by the Board of Trustees, shall be made or allowed to intrude upon any street, alley,
sidewalk, curb, gutter or other public place within the Town.

If an owner of any lot, block or parcel of ground within the Town, or any tenant or
agent in possession or in charge thereof, fails or refuses to remove or relocate encroachments
as required in this Article within thirty (30) days after being served notice to do
so by an agent or employee of the Town, the Town may have said encroachments removed,
abated or relocated by an employee of the Town or by a private firm or individual
as provided in Chapter 7 of this Code and charge the cost thereof, according to Section 31-20-105, et seq.,
C.R.S., and as provided in Sections 7-1-120 to 7-1-160 of this Code, to such owner,
tenant or agent, to include inspection and administration costs.

No person shall commence any excavation in any public street or right-of-way within
the Town until a permit is issued by the Town. Application for the permit shall be
obtained from the office of the Town Clerk. Information required in the application
shall include, but not be limited to, the timing, reason, location and method of repair
of the excavation, name of the contractor and anticipated date of completion. The
repair shall return the street or right-of-way to its condition prior to excavation
and shall comply with all Town ordinances and regulations. A bond or other guarantee
may be required of the applicant sufficient to ensure that the quality of the repair
meets the requirements of this Article. Timing of the excavation shall limit interference
with other uses of the street or right-of-way. The fees for excavation are set forth
in the Fee Schedule. Fees shall be paid at the time of application, except that any

All roadway and sidewalk design, construction, and final acceptance by the Town shall
be in accordance with the Milliken Design Criteria and Construction Specifications,
Volume III, Streets. The Public Works Department, in consultation with the Town Administrator
and the Public Works Director, shall develop additional standards, regulations and
specifications governing access, utilization and restoration of all streets, alleys
and rights-of-way within the corporate limits of the Town, which standards, regulations
and specifications shall not constitute a barrier to the use and excavation of Town
facilities, but shall preserve the integrity of the facilities at the least possible
cost to the Town's taxpayers. Such standards, regulations and specifications as developed
by the Town are hereby adopted by reference.
(Ord. 481 §1, 2003; Ord. No. 777 , § 2, 11-25-2019)
The Public Works Department, in consultation with the Town Administrator and the Public
Works Director, shall formulate, publish and from time to time amend written standards
and specifications setting forth details as to materials, designs, and methods pertaining
to the construction and installation of streets, storm drainage, potable water distribution,
sanitary sewer collection, and non-potable irrigation systems. The standards and specifications
shall reflect good engineering practice and shall include drawings, checklists, forms,
applications, instructions, and other supporting and informational material. The standards
and specifications shall be referred to as the "Milliken Design Criteria and Construction
Specifications" and shall be in three volumes: "Water and Sewer Utilities" (Volume

The Town Clerk has the authority to administer and enforce the special event permit
process set forth in this article. The term Town Clerk shall hereinafter refer to the Town Clerk or his or her designee for the purposes
of this article. Prior to the issuance of any special event permit pursuant to this
article, the Town Clerk shall refer special events applications to and request feedback
from any Town departments or special districts that will be affected by or involved
in the special event, including but not limited to those that provide public safety
services.
( Ord. 820 , § 3, 12-23-2024)
For the purposes of this article, the terms set forth in this section shall have the
following definitions:
Applicant means the person or organization identified as the permittee on the special event
permit application.
Demonstration means a rally, picketing, speechmaking, march, vigil, religious service or any similar
gathering that primarily involves the communication or expression of views or grievances
or other First Amendment protected activities.
Emergency action plan means a plan developed after consultation by the event organizer with, and approved
in writing by, the Milliken Police Department and the Front Range Fire and Rescue
District that follows those agencies' guidelines and requirements, including without
limitation requirements related to severe weather, fire, medical emergencies, security,
and crowd control.
Event organizer means the individual identified in the special event permit application who conducts,
manages, promotes, organizes, aids or solicits attendance at or for a special event.
Funeral procession means a coordinated array of motor vehicles in which the lead vehicle, unless led
by a state or local law enforcement vehicle, displays a sign, pennant, flag or other
insignia furnished by a funeral home indicating a coordinated procession and each
vehicle participating in the procession is operating its headlights.
Owner(s) of record means the owners of the special event area as determined by an examination of the
Weld County real property records.
Permittee means the person or organization who receives a special event permit issued by the
Town in accordance with this article.
Priority special event means a special event that has been held within substantially the same special event
area on or about the same date(s) for three (3) or more consecutive years. Any decision
regarding whether a special event is a priority special event shall be in the reasonable
discretion of the Town Clerk.
Private property means property owned by private parties, or anyone other than the government. It
can include real estate, buildings, objects, and intellectual property.
Special event means any of the following activities:
a)
Any event that requires the temporary closure of a public street, sidewalk, alley
or right-of-way, or portion thereof, or the erection of barricades.
b)
Any organized activity conducted by a person or organization for a common or collective
use, purpose, or benefit which involves the use of, or has an impact on, Town property,
Town facilities or routine Town services as determined by the Town Clerk in consultation
with the Town Administrator.
c)
Any occasion for which a group of twenty-five (25) or more persons assembles, and
which obstructs, delays or interferes with the normal flow of pedestrian or vehicular
traffic and controls or is expected to do so.
d)
Any event for which a special event permit has or will be sought pursuant to Chapter 6 , Article II, Division 2 of this Code.
e)
Any event held on private property that has the ability to cause impact to traffic
and pedestrian flows, parking impacts on or around the special event area, or impacts
on public rights of way or infrastructure.
Examples of special events include but are not limited to concerts, parades, circuses,
fairs, festivals, block parties, community events, organized participation sports
such as running events or bicycle races, or motorcycle rallies. The term special event
shall not include temporary uses for which a temporary use permit is required pursuant
to chapter 16 of this Code.
Special event area means the area within which a special event will be conducted and for which a special
event permit has been issued, including any areas necessary for staging or disbanding
the special event.
Town facilities means any improvements to Town property and any pipes, wire, cable, supportive structure,
inlets, outlets, equipment, machinery, wiring, fiber, computers, appurtenances, and
any other plant or infrastructure that is owned by the Town or that is lawfully located
in public rights of way of the Town.
Town property means any real property, including public streets, sidewalks, alleys, rights-of-way,
parks and open space located in the Town of Milliken and owned, leased, operated,
or otherwise under the control of the Town, except for the interior of Town-owned
buildings, enclosed courtyards of Town-owned buildings, other Town property used primarily
for Town business and governmental operations, and Town owned real property where
the right of possession or occupancy has been leased or licensed to a third party.

(a)
Time for Application Submission. An application for a permit under this article must
be filed with the Town Clerk not less than forty-five (45) days and no more than six
(6) months prior to the special event. It shall be within the discretion of the Town
Clerk whether to accept an application for a permit under this article less than forty-five
(45) days prior to the special event; provided however that the following shall apply
to such late applications:
(1)
Under no circumstances may the Town Clerk accept any application for a permit less
than fifteen (15) days prior to the special event.
(2)
If the application is denied, there shall be no right of appeal as provided under section 11-5-50 (f).
(3)
The processing of the application shall be entirely in the discretion of the Town
Clerk. The Town Clerk shall be under no duty to comply with deadlines set forth in
this article or to issue a permit.
(b)
Contents of Application. The application shall be in writing on a form assembled by
and available from the Town Clerk's office and shall provide the following information
to be deemed complete:
(1)
The name, address, telephone number and other contact information of the event organizer
and the applicant;
(2)
The date(s), time(s) and purpose of the special event;
(3)
A description of the anticipated or proposed special event area and areas surrounding
the special event area anticipated to be affected by the special event;
(4)
A proposed traffic plan for the special event area and surrounding areas affected
by the event or a traffic plan waiver request as set forth in this article;
(5)
The number and kind of vehicles, equipment, or animals that will be involved in or
necessary for the special event;
(6)
The number and location of portable sanitation facilities and trash receptacles to
be provided for the event;
(7)
A plan for cleanup after the special event;
(8)
A request for amplified music or sound, including specific date and times and description
of same, if desired;
(9)
Written permission from the owners of record;
(10)
The application fee as described in subsection (d) of this section; and
(11)
A detailed narrative providing proof of full compliance with all applicable Town,
county, state, and federal disability access requirements to the satisfaction of the
Town Clerk.
(c)
The following may be required after the initial review of submitted application; and
are required to be submitted to the Town Clerk by the date requested:
(1)
A list of retailers or vendors participating in the special event that require compliance
with Chapter 6 , Article I, of this Code regarding business licenses and registrations, including
for each such retailer or vendor a description of any merchandise or services to be
sold.
(2)
An emergency action plan, including a commitment to follow the plan in the event circumstances
trigger any portion of the plan.
(3)
Proof of insurance as required by this article, an insurance waiver request as set
forth in this article, or both if requesting a partial waiver of this article's insurance
requirements.
(4)
Any special or unusual requirements or requests that may be needed for or created
by the special event.
(5)
Copies of all permit applications required for the special event by any other state
or local entity or agency.
(6)
Proof of adjacent property owner and business notification as described in section 11-5-50 (d)(9) of this article.
(7)
Traffic Plan. The Town Clerk may waive the requirement for a traffic plan if requested
and if he or she determines, after consultation with the Town's public works directors,
the Chief of Police, and local fire district, that a traffic plan is not reasonably
necessary for the special event. The Town Clerk shall notify the applicant in writing
of the decision on the waiver request. If the Town Clerk determines that a traffic
plan is necessary for the special event, the applicant shall submit a traffic plan
within five (5) days of the date of the Town Clerk's decision on the waiver request
but no later than twenty (20) days before the special event.
(8)
Any other information reasonably deemed necessary by the Town Clerk.
(d)
Application Fee and Financial Security.
(1)
Application Fee. Each application for a special event permit shall be accompanied
by an application fee determined by the Town's fee schedule, which may be set by resolution
of the Town Board of Trustees. The application fee(s) set by the Town Board of Trustees
may take into account the size and location of the special event and the nonprofit
status of the permittee and the resulting impacts on Town services, including police
and fire services.
(2)
Financial Security. In addition to the application fee, financial security for cleanup
and repair costs in an amount set forth in the Town fee schedule may be required and
if required shall be submitted to the Town Clerk prior to issuance of the permit.
The special event area shall be inspected by the Town to ensure proper site cleanup
prior to refunding the financial security.
(e)
Application Meeting. The Town may require a meeting with the applicant prior to issuance
of a special event permit.
(f)
Fireworks. The Board of Trustees has the power to grant permits within the Town for
supervised public displays of fireworks by the Town, fair associations, amusement
parks and other organizations and groups, and to adopt reasonable rules and regulations
for the granting of such permits. A permit for display must be obtained in accordance
with Chapter 10 , Article X, of this Code.

(a)
Issuance. The Town Clerk, after determining that the application is complete, shall
approve or approve with conditions a special event permit application and issue a
special event permit to the applicant if the Town Clerk determines that all of the
following criteria have been met:
(1)
The applicant has submitted a timely and complete special event permit application
in accordance with Section 11-5-40 of this article.
(2)
The special event will not:
a.
Unreasonably obstruct public access or passage;
b.
Generate unreasonable noise as defined in and prohibited by Article XI, Chapter 10 of this Code;
c.
Constitute a health or safety hazard;
d.
Exceed the space available for the intended use;
e.
Result in excessive or unreasonable damage to Town property;
f.
Interfere with any construction or maintenance work scheduled to take place on or
near a public right-of-way or other Town property;
g.
Impose an undue burden on Town resources or Town facilities or divert such necessary
resources or facilities from other Town services;
h.
Violate any applicable law (except to the limited extent permitted by the special
event permit such as in regard to noise limits and right-of-way obstruction);
i.
Conflict with a previously permitted use of the property or a Town-planned or scheduled
use of Town property; or
j.
Conflict with a priority special event.
(3)
The applicant has complied with the conditions of approval or requirements of a previously
issued special event permit.
(4)
The applicant has submitted to the Town Clerk's office a copy of all permits for the
proposed special event required by any other state or local entity.
(b)
Denial. If the Town Clerk determines that a special event permit application fails
to meet one (1) or more of the criteria set forth in Section 11-5-50 (a) of this Code, then the application shall be denied. Prior to issuing a notice
of denial as set forth in this article, the Town Clerk may request that the applicant
submit an amended application addressing any deficiencies. If an amended application
still does not meet the criteria for approval, then it shall be denied.
(c)
Permit Issuance Not a Guarantee. The Town's approval of a special event permit does
not guarantee issuance of other permits or authorization required for the special
event including any special event permit requested pursuant to Chapter 6 , Article II, Division 2 of this Code.
(d)
Conditions of Issuance. The Town Clerk may impose reasonable terms, conditions and
limitations, reflected in a special event permit or in a written agreement to ensure
reasonable protection of the Town's interests, including but not limited to:
(1)
Additional Town Services. If a special event will require the provision of Town services,
equipment or personnel beyond the levels normally provided by the Town, the Town Clerk
is authorized to require, as a condition of the special event permit, a mechanism
for the recovery of Town expenses associated with the special event, including but
not limited to costs for application review and processing; traffic plan review, implementation
and devices; off-duty police, fire and ambulance equipment and services; traffic control;
sanitation facilities; public works services; parks and recreation coordination; trash
collection and other clean up.
(2)
Number and Frequency. The number and frequency of special events shall be determined
in a case-by-case review, taking into consideration public health, safety and welfare
as well as wear and tear and competing uses of Town property and competing demands
on Town services.
(3)
Designated Areas. The Town Clerk, in consultation with the Public Works Directors
and Chief of Police, shall have the authority, when reasonably necessary, to prohibit
or restrict the parking of vehicles and equipment to certain designated areas and
to restrict the location of any retailers or vendors participating in the special
event.
(4)
Amplified Sound. Special events with amplified music or sound are subject to the Town's
noise regulations codified in Chapter 10 of this Code. A special event permit approval may allow sound amplification subject
to reasonable conditions such as time limits.
(5)
Cleanup and Damage to Town Property. The permittee shall be responsible for cleaning
the special event area during and immediately following each day of a special event.
The permittee is also responsible for mitigating any potential adverse impact to any
Town property or adjacent properties caused by the event. Permittee shall take all
steps necessary to prevent injury to vegetation, wildlife, Town facilities or other
Town property and shall be responsible to the Town for any damage caused by the permittee
or any retailer, participant, or spectator of the special event. The permittee shall
preserve and maintain any Town property used for the special event in the same condition
as that existing prior to the event. If Town property is damaged during the special
event, the permittee shall pay for the actual cost of repair, replacement, or remedy
of such damage, and upon failure to do so the Town may apply any financial security
held to such cost and may recover any remaining amount due by way of legal action
either in the form of restitution upon conviction of violating this article in the
municipal court or in the form of an action in the state or federal courts, and in
case of any such legal action the Town's legal fees shall be recoverable as damages.
(6)
Other Agency Approvals. Issuance of a special event permit is contingent upon approval
of the special event by other affected agencies including but not limited to Front
Range Fire Rescue, Weld County Public Health, Thompson River Parks and Recreation
District, and any other agency as deemed necessary by the Town Clerk.
(7)
Additional Conditions. The Town Clerk is authorized to impose such conditions upon
issuance of the permit as are necessary to ensure that all of the criteria set forth
in Section 11-5-50 (a) of this Code shall exist during the special event.
(8)
Placement of all traffic control devices and barricades is the responsibility of the
permittee, unless the Town notes otherwise in the event approval. The Town reserves
to the Town police department or any other Town employee acting at the direction of
the Town Administrator the right to stop any activity if proper traffic control devices
are not in place during the event. For events that will exceed existing parking facilities,
a traffic control plan must be submitted including anticipated vehicle traffic for
the event, peak traffic times and any necessary agreement from adjacent private property
owners allowing use of their property for parking. All street closures are contingent
upon the Town's approval. The special event shall not interfere with the flow of traffic
nor create a safety hazard to pedestrians or drivers with the adjacent businesses.
(9)
The applicant is responsible for notifying all residents/businesses impacted by the
special event as well as any street closure by completing the special event street
closure notification form. The notification must provide the following information:
a.
Date, time, location, and name of event.
b.
On-site contact information.
c.
Event hours (load in and load out times).
d.
Street closures.
e.
Amplified sound hours (if applicable).
f.
A copy of the special event area map.
(e)
Revocation.
(1)
The Town Clerk may, at any time prior to an event, revoke or terminate a permit that
has been issued for the special event if conditions change so that the permit application
could have been denied in the first instance.
(2)
The Town Clerk or the Milliken Police Department may revoke or terminate the permit
prior to or during the course of the event due to a national special security event
under 6 USC § 601(9) or if continuation of the event presents a clear and present
danger to the participants or the public.
(3)
The Town Clerk may revoke the permit and terminate the event during the course of
the event for noncompliance with special event terms and conditions.
(4)
The applicant is not entitled to a refund of any fees paid, and the applicant may
be liable to pay additional costs incurred by the Town due to the revocation.
(f)
Notice of Decision and Appeal. The Town Clerk shall act upon the special event permit
application within fifteen (15) days of the filing thereof and provide notice of his
or her decision to the applicant in writing including the reason for any denial. The
applicant may appeal the denial of the special events permit application to the Town
Administrator or their designee by filing a written notice of appeal within five (5)
days of the date of the notice of denial. The Town Administrator or their designee
shall act on the notice of appeal within five (5) days after receipt of said written
appeal by applicant.
(g)
Priority special events shall have approval priority over other special events where
applications are received for the same or substantially overlapping special event
area on the same or substantially overlapping dates or where available resources are
insufficient to allow both events to be approved. Any decision regarding whether approval
priority shall be in the reasonable discretion of the Town Clerk.

(a)
A special event permit is required for a demonstration unless the following conditions
are true:
(1)
The demonstration will not involve the use of vehicles, equipment, animals, fireworks,
or pyrotechnics;
(2)
No fee or donation will be charged or required as a condition of participation in
or attendance at the demonstration; and
(3)
The demonstration is conducted in accordance with all applicable local, state, and
federal laws, rules, and regulations.
(b)
Any person desiring to conduct a demonstration in the Town is encouraged to file a
notification of demonstration with the Town Administrator in the manner established
by the Town Administrator at least twenty-four (24) hours prior to the demonstration.
