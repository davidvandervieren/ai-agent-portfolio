The purpose of this Article is to: remove parcels of real property in the Town from
a condition that consistently and repeatedly violates municipal law; make property
owners vigilant in preventing public nuisances on or in their property; make property
owners responsible for the use of their property by tenants, guests and occupants;
and provide locally enforceable remedies.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
This Article shall apply to all private property in the Town, except premises governed
by the Colorado Beer Code and Colorado Liquor Code, because regulations promulgated
under C.R.S. Title 12, Articles 46, 47 and 48, establish adequate local remedies to
address recurring disturbances or other activities occurring on such premises.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
For purposes of this Article, the following terms shall have the following meanings:
Abandoned property means a property over which the person in charge no longer asserts control due to
death, incarceration, or any other reason, and which is either unsecured or subject
to occupation by unauthorized individuals.
Abatement agreement means an agreement between the owner of a chronic illegal activity premises and the
Town setting forth specific measures that the owner or manager of the premises shall
be required to take by a date certain to abate a nuisance under this Article.
Chronic illegal activity premises means:
(a)
A property that is the subject matter of three (3) or more calls for law enforcement
on three (3) different days within any 90-day period resulting in a case report documenting
an investigation of illegal activity within the premises; provided, however, that
any illegal activity reported by the owner or the owner's agent shall not be counted;
(b)
A property where, pursuant to a valid search warrant, evidence of drug-related activity
has been identified two (2) or more times, or on which drug-related activity is observed
on seven (7) or more occasions during any 12-month period;
(c)
A property that is used for one or more separate incidents of illegal activity within
any six-month period; two (2) or more separate incidents of illegal activity within
any 12-month period; or three (3) or more separate incidents of illegal activity within
any 24-month period; or
(d)
An abandoned property where illegal activity is occurring.
Drug-related activity means any unlawful activity at a property which consists of the manufacture, cultivation,
growth, production, delivery, sale, storage, possession, use or giving away of any
controlled substance as defined in C.R.S. § 18-18-203 et seq.
Illegal activity means prostitution, illegal gambling, illegal possession or delivery of or trafficking
in controlled substances, or any other activity that constitutes a felony, misdemeanor,
business offense or petty offense under federal or state law, except incidents of
domestic violence, as defined in C.R.S. § 18-6-800.3 et seq., as amended; any act
or omission that constitutes an offense under Chapter 10 of this Code; or any act or condition that constitutes a public nuisance under this
Code or which has been declared to be a nuisance by state courts or statutes or known
as such at common law.
Rental unit means a residential building or part thereof which is rented to another and used
as a home, residence or sleeping place by one (1) or more persons.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
A chronic illegal activity premises is hereby declared to be a public nuisance.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
(a)
Notice. If he Town finds that a premises is a chronic illegal activity premises, the
Town shall notify the owner in writing of such determination, and shall provide the
owner with an opportunity to meet with appropriate Town staff to discuss the allegations
contained in the notice and the need for nuisance abatement at the premises and a
proposed abatement agreement.
(b)
Service. The written notice to abate shall be served by the authorized inspector by:
(1)
Personally delivering a copy of the notice to the owner if the owner also resides
at the property;
(2)
Personally delivering a copy of the notice to the nonowner occupant or resident of
the property and mailing a copy of the notice by certified mail, return receipt requested,
to the last known address of the owner as reflected in the Weld County real estate
records; or
(3)
If the property is unoccupied, mailing a copy of the notice by certified mail, return
receipt requested, to the last known address of the owner as reflected in the Weld
County real estate records, and posting a copy of the notice in a conspicuous place
at the unoccupied premises.
(c)
Contents. The notice shall contain the following:
(1)
A statement that the premises identified in the notice has been determined to be a
chronic illegal activity premises under this Article;
(2)
A description of the activities supporting the determination and the date on which
the activities occurred;
(3)
A statement that the owner may request in writing, by the date certain set forth in
such notice, a settlement meeting with appropriate Town staff to discuss the allegations
contained in the notice, the need for abatement measures at the premises and a proposed
abatement agreement;
(4)
A statement that if the owner fails to meet with appropriate Town staff in a timely
manner or fails to enter into an abatement agreement with the Town or otherwise fails
to abate the nuisance under this Article, the Town may institute an appropriate action
in a court of competent jurisdiction to abate such nuisance; and
(5)
A statement detailing the penalties for a violation of this Article.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
(a)
Filing. If no abatement agreement is executed or, if such agreement is executed and
the owner has failed to comply with such agreement, the matter may be referred to
the Town Attorney for filing of a civil action. An action under this Article may be
brought in any court of record, including the Platteville Municipal Court. An action
under this Article shall be commenced by the filing of a complaint, which may be accompanied
by a motion for a temporary abatement order.
(b)
Persons Liable. The persons liable under this Article may include any person owning
or claiming any ownership or leasehold interest in the premises, all tenants and occupants
of the premises, all managers and agents for the premises or the owner, and any other
person whose involvement may be necessary to abate the illegal activities from recurring,
provided that none of these parties shall be deemed necessary or indispensable parties.
Any person holding any legal or equitable interest in the parcel who has not been
named as a party may intervene, but no other person may intervene.
(c)
Defenses. If a defendant is the owner and is leasing the parcel to one (1) or more
tenants, or the defendant has been hired by the owner to manage and lease the parcel,
and the alleged public nuisance was committed by the tenants, it is a defense to an
action brought under this Article that said defendant has:
(1)
Evicted, or attempted to evict by commencing and pursuing with due diligence appropriate
court proceedings, all of the tenants that committed each of the separate violations
that constitute the alleged public nuisance; and
(2)
Has undertaken and pursued with due diligence, reasonable means to avoid a recurrence
of similar violations on the parcel by the present and future tenants.
(Ord. 740 §3, 2017)
(a)
The court may issue temporary or permanent abatement orders to restrain and abate
the chronic illegal activity premises. No bond or other security shall be required
of the Town.
(b)
Every abatement order under this Article shall be binding upon the parcel, the parties
to the action, their agents and employees and any other person named as a defendant
and served with a copy of the order.
(c)
Temporary or permanent abatement orders may: require any parties-defendant to take
steps to abate the public nuisance; authorize the Town to take reasonable steps to
abate the public nuisance activity and prevent it from recurring, considering the
nature and extent of the separate violations; require certain named individuals to
stay away from the parcel at all times or for some specific period of time; require
the temporary or permanent closure of a parcel and to keep the same effectually closed
against its use for any purpose until further order of the court; and require any
steps reasonably necessary to access, maintain or safeguard the parcel.
(d)
At a hearing on a motion for a temporary abatement order, the Town has the burden
of proving the existence of a public nuisance and, in the case of a temporary order
granted without notice, that such order is reasonably necessary to avoid some immediate,
irreparable loss, damage or injury.
(e)
At the trial on the merits of a civil action commenced under this Article, the Town
