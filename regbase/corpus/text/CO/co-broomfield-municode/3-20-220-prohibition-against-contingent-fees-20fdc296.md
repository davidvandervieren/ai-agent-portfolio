The purpose of this chapter is to provide for the fair and equitable treatment of
all persons involved in purchasing by the city, to maximize the purchasing value of
public funds in procurement, and to provide safeguards for maintaining a procurement
system of quality and integrity.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
This chapter applies to the procurement of supplies, services, and construction, entered
into by the city after the effective date of the ordinance codified in this chapter.
When the procurement involves the expenditure of federal funds, the procurement shall
be conducted in accordance with any mandatory applicable federal law and regulations.
Nothing in this chapter shall prevent the city from complying with the terms and conditions
of any grant, gift, or bequest that is otherwise consistent with law.

The defined terms used in this chapter, whether capitalized or not, shall have the
meanings given in this section 3-20-030 , B.M.C. as follows:
(A)
Bidder or offeror means a bidder or offeror whose submittal conforms in all material respects to and
terms set out by the city in the solicitation documents.
(B)
City manager means the city and county manager or his or her designated assistant or deputy city
and county manager.
(C)
Construction means the process of building, altering, repairing, improving, or demolishing any
public structure or building, or other public improvements of any kind to any public
real property. It does not include the routine operation of existing structures, buildings,
or real property.
(D)
Fixed asset purchase means a purchase that costs $5,000 or more and has a useful life of greater than
one year.
(E)
Purchase agreement means an agreement by the city for the purchase or lease of materials, supplies,
or services, and construction.
(F)
Professional services means and includes architectural, engineering, surveying, legal, consulting, software
maintenance or programming, or other services which involve primarily the furnishing
of labor, time or expertise.
(G)
Procurement means all solicitation, award and contractual process of an acquisition of materials,
supplies, services, and construction. Procurement does not include real estate under chapter 2-74 , B.M.C., water rights under chapter 13-20 , B.M.C., banking services under section 3-18-070 , B.M.C., or travel expenses.
(H)
Responsible bidder or offeror means a bidder or offeror who has the capability in all respects to perform in full
the contract requirements, and the integrity and reliability that will assure good
faith performance.
(I)
Responsive bidder or offeror means a bidder or offeror whose bid conforms in all material respects to and terms
set out by the government in the solicitation documents.
(J)
Services means the furnishing of labor, time, or effort by a contractor, not involving the
delivery of a specific end product other than reports which are merely incidental
to the required performance. This term shall not include employment agreements, collective
bargaining agreements, or payment of negotiated claims and/or settlements.
(K)
Solicitation means the request for written submittals from the public for a particular material,
supply, or service.
(L)
Subscription agreement means the proprietary arrangement of licensed software and off-site hardware not maintained
by the city and/or not considered a physical asset of the city.
(M)
Supplies means all property, including but not limited to equipment, materials, and printing.
(N)
Utilities means all utilities, including telecom, fiber, water, sewer, phone, mobile devices,
pagers, electric, fuel, and natural gas.

(A)
Every purchase agreement shall be evidenced by a written contract or by a standard
written or computerized purchase order.
(B)
Every individual purchase agreement or aggregate of multiple purchase agreements in
any calendar year or for a specific project with a single vendor for the same or similar
supplies or services, which, including any contingencies, exceeds or could exceed
$200,000.00 shall be approved or ratified by city council. An agreement for the purchase
of water permits, real estate permits, utilities, postage, insurance premiums, or
employee benefits/services need not be approved by express action, but shall be considered
approved by city council if the budget appropriation for such purchase has been approved.

in this section that is included in the Schedule of Capital Equipment Purchases approved
by city council and if cooperatively bid pursuant to subsection 3-20-160 (B), B.M.C., shall be considered approved by city council if the budget appropriation
for such purchase has been approved.
(C)
A purchase agreement for materials, supplies or professional services to be provided
on an on-going basis shall contain a maximum amount payable under the agreement or
a termination date for the agreement.
(D)
An individual purchase agreement or aggregate of multiple purchase agreements with
a single vendor for the same or similar supplies or services in any calendar year
or for a specific project of more than $100,000.00 and up to $200,000.00 or less shall
be approved by the city manager.
(E)
An individual purchase agreement or aggregate of multiple purchase agreements with
a single vendor for the same or similar supplies or services in any calendar year
or for a specific project of less than $100,000.00 shall be approved as set forth
in the rules and administrative procedures as adopted by the city manager consistent
with this chapter.
(F)
A purchase agreement shall be submitted to the city attorney and approved as to legal
form and content before it is executed whenever the agreement is for the purchase
of (1) professional services, (2) real estate, including leases, easements, or other
real property interests, or (3) supplies, materials, services, or construction not
procured pursuant to an approved city form of agreement.
(G)
Before any purchase agreement is executed, the finance department shall confirm that
an appropriation has been made or that other sufficient funds will be available to
pay the amounts required in the agreement.

(A)
Right to protest . Any actual or prospective bidder, offeror, or contractor who is aggrieved in connection
with the solicitation or award of a contract or solicitation must protest in writing
to the city manager as a prerequisite to seeking judicial relief. A protest shall
be submitted within five business days after such aggrieved person knows or should
have known of the facts giving rise thereto. A protest with respect to a solicitation
or award of contract or solicitation shall be submitted in writing prior to the opening
of solicitation unless the aggrieved person did not know and should not have known
of the facts giving rise to such protests prior to solicitation opening. This protest
procedure shall not apply to a suspension from consideration as set forth below.
(B)
Stay of procurements during protests . In the event of a timely protest under subsection (A) above, the city shall not
proceed further with the solicitation or award of the contract until all administrative
remedies have been exhausted or until the city manager makes a determination that
the award of a contract without delay is necessary to protect substantial interests
of the city.
(C)
Administrative remedies . The city manager shall review the protest, conduct any investigation he or she deems
necessary, and issue a written decision within twenty days. Any actual or prospective
bidder, offeror, or contractor may appeal the city manager's written decision to city
council. Any such appeal must be made in writing, within five business days of the
city manager's written decision. The city council shall review the appeal, conduct
any investigation it deems necessary, and issue a written decision. The city council's
decision shall be final.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
It is unlawful for any payment, gratuity, or offer of employment to be made by or
on behalf of a subcontractor under a contract to the prime contractor or higher tier
subcontractor for any person associated therewith, as an inducement for the award
of a subcontract or order.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
It is unlawful for a person to be retained, or to retain a person, to solicit or secure
a city contract or other procurement upon an agreement or understanding for a commission,
percentage, brokerage, or contingent fee except for retention of bona fide employees
or bona fide established commercial selling agencies for the purpose of securing business.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
It is unlawful for any city employee, who is participating directly or indirectly
in the procurement process, while employed by the city, to become or to be the employee
of any person or entity contracting with the city, or to have a significant, controlling
ownership interest in relation to the person or entity contracting with the city,
from which such city employee would directly benefit.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
It is unlawful for any employee or former employee knowingly to use confidential information
for actual or anticipated personal gain, or for the actual or anticipated personal
gain of any other person.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
(A)
General provisions . The value of anything transferred or received in breach of the provisions of sections 3-20-210 , B.M.C. through 3-20-240 , B.M.C. by a city employee or nonemployee may be recovered from both city employee
and nonemployee.
(B)
Recovery of unethical inducements by the city . Upon a showing that a subcontractor made an unethical inducement to a prime contractor
or a higher tier subcontractor in connection with the award of a subcontract or order
thereunder, it shall be conclusively presumed that the amount thereof was included
in the price of the subcontract or order and ultimately borne by the city and will
be recoverable hereunder from the recipient. In addition, that amount may also be
recovered from the subcontractor making such unethical inducement. Recovery from one
offending party shall not preclude recovery from other offending parties.
(Ord. 1988 §1, 2013)
Every officer and employee of the city is expressly prohibited from knowingly:
(A)
Seeking or accepting any personal gift or money rebate, directly or indirectly, from
any person, company, firm or corporation in connection with a purchase agreement;
(B)
Underestimating or exaggerating requirements to a prospective vendor for the purpose
of influencing submittals;
(C)
Misrepresenting the quality of a vendor's products or services; or
(D)
Influencing the city to enter into a purchase agreement which will benefit the officer
or employee either directly or indirectly or which any elective or appointive officer
of the city or member of the officer's family has a pecuniary interest.
