(A)
The abatement of vehicular public nuisances for the protection of public health, safety
and welfare is a matter of local concern. Crimes involving vehicles render Broomfield
residents, visitors, business, and neighborhoods insecure in life and in the use of
property. Such crimes and the motor vehicles used to commit such crimes constitute
a continuing threat to the comfort, safety, and health of the public. It is expressly
declared that vehicle public nuisances are a nuisance within the city and county that
should be eliminated or hindered, and thereby abated, by the means set forth in this
chapter.
(B)
Actions at law are not an adequate remedy, and the protection of public health, safety,
and welfare requires the temporary restraining orders provided in this section. Ex
parte temporary restraining orders are necessary to provide rapid relief from public
nuisances without the delay entailed by adversarial hearings and personal service
and to prevent persons from removing, concealing, destroying, encumbering, selling,
and transferring property that may be the subject of the remedies in this article.
(C)
The public nuisance offenses and the provisions of this chapter are entirely strict
liability in nature. No allegation or proof of culpability or mens rea of any type
or degree by any owner of a motor vehicle or defendant shall be required for any filing
of a vehicle public nuisance action, or the issuance of temporary restraining orders,
or other remedies under the provisions of this chapter.
( Ord. No. 2304 , § 1, 5-26-2026)
Notwithstanding any other definitions contained elsewhere in this Code, the following
terms and definitions shall apply to this chapter:
(A)
Abate means to bring to a halt, eliminate, or where that is not possible or feasible, to
suppress, reduce, and minimize.
(B)
City and county attorney means the Office of the Broomfield City and County Attorney, and includes any attorney
or staff member, including contracted outside counsel, working at the direction of
the Office of the City and County Attorney.
(C)
Close , to close , or closure means to exercise control over the motor vehicle and remove all owners, occupants
and other persons from the motor vehicle and to impound, lock, secure, and otherwise
close and prohibit all entry, access, and use of the motor vehicle, except access
and use as may be specifically ordered by the court for purposes of inventory, maintenance,
storage, security and other purposes, and to vest the sole right of possession and
control of the motor vehicle, in the City and County of Broomfield for a limited period
of time defined by court order.
(D)
Defendant . The primary defendant to the action is the motor vehicle itself. However, for purposes
of this chapter, the term Defendant shall include any person named in the action who
meets the definition of Owner-Defendant or Party-Defendant below. For purposes of
this chapter, whenever the term Defendant is used it shall be read as including both
Owner-Defendants and Party-Defendants.
(E)
Flight means to flee, escape, or leave the place where the vehicle public nuisance activity
was committed or conducted, whether or not law enforcement is present.
(F)
Motor vehicle means any self-propelled vehicle that is designed primarily for travel on the public
highways and that is generally and commonly used to transport persons and property
over the public highways; except that the term does not include electrical assisted
bicycles, electric scooters, low-power scooters, wheelchairs, or vehicles moved solely
by human power.
(G)
Non-involved owner means an owner of a motor vehicle that is implicated in a public nuisance abatement
action, who:
(1)
Did not directly or indirectly encourage, facilitate, allow, promote, participate,
aid, abet, conspire, permit, or condone the transaction constituting the vehicle public
nuisance act, or the flight or attempted flight therefrom; and
(2)
Neither knew, nor should have known, that the subject motor vehicle was used or would
be used in the commission of a vehicle public nuisance act; and
(3)
Neither knew, nor should have known, that the person who operated the motor vehicle
in the commission of the vehicle public nuisance act involving the motor vehicle had
a prior record or reputation for violating the laws, including traffic laws, of the
United States, State of Colorado, City and County of Broomfield, or any other locality
within or outside the State of Colorado.
A person who does not otherwise meet the definition of a non-involved owner may nonetheless
be deemed a non-involved owner if they were the reporting party who immediately informed
law enforcement of the vehicle public nuisance act giving rise to the abatement action
under this chapter.
(H)
Owner means the registered owner(s) with the Colorado Department of Revenue (or comparable
office), plus any other person or entity with a recorded bona fide legal or equitable
interest or right of possession on the vehicle's title, including a lienholder. The
City will determine ownership through reasonable efforts, including a registration
query and title search. Others may establish ownership with a bona fide bill of sale,
lease agreement, or sufficient documentation. A straw man is not an owner for this
chapter.
(I)
Owner-defendant means any Owner as defined above who is named as a Defendant in the action. All known
Owners must be named as Defendants in the action.
(J)
Party-defendant means any person who has been named in the action and who is not also an Owner, as
defined above. Party-Defendants are not necessary or indispensable parties, and the
city and county attorney is not required to name all Party Defendants in the action.
(K)
Vehicle public nuisance means any motor vehicle used to commit, conduct, promote, facilitate or aid in the
commission of the following acts:
(1)
Speeding, M.T.C. 1101(1), where the speed involved was 40 miles per hour or more over
the posted speed limit, or where the speed was equal to or greater than 100 miles
per hour regardless of the posted speed limit;
(2)
Speed contests, exhibition of speed, or aiding and facilitating, M.T.C. §1105 et seq;
(3)
Vehicular eluding, C.R.S. 18-9-116.5, or eluding or attempting to elude a police officer,
M.T.C. 1413(1);
(4)
Reckless Driving, M.T.C. 1401(1);
(5)
Minimum speed regulations, M.T.C. 1103;
(6)
Obstructing highways or other passageways as prohibited in C.R.S. 18-9-107 or Chapter
9-40, B.M.C.;
(7)
Unlawful destruction of property of another as prohibited by Section 9-49-020 , B.M.C.; or
(8)
Two or more offenses for disturbing the peace within any 180-day period as prohibited
by Chapter 9-34 , B.M.C.
Where a proscribed act references the Colorado Revised Statutes (C.R.S.), Broomfield
Municipal Code (B.M.C.), or Model Traffic Code (2010) (M.T.C.), it includes those
sections as amended and adopts their definitions. Application of this chapter does
not require a charge or conviction, and a plea to a lesser offense or dismissal does
not prevent enforcement where probable cause existed for the original vehicle public
nuisance act.
(L)
Straw man means a person who nominally takes part in the purchase, lease, or ownership of property,
often concealing the real purchaser's or lessee's identity from other parties (e.g.,
seller, financial institution, government agency), and may become financially obligated.
This person acts as an accommodation party or agent for the principal.

(A)
The remedies in this chapter are cumulative and supplementary to all other criminal,
traffic, or civil remedies, statutes, and administrative proceedings (such as license
revocation or fines). The city and county may pursue any or all of these remedies
simultaneously or successively.
(B)
This civil action will not be delayed by any other pending criminal, civil, or administrative
action, or any action filed by another person, unless all parties stipulate and the
court approves such stipulation in its sole discretion.
(C)
Pursuant to Colorado Constitution Article XX, Section 6, and Broomfield Charter Section
10-2 and Code chapter 2-28 , the municipal court for the City and County of Broomfield is granted the jurisdiction,
duties, and powers for this article.
(D)
All actions under this chapter shall be civil and remedial in nature. All closure,
receivership and destruction remedies under this article shall be against the property
(in rem). Injunctive remedies, civil judgements and fees, and the terms of an abatement
agreement or temporary restraining order may be partly against the person (in personam).
(E)
All issues of fact and law shall be tried to the court without a jury.
(F)
The burden of proof in all proceedings under this article, including proof of the
underlying activity forming the basis of a vehicular public nuisance, shall be by
a preponderance of the evidence, unless a different burden of proof is specified.
(G)
Unless otherwise agreed to by the city and defendant, no remedy under this section,
other than the permanent closure and final disposition of a motor vehicle, shall contain
obligations which extend more than 364 days from the date of final judgement or the
date of a court-approved abatement agreement.
(H)
Unless this chapter provides for a different rule or procedure, proceedings under
chapter shall be governed by the Colorado County Court Rules of Civil Procedure (C.R.C.P.).
Where this article or the C.R.C.P. fail to state a rule of decision, the court shall
look to the Public Nuisance Abatement Act, C.R.S. 16-13-301 et seq., and the related
cases.
(I)
Actions under this chapter shall be filed only by Broomfield's Office of the City
and County Attorney, or an agent thereof.
(J)
Any owner, with the exception of a straw man, as defined in this chapter who has not
been named as a defendant may intervene under the procedures of this chapter and shall
be added as a defendant. No other parties may intervene.
(K)
No party may file any counterclaim, cross-claim, third party claim, or set-off of
any kind in any action under this chapter.
(L)
Other than the pleadings permitted under this chapter, there shall be no additional
pleadings filed absent order of court.
(M)
Service upon all defendants shall be deemed sufficient:
(1)
If made in accordance with C.R.C.P. 304 with regard to the documents identified in
Section 10-27-050 (E)(3); or
(2)
If made in accordance with C.R.C.P. 305 with regard to all other pleadings or papers
required to be served under this chapter. The last known address of the person to
be served shall be determined by referencing either:
a.
the address listed in the court file;
b.
the records of the Colorado department of revenue, division of motor vehicles, or
any similar department of any other state;
c.
any government database; or
d.
any other address where the city can articulate a reasonable belief that the documents
will be received by the owner or defendant.
e.
the city shall make a good faith effort to identify and serve all owners as defined
by this chapter.

(A)
Legislative Intent. Impoundment of motor vehicles before filing a complaint and petition
for restraining order is necessary to prevent the continuance of the public nuisance
and the removal, destruction and concealment of motor vehicles to thwart the remedies
provided in this chapter.
(B)
Any police officer for the City and County of Broomfield may impound and detain a
motor vehicle under this section if they determine that there is probable cause to
believe that:
(1)
A public nuisance offense has occurred within the city; and
(2)
The public nuisance offense occurred in the motor vehicle, or the motor vehicle was
used to facilitate, allow, promote, participate, aid, abet, or permit the transaction
constituting the vehicle public nuisance act, or the flight or attempted flight therefrom;
and
(3)
The motor vehicle is capable of being concealed, destroyed, or removed from the city.
(C)
Where a motor vehicle is impounded under this section, the procedures of this section
shall exclusively apply unless otherwise specified.
(D)
Whenever a motor vehicle is impounded under this section, the Broomfield Police Department
shall submit an affidavit in support of impoundment to the city and county attorney's
office within three calendar days, excluding court weekends and city holidays.
(E)
The city and county attorney must submit the affidavit supporting impoundment for
a probable cause determination by the court, or instead declare the motor vehicle
available for release and provide notice to the owner(s). This action must occur within
three calendar days of receiving the affidavit, excluding court weekends and city
holidays.
(F)
If the court finds the affidavit in support of impoundment supports a finding of probable
cause for impoundment under this section, then within 14 days of the date the motor
vehicle was impounded as a public nuisance, the city and county attorney shall file
with the court:
(1)
The pleadings required for the commencement of actions under this chapter; or
(2)
A declaration that the motor vehicle is available for release and that the city made
good faith efforts to serve the registered owner with a notice to that effect; or
(3)
A declaration the motor vehicle is available for release, but that the motor vehicle
is relevant evidence in a criminal proceeding, and is being held as result of that
status. This filing shall also include a statement that the city made good faith efforts
to serve the registered owner with a notice as to the current circumstance of the
vehicle.
(G)
If the court finds the affidavit in support of impoundment does not support a finding
of probable cause for impoundment under this section, then it shall promptly inform
the city and county attorney who shall file a declaration that the motor vehicle is
available for release and that the city made good faith efforts to serve the registered
owner with a notice to that effect.
(H)
If the city and county attorney declines to submit the affidavit for court consideration
or if the court determines probable cause does not exist, then the defendant may request
a hearing regarding costs pursuant to Section 10-27-170 .

To commence an action under this chapter, the city and county attorney shall file
a complaint, including an affidavit in support of impoundment and petition for temporary
restraining order with the court.
(B)
The complaint shall include, at a minimum, the following:
(1)
Name of the defendant(s).
(2)
Date and approximate location of the vehicle public nuisance act offense(s).
(3)
Statutory and/or Code citation for the vehicle public nuisance act offense(s).
(4)
Brief statement or description of the offense(s) forming the basis for the vehicle
public nuisance complaint.
(5)
Direction to appear before a specified court at a stated date, time, and place.
(6)
A description of the motor vehicle and its Vehicle Identification Number.
(C)
Upon receipt of the complaint, including the affidavit in support of impoundment,
the court shall make a determination as to whether there is probable cause to believe
that:
(1)
A public nuisance offense has occurred within the city; and
(2)
The public nuisance offense occurred in the motor vehicle, or the motor vehicle was
used to facilitate, allow, promote, participate, aid, abet, or permit the transaction
constituting the vehicle public nuisance act, or the flight or attempted flight therefrom.
(D)
If the court determines probable cause does not exist, the court shall reject the
complaint and petition without prejudice and inform the city and county attorney of
the determination who must provide notice to the owner(s), if notice has not already
been provided. If the vehicle has already been impounded, then the defendant may request
a hearing regarding costs pursuant to Section 10-27-170 .
(E)
If the court determines probable cause exists, then the following procedures shall
apply;
(1)
The court shall issue an order on the petition for temporary restraining order with
all requirements as set forth in 10-27-060 .
(2)
The court clerk shall assign the matter a case number, docket the civil matter for
a first appearance in approximately 21 days unless otherwise ordered by the court;
(3)
The city and county attorney shall promptly, but no later than seven days before the
first appearance, serve a copy of the complaint, affidavit, petition and order granting
temporary restraining order, summons, and a copy of this chapter or the website address
to locate a copy of this chapter on the registered owner(s) of the subject motor vehicle,
any other owner actually known to the city at the time of filing, and any other defendant
identified by the city. The city and county attorney shall have an ongoing duty to
attempt to serve these documents on any other owners discovered during the pendency
of this matter.
(4)
No answer shall be filed. The city and county attorney is under no obligation to serve
a copy of a blank answer upon any defendant.

The temporary restraining order shall include the following orders for the impoundment
and closure of motor vehicles and restraining of persons as to the motor vehicles:
(1)
Declare the motor vehicle closed;
(2)
Order that the Broomfield Police Department impound and close the motor vehicle and
place the same in police custody, or to retain the same in police custody if previously
impounded, until further order of the court;
(3)
The Broomfield Police Department may use reasonable force, consistent with department

(4)
Broomfield Police officers and their agents may enter onto real property to impound

(6)
All persons shall be restrained from removing, concealing, damaging, destroying, selling,

Every temporary restraining order shall be binding upon the property, the parties

The issuance and execution of temporary restraining orders under this section shall

Prior to the trial on the merits, any defendant may seek to vacate or modify the temporary
restraining order allowing for the return of the motor vehicle on the basis that the
impound is a significant burden and that the negative impacts of the significant burden
on the defendant substantially outweigh the public safety interest served by the temporary

(B)
Within 14 days of service of the complaint, any defendant seeking to vacate or modify
a temporary restraining order shall:
(1)
File a motion under this section, which shall include, at a minimum:
a.
The challenges being raised or specific relief being requested; and
b.
A statement of the factual and legal grounds upon which the relief is being requested.
(2)
Personally serve a copy of the motion on the city and county attorney's office;
(3)
File a copy of the motion with the court; and
(4)
Contact the clerk of court at the time of filing to set the motion for a hearing to
be held as soon as practicable.
(C)
The court will not consider any challenges or requests for relief at the hearing that
were not included in the written motion. Challenges as to probable cause will not
be considered by the court.
(D)
Failure to serve, file, or set the motion for hearing, as provided above, shall constitute
a waiver of the motion.
(E)
Unless otherwise ordered by the court, the temporary restraining order shall remain
in effect and shall not be modified until after a trial on the merits or upon the
stipulation of the parties to an abatement agreement that is approved by the court.
(F)
Hearings on motions to modify a temporary restraining order:
(1)
Hearings under this section shall be to the court and the rules of evidence shall
be relaxed. The court, in its discretion, may take sworn testimony or rely on offers
of proof and argument.
(2)
Unless good cause is shown, a failure to appear by a defendant for a hearing set pursuant
to this section shall result in a waiver of the right to request a modification of
the temporary restraining order.
(3)
At hearing, the burden will be on the defendant to show by clear and convincing evidence:
a.
That the requested relief is necessary to avoid a significant burden; and
b.
That the impacts of the significant burden on the defendant substantially outweigh
the public safety interest addressed by the temporary restraining order term(s) being
challenged.
(4)
The court shall not grant an adjournment or continuance of the hearing on the motion
to modify or vacate the temporary restraining order unless it finds good cause to
do so. If a continuance is granted, the hearing shall be reset as soon as practicable.
(5)
The court shall enter its findings and order on the motion at the conclusion of the
hearing, unless the court finds that it is necessary to take the matter under advisement.
In that event, the court shall establish an expedited schedule for the determination.

At the first appearance, the court shall set the matter for a trial on the merits
within 35 days unless:
(1)
The parties stipulate to a continuance for the purpose of entering into an abatement

(2)
The parties present an abatement agreement and the court orders compliance with the

The court finds that a longer setting is reasonably necessary;

At the first appearance, either party may request from the opposing party a list of

Written requests for additional discovery may be filed with the court, and the court

If the city and county attorney claims that any material or statement ordered to be

Any subpoenas shall be issued in accordance with C.M.C.R.P. 217.
(F)
Trial on the Merits.
(1)
Trial shall be to the court and the Colorado Rules of Evidence shall apply.
(2)
At trial, the burden shall be on the city and county attorney to establish, by a preponderance
of the evidence, that the motor vehicle should be deemed a public nuisance. The elements
at trial shall be:
a.
That, within the City and County of Broomfield, State of Colorado;
b.
At or about the location, date and time specified in the complaint;
c.
A vehicle public nuisance act occurred in the motor vehicle, or the motor vehicle
was used to encourage, facilitate, allow, promote, participate, aid, abet, conspire,
permit, or condone the transaction constituting the public nuisance act, or the flight
or attempted flight therefrom.
(G)
At the conclusion of the trial, the court shall enter findings as to whether the city
met its burden.
(1)
If the burden was met, the motor vehicle shall be deemed a public nuisance and the
court shall enter a final judgment containing one or more of the following orders:
a.
The motor vehicle is to be permanently closed by the city;
b.
The motor vehicle is to be closed by the city for no less than 180 days and no more
than 364 days, which number of days may include the days the vehicle had already been
closed awaiting the trial.
c.
The motor vehicle is to be deemed abandoned property that may be disposed of or sold,
in the sole discretion of the city, in any manner provided for in the Code;
d.
A temporary protection order is to be issued allowing an owner-defendant to regain
the use of the vehicle, but with restrictions necessary to prevent future violations
of this code;
e.
Any order tailored to prevent the motor vehicle's future use as a public nuisance
by a defendant;
f.
Defendants shall pay a civil judgment and court costs as provided in Section 10-27-110 .
(2)
If the court concludes that the city did not meet its burden, the motor vehicle shall
not be deemed a public nuisance, and the court shall enter a final judgment containing
the following orders:
a.
The motor vehicle is to be released to the owner(s);
b.
Prior to release to the owner(s), the owner(s) shall pay all towing and impoundment
fees;
c.
Failure to pay all towing and impoundment fees as ordered will result in the motor
vehicle being deemed abandoned property that may be disposed of or sold, in the sole
discretion of the city, in any manner provided for in the Code.
(H)
Failure to Appear.
(1)
When a party-defendant fails to appear for first appearance or trial, the court may
impose court costs against that defendant. Additional fees and costs permitted by
this Code may be ordered in a discrete amount or determined at any time prior to the
entry of final judgment.
(2)
When a party-defendant fails to appear for the first appearance or trial, the party-defendant
shall have no standing to contest the orders of the court relating to an abatement
agreement or orders entered pursuant to this section.
(3)
When a party-defendant fails to appear for the first appearance or trial without giving
notice establishing good cause before the trial was set to begin, there shall be no
stay of the entry of default judgment and the court shall not have discretion to set
aside the default judgment as to that party-defendant. In considering whether a defendant's
pre-trial notice constitutes good cause for a failure to appear, the court shall also
consider the prejudice to any other owner-defendant that would be caused by a further
delay of the trial, to include the ability to reschedule and any increased impound
fees. If the court determines a delay is appropriate, the court shall make reasonable
efforts to reschedule the trial date within 14 calendar days from the date the owner-defendant
failed to appear.

(A)
At any time before trial, a non-involved owner has the option to obtain a quick release
of a detained vehicle upon the execution of a voluntary abatement agreement under
this subsection.
(B)
A voluntary abatement agreement to obtain a quick release of a vehicle to a non-involved
owner should have, at a minimum, the following terms and conditions unless otherwise
stipulated:
(1)
The non-involved owner agrees and stipulates that the vehicle at issue was involved
in the vehicular public nuisance activity.
(2)
If applicable, specified individual(s) will not be allowed access to the vehicular
public nuisance vehicle for a period of two (2) years following the execution of the
agreement.
(3)
The non-involved owner agrees that the vehicle will not be involved in any vehicular
public nuisance activity for two (2) years following the execution of the agreement.
(4)
The non-involved owner agrees to pay in full the accrued towing costs and any fees
associated with the impoundment or storage of the vehicle.
(5)
The non-involved owner agrees to waive any claims against the city, its agents or
representatives, and the Broomfield Police Department, relating to the towing, storage
and/or impoundment of the vehicle. Further, the noninvolved owner agrees to indemnify
the city for any claims against the city by third parties regarding the title or right
of possession to the vehicle and/or any items contained therein.
(6)
The non-involved owner agrees that the vehicle may be listed on law enforcement information
systems showing the person(s) who are restricted from access to said vehicle.
(7)
The non-involved owner agrees that upon a violation of this agreement, the city can
open, or re-open, a civil action under this article without any prior notice requirements,
and that upon written motion the court may impose any and all remedies authorized
by this chapter without limitation.
(8)
The non-involved owner agrees that no trial on the merits will occur. At most, there
will only be a hearing to prove by a preponderance of the evidence that there has
been a breach of the voluntary abatement agreement and order of the Court.
(C)
Once agreed upon and signed by the parties involved, the city and county attorney
will file the abatement agreement with the court for approval. Where a defendant who
is entering into an abatement agreement is a non-involved owner, the court shall consider
the least restrictive terms that are necessary to protect the community against a
future public nuisance involving the motor vehicle.
(D)
Upon the court's approval of the abatement agreement, the court will enter a final
judgment closing the matter. If the motor vehicle is still closed or impounded, it
shall be released upon the payment of any outstanding impoundment and storage fees.
Failure to pay such fees before entry of final judgment shall not prevent entry of
a final judgment order. After entry of final judgment order, return of the motor vehicle
to the non-involved owner will be subject solely to the policies and procedures of
the impound facility where the motor vehicle is being stored.

(A)
Pursuant to Sections 10-27-040 or 10-27-050 , if the city and county attorney declines to submit the affidavit for court consideration
or if the court determines probable cause does not exist, the owner of a motor vehicle
impounded under this chapter may petition for a hearing in the Broomfield Municipal
Court. Such hearing must be requested within 14 days of service of the notice required
by Sections 10-27-040 or 10-27-050 .
(B)
Prior to the commencement of a hearing, the City in its sole discretion may give notice
in writing or on the record that the City intends to pay all impound and towing fees.
Such notice shall be binding on the City and no hearing on the issue of probable cause
shall be held.
(C)
Hearing:
(1)
The sole issue before the court at hearing shall be whether there was probable cause
to impound the vehicle in question. "Probable cause to impound" shall mean such a
state of facts as would lead a person of ordinary care and prudence to believe that
there were sufficient facts to grant legal authority under this chapter for the impoundment
of the vehicle.
(2)
The court shall conduct the hearing in an informal manner and shall not be bound by
technical rules of evidence.
(3)
The person demanding the hearing shall carry the burden of establishing that such
person has the right to possession of the vehicle and that the court has jurisdiction
over the matter. The City shall carry the burden of establishing that there was probable
cause under this chapter to impound the vehicle in question. The burden of proof shall
be by a preponderance of the evidence.
(4)
The court's decision as to probable cause shall be a final judgment of the Broomfield
Municipal Court.
(5)
Failure of the registered or legal owner, or their agent, to timely request or attend
a scheduled post-impoundment hearing shall be deemed a waiver of the right to such
hearing.
(D)
Reimbursement for Improper Impoundment:
(1)
At the conclusion of a hearing under this section, if the court finds that the City
failed to meet its burden under subsection (C) above, the City shall either fully
reimburse the owner of the impounded vehicle for all towing and storage fees paid
by the owner, or directly pay the towing and storage company for such accrued fees.
(2)
If the subject vehicle is towed and stored by a private towing and storage facility
designated by the City, the court may not waive fees associated with the towing or
storage of the vehicle unless authorized by the designated towing and storage company.
(E)
No vehicle impounded under this chapter shall be released to its owner until all towing
charges, storage charges and administrative fees incurred by the City have been reimbursed
to the City by the owner. If towing and storage fees are due to a private towing and
storage company designated by the City to perform such services, no vehicle impounded
under this chapter shall be released to its owner until all such fees have been paid
directly by the owner to such designated company.
(F)
The provisions and procedures in this section apply to vehicles impounded within the
City and County of Broomfield, by a Broomfield Police Officer, and stored at a facility
designated by the City and County of Broomfield. The Broomfield Municipal Court shall
only have jurisdiction to hear matters under this chapter when the motor vehicle was
physically impounded within the City and County of Broomfield by a Broomfield Police
Officer or other authorized City employee.
