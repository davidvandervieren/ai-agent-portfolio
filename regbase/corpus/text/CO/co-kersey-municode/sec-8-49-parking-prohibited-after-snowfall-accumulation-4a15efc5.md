machinery, and shall include, without limitation, automobiles, airplanes, trucks,
trailers, campers, motorcycles, motor scooters, snow machines, recreational vehicles,

(a)
If the town detects any alleged or suspected violation of a county or municipal traffic
regulation or traffic violation under state law through the use of an automated vehicle
identification system, then the town shall issue, or cause its vendor to issue, to
the registered owner of the motor vehicle involved in the alleged or suspected violation,
by first-class mail or personal service, a notice of violation.
(b)
The town may only issue a notice of violation for violations that occur:
(1)
Within a school zone;
(2)
Within a residential neighborhood;
(3)
Within a maintenance, construction, or repair zone designated pursuant to C.R.S. §

(4)
Along a street that borders a town park; or
(5)
Along a street, or portions of a street, which the town designates as an automated

(c)
The notice of violation must contain:
(1)
The name and address of the registered owner of the motor vehicle involved in the
alleged violation;
(2)
The license plate number of the motor vehicle involved in the alleged violation;
(3)
The date, time, and location of the alleged violation;
(4)
The amount of the civil penalty prescribed for the alleged violation;
(5)
The deadline for payment of the prescribed civil penalty and for disputing the alleged
violation; and
(6)
Information on how the registered owner may either dispute the alleged violation at
a hearing or pay the prescribed penalty.
(d)
To protest the notice of violation, the registered owner must request, in writing,
a hearing to dispute the alleged violation. The deadline to request a hearing to dispute
the notice of violation shall be 50 days after the date of the notice of violation.
The hearing shall be held within the municipal limits of the Town at a location determined
by the town. The town in its discretion may offer remote or virtual hearings. Each
hearing shall be presided over by an impartial hearing officer appointed by the town,
who shall establish procedures for the conduct of hearings, administer oaths, and
keep order. In a hearing the town shall not be permitted to require the registered
owner of the vehicle to disclose the identity of the driver of the vehicle who is
detected through the use of AVIS, but may require the registered owner to submit evidence
that the owner was not the driver at the time of the alleged violation. There shall
be rebuttable presumptions that (1) the registered owner was the driver at the time
of the alleged violation, and (2) that a posted speed limit constitutes the maximum
reasonable and prudent speed under normal driving conditions. The town shall have
the burden of proving the elements of a violation beyond a reasonable doubt, and the
protestant shall have the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence the
issue of who was driving and any affirmative defenses.
Notwithstanding the requirement that the town's burden of proof shall be beyond a
reasonable doubt, all hearings shall be civil and not criminal proceedings. At the
conclusion of each hearing, the hearing officer may issue a verbal decision or take
the matter under advisement; in either case, the hearing officer shall issue written
findings of fact, conclusions of law, and the judgment within 21 days after the hearing,
which shall be mailed to the protestant and the town by U.S. Mail, first class, postage
prepaid, and a copy electronically transmitted to the town manager.
