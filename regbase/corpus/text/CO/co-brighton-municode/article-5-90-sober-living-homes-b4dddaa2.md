Editor's note— Ord. 2435 §10, adopted October 19, 2023, repealed the former Art. 5-90, §§ 5-90-10—5-90-90,
and enacted a new Art. 5-90 as set out herein. The former Art. 5-90 pertained to group
homes and derived from Ord. 1391 §1(part), adopted 1991; Ord. 1589, adopted 1999;
Ord. 1597 §§1—6, adopted 1999; Ord. 1639 §2, adopted 2000; Ord. 2123 §7, adopted 2011.
A "sober living facility," "recovery residence," or "sober home" means any premises,
place, facility, or building that provides housing accommodation for individuals with
a primary diagnosis of a substance use disorder that is free from alcohol and nonprescribed
or illicit drugs; promotes independent living and life skill development; and provides
structured activities and recovery support services that are primarily intended to
promote recovery from substance use disorders.
Any sober living facility, sober home, or recovery residence operating within the
City must be certified by the State Department of Human Services, or its authorized
agent, or be charted by Oxford House or its successor organization, or have been operating
as a recovery residence in the City for thirty (30) or more years as of May 23, 2019.
Any City official may inspect the home and the facility must provide the official
with a copy of its certification or Oxford House charter to continue to operate. It
shall be unlawful to operate without State certification or an Oxford House charter.
(Ord. 2435 §10, 2023)
