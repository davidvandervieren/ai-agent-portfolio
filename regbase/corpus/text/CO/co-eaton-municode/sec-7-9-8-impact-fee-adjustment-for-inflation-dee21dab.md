The purposes and intent of this Impact Fee Ordinance is:
(1)
To establish uniform procedures for the imposition, calculation, collection and administration
of impact fees imposed on new land development;
(2)
To implement the goals, objectives and policies of the Town's Comprehensive Plan and
ensure that new land development contributes its fair share toward the costs of capital
improvements reasonably necessitated by such new land development;
(3)
To ensure that new land development is reasonably benefited by the capital improvements
provided with the proceeds of impact fees;
(4)
To ensure that all applicable legal standards and criteria are properly incorporated
into these procedures; and
(5)
To ensure that all applicable procedures and requirements of state law, including
Sections 29-1-801 through 29-1-804, C.R.S., and Sections 29-20-101 through 29-20-107
C.R.S., have been met.
( Ord. 660 §1, 2025)
The words or phrases used herein shall have the meaning set forth below, except as
otherwise indicated:
Applicant means any person who files an application for a building permit with the Town, or,
as the context provides, has obtained a building permit from the Town for new land
development. Applicant, where appropriate, includes the initial applicant's successor
in interest.
Building permit means any permit issued by the Town before building, land use or construction activity
may be initiated for new land development.
Capital improvement means an improvement, facility or equipment necessitated by new land development
that is related to a service that the Town provides and has an estimated useful life
of five (5) years or longer. In addition to others, capital improvements and expenditures
related thereto may be for any or all of the following: storm drainage and flood control
facilities; transportation and street; law enforcement, including lands, buildings
and vehicles; municipal facilities, including lands and buildings; and parks, recreation,
open space and trails An expenditure for a capital improvement includes amounts appropriated
in connection with the planning, design, engineering and construction of capital improvements;
planning, legal, appraisal and other costs related to the acquisition of land, financing
and development costs; the cost of compliance with purchasing procedures and applicable
administrative and legal requirements; and all other costs necessarily incident to
provision of the capital improvement.
Dwelling unit means one (1) or more rooms in a dwelling designed or used for residential purposes
and occupancy by one (1) or more persons for living and sleeping purposes and having
not more than one (1) kitchen. Dwelling unit shall not include those units designed primarily for occupancy for a period of less
than thirty (30) consecutive days.
a.
Single-family dwelling unit means a detached or attached residential building with each dwelling unit connected
to the Town's water system by a single water tap.
b.
Multi-family dwelling unit means a residential building containing multiple dwelling units connected to the
Town's water system by a single water tap shared among the dwelling units.
Impact fee means a fee relating to a capital improvement that is imposed on new land development
on a pro rata basis in connection with and as a condition of the issuance of a building
permit, is calculated to defray all or a portion of the cost of capital improvements
required to accommodate the new land development at Town-designated level of service
standards and reasonably benefits the new land development.
Impact Fee Ordinance refers to this Section IX.
Impact Fee Schedule refers to Appendix A, which is attached hereto and incorporated herein by reference.
Land development means any construction, reconstruction, expansion or conversion of a building, structure
or use, or any change in the use of any land, building or structure, that creates
additional demand for public services.
Multiple uses means a new land development consisting of both residential and nonresidential uses,
or one (1) or more different types of nonresidential use, on the same site or part
of the same new land development.
Town Administrator shall include the Town Administrator's authorized designee(s).

(a)
There are hereby established the following categories of impact fees: police facilities
impact fees; park facilities impact fees; public building facilities impact fees;
storm drainage impact fees; and street impact fees. The impact fees shall be imposed
as provided in this Impact Fee Ordinance and in the amounts set forth in the Impact
Fee Schedule. The Town Administrator is hereby delegated authority to implement the
provisions of this Impact Fee Ordinance and, except for appeals, render the decisions
required herein on behalf of the Town.
(b)
The following types of development shall be exempt from payment of all or certain
of the impact fees:
(1)
Reconstruction, expansion, alteration or replacement of a previously existing dwelling
unit that does not create any additional dwelling units;
(2)
Reconstruction, alteration or replacement of nonresidential development that does
not add square footage, unless the new nonresidential development increases the demand
for capital improvements for which particular impact fees are imposed;
(3)
Replacement of a building or structure that was destroyed or partially destroyed by
fire or other catastrophe with a new building or structure of the same size and use;
(4)
Construction of an unoccupied, detached accessory structure related to a dwelling
unit; unless the unoccupied, detached accessory structure increases the demand for
capital improvements for which particular impact fees are imposed;
(5)
Later stages of a development permit or approval process if impact fees have been
fully paid for such category of capital improvements at an earlier stage in the development
permit or approval process;
(6)
Land development for which a building permit has been issued prior to the effective
date of this Impact Fee Ordinance, except in the event of a change of use; and
(7)
A use, development, project, structure, building, fence, sign or other activity that
does not result in an increase in the demand for capital improvements for which a
particular impact fee is being imposed.
(c)
The applicant shall make a written claim for exemption, containing an explanation
for the basis of the request, on or before the applicant applies for the first building
permit for the proposed development that creates the obligation to pay the impact
fee. Any claim for exemption not made on or before that time shall be waived. The
Town shall determine the validity of a claim for exemption pursuant to the standards
set forth above.
(d)
In addition to the payment of impact fees, to obtain a building permit, an applicant
must satisfy all other applicable land use, zoning, planning, platting, subdivision
and other related requirements, standards and conditions required for issuance of
such building permit.
( Ord. 660 §1, 2025)
(a)
In general. Impact fees shall be calculated by the Town at the time of application for a building
permit and shall be paid by the applicant prior to the issuance of a building permit.
Impact fees shall be calculated based on the impact fee amount in effect at the time
of application for a building permit.
(b)
Calculation.
(1)
Upon receipt of an application for a building permit, the Town shall determine:
a.
Whether it is a residential or nonresidential use or contains multiple uses;
b.
The specific category of residential or nonresidential development, if applicable;
c.
If multi-family residential, the number of new dwelling units; and
d.
If nonresidential, the number of new or additional square feet of gross floor area
(rounded up to the nearest square foot) and the proposed use.
(2)
Upon receipt of an application for a building permit, the Town shall determine whether
the application is for a change in use. In such cases, the impact fee due shall be
based only on the incremental increase in the fee for the additional demand for capital
improvements resulting from the change in use.
(3)
After making the foregoing determinations, the Town shall calculate the applicable
impact fees pursuant to the terms of this Impact Fee Ordinance and the Impact Fee
Schedule.
(4)
If the type of land use proposed for land development is not expressly set forth in
this Impact Fee Ordinance, the Town shall:
a.
Identify the most similar land use type listed and calculate the impact fee based
on that land use; or
b.
Identify the broader land use category within which the specified land use would fit
and calculate the impact fee based on that land use category
(5)
An applicant may request a nonbinding estimate of impact fees due for a particular
land development at any time prior to issuance of a building permit by submitting
a request to the Town; provided, however, that such estimate may be subject to change
when a formal application for a building permit for land development is made. Such
nonbinding estimate is solely for the benefit of the prospective applicant and shall
in no way bind the Town nor preclude the Town from making amendments or revisions
to any provisions of this Impact Fee Ordinance or the Impact Fee Schedule.
(6)
The calculation of impact fees due from a multiple-use land development shall be based
upon the aggregated demand for each category of capital improvements generated by
each land use type in the land development.
(7)
The calculation of impact fees due from a phased land development shall be based upon
the demand generated by each specific land use within the phase of development for
which a separate building permit is requested.
(8)
Impact fees shall be calculated based on the impact fee amount in effect at the time
of application for a building permit or a change in use.
(c)
Offsets.
(1)
Offsets against the amount of an impact fee due from a land development may be provided:
(a) for contributions made or to be made in the future in cash; (b) by dedication
of land; (c) by the applicant's construction of all or part of a capital improvement
meeting or exceeding the demand generated by the new land development when the contribution
is determined by the Town to be an acceptable substitute for the cost of capital improvement
that is included in the particular impact fee; or (d) by other lawful means that are
acceptable to the Town.
(2)
The amount of offset, if any, shall be determined by the Town upon the applicant's
written request, containing an explanation for the basis of the request; provided,
however, that:
a.
The Town will make no reimbursement for excess contributions unless and until the
particular impact fee fund has sufficient revenue to make the reimbursement without
jeopardizing the continuity of the Town's capital improvements program; and
b.
The excess contribution may not be transferred or credited to any other type of impact
fees calculated to be due from that development for other types of capital improvements.
(3)
No offset shall be allowed unless the Town has approved the contribution or expenditure
before it is made.
(4)
Offsets for dedication of land or provision of capital improvements shall be applicable
only as to impact fees imposed for the same types of capital improvements that are
proposed to be dedicated or provided. Even if the value of the dedication of land
or provision of a capital improvement exceeds the impact fee due for the type of capital
improvement, the excess value may not be transferred to impact fees calculated to
be due from the applicant for other types of capital improvements. Offsets may be
transferred to other applicants within the same development for the same type of capital
improvement.
(d)
Collection.
(1)
The Town shall collect all applicable impact fees at the time of issuance of a building
permit unless the Town determines that:
a.
The applicant is entitled to a full offset; or
b.
The applicant is not subject to the payment of an impact fee; or
c.
The applicant has filed an appeal and provided a letter of credit or other surety,
in a form approved by the Town, in the amount of the applicable impact fees.
(2)
The Town shall collect an impact fee at the time of issuance of a building permit
even if impact fees were paid by the applicant at an earlier stage in the development
permit or approval process if the amount of the impact fees have increased since such
prior approval. The applicant shall only be liable for the difference between the
impact fees paid earlier and those in effect at the time of issuance of the subsequent
building permit.
( Ord. 660 §1, 2025)
(a)
Impact fee accounts. An impact fee account shall be established by the Town for each category of capital
improvements for which impact fees are imposed. Such account shall clearly identify
the category, account or fund for which the impact fee has been imposed. All impact
fees collected by the Town shall be deposited into the appropriate impact fee account,
which shall be interest-bearing. All interest earned on monies deposited to such account
shall be credited to and shall be considered funds of the account. The funds of each
such account shall be capable of being accounted for separately from all other Town
funds. The Town shall establish and implement necessary accounting controls to ensure
that the impact fee funds are properly deposited, accounted for and appropriated in
accordance with these provisions and any other applicable legal requirements.
(b)
Appropriation of impact fee funds. Impact fee funds may be appropriated for capital improvements and for the payment
of principal, interest and other financing costs on contracts, bonds, notes or other
obligations issued by or on behalf of the Town or other applicable local governmental
entities to finance capital improvements. Impact fees shall be appropriated only for
the particular category of capital improvements for which they were imposed and collected.
Impact fees shall not be appropriated for funding maintenance or repair of capital
improvements nor for operational or personnel expenses associated with the provision
of the capital improvements.
(c)
Procedure for appropriation of impact fee funds.
(1)
The Town shall each year identify capital improvement projects anticipated to be funded
in whole or in part with impact fees.
(2)
The Town Board may include impact fee-funded capital improvements in the Town's annual
budget and capital improvements program.
(3)
The Town Board may authorize impact fee-funded capital improvements at such other
times as may be deemed necessary and appropriate by the Town Board.
(4)
The Town Administrator shall verify that adequate impact fee funds are or will be
available from the appropriate impact fee account for the particular category of capital
improvements.
(d)
Refunds.
(1)
The following applicants may apply for a refund of impact fees paid:
a.
An applicant who has paid an impact fee for land development for which the necessary
building permit has expired or for which the building permit has been revoked prior
to construction; or
b.
An applicant who has paid an impact fee for new land development for which a building
permit has been issued and pursuant to which construction has been initiated, but
which construction is abandoned prior to completion and issuance of a certificate
of occupancy if the incomplete building is demolished pursuant to a Town-issued demolition
permit.
(2)
Refunds shall only be made to the applicant of the new land development that was proposed
or occurred.
(3)
A request for a refund shall be made in writing within sixty (60) days following expiration
or revocation of the building permit or abandonment of the construction and demolition
of the structure. Requests shall be in writing and shall include the following information:
(a) evidence that the requesting party is the applicant; (b) the amount of the impact
fees paid by capital improvements category and, if requested, receipts evidencing
such payments; and (c) documentation evidencing the expiration or revocation of the
building permit or demolition of the structure pursuant to a Town-issued demolition
permit. Failure to apply for a refund within sixty (60) days shall constitute a waiver
of entitlement to a refund.
(4)
Upon receipt of a complete request for a refund, the Town shall review the request
and documentary evidence submitted by the applicant, as well as such other information
and evidence requested by the Town, and make a determination as to whether a refund
is due. If a refund is due, the Town may, at its option, make the refund by direct
payment, by offsetting the refund against other impact fees due for the same category
of capital improvements for new land development on the same property, or by other
means subject to agreement with the property owner.
(5)
No interest shall be paid by the Town when providing a refund. A ten-percent administrative
fee shall be deducted from the amount of any refund granted and shall be retained
by the Town in the appropriate impact fee account to defray the administrative expenses
associated with the processing of a refund application

An applicant may submit a written request to the Town for exemption from or waiver

(a)
An applicant may appeal the following decisions to the Town Board: the applicability
of the impact fees to the land development; the amount of impact fees to be paid for
the land development; the availability or amount of an offset; the availability or
amount of a refund; the availability or amount of an exemption or waiver; or the rejection
in whole or in part of an independent impact analysis.
(b)
A written notice of appeal shall be filed with the Town Clerk within thirty (30) days
following the final decision that is being appealed. The notice of appeal shall state
with specificity the reasons for the appeal and shall contain such data and documentation
upon which the applicant/appellant seeks to rely.
(c)
Subsequent to receipt of a timely and complete notice of appeal, the Town Board shall
conduct a hearing. The burden of proof shall be on the applicant/appellant to demonstrate
that the decision of the Town Administrator is erroneous.
(d)
The filing of a notice of appeal shall not stay the imposition or the collection of
the impact fee as calculated by the Town Administrator prior to the issuance of a
building permit unless the notice of appeal is accompanied by a letter of credit or
other surety, in a form approved by the Town, in an amount equal to the impact fee
calculated to be due.
( Ord. 660 §1, 2025)
(a)
On January 1, 2026, and on January 1 of each year thereafter in which impact fees
are in effect, the Town Board may, at its discretion, adjust the amount of the impact
fees per dwelling unit for residential development and per square foot of gross floor
areas for nonresidential development to account for inflationary increases in the
cost of providing public facilities utilizing the most recent data from the Engineering
News Record construction cost index for the Denver Metropolitan Area.
(b)
The Town Board may amend the Impact Fee Schedule to adjust the impact fees as provided
herein by resolution.
