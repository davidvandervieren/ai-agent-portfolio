The purpose of this chapter is to promote the public health, safety, welfare, and
cultural well-being of the community by way of:
A.
The protection and preservation of the town's historical and cultural heritage through
historic landmark designations, by appropriate regulations;
B.
The establishment of a citizen committee designed and empowered to protect and preserve
items of historical value;
C.
The orderly and effective acquisition, preservation and management of all significant
historical documents, artifacts and structures;
D.
The regulation of alternation, relocation, removal or destruction of historically
significant sites or artifacts;
E.
The potential enhancement of property values;
F.
The encouragement and provision of educational opportunities to increase the appreciation
and understanding of the unique heritage of the town and surrounding area.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 1)
The board of trustees has established an historical preservation committee, per the
Home Rule Charter of the Town of Silt and is governed by the provisions of this code.
This committee shall have principal responsibility for matters of historical preservation
as set forth in this chapter.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2)
(Ord. No. 6-2016, § 1, 11-28-2016)
The historical presentation committee shall be composed of five members and shall
be the members of the town's planning and zoning commission, not including alternate
members. The board of trustees may appoint one ex officio member who is also a member
of the Silt Historical Society.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
(Ord. No. 6-2016, § 1, 11-28-2016)

to historical preservation committee appointment and terms of office, and derived

A quorum for the committee shall be a majority of the regular membership (excluding
vacant seats). A quorum is required for the committee to hold meetings and to make
decisions. A roll call vote shall be taken upon request by any member, and a tie vote
shall be deemed a denial of a motion or recommended action.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
The committee, if it so chooses, may elect one of its members by majority vote to
serve as chairperson to the committee, and one person as vice-chairperson. The members
so elected shall serve in these capacities for a term of one year, and may serve for
successive terms without limitation.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
The committee may hold regular monthly meetings, if necessary, and shall keep written
minutes of the commission's proceedings, to be forwarded to the board of trustees,
upon the committee's approval. The town shall schedule such meeting to be held preceding
or following a regularly scheduled planning and zoning commission meeting, or at another
time as agreed upon by the committee.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
(Ord. No. 6-2016, § 1, 11-28-2016)
The historical preservation committee shall:
A.
Adopt criteria for review of historic resources and for review of proposals to nominate,
alter, move, remove or demolish such resources. These criteria are subject to board
of trustee approval;
B.
Review resources nominated for a historical landmark and make recommendation to the
board of trustees, and if the board accepts the nomination, to accept such nomination
by ordinance;
C.
Review and make decisions on any alteration to a designated historic landmark;
D.
Review and make recommendations on any proposed moving, removal or demolition of a
historical landmark;
E.
Advise and assist owners of historic properties and/or potential historic properties
on the physical and financial aspects of preservation, renovation, rehabilitation
and reuse, including the nomination process;
F.
Develop and/or assist in public education programs about local history;
G.
Conduct or assist in conducting historical surveys concerning historic resources;
H.
Advise the board of trustees as necessary on matters related to preserving local history;
I.
Actively pursue financial assistance for preservation-related programs and activities.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
The committee may establish any additional rules of operation which are consistent
with this ordinance and the ordinance codes of the town.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
The historical preservation committee has no budget, nor will it receive funds from
the town unless the board of trustees so chooses to give funds to the committee. As
such, the committee is encouraged to seek funds by such means as it is legally able
to, be they donations, gifts, grants or other. The committee may not enter into any
financial contracts involving loans or grants, or any other legally binding agreement
as a legal representative of the town without prior approval by the board of trustees.
Gifts, donations and fundraising activities are exempt from board approval.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 2 (part))
1.
Pursuant to the procedures hereinafter set forth in this section, the board of trustees
of the town may by ordinance designate as a landmark any individual structure, other
feature or item, or an integrated group of structures and features on a single lot
or site within the legal boundaries of the town as having special historical or architectural
value to the community. Each designation ordinance shall include a description of
the characteristics of the landmark which justify its designation. Each designation
ordinance shall include the legal description and property boundary of the location
if applicable. The adopted ordinance shall be filed with the county clerk.
2.
The ordinance, if applicable, may outline any alterations or activities which are
either critical to the preservation or potentially damaging to the landmark.
3.
The property included in any such designation shall be subject to any restrictions
within this section, and eligible for any such incentive or financial programs developed
by the committee.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 3 (part)).
A.
Designation procedures. A property owner or a citizen may make a nomination for historical
designation by filing a written proposal with the town. Town staff shall review same
and then forward the nomination to the committee, with its recommendations for review
by the commission, using the nominating criteria in this chapter.
B.
Review With Owner(s) Consent.
1.
If the owner(s) consents, the committee shall hold a public meeting no more than sixty
days after receiving written consent for the nomination.
2.
Notice of this public hearing shall be posted at least once, fifteen days prior to
the actual meeting at city hall, as well as one other frequented public place. The
notice shall include time, date and location of the meeting, as well as a summary
of the nomination.
3.
At this meeting the committee, the owner(s) and any other interested or affected citizen
shall discuss the nomination. The committee shall modify, modify and approve or disapprove
the proposal at this time. Whether approved or disapproved, the proposal shall be
forwarded to the board of trustees along with any recommendations and comments of
the committee.
C.
Review Without Owner(s) Consent.
1.
If the owner(s) does not consent to the nomination, the committee will hold a public
hearing within sixty days whether or not it receives written notice of nonconsent.
2.
Notice of this public hearing shall be posted at least once, fifteen days prior to
the actual meeting at City Hall, as well as one other frequented public place. The
notice shall include time, date and location of the meeting, as well as a summary
of the nomination.
3.
In addition, the committee will mail written notice of the hearing to the property
owner(s) at least fifteen days prior to the hearing.
4.
Failure to send such notice to a property owner(s) where the address is unknown, or
not a matter of public record, or failure to actually receive the sent notice shall
not invalidate any proceedings or decisions in connection with the nomination or designation.
5.
At the public hearing, the committee, the owner, and any other interested or affected
citizen shall discuss the nomination. The committee shall modify, modify and approve
or disapprove the proposal at this time. Approval of a nonconsented proposal shall
require a unanimous vote of the committee members present. Whether approved or disapproved,
the proposal shall be forwarded to the board of trustees along with any recommendations
and comments of the committee.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 3 (part))
(Ord. No. 6-2016, § 1, 11-28-2016)
A.
All decisions required by the board in this chapter shall be pass or fail based on
a two-thirds majority quorum vote.
B.
Within thirty days after the date of any nomination referral or appeal to the board,
the board will hold a public hearing on the proposed designation. Notice of this public
hearing on the proposed designation. Notice of this public hearing shall be published
at least once fifteen days prior to the actual meeting in a locally distributed newspaper.
The notice shall include time, date and location of the meeting, as well as a summary
of the nomination. City hall will mail written, certified notice of the hearing to
the property owner(s) at least fifteen days prior to the hearing.
C.
Failure to send such notice to a property owner(s) where the address is unknown, or
not a matter of public record, or failure to actually receive the sent notice shall
not invalidate any proceedings or decisions in connection with the nomination or designation.
D.
At the conclusion of the public meeting, the board shall modify, modify and approve
or deny the designation. When a landmark has been designated by the board of trustees
as provided above, city hall will promptly notify the property owner(s) included therein
and shall cause a copy of the designating ordinance to be recorded with the county
clerk.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
A.
If a landmark is lawfully moved, removed or demolished, the owner can apply to the
committee for a revocation of designation.
B.
The committee shall support to revoke the designation if it determines that without
the structure or feature the site, as a whole, no longer meets the criteria standards
for designation. A copy of the revocation shall be forwarded to the board of trustees,
and if the board deems the revocation appropriate, shall adopt an ordinance to that
effect. Designation revocation ordinances are final.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
A.
Any citizen involved with, or any owner(s) of property affected by this chapter, may
appeal to the board concerning a decision by the committee by filing a notice of appeal
within ten days of the contested committee decision.
B.
The board may review any decision by the committee by serving written notice to the
committee within thirty days of the committee decision.
C.
Within sixty days of the original committee decision, unless an agreement is reached
prior between the property owner and the committee, the board shall review the appeal
and comment on the committee decision during a regular board meeting. The board will
at that time uphold or reverse the committee decision, with or without modification.
All decisions by the board and final.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
No disapproved proposal may be resubmitted for at least six months from the date of
the final decision of the council or committee involving the proposal.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
Amendments to a designation ordinance may be made to add features or property to the
landmark provided the owner(s) make a written request to the committee. After review,
the committee will forward the request with recommendations to the board. The board
will modify, modify and approve or deny the request during a regular meeting, and
shall cause the amendment to become effective by ordinance.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
A.
Designated landmark alterations. No person shall construct, alter, remove, move or
demolish a designated landmark without first obtaining the committee's written landmark
alteration decision, as well as all other applicable permits.
B.
Alterations on Proposed Landmarks. No person shall carry out or permit to be carried
out any new construction, alteration, removal, moving, or demolition of a proposed
landmark after the date the proposal application was given to the committee. No permission
to alter will be given while the proposal is pending.
C.
Alteration Review Period. The maximum review period for a proposed alteration is sixty
days from the date when the alteration proposal was submitted the committee. The committee
must make a decision within this time. Failure of the committee to make a determination
within this time frame constitutes approval of the proposed alteration, although not
for other alterations not specifically mentioned in the proposal.
D.
Alteration Exempted Conditions. Notice in this chapter shall be construed to prevent
any measures taken to abate the unsafe or dangerous condition of any landmark, if
that condition has been declared by the town to exist, and specific remedies have
been identified. This includes, but is not limited to, construction, alteration, removal,
moving or demolition of the landmark.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
(Ord. No. 6-2016, § 1, 11-28-2016)
A.
The board intends to preserve from deterioration due to deliberate or inadvertent
neglect any landmark, when that deterioration threatens the existence of the landmark.
No owner(s), lessee or legal occupant of any landmark shall fail to prevent significant
deterioration of the exterior or interior of that landmark, beyond the condition of
the landmark on the date when it effectively became a landmark by ordinance. To this
end all designation ordinances shall include the following:
1.
The committee will be allowed to inspect the landmark once per year. Upon the completion
of the inspection a written maintenance report shall be given to the owner(s), lessee
or occupant. If serious problems exist, the committee will ask the owner(s), lessee
or occupant to address the contents of the report to the committee within thirty days.
2.
If there is a visible serious external or internal deterioration, the committee will
immediately contact the owner(s) lessee or occupant. The committee must be allowed
to thoroughly inspect the landmark no later than ten days from the date of contact.
Regarding internal deterioration, if an inspection cannot take place in ten days,
the earliest possible date will serve as a reasonable date.
3.
The owner(s), lessee, or occupant may appeal the results of the committee inspection
to the board.
B.
Owner Notification. Before the town takes action for failure to maintain the landmark,
the committee shall first make every effort to notify the owner(s), lessee, or occupant
of the need to repair, maintain or restore the landmark. The committee shall assist
the owner(s), lessee or legal occupant in determining how to preserve the property,
and shall give them a reasonable time to perform such work.
C.
Hardship.
1.
The owner(s), lessee or occupant may request from the committee an extension due to
a time and/or economic hardship. If the owner is granted a time or economic hardship
extension, the original timeframe for completing the needed work may be extended by
the committee, the duration being at the discretion of the committee.
2.
If the owner(s), lessee, or occupant refuses to comply with this ordinance, they will
be issued a ticket, informing them they are in violation of a Silt ordinance, and
subject to all penalties outlined in this chapter as well as Chapter 1.12 of the Silt Municipal Code. In addition, the landmark designation may be revoked
in addition to any other applicable penalties.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4)
If the committee so chooses, they may establish a program to recognize outstanding
achievements or individual efforts in preservation. The committee, at their own expense,
may issue certificates or authorize plaques to be affixed to buildings or sites.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
No owner, lessee or legal occupant of any landmark shall fail to comply with all applicable
provisions of this section, or any other applicable ordinances of the town.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
No person shall violate or permit to be violated any of the requirements of Sections 2.40.130 through 2.40.210 . Violators, if found guilty, shall be subject to a fine as outlined in Chapter 1.12 of the Silt Municipal Code. In addition to those penalties, the violator(s) are subject
to the following penalties as well:
A.
Alterations to a landmark without approval will result in a one-year moratorium on
all building permits for the subject property.
B.
Moving or demolishing a landmark without approval will result in a five-year moratorium
on all moving, demolition or building permits for the property at the structure's
original location.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
As the character of Silt is such that historical districts are not possible; and as
the Silt Historical Park is the only one of its kind in the area and as it already
meets or exceeds the requirements put forth in this chapter; and the members of the
Silt Historical Society have agreed to the designation; the following applies to the
Silt Historical Park:
A.
Upon the adoption of the ordinance codified in this chapter, the Silt Historical Park
shall be considered the first designated landmark in the town, subject to all provisions
of this chapter.
(Ord. 20, Series of 1992 § 4 (part))
