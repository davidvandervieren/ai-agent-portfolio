A.
A major subdivision is one which includes six or more lots proposed for subdivision,
and for which the applicant may propose zoning into one of the town's existing zone
districts as enumerated in Title 17 of this Code, if existing zoning is not adequate. An applicant for a major subdivision
shall submit to the town the following applications in this order, receiving approval
from the board prior to elevation to the next application:
1.
Major subdivision sketch plan, in conformance with this title;
2.
Major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat, in conformance with this
title;
3.
Major subdivision final plan and final plat, in conformance with this title.
B.
A major subdivision planned unit development is one which includes four or more lots
proposed for subdivision, and for which the applicant proposes planned unit development
zoning. An applicant for a major subdivision planned unit development shall submit
to the town the following applications in this order, receiving approval from the
board prior to elevation to the next application:
1.
Major subdivision sketch plan, in conformance with this title and specifically Chapter 16.12 ;
2.
Major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat, in conformance with this
title and specifically Chapter 16.12 ;
3.
Major subdivision final plan and final plat, in conformance with this title and specifically Chapter 16.12 ;
C.
The town administrator or his designee, in his sole discretion, may allow for a combination
application of major subdivision sketch plan/preliminary plan (plat), or a combination
application of major subdivision preliminary plan (plat)/final plan (plat), if the
combined application includes conformance with all the requirements of this title,
and if the town finds that the combined application would be in the best interests
of the town. However, in this case, no decrease in land use application submittal
fees shall be allowed.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
The purpose of the major subdivision sketch plan is to provide a general concept of
the proposed subdivision for review by the town staff, the commission, the board and
the public. The intent is to identify the potential major problems and the overall
infrastructure feasibility of the request. Detailed engineering plans are not required
at this time.
B.
Application requirements. An applicant for major subdivision sketch plan shall provide
such written information on the land use application form and on forms provided by
the town, plus any other supplemental information needed to convey the information
to the town, including the following:
1.
Disclosure of ownership—A certificate (not older than ninety days) from a title insurance
company or attorney licensed in the state of Colorado, which shall set forth a legal
description of the property, the names of all owners of property included in the sketch
plan map and shall include a list of all mortgages, judgments, liens, contracts or
agreements of record in Garfield County regarding such property. If the certificate
of title discloses any of the above, the owners or holders of such mortgages, judgments,
liens, contracts, or agreements shall be required to consent to the application. If
the applicant for major subdivision sketch plan is not the property owner, then both
the applicant and the property owner shall sign the land use application and be subject
to all the provisions of this Code.
2.
A description of the proposed land use(s);
3.
A statement of the planning objective(s);
4.
A description of adjoining land use(s) and zoning;
5.
Existing and proposed zoning of the subject property;
6.
An estimate of proposed residential units and/or an estimate of population on square
footage of commercial area;
7.
The name and address of the individual who prepared the major subdivision sketch plan;
8.
The total area of the parcel;
9.
A statement as to how the development will be served by utilities;
10.
A general statement describing the geological characteristics of the land, soils types,
slope stability and floodplain information;
11.
Site plans and supporting documents. The major subdivision sketch plan shall be drawn
to a scale of one inch equals one hundred feet or larger and include the following:
a.
A vicinity map, drawn at a scale of one inch equals two thousand feet, showing the
general location of the land for consideration and the surrounding area within a one-mile
radius;
b.
The topography of the land;
c.
The location of the proposed land uses;
d.
The approximate location of proposed public or private open space areas;
e.
The location of existing or proposed water and sewer lines to serve the area;
f.
The proposed lot or block pattern and street layout;
g.
A provision for sufficient off-street parking and adequate school bus stops, the latter
for school district (Re-2) approval;
h.
Significant natural and manmade features on the site, such as streams, lakes, natural
drainage-ways; vegetation types including locations of wooded areas; existing buildings,
utility lines and easements, irrigation ditches, bridges and similar physical features;
and existing development on adjacent property.
12.
A general statement regarding the proposal for water rights dedication, including
the estimate of EQRs per day of water system requirements for proposed subdivision;
13.
Evidence of legal access from a town street, county road or state highway.
C.
Number of copies. The applicant for major subdivision sketch plan shall submit eighteen
copies of the major subdivision sketch plan and supplemental material as required
in this title, sized eight and a half inches by eleven inches for textual material
and eleven inches by seventeen inches for maps that will be included in the commission
public hearing packets. Additionally, four copies of all maps shall be submitted for
staff review, each measuring no less than twenty-four inches by thirty-six inches,
plus four copies of all other textual supplemental materials. The applicant for major
subdivision sketch plan shall submit additional copies as needed and as requested
by the planning department for the board hearing to consider the application.

A.
The applicant for major subdivision sketch plan, upon submittal of a complete application,
shall schedule with the planning department a staff-applicant conference. At this
conference, the applicant shall explain to staff the entire proposal, using all submittal
documents;
B.
Following the submittal of major subdivision sketch plan application and the staff-applicant
conference, the planning department shall have fifteen days to check the application
for completeness. This cursory review shall not be a review for strict conformance
with the Code related to the type of proposed development, zoning compliance, comprehensive
plan conformance, or conformance with the town's master plans. Town staff may notify
the applicant that a longer period of time is needed to check for application completeness,
and in such case, the planning department shall have an additional fifteen days to
check the application for completeness;
C.
If the application is incomplete, the planning department shall notify the applicant
in writing within fifteen days, and the applicant shall have no more than thirty days
to respond with additional submittal materials that make the application complete.
The town may grant another thirty-day extension, upon good reason therefore. If the
applicant is unable to complete the application within thirty days, or sixty days
if continuance is requested and granted, the application is deemed abandoned, and
the applicant shall submit a new application. In no case shall the applicant have
more than two continuances granted;
D.
If the application is complete, the planning department shall have no fewer than thirty
days, but no more than forty-five days to review the major subdivision sketch plan
application for Code compliance with this title and the provisions of this Code;
E.
Within fifteen days following the planning department's review of the application,
the town shall schedule a public meeting before the commission to consider the application,
using the following guidelines:
1.
Conformance to the town's comprehensive plan, as amended from time to time;
2.
Relationship of development to topography, soils, drainage, flooding, potential natural
hazard areas, and other physical characteristics;
3.
Availability of domestic water means of wastewater collection and treatment, access,
and other utilities and services;
4.
Compatibility with the natural environment, wildlife, vegetation and unique natural
features;
5.
Public costs, inefficiencies and tax hardships; and
6.
Conformance to the town's zoning code, if land has been previously annexed and zoned
within the town.

of the major subdivision sketch plan is continued), the commission shall recommend

B.
A full written description of the commission's recommendation on the application shall

recommendation for denial of the major subdivision sketch plan, the minutes of such
commission meeting shall be promptly delivered to the board for their consideration,

B.

discuss the application;
C.
If the board continues the public hearing, the application shall be placed on the
next regularly scheduled board agenda, if practicable, provided that the applicant
agrees to such placement;
D.
If the board does not act on the commission's recommendation, either by continuing
the application, approving, approving with conditions, or denying the application
within six months of the commission's recommendation, the major subdivision sketch
plan is deemed acceptable and the applicant may submit a major subdivision preliminary
plan and preliminary plat application in conformance with this title. The town and
the applicant may jointly agree to a different time period for board review;
E.
If the board approves or approves with conditions the application, such approval or
conditional approval shall last six months;
F.
The applicant may request that the board of trustees approve a thirty-day extension
to the major subdivision sketch plan at a regularly scheduled meeting, if requested
prior to the six-month deadline to submit a major subdivision preliminary plan. If
no extension is granted by the board, the sketch plan is deemed void.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Based on an opinion by a qualified professional engineer or engineering geologist
or other professional, the commission may reach a finding at the major subdivision
sketch plan hearing that the particular land is unsuitable for subdivision by reason
of one-hundred year flooding frequency, high water table, mudflow, rockslide or other
potential natural hazard, feature or condition likely to be harmful to the health,
safety or welfare of the town, its residents or future residents in the proposed subdivision.
In the case of land which is partially affected by any of the above features, the
commission may reach a finding at the major subdivision sketch plan meeting that the
land may be subdivided; provided, however, that the commission finds that the proposed
use of the lots will not be adversely affected by the hazard or special condition
through special mitigation.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The commission may reach a finding at the major subdivision sketch plan meeting that
particular land may be premature for subdivision when subdivision approval would create
growth patterns of such physical form and size that governmental inefficiencies, duplication
of municipal facilities, unnecessary public costs or financial burdens to the town
may result from providing the extension of public services, and planned support facilities
cannot be accomplished in a planned, orderly or efficient manner.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The purpose of the major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat is to provide
the necessary information to permit the town staff, the commission, the board and
the public to properly review the proposal and to resolve potential planning or engineering
details that may arise before the major subdivision final plan and final plat are
prepared. The major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat therefore require
extensive information and engineering studies.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)

within any extension thereof, an applicant shall submit a complete major subdivision

regarding the major subdivision sketch plan.
C.

land use application if required by this Code, at the time of submittal for major
subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat.

A.
Geology, soils, vegetation and street sections. An applicant for major subdivision
preliminary plan shall provide the following information from a registered professional
geotechnical engineer:
1.
Geological report. Geology-bedrock lithology and the stratigraphy of overlaying unconsolidated
materials shall be generally described and/or illustrated in sufficient detail to
indicate any potential development problems resulting from leaching of sewage, groundwater,
subsidence (setting), instability of road excavations and fills, expansive soils,
drainage, structural bearing strength for building foundations and streets and any
other condition pertaining to the site;
2.
Soils report. Soil types and their boundaries, based on the National Cooperative Soil
Survey, USDA, Soil Conservation Service, and a table of interpretations for included
soil types, with mapping and descriptions as necessary;
3.
Vegetation. Plant associations, including a description of adapted materials, mapped
and described following practices of the Soil Conservation Service. Major tree masses
shall be shown on the plan.
B.
Grading and street profiles. An applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan shall
provide the following information from a licensed Colorado civil or structural engineer:
1.
Cut/fill slopes. Generalized grading shall be indicated for all cut-and-fill slopes,
including existing contours, proposed contours, any proposed retaining structures
and indication of any slopes that exceed twenty percent.
2.
Grade relationships. Cross-sections shall be indicated at one hundred foot intervals
along the street alignment to illustrate grade relationships between proposed roads
and adjacent building sites or intended land uses;
3.
Street plan and profile. Street plan views with corresponding centerline profiles
of proposed streets shall be plotted for each street, at a horizontal scale of one
inch equals fifty feet, or less and an exaggerated vertical scale, with sufficient
detail to ensure that proposed streets meet gradient limitations established by this
title, and bear a logical relationship to the grade of existing public streets at
points of intersection. A typical cross-section shall be provided for each street
which includes the structural street section.
C.
Traffic study. An applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan shall provide a
traffic study prepared by a registered professional engineer specializing in traffic,
including the following information:
1.
Baseline data for each of the major roads that will be used to access the subdivision,
including major roads utilized off State Highway 6;
2.
Computations for the number and type of proposed vehicle trips per day, utilizing
the Institute of Transportation Engineers, Trip Generation, latest edition.
3.
Conformance with the recommendations of the Town's Access Control Plan and Colorado
Department of Transportation regulations and guidelines, as amended from time to time;
4.
Design parameters for all required improvements to any offsite roadway and/or intersection.
D.
Drainage study. An applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan shall provide
a drainage study prepared by a registered professional engineer specializing in drainage,
including the following information:
1.
Limits of all on-site and off-site tributary areas affecting the site;
2.
Computations of expected pre-development and post-development tributary flows using
the Rational or TR-20 method;
3.
A map of areas subject to inundation under the twenty-five year and one-hundred year
storm events.
4.
The design of drainage facilities to prevent the flooding of habitable structures
and/or damage to structures on and downstream of the site under the one-hundred year
storm event;
5.
The design of drainage facilities to significantly reduce the release of hydrocarbons
and debris from the site;
6.
The design of drainage facilities to remove or pass-through any mud or debris expected
to enter the site;
7.
Calculations, sizes and locations of all proposed drainage structures, including storm
sewers, culverts, bridges, swales, channels, ponds or other mitigation facilities;
8.
Easements for all proposed drainage structures and facilities.
E.
Utility plans. An applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan shall provide utility
plans prepared by a registered professional engineer, including the following:
1.
A municipal plan at a scale of one inch equals fifty feet, or less, showing the line
locations, sizes of the proposed water distribution and wastewater collection mains
within the proposed subdivision in relation to existing utilities, including any pump
stations or lift stations proposed;
2.
Profiles for wastewater collection mains at the same horizontal scale as the municipal
utility plan and an exaggerated vertical scale;
3.
A shallow utility plan at the same scale as the municipal utility plan showing the
underground distribution of electrical power, communications lines, cable television,
and natural gas.
F.
Water rights. An applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan shall provide an
historical use affidavit concerning the water rights proposed for dedication to the
town pursuant to this title. The historical use affidavit shall contain the following
information:
1.
The name(s) and address(es) of the owner(s) of the water rights proposed for dedication;
2.
A legal description of the land to be annexed, subdivided, re-subdivided and/or provided
with municipal water services;
3.
Existing and proposed use of property;
4.
The total number of acres to be annexed and/or subdivided, re-subdivided and/or provided
with municipal water services;
5.
The total number of acres presently being irrigated and/or intended to remain in irrigation;
6.
A copy of all decrees concerning all water rights appurtenant to the property and
all water rights proposed for dedication;
7.
A copy of any court decree, judgment, order or decision that affects the title or
possession of such water rights;
8.
A copy of the documents by which owner received title to the water rights appurtenant
to the property and/or proposed for dedication;
9.
A copy of all diversion records for the last six years of the water rights proposed
for dedication;
10.
The owner's statement as to the historic use of the water rights appurtenant to the
property and/or proposed for dedication.
11.
Disclosure of ownership—A certificate (no older than ninety days) from a title insurance
company or attorney licensed in the state of Colorado, which shall set forth a legal
description of the property, the names of all owners of property included in the preliminary
plan map and shall include a list of all mortgages, judgments, liens, contracts or
agreements of record in Garfield County regarding such property. If the certificate
of title discloses any of the above, the owners or holders of such mortgages, judgments,
liens, contracts, or agreements shall be required to consent to the application. If
the applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan is not the property owner, then
both the applicant and the property owner shall sign the land use application and
be subject to all the provisions of this Code.
G.
Copies. The applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary plat
shall submit thirty-six copies of the major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary
plat, including supplemental material as required in this title, sized eight and one-half
inches by eleven inches for textual material and eleven inches by seventeen inches
for any maps and plat documents that will be included in the commission hearing packet.

no less than twenty-four inches by thirty-six inches, plus four copies of all other
textual supplemental materials. The applicant for major subdivision preliminary plan
and preliminary plat, at his sole cost, shall submit additional copies as needed and

The above submittal requirements apply to major subdivision preliminary plan and preliminary
plat revisions and re-submittals. Further, applicant shall submit an electronic copy
of all materials.

A.
An applicant for a lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution shall conform to the
following:
1.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution describes the adjustment or dissolution
of one lot line;
2.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution would not result in the creation of
a lot or lots;
3.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution would not result in a lot or lots
that would require a zoning variance(s), or would make a non-conforming lot;
4.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution would not result in the creation of
any additional lot or lots which are capable of separate ownership and development
under the provisions of this Code;
5.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution would not result in the creation of
a lot or lots in a residential zone district on which the owner could construct more
residential dwelling units on the two affected lots after the lot line adjustment
or lot line dissolution than previously were allowed on the two affected lots prior
to the lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution;
6.
The lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution would not result in the encroachment
of a building.
B.
An applicant for a lot line dissolution shall sign a waiver that his single-family
home or commercial structure is proposed for placement over the lot line, thereby
eliminating that lot line, and creating no setback non-conformance.
C.
Boundary adjustment or lot line adjustment map indicating the following:
1.
An appropriate engineering scale;
2.
Entire area proposed for subdivision shown on one sheet if practicable, with no map
larger than twenty-four inches by thirty-six inches;
3.
Statement by the surveyor who prepared the lot line adjustment plat or lot line dissolution
plat that the plat meets requirements established by this Code and C.R.S. 38-51-102,
as amended, or any other applicable state standard;
4.
North arrow, graphic scale and date of survey;
5.
Departing property lines and owners of record of all parcels adjoining the affected
parcels;
6.
Adjacent street(s) providing primary access to affected lots;
7.
Existing easements for drainage, irrigation, access, utilities or other facilities,
with widths, dimensions and area of each easement;
8.
Lot area and dimensions of each lot;
9.
Existing perimeter lot lines shown in bold solid lines; existing internal lot lines
proposed for adjustment or dissolution shown in dashed lines; proposed lot lines shown
as un-bolded solid lines.
10.
List of names and addresses of the surface owners, mineral owners, and any lessees
of the mineral owners, as such names and addresses appear upon the county records;
11.
Name and address of any mortgagee of the property;
12.
Name and address of applicant for the land use;
13.
Name and address of property owner, if not the applicant for the land use;
14.
Name and address of surveyor who prepared the lot line adjustment plat or lot line
dissolution plat;
15.
Certifications as follows:
a.
Certificate of dedication and ownership;
b.
Mortgage or lien holder certificate;
c.
Clerk and recorder's certificate;
d.
Lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution staff certificate;
e.
Surveyor's certificate; and
f.
Title company or attorney certificate.
16.
Legal description of the property;
17.
A reference to any protective covenants applicable to the affected lots.
D.
An applicant for a lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution shall submit to the
town an application in a form acceptable to the town, and at a minimum, shall include
the following:
1.
Statement detailing the proposed lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution;
2.
Statement regarding the reason(s) for the lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution
request; and
3.
Statement regarding conformance with this title.
E.
The town administrator, or his designated representative, shall, within fifteen business
days of the receipt of a lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution plat, review
the materials with other town staff to determine whether the materials meet all technical
requirements and are complete under the provisions of this title.
F.
If the town administrator, or his designated representative, determines that the application
for lot line adjustment or lot line dissolution complies with this title, in his sole
discretion, he shall render a decision on the application within thirty days and shall
sign the lot line adjustment plat or the lot line dissolution plat, upon the applicant's
submittal of any required changes (if any) to the lot line adjustment plat or the
lot line dissolution plat.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
