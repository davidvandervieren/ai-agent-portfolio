A.
1.
"Availability fee" means the minimum monthly service fee the town will charge every
owner of a building lot within town limits by which town water pipes or infrastructure
pass, whether or not such owner has purchased or reserved a tap for town water services.
The availability fee shall be assessed in addition to any applicable town water service
rates. The town eliminated availability fees through Ordinance No. 15 Series 2009,
effective January 14, 2010.
2.
"Bulk rate" means the charge for water dispensed through town-metered facilities into
private containers, tanks, hoses or other attachments or means of a nonpermanent nature,
and transported by the user to the place of use.
B.
"Carrying water rate" means the rate charged for water dispensed from an outside tap
located on the landowner's property for domestic use inside the dwelling unit or other
establishment which exists on the same property that the outside service tap is located
upon.
C.
"Church" means a building or part thereof, whose primary function is that of a place
of worship.
D.
1.
"Consumer" means any person, partnership, corporation, or any other legal entity,
public or private, their lessees and tenants, supplied with water for domestic, commercial
or any other use.
2.
"Bulk consumer" means any person, partnership, corporation, or any other legal entity,
public or private, their lessees and tenants, supplied with water for commercial uses
whereby domestic water is a main processing ingredient and/or service. The use shall
not constitute a rather consumptive use nor shall the use produce wastewater that
has substantial amounts of other compounds associated (i.e., oil, grease, food or
harsh chemicals).
E.
"Dwelling unit" means a building or portion thereof providing complete and permanent
living facilities for one family, containing cooking, living, sleeping and sanitary
facilities and having a separate entrance.
F.
"Fixture unit" means the standard definition for fixture unit, as found in the United
States of America Standards Institute (USASI), National Plumbing Code, 1955.
G.
"Lead income recipient" means the person whose gross income under Section 61 of the
Internal Revenue Code, 1976, exceeds the gross income of any other person who resides
within the dwelling unit.
H.
1.
"Raw water" means any water which has not been treated by or under the supervision
of the town to make it fit for human consumption. Raw water includes irrigation water,
water pumped from wells and water in streams.
2.
"Reserved service" (also known by past practice as "stand-by") means the water user
has paid the system improvement fee but is not yet tapped onto the system and/or not
currently using water.
I.
"Service connection" means the pipeline extending from the water main to the curb
box or the edge of the dedicated street or road, or the edge of the easement in which
the water main is laid; provided, however, that where there is no street, road or
easement, the service connection shall not extend more than forty feet from the main.
J.
"Service pipe" means the water line extending from the curb box or end of the service
connection to the place of use.
K.
"Treated water" means water treated by the town or under supervision of the town to
conform to applicable state and federal standards for human consumption.
L.
"Water main" means a principal artery of the water system to which service is or may
be connected.

The town may, from time to time, allow for the sale of water from a hydrant for purposes
of construction or commercial activity. In such case, the town shall charge a fee

1.
Consumers requesting to purchase water from a fire hydrant shall make application,
on a form provided by town, and pay the applicable fee and deposit, as set by the
board annually (or more often as necessary) by resolution, prior to making connection
with the fire hydrant. The deposit shall be returned to the consumer in a reasonable
amount of time, once the town inspects and discovers that consumer has not damaged
the fire hydrant. If damage is done to the fire hydrant, the town shall use the deposit
to pay for such repairs/replacement, including labor, and shall promptly return to
the consumer any funds not expended.
2.
Within two business days of application, the town administrator or his/her designee
shall determine the appropriateness of the town's selling of water from a hydrant,
and if so, shall determine, in its sole discretion, which hydrant may be used.
3.
Consumers shall submit records monthly on the 20th of the month of the actual amount
of water taken from the fire hydrant, and shall promptly pay the fee due.
4.
Consumers shall use only two or two and one-half inch gate valves for flow regulation.
Consumer's truck must have an air gap. Consumer shall use only town-approved fire
hydrant wrench and shall use such a valve-opening technique as to not damage fire
hydrant.

The upper pressure overlay zone is created as a means for the town to plan for and
collect additional fees for construction of pumping stations, potable water storage
facilities and telemetry systems, to serve those properties that, because of continuing
development in the town north of the Colorado River, will be inadequately served by
existing lower zone facilities, both in terms of quantity or pressure of water delivered
and the ability of the system to deliver potable water by gravity.
A.
The upper pressure overlay zone includes all developable lands generally located above
an elevation of 5,560 feet mean sea level and below 5,760 feet mean sea level, within
the town north of the Colorado River, as shown on a USGS map. This map generally depicting
the upper pressure overlay zone is on file and available for review during normal
business hours at the town clerk's office at town hall.
B.
The town administrator, in consultation with the town public works director and the
town's engineer, shall make the final determination of whether a specific parcel of
land is located within the upper pressure overlay zone and must receive water from
the upper pressure overlay zone storage tanks.
C.
The upper pressure overlay zone impact fee is a separate and additional charge to
the residential water system development fee established by this code; this fee shall
be collected prior to building permit issuance for any new residential construction
for which additional burden is put on the town's water storage facility.
D.
The upper pressure overlay zone impact fee shall be set by the board annually (or
as more often as necessary) by resolution.
E.
Developable lands above or near elevation 5,760 and other fringe areas may be inadequately
served by existing upper pressure zone facilities. Such lands may be subject to additional
impact fees, as determined by the town administrator.

any such action, the town shall be entitled to recover its costs and attorneys' fees
from such individual/property owner, which costs and attorneys' fees shall become
part of the foreclosure judgment.
