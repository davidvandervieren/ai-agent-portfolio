For the purposes of this chapter, the following terms have the following meanings:
A.
"Abandon" means that an owner or other person responsible for its care of custody
has left the animal without making effective provisions for its proper care.
B.
"Aggressive animal" means any animal that, without provocation, threatens to attack
a human being or domestic animal; or without provocation, chases or approaches a person
upon the streets, sidewalks, or other areas open to the public, in a menacing fashion,
or has a known propensity, tendency, or disposition to attack without provocation;
or an animal causing an injury to a person or domestic animal that is less severe
than a bodily injury or serious injury, including slight puncture wounds, slight abrasions,
or other superficial wound caused by the animal's teeth or claws.
C.
"Animal" means any live non-human creature, domestic or wild, excluding fish.
D.
"Bodily injury" means any physical injury that results in severe bruising, severe
muscle tears, or severe skin lacerations requiring professional medical treatment
or any physical injury that requires sutures, or corrective or cosmetic surgery.
E.
"Dwelling unit" means any structure or part thereof which is occupied as the living
quarters of a single-family or housekeeping unit.
F.
"Habitual offender" is an animal or owner who has more than two convictions of any
section of this chapter.
G.
"Inoculated," "inoculation," "vaccination," or "vaccination for rabies" means a Colorado
licensed veterinarian administering a shot or medication for the rabies virus in an
animal.
H.
"Mistreatment" includes every act or omission which causes, or unreasonably permits
the continuation of, unnecessary or unjustifiable pain or suffering of an animal.
I.
"Neglect" includes failure to provide food, water, protection from the elements, opportunity
for exercise, or other care normal, usual, and proper for an animal's health and well-being.
J.
"Owner" or "owning" shall mean any person who controls, keeps, or purports or is deemed
to have control over any animal; the person named on the licensing records of any
animal as the owner; the occupant of the premises where the animal is usually kept
if such premises are other than the premises of the owner as shown on the licensing
record; or any person in possession of, harboring, feeding, or allowing any animal
to remain about their premises. The parent or guardian of an owner under 18 years
of age shall be deemed the owner of an animal, as defined herein. If an animal has
more than one owner, all such persons are jointly and severally responsible and liable
for the acts or omissions of an owner under this chapter even if the animal was in
the possession of fewer than all owners or under the control of a keeper at the time
of the offense.
K.
"Running at large" means an animal away from the premises of the owner, possessor,
custodian or supervisory controller and not on a secure leash or under the immediate
control of such owner, possessor, custodian or supervisory controller.
L.
"Serious injury" means bodily injury, which, either at the time of the actual injury
or at a later time, involves a substantial risk of death, a substantial risk of serious
permanent disfigurement, a substantial risk of protracted loss or impairment of the
function of any part or organ of the body, or results in breaks, fractures, or burns
of the second or third degree.
M.
"Vicious animal" means any animal that:
1.
Causes a bodily injury or serious injury to a person or domestic animal;
2.
Has a previous, aggressive animal conviction and continues to engage in behavior that
poses a threat to public safety as described in the definition of "aggressive animal";
3.
Has been determined by another court of record within the State of Colorado to be
an aggressive animal;
4.
Has engaged in or been trained for animal fighting as described and prohibited in
C.R.S. § 18-9-204; or
5.
Has demonstrated a history of behavior that would cause a reasonable person to believe
that the animal may inflict bodily injury or serious injury upon any person or domestic
animal.
N.
"Pot-bellied pig" is a dwarf swine breed between 70 and 150 pounds, with a straight
tail, potbelly and swayback.

A.
Any person having knowledge that an animal other than a rodent, rabbit, bird or reptile
has bitten a human shall immediately report the incident to the town police. Every
physician or other medical practitioner who treats a person for such bites shall,
within 12 hours, report such treatment to the town police, giving the name and precise
location of the bitten person and such other information as the officer may require.
B.
Any dog or cat which has bitten a person or is suspected of having rabies must either
be observed for a period of ten days from the date of the bite or analyzed for rabies
virus by a laboratory. During the ten-day observation period, no rabies vaccine shall
be administered to the animal. Ferrets, potbellied pigs, wolf hybrids, wolves and
other wildlife which have bitten a person must be observed for a period of not less
than 30 days or tested for rabies if required by Colorado Division of Wildlife or
Department of Public Health and Environment regulations. The procedure and place of
observation or analysis shall be designated by the investigating officer or responsible
agency. If the animal is not confined on the owner's premises, confinement shall be
by impoundment in an animal shelter or at any veterinary hospital of the owner's choice.
Such confinement shall be at the expense of the owner. Stray animals whose owners
cannot be located shall be confined as designated by the town. The owner of any animal
that has been reported to have inflicted a bite on any person shall on demand produce
said animal for impoundment, as prescribed in this section, or for laboratory analysis.
Refusal to produce said animal constitutes a violation of this section, punishable
as a municipal offense, and each day of such refusal shall constitute a separate and
continuing violation.
C.
It shall be unlawful for any person to kill, remove or release any animal which has
been confined, impounded or is in the custody of the town or its agents, as authorized
under this chapter, without the consent of the town or the impounding agency.
D.
For the purposes of this section, rabies confinement at owner's residence shall mean
that the animal is kept inside a secure building or is within a fence or other enclosure
which limits the animal to a particular premise for a ten-day period, so that the
animal cannot come into contact with people or animals outside of the immediate family
household. During such period of confinement, the animal must not be confined or unsupervised
without being on a leash and handled by a person capable of physically restraining
the animal. The intent of this section is to prohibit nose-to-nose mucus membrane
contact with another animal or contact with a person. As a result, the confined animal
should not be permitted to approach a fence which also constitutes the perimeter of
the owner's property if such proximity to the fence could potentially produce contact
with another animal or person. Restraint by solely a tether is insufficient restraint.
If such animal is otherwise found outside the owner's residence during the confinement
period, it shall be taken and confined at the animal shelter or at a veterinary hospital
of the owner's choice, at the expense of the owner, for the remainder of the confinement
period.
E.
If any animal has been bitten by another animal suspected to have rabies, the owner
of such animal exposed to rabies shall report such fact to town police. The town police
shall have the power, in the officer's discretion, to have the animal suspected of
having rabies or of being exposed to rabies removed from the owner's residence to
a veterinary office or hospital and placed under observation for a period of up to
six months at the expense of the owner, provided that the owner may elect to have
such animal destroyed in lieu thereof.
F.

by the state department of health for the particular species of animal that was bitten,
it shall be summarily destroyed. If the animal has been in contact with another animal
or human, appropriate testing shall be performed by a certified laboratory to determine
rabies contamination at the expense of the owner.

Affirmative defense. It shall be an affirmative defense to the violation of this Section 6.04.180 that:
1.
The animal is used by a law enforcement officer while the officer is engaged in the
performance of official duties;
2.
At the time of the aggressive or vicious behavior against a domestic animal, said
domestic animal was at large and entered upon the property of the owner and the threat,
behavior, or attack began, but did not necessarily end, upon such property;
3.
At the time of the aggressive or vicious behavior against a domestic animal, said

4.
At the time of the aggressive or vicious behavior against a person, the victim was

8.
At the time of the aggressive or vicious behavior, the victim was attempting to aid
the animal when it was injured;
9.
At the time of the aggressive or vicious behavior, the victim was not on the victim's
property and attempting to capture the animal in the absence of the owner, with the
exception of a peace officer, firefighter, authorized town official, or other code
enforcement officer in the performance of his or her duties;

A.
Owner responsibility and liability. Any owner of an aggressive or vicious animal shall
be jointly and severally responsible with all owners of such animal for compliance
with the requirements of this section. All violations of this title are strict liability
offenses.
B.
Requirements for possession of an aggressive animal. It shall be unlawful for any
person to own, possess, keep, harbor or have custody or control of an aggressive animal,
except in compliance with all of the following requirements:
1.
The owner(s) shall be 18 years of age or older;
2.
The owner(s) shall not permit an aggressive animal to be outside a proper enclosure
on the owner's property unless such animal is under the physical control of a responsible
person and restrained by a leash not exceeding four feet in length;
3.
The aggressive animal and owner(s) complete a socialization and/or behavior program
approved by the municipal court;
4.
The aggressive animal shall be spayed or neutered by a licensed veterinarian or a
licensed animal shelter;
5.
The aggressive animal shall be permanently identified through implantation of a microchip
containing owner identification by a licensed veterinarian or a licensed animal shelter.
The microchip information must be registered with the appropriate company responsible
for maintaining such information for the microchip;
6.
The aggressive animal shall be properly licensed with the town;
7.
The owner of the aggressive animal shall notify the town in person or by telephone
as soon as practicable but not later than one hour after the owner's knowledge of
the occurrence of either of the following events:
a.
The animal has escaped or has otherwise ceased to be in the custody of the owner for
any reason, unless the owner knows such animal to be physically secured and restrained
or confined in the custody of another responsible person; or
b.
The animal has attacked a person or domestic animal.
8.
The owner of an aggressive animal shall notify the town in person or by telephone
within 24 hours of the occurrence of any one of the following events:
a.
The animal's owner has moved into town from another jurisdiction;
b.
The animal has been sold, given, or otherwise transferred to the ownership or possession
of another person, including the name, address, and telephone numbers of the new owner
and the effective date of the transfer; or
c.
The animal has died.
C.
Requirements for possession of a vicious animal. It shall be unlawful for any person
to own, possess, keep, harbor, or have custody or control of a vicious animal, except

1.

2.
The owner of a vicious animal shall not permit the vicious animal to be outside a
proper enclosure on the owner's property unless such animal is caged or under the
physical control of a responsible person and properly muzzled. The muzzle shall be
made in a manner that will not cause injury to the animal or interfere with its vision
or respiration, but shall prevent it from aggressive and dangerous behavior; and
D.

A.
No owner of an animal shall fail to prevent it from disturbing the peace and quiet
of any other person by loud and persistent barking, baying, howling, yelping, whining,
or other audible sound in an excessive, continuous or untimely fashion, whether the
animal is on or off the owner's premises.
B.
The provocation of an animal whose noise is complained of is an affirmative defense
to any charge for violation of subsection (A) of this section.
C.
Any owner of an animal which is alleged to have disturbed the peace and quiet of another
person may be charged with a violation of subsection (A) of this section regardless
if the owner has received a prior written or verbal warning.
D.
The warning process to be employed prior to a charge being instituted for a violation
of subsection (A) of this section shall be substantially as follows:
1.
A police officer or animal control officer may issue a warning after receiving a complaint
of a disturbance, or upon observing (hearing) a violation.
2.
Two or more complaints must clearly identify the complainant by stating his or her
name, address and telephone number. The complainants shall further state, if known,
the name of the animal's owner, the owner's address and telephone number, a description
of the animal, description of the offense, the date, time, place and duration of the
offense. These complaints need not be from separate individuals.
3.
A record or incident report shall be kept of any such complaints and investigation.
4.
The warning shall state that complaints have been received, recite the date of the
alleged offense, and conclude that the owner's animal may have disturbed the peace
or other individuals. The warning shall advise the animal owner of the possible penalties
for a violation of this section and advise the owner that the next complaint may result
in a summons being issued against the owner. The warning shall be identified as being
issued by any police or animal control officer empowered by the town to enforce the
provisions of this title.
5.

The purpose of this chapter is to establish certain requirements for the keeping of
bees with the intention to avoid conflicts between beekeepers and non-beekeepers,
and between bees and other animals. Further, it is the intention of this Code to provide
guidelines for good beekeeping according to industry standards so as to avoid the
unnecessary disposal of bee populations that are not properly maintained.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
For purposes of this chapter, the following definitions apply:
A.
"Apiary" shall mean a physical location where one or more colonies of bees and their
hives are kept by humans for purposes of education, pollination, production and/or
observation.
B.
"Bee" or "honeybee" shall mean either a honeybee or a bumblebee when kept by a human
beekeeper for purpose of education, pollination, production and/or observation. In
the case of honeybees, the species shall be Apis mellifera, known as the common domestic
honeybee, and includes all life stages.
C.
"Beekeeper" shall mean any person desiring to keep beehives within the town on his/her
property within the town, properly following the provisions of this chapter.
D.
"Beekeeping equipment" shall mean anything used in the operation of the apiary, such
as hive bodies, supers, frames, top and bottom boards and extractors.
E.
"Colony" or "beehive" or "hive" shall mean a settlement or aggregate of bees consisting
of a queen, workers and drones, including brood, bombs, honey and the receptacle inhabited
by the bees. A wild honeybee colony can be in a hive or as a swarm, but for purposes
of this Code, honeybees shall be kept in movable frame hives.
F.
"Flyway barrier" shall mean a natural or man-made obstacle causing the bees to go
up after exiting the hive. Barriers may be fences, walls, or shrubs, typically six
feet or more in height.
G.
"Swarm" shall mean a natural behavior exhibited by honeybees which aggregate together,
moving en masse to or from a location.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
Any person desiring to keep beehives within the town must first obtain a special use

safety and well-being, up to and including destruction of the beehive(s) and revocation

(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
Any applicant for beehives within the town shall conform to the following regulations
regarding the maintenance and location of beehives.
A.
Each beekeeper shall ensure that bees are kept in Langstroth-type beehives and shall
be maintained in good condition, free of dirt and debris, according to good beekeeping
practices in the industry.
B.
Each beekeeper shall ensure that a convenient source of fresh water is available at
all times so that the bees are discouraged from congregating at swimming pools, hose
bibs, pet water bowls, birdbaths, or other water sources where bees may interact with
humans and/or domestic pets.
C.
Each beekeeper shall take beekeeping classes, if available, or shall instruct himself
on the current practice of beekeeping. Town may request proof of classes and/or self-instruction.
D.
Each beekeeper shall actively maintain beehives. If beekeeper does not actively maintain
beehive(s), the town administrator or his designee, in his sole discretion, may cause
the dismantling of the beehives, disposal of the bees and/or the revocation of the
special use permit.
E.
Each beekeeper shall only harbor gentle bees. In the event that bees repeatedly (more
than two times) exhibit aggressive behavior characteristics, beekeeper shall remove,
destroy or re-queen the beehive. Since there are weather or other circumstances in
which bees may exhibit temporary defensive behavior, the town administrator or his
designee has sole discretion regarding the removal, destruction or re-queening of
a beehive.
F.
In addition to compliance with the requirements of this section, no beekeeper shall
harbor bees or keep beehive(s) that cause unhealthy conditions, or that interfere
with the normal use and enjoyment of human or animal life of others, any public property
or property of others.
G.
Each beekeeper shall locate beehive(s) in a manner which is safe for bees and the
community.
H.
Each beekeeper shall ensure that the natural flight path of the bees is not directly
onto the adjoining or adjacent property. If the beekeeper locates the beehive(s) within
20 feet of the property lines, he shall install a six-foot high privacy fence, closed
hedge, building or other solid flyway barrier, if allowed by the zoning and/or building
code. If construction or installation of such a solid flyway barrier is not possible
by zoning and/or building code, homeowners' association covenant or other legal reason,
the beekeeper shall relocate beehive(s) in order to be in compliance with this section.
I.
Each beekeeper shall ensure that beehive(s) and any other related structures that
form an apiary, are located in the rear yard, and in no case less than 40 feet from

Each beekeeper shall ensure that beehive(s) are located a minimum of 20 feet from
dwellings, porches, gazebos, decks, swimming pools, stationary play equipment and
any other habitable area on any adjoining and/or adjacent lots.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
The keeping of any apiary within the town which is not in compliance with this code
and this chapter is prohibited. Any bee colony not residing in a hive structure intended
for beekeeping, or any swarm of bees, should be reported to the town. Any colony residing
in a standard or homemade hive which, by virtue of its condition, has obviously been
abandoned or improperly maintained by the beekeeper is prohibited. The beekeeper shall
have 30 days following written notice from the town in order to bring the beehive(s)
into compliance. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the town administrator or his designee,
in his sole discretion, may cause the removal, destruction or re-queening of a beehive.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
In order to ensure that beehive(s) are in compliance with this section and that there
are no hazards or other issues present, an annual inspection of the licensed premises
may occur. Town administrator or his designee shall contact beekeeper, if possible,
and schedule a time for the inspection. If town administrator, or his designee, is
unable to contact the beekeeper, the town administrator, or his designee, has full
authority to inspect beehive(s) at the time of his choosing. Town may inspect the
beehive(s) or may contract with a professional knowledgeable of industry standards,
in order to determine compliance with this section.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012)
The town may issue a citation to a violator of Chapter 6.16 (beekeeping) in accordance with Chapter 1.12 of this code.
(Ord. No. 25-2012, § 1, 10-8-2012; Ord. No. 6-2016, § 4, 11-28-2016)
