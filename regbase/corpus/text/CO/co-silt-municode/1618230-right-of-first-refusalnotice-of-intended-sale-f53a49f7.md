The ordinance codified in this chapter shall be known and may be cited as the "Town
of Silt Water Rights Dedication Ordinance."
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The intent of this chapter is to require the dedication of adequate water rights to
satisfy both irrigation and domestic needs of new customers prior to the extension
of treated water service to new customers; to thereby assure an adequate and stable
supply of both potable and non-potable water to the town service area; to prevent
the abandonment of water rights to the detriment of the town; to ensure the financial
stability of the town water utility; and to promote the general welfare of the public.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
This chapter, in part, provides a supplemental requirement for annexation and development
as provided by state statutes and also supplemental requirements to any and all subdivision
requirements of the town, and does not eliminate, modify, or replace any requirements
set forth in those statutes or ordinances.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
As used in this chapter, unless the context otherwise requires:
1.
"Annexation" means the act of attaching, adding, joining, or uniting a parcel of land
to the town.
2.
"Appurtenant" means belonging to, accessory or incident to, adjunct, appended, or
annexed to.
3.
"Conveyance of water rights" means the legal process by which legal title to the water
rights to be dedicated is transferred to the town by deed, stock assignment, or other
appropriate instrument.
4.
"Dedication" means an appropriation of an interest in water to some public use, made
by the owner, and accepted for such use by or on behalf of the public, and accomplished
by means of a transfer of water rights.
5.
"Engineering report" means a written report and analysis prepared by a registered
professional engineer on behalf of the new user. Such analysis and report shall include,
but is not limited to, the needs, demands, and consumptive use burdens for all proposed
uses occasioned by the new user as a result of the proposed annexation, subdivision,
replatting, or water extension, as well as the historic consumptive use of water on
the property proposed to be served by the town's water system.
6.
"Equivalent residential unit" (EQR) means the volume of water that is the sum of the
"domestic EQR" and the "irrigation EQR," as follows:
a.
"Domestic EQR" means the volume of potable water consumptively used indoors in any
one given year by the average single-family residential unit housing a statistical
average of three and one-half persons. The demand for water represented by one domestic
EQR assumes (i) an annual delivery requirement of no more than 0.40 acre feet, (ii)
consumptive use of no more than 0.02 acre feet (five percent of the delivery requirement
- See Ord. 3, Series of 2008), (iii) a peak monthly water demand of no more than eleven
thousand gallons, (iv) a peak daily demand of no more than three hundred fifty gallons,
and (v) return of wastewater to the Colorado River by the town's municipal wastewater
treatment facilities or another wastewater treatment facility of equal efficiency.
The consumptive use of all other domestic water uses is considered to bear the same
ratio to the consumptive use of an average single-family residence as the EQR value
assigned to that use in the table of EQRs referenced in Title 13 , bears to the EQR value assigned to the average single-family residence in the table
of EQRs.
b.
"Irrigation EQR" means the volume of non-potable water consumptively used to irrigate
no more than three thousand five hundred square feet of outdoor lawn or garden area,
plus trees and shrubs, between approximately May 1 and September 30 of any given year.
The demand for water represented by one irrigation EQR assumes (i) an annual delivery
requirement of no more than 0.2633 acre feet, (ii) consumptive use of no more than
0.2501 acre feet (ninety-five percent of the delivery requirement), (iii) a peak monthly
water demand of no more than fourteen thousand three hundred gallons, (iv) a peak
weekly demand of no more than three thousand two hundred sixty-nine gallons, and (v)
delivery of such water exclusively through the town's non-potable irrigation water
distribution system between the above referenced dates.
c.
In the event the proposed water use is inconsistent with the domestic EQR assumptions
or the irrigation EQR assumptions set forth above, the town shall have sole and exclusive
discretion to determine whether the basic dedication requirement should be increased
or decreased, on a case-by-case basis.
7.
"Historical use affidavit" means a document which sets forth the following information
concerning the water rights proposed for dedication:
a.
The name(s) and address(es) of the owner(s) of the water rights proposed for dedication;
b.
A legal description of the land to be annexed or provided with municipal water service;
c.
The total number of acres to be annexed or provided with municipal water services
and the current use of the property;
d.
The total number of acres presently being irrigated and/or intended to remain in irrigation;
e.
A copy of all decrees concerning all water rights appurtenant to the property, including
the water rights proposed for dedication, and a copy of any legal decree or judgment
which affects the title of those water rights;
f.
A copy of the documents by which the owner received title to the water rights appurtenant
to the property, including those proposed for dedication;
g.
A summary of all material information disclosed by the diversion records of the water
rights proposed for dedication, including as a minimum the amount of water diverted
in cubic-second-feet and in acre-feet, the periods of time of the diversions, and
the number of acres irrigated if the water rights are decreed for irrigation purposes;
h.
The owner's statement as to the historic use of the water rights appurtenant to the
property, including those proposed for dedication; and
i.
Such additional information as the town determines is necessary or appropriate.
8.
"Lease" means any grant for permissive use which results in the creation of a landlord-tenant
relationship;
9.
"Person" means an individual, a partnership, a corporation, a municipality, or any
other legal entity, public or private;
10.
"Plat" is an accurately surveyed map or chart of a piece of land subdivided into lots
with streets, alleys, roads, and other such avenues of transportation delineated thereon
and drawn to scale;
11.
"Replat" means to make a change in the original plat.
12.
"Subdivide" means any division of land as defined in this title.
13.
"Sufficient legal priority" means that the water rights proposed for dedication may
be reasonably expected to provide a dependable water supply throughout the season
of use in the amount for which they are decreed. In making this determination, factors
to be considered shall include, but not by way of limitation, the adjudication date
and appropriation date of the water rights, the decreed use(s), the historic use of
the water under the decree, the source of supply, the physical flow available, and
the administration practices of the office of the state engineer.
14.
"Transfer of water rights" means the conveyance of legal title to water rights to
the town in addition to referring to all actions required under the laws of the state
of Colorado to be brought in the water court to ensure that the dedication requirement
is fulfilled. Such actions may include, but not by way of exclusion or limitation,
a "change of water right" as that term is defined in the Water Right Determination
and Administration Act of 1969, as now in effect or as hereafter amended. "Transfer
of water rights" includes transfer of conditional water rights, as well as transfer
of water rights.
15.
"Water right" means a decreed right to use absolutely in accordance with its priority
a certain portion of the waters of the state by reason of the appropriation of the
same.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A dedication of direct flow and/or storage rights to the town shall be required in
any of the following circumstances:
1.
The approval of annexation of land to the town (the specific requirements for which
may be deferred, in the town's sole and absolute discretion, until the approval of
zoning and/or a subdivision plat for such annexed land);
2.
The extension of municipally treated water service outside the town limits;
3.
The subdivision or replatting of any land now located with the town, if such subdivision
or re-platting requires a change of zone district or an increase in the number of
units.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
Prior to the occurrence of any one of the events listed in the previous Section 16.18.050 above, a dedication or transfer of direct flow and/or storage water rights of sufficient
legal priority shall be made to the town in a quantity adequate to meet or exceed
the delivery requirements, consumptive use requirements and other use assumptions
for each EQR, including both domestic EQRs and irrigation EQRs, that may be ultimately
required to satisfy those uses contemplated by the new user based on the table of
EQRs set forth in Title 13 ("the basic dedication requirement");
B.
For irrigation or domestic water uses not calculated under the table of EQRs, the
basic dedication requirement shall be the quantity of water, as determined by the
town, to be required ultimately in satisfaction of those uses contemplated by the
new user.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The basic dedication requirement shall be satisfied by the person who is the applicant
or entity seeking approval of or causing the occurrence of the event described in
Section 16.18.050 , whether or not such applicant or entity will be the ultimate user. Such applicant
or entity shall be referred to as a "new user".
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Any water rights dedicated to the town pursuant to the basic dedication requirement
shall enable the town to divert such dedicated water at any point of diversion it
may determine. In the event the town requires a change in the point of diversion,
and additional fee may be imposed upon the new user pursuant to Section 16.18.130 of this chapter.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The board may, in its sole discretion, substitute, modify, or waive any conditions
or requirements of this chapter, provided its purposes otherwise can be met to the
satisfaction of the town.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The new user may request the board to consider that a prior water dedication to the
town has occurred in full or partial satisfaction of the basic dedication requirement.
Upon submittal of evidence of such prior dedication, the board, in its sole discretion,
shall determine whether such prior dedication satisfies the anticipated water service
demands of the new user.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
Each new user shall submit to the town administrator or his designee his written proposal
for dedication of water rights, together with the historical use affidavit and the
engineering report. The town administrator or his designee and town attorney shall
then evaluate whether in their opinion the water rights proposed for dedication pursuant
to the provisions of this chapter are adequate to ensure the town's ability to meet
the service demands of the new user. These town officers may, during such process
of evaluation, enter into negotiations with the new user concerning terms and conditions
of the proposal, which negotiations are non-binding on the town until accepted by
the board.
B.
In evaluating the acceptability of a proposal for dedication of water rights, the
town shall consider, among other factors:
1.
Whether the water rights are of sufficient legal priority to meet the town's expected
service demands;
2.
The point of diversion of such water rights, and the feasibility, as well as the physical,
legal and or administrative cost of changing the point of diversion of such water
rights to a location acceptable to the town;
3.
Whether augmentation of such water rights will be required to meet the town's expected
service demands and, if so, the expected cost of augmenting such rights in perpetuity;
4.
The on-going administrative costs, if any, associated with the maintenance or perfection
of such water rights, including, without limitation, all expected legal, engineering,
record-keeping and other administrative costs.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
The town shall have the right, in its sole discretion, to accept as full satisfaction
of the basic dedication requirement, accept as partial satisfaction of such requirement,
or reject in full any water rights proposed for dedication pursuant to the provisions
of this chapter. If the town rejects any such water rights proposed for dedication
or determines that such water rights dedication does not fully satisfy the basic dedication
requirement based on, among other factors, those set forth in Section 16.18.120 (B), the town may, in its sole discretion, accept from the new user any in lieu fee
amount equal to the fair market value of the water rights, or portion thereof, necessary
to satisfy the basic dedication requirement, which in lieu fee shall be fixed on a
per EQR basis by the board periodically by resolution.
B.
In the event the board accepts the dedication of water rights as provided herein in
partial satisfaction of the basic dedication requirement and requires a fee to fully
satisfy the basic dedication requirements, the board shall determine the amount of
a partial in lieu fee to be paid on a case by case basis and in consideration of the
factors set forth in a resolution by the board.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Following favorable action by the board on the new user's written proposal, subject
to modifications agreeable to the new user and the town, the new user shall dedicate
the water rights determined necessary by the town by a conveyance of water rights
and shall agree, in writing, such as in an annexation and development agreement (ADA)
or a subdivision improvements agreement (SIA), to accomplish any transfer of water
rights required by the town.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
It is the intent of this chapter that no water service shall be extended to a new
user until the agreed to water rights have been dedicated to the town. However, if
there are matters pending resolution in the water court concerning the water rights
to be dedicated, or if there is other delay beyond the control of the new user, the
town shall have the discretion to approve the extension of such water service prior
to the dedication of water rights to the town by the person seeking approval of annexation
or new extension of municipally treated water service.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
All costs and expenses attendant to the conveyance and transfer of water rights dedicated
to the town, whether incurred prior to or subsequent to the dedication, shall be borne
and paid by the new user. The town shall have the right and privilege of direction
and controlling all conveyance and transfer proceedings.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Any decision made by the town administrator or his designee or other town officer
under the delegation of powers contained within this chapter shall be submitted by
him to the board, and said decision shall not become final until the same has been
ratified by the board. All interested parties shall have the opportunity to be heard
with respect to the decision.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
If the town in its discretion rejects any water rights proposed for dedication hereunder,
and further if the town in the exercise of its option and discretion determines that
the best interests of the town would not be served by accepting an "in-lieu of" cash
amount as provided by Section 16.18.120 , then the purposes and basic requirements of this chapter shall be deemed not to
have been satisfied.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
If the owner of the property proposed to be annexed or served desires prior to development
to retain the land or any portion thereof in agricultural production or as open space,
he shall be permitted to lease back, on an annual basis, and for irrigation, aesthetic
and recreational purposes only, the water rights transferred pursuant to this chapter.
The owner of the property and the town shall negotiate the terms of the lease.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
The owner of any property proposed for annexation or subdivision who dedicates or
proposes to dedicate property to the town, pursuant to open space dedication requirements
of the town, to be used for open space, park, aesthetic, recreation, or irrigation
purposes, shall also comply with the provisions of this chapter for the property to
be dedicated for such open space purposes.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Any person required to comply with the basic dedication requirement shall also grant
to the town the option to purchase any and all water rights which are appurtenant
to the land to be annexed but which are in excess of the basic dedication requirement.
The town may exercise said option at any time for a period of one year following the
date of the grant to the town with regard to any or all of the water rights subject
to the grant.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
The option price shall be that price agreed upon by the parties. If the parties do
not agree upon an option price within thirty days after notice of the town's intent
to exercise its option is received by the owner, an appraisal at the new user's expense
and in compliance with the requirements of this section shall establish the option
price that reflects the fair market value of the appurtenant water rights.
B.
The appraisal of the subject water rights referred to in Section 16.18.210 (A) shall be conducted by three appraisers, one of whom shall be appointed by the
town, one of which shall be selected by the owner of the water rights, and the third
who shall be selected by the first two appraisers. The average of the three appraisals
shall constitute the fair market value and the option price for the water rights.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Should the town not exercise its rights to purchase said appurtenant rights, the new
user shall grant to the town the right of first refusal regarding the water rights.
The town shall retain said right of first refusal in the event the water rights are
sold independently of the land, for a period of ten years following annexation or
extension of water service.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
If the owner of the water rights subject to said right of first refusal wishes to
sell said water rights to a third party, he shall give to the town at least ninety
days' notice of his intention to effect a sale of said water rights by delivering
to the town a bona fide written offer to purchase made by a third party.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
During the ninety-day period provided for in the previous section, the town shall
enjoy its right first refusal entitling it to purchase the water rights proposed for
sale. If within ninety days following notice by the owner of his intention to sell
his water rights, the town chooses to exercise its right of purchase, then the town
shall pay to the owner a price for the water rights equal to the amount tendered to
the owner in a bona fide offer by the third party. In the event the town determines
not to exercise its right to purchase the water rights offered for sale, the owner
shall be free to sell the water rights to the third party for the price set forth
in the bona fide written offer to purchase.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
