This title shall be known as the "Town of Silt Subdivision Regulations."
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
This title is authorized by the Colorado Revised Statutes and the Town of Silt Home
Rule Charter, and is declared to be in accordance with all provisions of these statutes
and the intent of this title.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
The Town of Silt Board of Trustees hereby finds, determines and declares that the
uniform regulation of land development in the town, in accordance with the comprehensive
plan and as established by the standards, procedures, and enforcement provisions of
this title, is necessary for the immediate preservation of the public peace, health,
safety, convenience, and welfare.
B.
Intent to supersede General Colorado Law. The Town of Silt recognizes that zoning
and subdivision are ultimately local and municipal matters, and intends with this
title to supersede within the territorial limits and other jurisdiction of the town
any general law of the state of Colorado in conflict herewith.
C.
Specific purposes. The regulations of this title are intended to implement the Town
of Silt Comprehensive Plan, as amended, and more specifically to:
1.
Implement the town's goals, policies, plans, and programs to guide its future;
2.
Encourage traditional neighborhood residential mixed and multiple use developments
so that the demand for housing may be met by development of pedestrian oriented neighborhoods,
in character with existing areas of town;
3.
Ensure logical and economical growth of the various physical elements of the town,
in a manner which promotes efficient use of the town's infrastructure;
4.
Conserve and stabilize the value of property;
5.
Provide for adequate transportation systems including alternate modes of transportation;
6.
Facilitate adequate provision of utilities and facilities such as water, wastewater,
schools, trails, parks, irrigation, storm water, and other public facilities;
7.
Protect the environmental assets of the town, including but not limited to the Colorado
River, its riparian zone and wildlife areas; and
8.
Ensure quality development that will preserve and enhance the quality of life for
residents of the town.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Should any section or provision of this Code be declared by the courts to be unconstitutional
or invalid, such decision shall not affect the validity of this Code as a whole or
any part thereof, other than the part so declared to be unconstitutional or invalid.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
In interpreting and applying the provisions of this Code, they shall be held to be
the minimum requirements for the promotion of public health, safety, morals, convenience,
and the general welfare. It is not intended by this Code to interfere with or annul
any easements, covenants or agreements between parties; however, that wherever this
Code imposes a greater restriction upon the use of buildings or land upon the location
or height of buildings or structures, or any other requirement than are imposed or
required by other laws, resolutions, or by easements, covenants, or agreements between
parties, the provisions of this Code shall govern.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
These regulations shall apply to all subdivisions of land into two or more parcels,
building sites, tracts or lots, as defined in Section 16.02.080 (76).
B.
The territorial jurisdiction of these regulations shall include all land located within
the legal boundaries of the town.
C.
The town may institute the procedures outlined in this title to subdivide land in
any area proposed for annexation at any time after passage of a resolution of intent
to annex, or after a petition for annexation or a petition for an annexation election
has been found to be valid; however, the ordinance accepting the proposed subdivision
shall not be passed on final reading prior to the date when annexation is passed on
final reading.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
Subdivisions evidenced by a final plat recorded prior to the effective date of September
24, 1979, shall not be regulated by this title unless proposed for re-subdivision
in such a manner as to fall within the definition of subdivision in this chapter.
In the instance of large tracts or blocks of land contained within a recorded subdivision
and intended or designed for re-subdivision into smaller tracts, lots, building sites,
condominium units, apartments, multifamily dwelling units or otherwise so as to constitute
a subdivision under this chapter, such re-subdivision shall comply with all provisions
of this title.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)

For the purpose of this Code, certain words listed in this chapter are hereby defined:
1.
"Access drive" means a street or right-of-way providing ingress and egress to properties
adjacent to a local street, collector, arterial street, or major collector street.
2.
"Alley" means a public vehicular way or right-of-way which affords a secondary means
of vehicular access to abutting property and which is not intended for general traffic
circulation, but may be used for utility access and installation;
3.
"Applicant" means the owner of land or the applicant's authorized representative,
or the town or other quasi-governmental entity that is proposing an action requiring
review and approval pursuant to one or more of the sections in this Code. An applicant
may subsequently become the developer once approval is granted, and in this case the
terms shall be interchangeable. Further, in the case of an applicant having permission
of the landowner to submit applications for subdivision or any other land use action,
both the applicant and the landowner shall be considered applicants.
4.
"Appurtenance" means accessory equipment which extend above the roof or outside walls
of a structure including but not limited to ventilators, communication equipment,
microwave dishes, satellite dishes, elevator houses, mechanical equipment and other
similar items.
5.
"Berm" means an earthen barrier of compacted soils intended to prevent the passage
of liquid materials, and/or to provide landscaped screening between adjacent uses.
6.
"Block" means an area of land within a subdivision which is entirely bounded by streets,
railroad rights-of-way, waterways or exterior boundaries of the subdivision.
7.
"Board" means the board of trustees of the Town of Silt.
8.
"Building" means a structure having a roof supported by columns or walls and intended
for the shelter, housing or enclosure of persons, animals or personal, public or private
property.
9.
"Commission" means the planning and zoning commission of the Town of Silt.
10.
"Common area" means an area of land and/or buildings within a residential or commercial
development which is developed for the use and enjoyment of all residents of the project,
as distinguished from land designated for their individual, private use.
11.
"Compatible" means having harmony in design and/or appearance between two or more
attributes of a structure; having harmony in design and/or appearance between two
or more structures; having harmony in design and/or appearance between two or more
attributes of a neighborhood; or having harmony in use or function between two or
more attributes of a neighborhood or area.
12.
"Comprehensive plan" or "master plan" means the plan prepared and adopted by the town,
indicating the planned physical development of the town and containing the information
required by state statutes, as amended from time to time.
13.
"Crosswalk" or "pedestrian crossing" means the area designated for pedestrian crossing
at an intersection or other designated location, sometimes constructed of a different
material than the roadway for aesthetic and functional purposes.
14.
"Cul-de-sac" means a comparatively short minor street having one end open to traffic
and the other end permanently terminated by a vehicular turnaround.
15.
"Development application" means the process through which all land use reviews are
conducted by the town, beginning with a development application form listing the name
of the property owner, the name of the applicant if different, the legal description
of the real property, and including all the various land use review processes proposed
by an applicant, all phases of the project, and every subsequent stage of review required
by the Silt Municipal Code. Additionally, and notwithstanding the foregoing, "development
application" shall include any application involving land use review pending before
the town as of the effective date of this section, and any amendment to or modification
of an existing subdivision final plat or approved planned unit development shall be
deemed to be undertaken pursuant to and as part of the application originally filed
with the town for such subdivision or planned unit development. The list of types
of land uses or applications herein is not intended to be exclusive. Other types of
applications may be considered "valid development applications," and this definition
shall not be construed to limit the town's position as to what other types of applications
may be considered "valid development applications."
16.
"Double frontage lot" means a lot having frontage on two parallel or approximately
parallel streets.
17.
"Driveway" means a private access to a single building site or lot, upon which may
be placed parking spaces off the street.
18.
"Duplex" means a two-dwelling detached principal building containing no more than
two dwelling units sharing a common wall between both dwelling units or sharing a
common ceiling or floor, in whole or in part, connecting the two dwelling units, each
of which is designed for and used as a dwelling unit exclusively by one family. Each
duplex structure may be further subdivided, legally creating two separately conveyable
single family dwelling units, each with their appurtenant lots. A duplex is not to
be considered a single-family dwelling with an attached or detached additional dwelling
unit, as defined by this Code. Stacked duplex units may only be subdivided through
a condominiumization process.
19.
"Dwelling unit" means any building or portion thereof, used exclusively for residential
occupancy, that contains separate living facilities, including provisions for sleeping,
eating, cooking and sanitation, and is intended for occupancy by a family and its
guests, independent of other families or guests. A dwelling unit shall contain only
one kitchen and does not include hotels, motels, tents, seasonal vacation cabins,
camper trailers or other structures designed for temporary occupancy. A dwelling unit
may also be referred to as a dwelling.
20.
"Easement" means a grant by a property owner to the use of a portion of land by the
public or persons for specific purposes, such as but not necessarily limited to utilities,
drainage-ways and access ways.
21.
"Engineer" or "town engineer" means the staff or consultant engineer duly authorized
by the board to review applications per this title for the town.
22.
"Final acceptance" means the written acknowledgement by the town following the final
infrastructure inspection, whereby the town is satisfied that all provisions of the
subdivision improvements agreement have been met, that there are no outstanding items
to be corrected, and that date by which the warranty period ends.
23.
"Final plan" means those materials and studies required by this title to be submitted
by an applicant for subdivision that provide the town adequate information with which
to consider the final plat.
24.

water collects and flows.
26.

27.

land to be dedicated for streets and other public purposes.
28.

the maintenance of common areas, landscaping, parks, building exteriors, and private

30.

land and/or structures placed on or under the land surface in order to change the
natural or pre-existing conditions of the land in preparation for the construction

purpose. Land improvements include, but are not limited to, grading, street pavement,
curbs and gutters, sidewalks, drainage facilities, storm and sanitary sewers, and
utility facilities.

to vegetative and non-vegetative materials of varying height, width and length, that

elements in the natural environment.
35.
"Loading area" means a parking space, other than a public street or alley, for the

36.

38.
"Lot width" means the mean horizontal distance between the side property lines of

39.
"Multi-family dwelling" means a building providing separate dwelling units for three

"Mixed use development" means a development of a lot, tract, or parcel with two or

42.
"Multiple-purpose or multi-purpose path" means an asphalt or concrete path used for

43.
"Net density" means the number of dwelling units per acre of land, excluding areas

45.

46.
"Off-street parking space" means the permanent surface space required to park one

47.
"Open space" means any parcel or area of land essentially unimproved and set aside,

48.
"Parkway" means that portion of the public right-of-way between the curb-line and

49.
"Percentage of grade" means the rise or fall of a street, in feet and tenths of a
foot for each one hundred feet of horizontal distance measured at the centerline of

50.
"Planned unit development subdivision" means a subdivision to be developed under a

to the PUD zoning ordinance approving such PUD. It shall contain sufficient total
area in relation to the intended use or uses to permit effective application of planning

51.
"Preliminary plan" means those materials and studies required by this title to be
submitted by an applicant for subdivision that provide the town adequate information

52.

permit the evaluation of the proposal, with detailed engineering and design.
53.
"Shared private road" or "shared drive" means a private access to more than one lot,
building or unit, which will be maintained and replaced by the owner(s) of the lots,
buildings or units.
54.
"Private improvement" means any improvement required by this Code or as a part of
the conditional approval of a subdivision which is provided by the developer and subsequently
maintained and replaced by the owner(s) of the lots, buildings or units.
55.
"Property line adjustment" means the relocation of a property line which does not
create new lots, nonconforming lots or structures, or a change in use, or negatively

to zoning regulations.
56.
"Public hearing" means a hearing held after a public notice has been posted in conformance
with this Code, to allow interested persons to present their views to the board or
the commission.
57.
"Public improvement" means any improvement required by this Code for which the town
or other governmental entity agrees to assume responsibility for maintenance and operation,
or which may affect an improvement for which the town or other governmental entity
is already responsible. Such facilities include but are not limited to streets, parks,
trails, curbs, sidewalks, drainage facilities, water and wastewater facilities, natural
gas, electricity, telephone, cable television, and other utility facilities.
58.
"Quasi-public agency" means an institution constituted with a governing board and
obtaining more than fifty-one percent of its funds (budget) from tax revenue.
59.
"Registered professional engineer" means an engineer currently licensed and registered
in the state of Colorado.
60.
"Registered professional land surveyor" means a land surveyor currently licensed and

61.
"Re-subdivision" or "re-plat" means an application for any amendment to a recorded
subdivision plat, except a property line adjustment as defined herein, or any request
to change any map, plan, or plat legally recorded with the Garfield County clerk and

62.
"Right-of-way" means an area dedicated to the public use for pedestrian or vehicular
movement; railroads; public utilities; and/or water and wastewater facilities.
63.

traffic.
64.

65.
"Setback line" or "building setback line" means a line marking the minimum distance

nearest line or point of the building foundation, including porches, covered entries,
attached decks and the like.
(Note: A lot that is adjacent to two streets has a front yard setback from each street
right-of-way line.)
66.

surface for use by pedestrians.
67.

68.

70.

b.
"Sub-arterial street" means a street designed for high-volume, moderate-speed circulation

c.
"Collector street" means a street connecting a series of local streets to each other

d.
"Local street" or "residential street" means a street primarily intended to serve

71.

including both natural and manmade elements.
72.

"Structure" means anything constructed or erected which requires location on the ground

74.
"Subdivider" or "developer" means a person who submits a plat for the purpose of land

The division of land by order of any court in this state or by operation of law;

c.
The division of land by a security or unit of interest in any investment trust regulated

d.
The division of land which creates cemetery lots;
e.

f.
The division of land by the acquisition of an interest in land in the name of a husband

g.
The division of land by reason of the dissolution of a joint venture or partnership;
h.
The division of land by reason of the conveyance of property to the Town of Silt,

76.
"Subdivision improvements agreement" means an agreement reached between the town and
the subdivider, developer and/or landowner, whereby one or more security arrangements
which may be accepted by the town to secure the cost of construction of such public
improvements as are required by these subdivision regulations within the subdivision,

or property bonds, private or public escrow agreements, loan commitments, assignments
of receivables, liens on property, letters of credit, deposits of certified funds
or other similar surety agreements sufficient in the judgment of the town to make
reasonable provision for the completion of such improvements in accordance with time
and design specifications.
77.
"Subdivision (major)" means any subdivision that involves the division of a parcel,
lot or tract into six or more lots or tracts.
78.
"Subdivision (minor)" means any subdivision that involves the division of a parcel,
lot or tract into five or fewer lots or tracts.
79.
"Subdivision plat" means the map(s) and plans, conditions and requirements and any
other materials required by this Code which are submitted for review, approval and
recordation by the town.
80.
"Town" means the Town of Silt, Colorado, a home rule municipality.
81.
"Urban" means an area characterized by higher population density and vast human features
in comparison to areas surrounding it. With regards to annexation, the term "urban"
relates to the ability of the property to be put in a higher population density area
and removed from the county rural zoning.
82.
"Warranty security" means that bond, letter of credit, or other security that is submitted
after the developer has completed the infrastructure within a phase and such infrastructure
has been approved by the town.

The board and the commission shall utilize the comprehensive plan in their review
and consideration of all land use applications, including annexation. Since the comprehensive
plan is advisory, and is known as the planning guide and master plan, applicants shall
bear in mind the following:
A.
All land use proposals shall strive to substantially conform to the town's comprehensive
plan then in effect and as amended, unless the board of trustees determines that an
applicant's non-compliance with the comprehensive plan is in the town's overall best
interest;
B.
The town staff shall consider the guidelines, goals and provisions of the town's comprehensive
plan then in effect and as amended, when reviewing and making recommendations regarding
land use proposals;
C.
The town staff shall review the comprehensive plan every two to three years and recommend
updates as needed;
D.
Any amendment to the comprehensive plan shall be made only after the commission and
the board have thoroughly reviewed the amendment and its ramifications.
(Ord. No. 9-2011, § 1, 12-12-2011)
A.
The commission may undertake a comprehensive plan amendment of its own volition, or
as a result of a request by an applicant;
B.
Should the commission decide to amend its comprehensive plan, the commission shall
hold a public hearing as required by this title;
C.
Application requirements. An applicant for comprehensive plan amendment shall provide
such written information on the land use application form and on forms provided by
the town, plus any other supplemental information needed to convey the information
to the town, including the following:
1.
Disclosure of ownership—A certificate (not older than ninety days) from a title insurance
company or attorney licensed in the state of Colorado, which shall set forth a legal
description of the property, the names of all owners of the property and shall include
a list of all mortgages, judgments, liens, contracts or agreements of record in Garfield
County regarding such subject property. If the certificate of title discloses any
of the above, the owners or holders of such mortgages, judgments, liens, contracts,
or agreements shall be required to consent to the application. If the applicant for
comprehensive plan amendment is not the property owner(s) of the affected parcel(s),
then both the applicant and the property owner(s) shall sign the land use application
and be subject to all the provisions of this Code;
2.
A description of the existing comprehensive land use designation and its goals;
3.
A description of the proposed comprehensive land use designation and its goals;
4.
A description of the proposed and/or existing land use(s);
5.
A statement of the planning objective(s);
6.
A description of adjoining land use(s) and zoning;
7.
Existing and proposed zoning of the subject property;
8.
The total area of the parcel;
D.
Comprehensive plan map and supporting documents. The comprehensive plan map shall
be drawn to a scale of one inch equals one hundred feet or larger, which indicates
the following;
1.
A vicinity map, drawn at a scale of one inch equals two thousand feet, showing the
general location of the land for consideration and the surrounding area within a one-mile
radius;
2.
The topography of the land;
3.
The location of the proposed land uses;
4.
Significant natural and manmade features on the site, such as streams, lakes, natural
drainage-ways; vegetation types including locations of wooded areas, existing building,
utility lines and easements, irrigation ditches, bridges and similar physical features;
and existing development on adjacent property.
E.
Number of copies. The applicant for comprehensive plan amendment shall submit eighteen
copies of the application and supplemental material as required in this title, sized
eight and a half inches by eleven inches for textual material and eleven inches by
seventeen inches for maps that will be included in the commission public hearing packets.
Additionally, four copies of all maps shall be submitted for staff review, each measuring
no less than twenty-four inches by thirty-six inches, plus four copies of all other
textual supplemental materials. The applicant for comprehensive plan amendment shall
submit additional copies as needed and as requested by the planning department for
the board hearing to consider the proposed amendment.
F.
An applicant for a comprehensive plan amendment shall pay to the town a fee as set
by the board of trustees by resolution annually, or more often as necessary.

The applicant for comprehensive plan amendment, upon submittal of a complete application,
shall schedule with the planning department a pre-application conference. At this
pre-application conference, the applicant shall explain to town staff the entire proposal,

B.
Following the pre-application conference and submittal of comprehensive plan amendment
application, the planning department shall have fifteen days to check the application
for completeness. Town staff may notify the applicant that a longer period of time
is needed to check for application completeness, and in such case, the planning department
shall have an additional fifteen days to check the application for completeness.
C.
If application is incomplete, the planning department shall notify the applicant in
writing within fifteen days of receiving the incomplete application, and the applicant
shall have no more than thirty days to respond with additional submittal materials
that make the application complete, unless a thirty-day extension is requested by
the applicant and approved by the planning department. If the applicant is unable
to complete the application within thirty days, the application is deemed abandoned,
and the applicant may submit a new application in order to be considered for a comprehensive
plan amendment.
D.
If application is complete, the planning department shall have no fewer than thirty
days or more than forty-five days to review the comprehensive plan amendment application
for compliance with this title.
E.
The applicant for comprehensive plan amendment shall publicly notice the time, date
and location of the 1 st commission meeting to consider the application per the guidelines set forth in this
title.
F.
No public hearing may occur if the public notification has not occurred per the provisions
of this title.
G.
Within fifteen days following the planning department's review of the application,
the town shall schedule a public hearing before the commission to consider the application,
using the following guidelines:
1.
Appropriateness of the request;
2.
Compatibility with surrounding land uses within the town;
3.
Economic impacts of the request, if any;
4.
Adjacency to public services, including but not limited to, parks, streets, water,
wastewater and irrigation facilities; and
5.
Conformance to the town's zoning code, if land has been previously annexed and zoned
within the town.

No lot in a subdivision shall be sold, no permit shall be issued to alter or erect
any structure upon land in a subdivision, and no building shall be altered or erected
in a subdivision unless the town approves a subdivision plat and the applicant completes
town approved improvements per this title. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the board

B.
It is unlawful for any person or entity to act as subdivider or developer or to create
subdivided land without first obtaining approval of a final subdivision plat as provided
in this title. It shall also be unlawful for any person or entity to convey land that
has not been legally subdivided in conformance with this Code.
C.
In addition to all other remedies, including the denial of any building permit, the
town shall have the power to bring an action to enjoin any individual or developer
from selling or otherwise conveying any parcel of land or other interest, which sale
or conveyance would constitute a subdivision as defined by this Code. The town shall
also have the power to require the developer to annul any transaction brought about
due to the unlawful subdivision of land.

A.
Payment of fees; deposit requirements.
1.
Application fee. The applicant for a land use application pursuant to this title shall
pay a non-refundable application fee upon filing the application in an amount as described
below. This fee shall be set by the board annually or as often as necessary, by resolution.
The application fee includes a credit for a designated number of staff review hours
as set forth in Title 2 of this Code.
2.
Deposit for land use application. To secure the payment of the town's review expenses,
all applicants filing applications pursuant to this title shall pay to the town a
deposit in the amount designated in the board's resolution to set such fees. In the
event the applicant's deposit exceeds the actual review expenses owed to the town
pursuant to subsection (1) above and Title 2 of this Code, upon completion of all aspects of land use application review, including
the payment of all costs associated therewith, such excess deposit shall be refunded
to the applicant within a reasonable time after final action by the town or withdrawal
of the application and payment of all costs associated with the application. If filing
multiple applications, the applicant shall submit the highest deposit required, but
the town shall not require multiple deposits be submitted, unless, in the town administrator's
sole opinion, the applicant has had difficulty in the past paying town invoices.
B.
Payment of fees and costs. The applicant for a development review approval pursuant
to this chapter shall reimburse the town for any and all costs, staff fees and consultant
fees incurred by the town during and after a review process associated with the property.
Costs include, but are not limited to, publication fees, recording fees and any other
actual out-of-pocket costs incurred by the town with regard to the review of the application.
Staff fees include, but are not limited to, reimbursement of time spent by the town
administrator or his designee, planning department staff, public works staff, town
attorney and administrative staff. Consultant fees shall include, but are not limited
to, all actual out-of-pocket costs incurred by the town regarding review of the application
by legal, planning, engineering, landscaping, and other professional advice. The town,
in its sole discretion, will require consultants to provide as detailed a billing
statement as is possible, regarding the review of the application. Staff time billed
shall be number of hours and a brief description. Additionally, to partially defray
administrative handling and review costs incurred by the town with regard to review
of the application, the applicant for any land use application shall pay an administrative
fee in an amount equal to fifteen percent of all consultant fees.
C.
Monthly payment and interest. The town shall bill the applicant on a monthly or a
quarterly basis for all review expenses. All bills for review expenses are due and
payable to the town within fifteen days of the date of the bill. Interest per Chapter 3.08 of the Code shall be charged on any overdue amounts unpaid within one month of the
date of the bill until paid;
D.
Continuing obligation. By filing a land use application for review under this title
and accepting the benefits thereof, the land use applicant and/or property owner agrees
and acknowledges that there is a continuing and binding obligation on the applicant
to reimburse the town for all town costs incurred as a result of the review of the
land use application;
E.
Processing applications. The town shall not review any land use applications pursuant
to this title, or Title 17 , unless or until applicant submits the correct application fee and deposit, as set
by the board of trustees by resolution annually, or more often as necessary. The town
shall not accept any land use application from an applicant who owes money to the
town for any reason, including, but not limited to, utility bills, outstanding judgments,
accounts receivable billings, etc. If at any time during the review of the land use
application, the town discovers that the applicant has not paid to the town any fee
owed for longer than 30 days, and/or has not paid the land use application review
fees in a timely manner, then the town shall cease all review of application and shall
schedule no public hearings, until such time as the applicant's billing is made current.
