# Oil & Gas Drilling and Production
Oil and gas drilling in Southlake is regulated through City ordinances, zoning requirements, and a local permitting process. These requirements help the City review proposed drilling sites, provide opportunities for public input, and confirm that approved operations meet applicable standards. This page provides information about Southlake’s oil and gas regulations, application process, fees, and environmental reports.

Drilling Approval Process A company must complete two steps before drilling a well in Southlake:
Obtain approval of a Specific Use Permit.
Obtain a gas permit for each proposed well.
A Specific Use Permit, commonly called a SUP, determines whether the proposed use is appropriate at a specific location. The SUP process includes staff review, public meetings, and consideration by the Planning and Zoning Commission and City Council.

<!-- heading-path: Oil & Gas Drilling and Production > Oil and Gas Regulations > SUP Process: -->
### SUP Process:
Application: The company submits an SUP application. The application must include information about the proposed site, access road and any required pipeline permit.
Staff review: City staff reviews the application for compliance with applicable requirements.
SPIN meeting: The proposal is presented through the Southlake Program for the Involvement of Neighborhoods to provide information and gather community input.
Planning and Zoning Commission: The commission holds a public hearing and makes a recommendation to City Council.
City Council: City Council holds a public hearing and considers final action on the SUP.
If City Council approves the SUP, the company must then apply for a gas permit for each well proposed at the site. Gas permits are reviewed by City staff and must comply with Article IV of Chapter 9.5 of the City Code and all conditions of the approved SUP.
Approval of an SUP does not replace the requirement to obtain a gas permit.

## Environmental Reports
The City commissioned studies to better understand environmental conditions related to oil and gas activity in and around Southlake.
Hydrogeologic Evaluation In September 2010, the City contracted with Kleinfelder to complete a limited hydrogeologic evaluation. The study reviewed available records and water-well data to summarize geological and groundwater conditions beneath Southlake, including formations extending into the Fort Worth Basin and Barnett Shale.
Air Quality Report In July 2010, the City contracted with Kleinfelder to conduct a limited ambient air-quality evaluation. The study collected 24-hour air samples from 19 monitoring locations in and around Southlake and tested them for volatile organic compounds and formaldehyde.
