SECTION 4. Section 8-5-1 of the Duchesne County Code is amended to read: 127 
128 
8-5-1: ZONING DISTRICTS SPECIFIED; PURPOSE: 129 
130 
Consistent with the goals and policies of the county general plan, the zoning districts are 131 
formulated to provide and achieve the following purposes: 132 
133 
A. Agriculture Districts (A): 134 
135 
1. A-10, ten (10) acre minimum zone, is provided and designed to protect and 136 
encourage the continued use of land for agricultural purposes and to discourage 137 
the preemption of agricultural land for other uses. Other purposes of this district 138 
include the protection of the economic base of the county for such uses as 139 
forestry, oil and gas drilling, pipelines, petroleum storage and distribution and the 140 
protection of significant natural features of land, creeks, lakes, wetlands, air and 141 
the preservation of open areas for wildlife habitat and range livestock. This 142 
district is characterized by production farms and ranches, including smaller hobby 143 
farms. The A-10 district requires, for residential development, either a Tri-County 144 
health department approved nonpublic water source or a connection from a public 145 
water source to the family dwelling and a wastewater system permit. 146 
Representative uses within this district include, but are not limited to: family 147 
dwellings, barns, corrals, crops, livestock raising, farm dwellings, guest ranches, 148 
produce retail sales and petroleum drilling and storage. In no case shall parcels in 149 
the A-10 zone be reduced below ten (10) acres in size. 150 
151 
2. A-5, five (5) acre minimum zone. The purpose of the A-5 zone is the same as 152 
that of the A-10 zoning district. Construction of buildings for human occupancy 153 
in the A-5 district requires either a Tri-County health department approved 154 
nonpublic water source or a connection from a public water source to the building 155 
and a wastewater system permit. Proof of a public water connection is all that is 156 
required for property mapped in the A-5 zone to be zoned R-1 and proof of both a 157 
public water connection and a public sewer is all that is required for property 158 
mapped in the A-5 zone to be zoned R-1/2. It is not necessary to rezone property to 159 
the higher density zone when the official zoning map shows available public 160 
water service. 161 
162 
3. A-2.5, two and one-half (21/2) acre minimum zone, is to conserve and protect 163 
farms and other open land uses, foster orderly growth in rural areas, and prevent 164 
land development and agricultural land use conflicts. Principal uses permitted in 165 
this zoning district include both farm and nonfarm residential uses. Construction 166 
of buildings for human occupancy in the A-2.5 district requires either a Tri-167 
County health department approved nonpublic water source or a connection from 168 
a public water source to the building and a wastewater system permit. Proof of a 169 
public water connection is all that is required for property mapped in the A-2.5 170 
zone to be zoned R-1 and proof of both a public water connection and a public 171 
sewer is all that is required for property mapped in the A-2.5 zone to be zoned R-172 
1/2. It is not necessary to rezone property to the higher density zone when the 173 
official zoning map shows available public water service. Representative uses 174 
within this district include, but are not limited to, family dwellings, barns, corrals, 175 
crops, livestock raising, farm dwellings and produce retail sales. 176 
177 
B. Residential Districts: The residential districts of the county (R-1, 1 acre minimum and 178 
R-1/2, 1/2 acre minimum) are formulated to provide family housing choices to meet the 179

C. Commercial District: The commercial (C) district is intended to provide controlled and 191 
compatible locations for retail, office and business/commerce activities, to enhance 192 
employment opportunities, to encourage the efficient use of land, to enhance property 193 
values and to strengthen the county tax base. Typical uses allowed in this district include, 194 
but are not limited to, retail sales and services, offices and institutional uses. There is no 195 
minimum area requirement for the commercial district. 196 
197 
D. Industrial District: The industrial (I) district is formulated to recognize existing 198 
industrial sites and activities within the county, to provide opportunities and economic 199 
stability to provide employment opportunities for county residents. Typical uses allowed 200 
in this district include, but are not limited to, all uses that are not permitted in any other 201 
zoning district. There is no minimum area requirement for the industrial district. 202 
203 
E. Lot Size Rounding: Because of the way land was originally surveyed by the general 204 
land office, a section of land in Duchesne County may not be a full six hundred forty 205 
(640) acres, a quarter section may not be a full one hundred sixty (160) acres, a "quarter-206 
quarter" section may not be a full forty (40) acres, and so on. According to the county 207 
surveyor and the "Manual Of Surveying Instructions", section 3-34, the distance between 208 
regular corners is to be normal according to the plan of survey, with certain allowable 209 
adjustments not to exceed twenty five (25) links (16.5 feet) in forty (40) chains (2,640 210 
feet) or thirty three feet (33') in five thousand two hundred eighty feet (5,280'). Thus 211 
sections as small as five thousand two hundred forty seven feet by five thousand two 212 
hundred forty seven feet (5,247' x 5,247') or as large as five thousand three hundred 213 
thirteen feet by five thousand three hundred thirteen feet (5,313' x 5,313') are considered 214 
regular sections. Thus, lot sizes within 98.75 percent of the minimum lot sizes specified 215 
by this title may be rounded up to meet said standards. 216 
217 
F. Lot Size And Rights Of Way: For existing parcels of land and when new parcels of 218 
land are created, those portions of said parcels containing easements or rights of way may 219 
be counted toward the acreage needed to meet the minimum lot size standards of this 220 
code. 221 
222 
G. Parcels In More Than One Zoning District: When a single parcel of land is located 223 
within more than one zoning district, the provisions of each zoning district shall apply 224 
only to those portions of the parcel within said district boundaries. 225 
226 
H. Un-manned Utility or Telecommunications Facilities: The minimum lot size 227 
standards of the zoning districts do not apply to parcels created for the purpose of 228 
establishing an un-manned public or private transportation, utility service or 229 
telecommunications facility. 230

8-16-1: ZONING ADMINISTRATOR DECISIONS: 241 
242 
A. Appeal to Planning Commission: Decisions of the zoning administrator in applying 243 
the provisions of this title may be appealed to the Planning Commission by filing an 244 
application and all necessary materials supportive documentation within ten (10) days 245 
of the date of the zoning administrator's decision. The only persons with standing to 246 
appeal are the applicant, a board or officer of the county or an adversely affected party 247 
any person adversely affected by the decision of the zoning administrator. 248 
249 
B. Notice, Hearing, Decision: Following receipt by the Planning Commission of an 250 
appeal of a decision by the zoning administrator, the Planning Commission shall give 251 
notice and schedule a public hearing. The appellant shall present all theories of relief to 252 
the Planning Commission, as failure to raise such theories at this stage of appeal 253 
precludes presentation of such theories at later stages. Following the hearing and the 254 
submission review of all necessary and relevant information, the Planning Commission 255 
shall render a decision on the appeal. 256 
257 
8-16-2: BOARD OF ADJUSTMENT DECISIONS: 258 
259 
Any person An adversely affected party aggrieved by a decision of the board of 260 
adjustment may, within thirty (30) days after the decision is made, present to the district 261 
court a petition specifying the nature of the appeal and the grounds on which they are 262 
adversely affected. 263 
264 
8-16-3: PLANNING COMMISSION DECISIONS: 265 
266 
Any person deemed to have standing by virtue of being the applicant, a board or officer 267 
of the county, or an adversely affected party any person adversely affected by a 268 
decision of the planning commission, may file an appeal with the county commission 269 
within ten (10) days of the date of the decision specifying the nature of the appeal. 270 
Following receipt by the county commission of an appeal of a decision by the planning 271 
commission, the county commission shall give notice and schedule a de novo public 272 
hearing. The appellant shall present all theories of relief to the county commission as 273 
failure to raise such theories at this stage of appeal precludes presentation of such theories 274 
to district court. Following the hearing and the submission review of all necessary and 275 
relevant information, the county commission shall render a decision on the appeal. 276 
277 
8-16-4: JUDICIAL REVIEW: 278 
279 
A land use applicant or an adversely affected party Any person aggrieved by a 280 
decision of the board of county commissioners may file an appeal to district court within 281 
thirty (30) days of the date of the decision pursuant to the provisions of Utah Code 282 
Annotated. No petition for judicial review may be filed unless and until the applicant or 283 
adversely affected party has exhausted all manner of relief and processes available with 284 
the county as are provided by this title. Pursuant to the Utah Code, the County may 285 
not require that a land use applicant or adversely affected party pursue duplicate or 286 
successive appeals before the same or separate appeal authorities as a condition of 287 
the appealing party’s duty to exhaust all administrative remedies. 288 
289 
8-16-5: OPTION TO SELECT THIRD PARTY APPEAL AUTHORITY: 290 
291 
The County Commission shall act as the appeal authority to hear appeals of Planning 292
