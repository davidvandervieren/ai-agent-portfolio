Reasonable Cost Analysis of Land Use Application Fees for the Planning Division of the 
Community Development Department in Duchesne County, UT 
What is a Reasonable Cost Analysis? 
Utah Code 10-9a-510 states that “a municipality may not impose or collect: (a) a land use 
application fee that exceeds the reasonable cost of processing the application or issuing the 
permit; or (b) an inspection, regulation, or review fee that exceeds the reasonable cost of 
performing the inspection, regulation, or review." It also states that “If requested by an applicant 
who is charged a fee or an owner of residential property upon which a fee is imposed, the 
municipality shall provide an itemized fee statement that shows the calculation method for each 
fee.” This document, with its accompanying calculations, serves as the Duchesne County Planning 
Division’s reasonable cost analysis of land use application fees proposed to be amended by 
ordinance by the Duchesne County Commission in early 2025. 
Reasonable VS Unreasonable 
Per the Utah Supreme Court in its ruling from Utah Sage, Inc. v. Pleasant Grove City, a fee is 
reasonable if it is in proportion to the benefits conferred. A fee may also be reasonable even if it 
raises revenue that exceeds the cost of service, and does not have to be based on, or “insist” on, 
exact mathematical precision. A fee is unreasonable if it is not reasonably related to some need 
created by the one paying the fee, and if services provided through the fee are not of demonstrable 
benefit to the one paying the fee. 
Calculating Land Use Application Fees 
There are various approaches to calculate land use application fees, including: 
Approach 1: Base fees on an average of revenues and expenses over time; 
Approach 2: Use prior year expenses and demand as a basis for the fees for the following 
year; and 
Approach 3: Base fees on calculated direct costs, indirect costs, and supplies required 
to process each land use application process.1 
Each of these approaches have advantages or disadvantages. Because the Duchesne County 
Planning Division did not base previous land use application fees on revenues or expenses, both 
Approaches 1 and 2 cannot be used. Also, individual fees based on Approach 1 and 2 are not 
necessarily proportional to services provided, and Approach 2 may not keep up with inflationary 
costs. Approach 3 may be subject to potential dramatic revenue variations year to year while 
expenses remain relatively constant.

New Land Use Application Fees 
As of the date of the 2023 Land Use Application Fees being adopted, all oil and gas development 
facilities within the R-1 Residential, R ½ Residential, and A 2 ½ Agricultural zoning districts required 
administrative conditional use permits, and the Conditional Use Permit fee of $200.00 would be 
applied. With the passage of Ordinance #24-409 on November 18, 2024, all oil and gas drilling and 
production sites, including compressor stations, water handling facilities, and vertical wells, 
proposed to be located in any zoning district would require an administrative conditional use 
permit. With a new or amended administrative conditional use permit process, the Planning 
Division reviewed the option of a land use application fee for oil and gas drilling and production 
sites separate from the existing conditional use permit land use application fee. 
No fee has been collected for the submittal of a land use application regarding appeals of 
administrative land use decisions to the Duchesne County Planning Commission. With the 
expansion of administrative conditional use permits being required for oil and gas drilling and 
production sites in all zoning districts, an updated appeals process for administrative decisions is 
now adopted, and a land use application fee to be collected as part of this process, was deemed 
reasonable by the Planning Division. 
Also, while Duchesne County required a Floodplain Development Permit for the construction of 
structures within the 1% annual chance floodplain or for stream alterations due to development, 
no fee has been collected to process them. This has largely been due to the lack of accurate or 
reliable floodplain mapping in the County. With the recent Risk MAP project undertaken by the Utah 
Department of Emergency Management and funded by the Federal Emergency Management 
Agency, the 1% and 0.2% annual chance floodplains have been mapped in most of Duchesne 
County. The County’s flood damage prevention ordinance is now matched with data to indicate 
whether a proposed construction project or development would occur in the 1% annual chance 
floodplain, and the at what base flood elevation is estimated at each construction site. With these 
changes, the Planning Division anticipates an annual increase in the number of floodplain 
development permits and deemed it reasonable to review the addition of a floodplain development 
permit fee.

Fee Calculation Approach Process for 2025 
The Planning Division used Approach 3 (see page 1) in reviewing and amending its land use 
application fees, and also introduced new fees not previously collected. Specifically, each land 
use application item was reviewed using direct costs, supplies, and equipment, measured in costs 
per hour. Indirect costs were not included or taken into account in the calculation of these land use 
application fees in an effort to control costs. 
Fee = Direct Costs + Supplies + Equipment 
Direct costs are the wages and benefits for each Duchesne County staff position required as part of 
the land use application review process measured in costs per hour per land use application item. 
Any supplies or equipment required directly for the effective processing of each land use 
application item was also accounted for. The total annual cost of either the supplies or equipment 
was divided into the total annual hours per staff person, creating a per-hour supply or equipment 
cost. With this per-hour supply or equipment cost, the cost of supplies or equipment used per 
permit can be calculated based on the number of hours each permit item takes to complete per 
staff person involved. An exception to this is if a piece of equipment is used only as part of one 
specific land use application item and not another. In this case the cost of the equipment is divided 
into the total number of permits estimated for the specific land use application item for the year. 
Approach 3 was applied to reasonably estimate the costs of the following adopted and proposed 
land use application items: 
1. Minor Subdivision 
2. Plat Amendment (if public hearing required) 
3. Variance of Zoning Ordinance 
4. Zoning District Change or Text Amendment 
5. Floodplain Development Permit 
6. Conditional Use Permit 
7. Conditional Use Permit for Oil and Gas Drilling and Production Sites 
8. Appeal of Staff Decision to Planning Commission 
9. Appeal of Planning Commission Decision 
The Subdivision – Preliminary Plat and Subdivision – Final Plat fees, calculated on a per/lot basis 
was not subject to review as part of this analysis, but will be reviewed in fall 2025 as part of the 
Duchesne County Commissioner’s annual routine review of fees. 
Analysis Layout 
The estimated cost for each land use application item reviewed includes three (3) parts: Fee 
Estimate Overview, Fee Estimate Worksheet, and Permit Hours Worksheet. 
Cost Savings 
After estimated costs were calculated for each land use application item, the Planning Division 
made two recommendations to further save costs beyond the reasonable cost analysis. These are 
to reduce the estimated staff time required to complete each land use application item by 67% and 
to reduce the total fee for Appeals of Staff Decision to the Planning Commission by 60%. These 
recommendations are to ensure competition and affordability are maintained as part of the
